## THE FIVE RULES THIS OBEYS

1. **THE OPENING LAW** — *the title promises, the thumbnail amplifies, the opening fulfils it in the first frame.*
   Title = ENOCH. Thumbnail = the giants. **So the film opens on the giants.** Not on a monastery, not on a
   scholar, not on "for centuries, historians have wondered." Maximum intensity from frame one — the viewer is
   dropped into Act III of an epic already in motion. The buried-book context arrives at second nine, *after*
   the spectacle has earned the right to explain itself.
2. **THE ATTRIBUTION MOAT** — every supernatural claim is *the book describes / it says / the text records.*
   Nothing is asserted in our own voice. This is the YPP firewall and the channel's whole position: we are not
   preaching, we are reporting what a text claims. It is also, conveniently, more interesting.
3. **THE CURIOSITY ENGINE** — no question is answered. Every line opens a wider one, and the stack stays open:
   *what were they? who buried it? why? what did he see?* The last line closes nothing.
4. **PROSODY** — em-dashes and commas carry the contour; full stops are rationed and land like weights.
   Every number spelled out. Written for the breath.
5. **SACRED DAWN REGISTER** — wonder, not doom. Awe-and-spectacle. *Holy cow, look at THAT.* Zero banned words
   (no dread, reverent, solemn, grave, mournful).

**Why the flood goes first:** everyone already knows there was a flood. It is the one claim in the open the

**Why the buried-book line goes LAST (and reluctantly):** it is what converts spectacle into a *mystery*.
Without it you have a list of strange claims; with it you have a cover-up. At 0:58 you are trading the film's
frame for twenty seconds — only do it if the retention curve demands it.

**Do not cut the final paragraph.** It is the loop. See the last section of this doc.

## WHY THE LAST LINE IS THE LAST LINE

The Curiosity Engine says: *never answer a question without opening a larger one.* The whole open is a list of
extraordinary claims — and a list is closable. The viewer can hear "giants, angels, the moon, the dead" and
think *fine, I've got the gist.*

The final line refuses that. It moves the mystery **off the text and onto the people who buried it** — a new
question, larger than any claim in the list, and one the film's own title promises to answer. *Somebody decided
you should not read this.* That is not a fact about Enoch. That is a fact about **you**, and it is the only
loop in the open that a viewer cannot close by guessing.

It also does the channel's positioning work for free: we never asserted a single supernatural thing. We said
what the text says, and then we pointed at the silence around it.
