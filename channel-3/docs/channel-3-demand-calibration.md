# Channel 3 — Demand Calibration & Audience Reality Check
*Cross-checking the candidate works against actual YouTube demand — May 2026*

The strategy documents identified Saki, James, Glaspell, and the broader dramatic canon as the target source material for Channel 3. The competitive analysis confirmed the lane is empty. This document closes the loop: **does anyone actually search for these works on YouTube right now?**

Tested by direct YouTube search across all candidate authors and works, with attention to view counts, channel sizes, format types, and competitive saturation. The findings sharpen the launch sequence considerably.

---

## Headline finding

**Author recognition is real and asymmetric.** Some candidate authors have substantial existing YouTube audiences (M.R. James, Saki, Ibsen, Strindberg); others have minimal organic search demand (Glaspell, Bierce, Lord Dunsany). The Phase A launch sequence in the strategy doc treats them equivalently — the demand data says they aren't.

**The dominant existing format is audiobook with static imagery.** Across every author tested, the top-performing videos are audio narration with a single still image or slowly-panning artwork. Almost nothing produced is genuine cinematic adaptation. This is exactly the opportunity gap Channel 3 is designed to exploit, but it sharpens the value proposition: **Channel 3 isn't competing with cinema (there is none); it's the first proper cinematic alternative to audiobooks in this lane.**

**The "academic syllabus" dimension is significant.** Multiple candidate works appear on university English-literature syllabi globally — particularly in South Asian education systems (Pakistan, Nepal, India). This creates persistent organic search traffic for "summary" and "analysis" videos which drives view counts that have nothing to do with cinematic adaptation demand. Worth knowing because the raw view numbers are inflated by educational search relative to entertainment demand.

## Author-by-author demand snapshot

### M.R. James — strongest demand signal of all candidates

The most surprising finding. M.R. James generates **2,590,103 estimated results** on YouTube — orders of magnitude more than any other candidate. Top videos:

- *Classic Summer Ghost Stories* (M.R. James and others) — **1,065,512 views** (Bitesized Audio Classics, 3 years)
- *Classic Ghost Stories with Robert Powell BBC 1986* — **863,928 views** (2 years old, audio omnibus)
- *A Warning to the Curious with Christopher Lee* — **561,606 views** (BBC adaptation)
- *Ghost Stories of M.R. James: A Collection* — **295,825 views** (HorrorBabble, 7 years)
- *12 Hours of Scariest Stories* (Tony Walker channel, includes M.R. James) — **232,520 views**

The audience is large, active, and underserved by serious cinematic production. The Bitesized Audio Classics channel has built a substantial business on James audiobook compilations. The HorrorBabble channel produces atmospheric audio with static visuals. None of them are producing the photoreal AI cinematic recreation Channel 3 would.

**Strategic implication: M.R. James should be the channel's first non-Saki cluster, not video three or four.** Demand evidence is overwhelming. *Casting the Runes* alone has 91,435 views on a single audiobook channel. *Count Magnus* has 82,599. *The Mezzotint* and *Oh Whistle and I'll Come to You* are canonical ghost stories with sustained search demand decade after decade.

### Saki — strong author recognition, format-saturated by educational content

Saki's *Sredni Vashtar* shows ~2,026 total results. Top videos:

- *A Selection from Saki* — **106,170 views** (Bitesized Audio Classics, 3 years)
- *Sredni Vashtar by Alberto Laiseca* — **101,391 views** (Spanish narration, 16 years)
- *Alberto Laiseca - Sredni Vashtar* — **86,259 views** (Spanish, 17 years)
- *Sredni Vashtar by Saki* (David Bradley Film) — **38,197 views** (live-action short, 17 years)
- *Sredni Vashtar 1981* (Andrew Birkin film) — **32,178 views**

*The Open Window* generates ~18,343 results. Top videos:

- *Short Story - The Open Window by Saki* — **653,351 views** (13 years old)
- *The Open Doors / Future Shorts* — **535,712 views** (UK live-action adaptation, 2004)
- *La Ventana Abierta / The Open Window* — **500,100 views** (Argentine adaptation, 10 years)
- Multiple Class 8 educational summaries — **429k, 373k, 280k views**

**Strategic implication for Saki:** the audience exists, but a large fraction of search demand is academic-summary traffic (educational systems syllabus the story). Channel 3's adaptation needs to clearly *not* be a class summary — different thumbnail register, different title structure, signal cinematic adaptation immediately. The existing live-action adaptations (Birkin 1981, Future Shorts 2004) prove the cinematic appetite is there; they're just old and small in number. Channel 3 enters this gap directly.

### Susan Glaspell's *Trifles* — substantial demand but heavily educational

*Trifles* generates ~3,018 results. Top videos:

- *Concrete Timbre & d'moiselles NYC production* — **549,428 views** (13 years)
- *Edge Ensemble filmed production* — **520,128 views** (5 years, 4K)
- *Trifles Summary in Nepali (Gurubaa)* — **460,811 views**
- *Class 11 Compulsory English Summary* (multiple channels) — **241k, 227k, 213k views**

Important honest finding: *Trifles* is on the Nepal Class 11 English syllabus. A huge portion of *Trifles*' YouTube traffic is Nepali students searching for class summaries. The two top genuine theatrical recordings (549k and 520k views) prove the actual adaptation demand exists, but the raw view counts are inflated by educational search.

**Strategic implication:** *Trifles* still belongs in the Channel 3 launch sequence, but probably not as the very first video. It launches better as video three or four — once Saki and James have established the channel's cinematic identity and the audience is signalling that they want adaptation, not summary. Launching with *Trifles* could miscode the channel as another educational summary.

### Ibsen's *A Doll's House* — major theatrical canon, major demand

*A Doll's House* generates ~5,712 results. Top videos:

- *Center Players Presents Full Play* — **1,025,999 views** (live theatre, 10 years)
- *Full Audiobook* — **587,202 views** (12 years)
- *Animated Book Summary* — **445,607 views** (Book Tutor, 8 years)
- *Summer Light Theatre Full Play 2021* — **442,291 views** (4K subtitled)
- *Course Hero Summary* — **428,766 views**

The numbers prove that Ibsen has serious YouTube audience: million-view live theatre recording, half-million-view audiobook, multiple 400k+ summary videos. **The demand for Ibsen is real and substantial — but Ibsen is dialogue-driven drama and falls into Tier 3 of the genre matrix (needs Phase 5 multi-voice + lip-sync architecture).** Channel 3 cannot ship a satisfying *Doll's House* until those capabilities exist.

**Strategic implication:** Ibsen is the prize, not the launch. When Phase 5 architecture ships in 2027-2028, *A Doll's House* becomes the channel's defining work for the year. Save the strongest demand for the strongest production capability.

### Strindberg's *Miss Julie* — substantial demand including very recent breakout

*Miss Julie* generates ~3,554 results. Top videos:

- *1999 - A Dangerous Game of Class and Seduction* — **910,060 views** in just 3 months (full film upload). Genuinely recent breakthrough.
- *Miss Julie 2014 trailer* (Colin Farrell, Chastain) — **563,714 views** (11 years)
- *Miss Julie Royal Shakespeare Company* (Helen Mirren) — **178,140 views**

The 910k-view three-month-old upload is interesting — it suggests recent algorithmic interest in *Miss Julie* specifically. Possibly tied to a Broadway production or critical anniversary. Worth banking as a data point: the audience for serious dramatic cinema is not theoretical.

**Strategic implication:** *Miss Julie* is Phase 5 material — three characters with dialogue throughout, cannot work as pure narration. But the demand evidence is strong. Same conclusion as Ibsen: save for when the architecture supports it.

### Ambrose Bierce — *Owl Creek Bridge* shows modest but consistent demand

*Owl Creek Bridge* generates ~3,285 results. Top videos:

- *Chilling Tales for Dark Nights audiobook* — **291,477 views** (12 years)
- *Dog Island Creative adaptation* — **144,005 views**
- *Twilight Zone explainer* — **124,896 views**
- *A Hanging at Owl Creek Bridge* (fan film) — **104,761 views**

Lower numbers than M.R. James but a clear pattern: every major upload in this lane crosses 100k views. Bierce has a real audience, just smaller and more genre-niche (Twilight Zone fans, horror history enthusiasts). The famous Robert Enrico 1962 French short film is the canonical existing version most viewers know.

**Strategic implication:** Bierce is a legitimate Channel 3 candidate but probably video five or six rather than the launch. American Civil War setting also requires fresh rulebook accumulation (no existing moat from Final Hours' European/Pacific historical work). Defer until the channel has shipped and earned the right to a more demanding production.

### Wilde's *Importance of Being Earnest* — large audience, dialogue-locked

*Earnest* generates ~7,888 results. Top videos:

- *Miramax 2002 trailer* (Colin Firth, Rupert Everett) — **262,867 views**
- *Top 10 Notes by WatchMojo* — **164,210 views**
- *Wilde in 10 minutes English version* — **60,359 views**
- *National Theatre Live trailer* (2024) — **53,926 views**

The audience is large but the format is locked: *Earnest* is pure dialogue comedy. Cannot work in narrated form without losing the entire point. Like Ibsen, this is Phase 5 work — save for when lip-sync stabilises.

### Lord Dunsany — the negative finding

This was the one I most expected to surprise. *Lord Dunsany* generates ~2,965 results — substantial — but the top videos are very different from the cinematic adaptation lane:

- *A Brief Guide to Pre-Tolkien Fantasy* — **378,792 views** (video essay about Dunsany, not adaptation)
- *The 20th Century Renaissance Man* — **197,729 views** (biographical video essay)
- *Extra Sci Fi announcement* — **81,314 views** (channel pivot announcement)
- Actual story adaptations: **18,976**, **17,888**, **14,326**, **10,759** views

**Honest reading: Dunsany has a real audience but it's primarily a "learn about fantasy history" audience, not a "watch adaptations of Dunsany" audience.** The video essays about him outperform any actual adaptation of him by 20x.

**Strategic implication:** the "unfilmable canon" thesis (Dunsany, Hodgson, Chambers, Hope Mirrlees) was correct in spirit — these writers have imagination that exceeded their era's production technology and almost no serious modern adaptation. But the demand signal for adapting them is much weaker than the demand for *talking about* them. Channel 3 should not lead with Dunsany. Save the unfilmable-canon work for later when the channel has built its own audience that can be redirected to whatever Peter wants to make, rather than relying on existing organic search demand.

## The format saturation analysis

Across every author tested, the same pattern emerges:

**Dominant format: audiobook narration with static or slowly-panning still imagery.** Channels like Bitesized Audio Classics, HorrorBabble, Classic Ghost Stories Podcast, Mike Bennett, Tony Walker — all earning real audience by reading classic stories aloud over a single image (a candle, a bookshelf, an antique book cover). These channels have hundreds of thousands of subscribers and millions of cumulative views. They are *the existing competitive landscape* for Channel 3's source material.

**Secondary format: filmed stage productions.** Live theatre uploaded to YouTube. *Trifles* productions, *A Doll's House* productions, *Miss Julie* productions. Often hundreds of thousands of views. Production values vary from drama-school-quality to professional.

**Tertiary format: educational summary videos.** "Class 8 English Chapter 7" or "Course Hero analysis" — academic content driven by syllabus inclusion. Very large view counts but a different audience entirely.

**Nearly absent format: serious cinematic adaptation.** A handful of live-action short films from 10-17 years ago (Sredni Vashtar 1981, Open Window 2004 short, Owl Creek fan films). One or two recent indie efforts. *No AI cinematic recreation in any meaningful form across the entire candidate list.*

**The opportunity gap is precisely defined.** Channel 3 isn't competing for audience attention against existing cinematic adaptations (there aren't any). It's positioned as the cinematic alternative to audiobooks. The audience that currently watches a Bitesized Audio Classics audiobook of *The Open Window* on a Wednesday evening with a single image of a fireplace is the audience that will watch Channel 3's adaptation when it exists. They have demonstrated demand for these stories; they've never been given a cinematic option for them.

## Revised launch sequence — informed by demand

The original Channel 3 strategy proposed: Sredni Vashtar first, then Open Window and Mezzotint, then opening up to other authors.

Looking at the demand data, **a sharper launch sequence emerges:**

**Video 1: Sredni Vashtar (Saki).** The strategy doc had this right. Strong author recognition, ideal pipeline fit (Edwardian period the Final Hours rulebook already covers), short enough to be the establishing video. Demand signal good (~106k views on the leading audiobook channel). Existing competition is mostly old or amateur — the live-action 1981 Birkin film and 2007 student film are the strongest precedents.

**Video 2: Oh, Whistle, and I'll Come to You, My Lad (M.R. James) — promoted earlier in the sequence than originally planned.** The demand evidence for M.R. James is the strongest across all candidates by orders of magnitude. Audience size, channel ecosystem, sustained interest, multiple million-view existing audio versions. The first video establishes "cinematic AI adaptation of dark short fiction." The second video proves the channel can do the canonical M.R. James title — instantly recognizable to the existing audiobook audience. This is the breakout candidate.

**Video 3: The Open Window (Saki).** Returns to the cluster discipline. Two Sakis + one James in first three videos creates the "atmospheric Edwardian short fiction" identity. Open Window's strong demand signal (~653k on the top audiobook channel) confirms genuine audience.

**Video 4: The Mezzotint (M.R. James).** Doubles down on James cluster. *Mezzotint* is one of his most cinematic stories (the changing painting is genuinely visual) and the Bitesized Audio Classics version pulls 295k+ views across the collection. Channel 3's adaptation can immediately position as "the version this story has been waiting for."

**Video 5: The Interlopers (Saki) or Casting the Runes (M.R. James).** Continues either cluster. By video 5 the algorithm has enough signal about the channel's register; cluster choice depends on which earlier video showed strongest retention.

**Video 6+: Begin branching.** Trifles (Glaspell), An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge (Bierce), Riders to the Sea (Synge) — once channel identity is established. These have smaller direct demand signal but the channel's own audience can now be redirected.

**The Phase B transition (when Phase 5 architecture ships):** Open up to dialogue-driven dramatic adaptation. *Miss Julie* and *A Doll's House* become the breakthrough videos — by which point the channel has 12+ shipped videos, a recognizable identity, and an audience trained to expect serious adaptation. The 910k-view *Miss Julie* recent breakout suggests there's a hungry audience ready to be picked up.

## What this changes in the broader strategy

Five concrete updates worth banking:

**One.** M.R. James is more important than the strategy doc gave him credit for. He's the most-demanded author in the candidate list by a wide margin. The launch sequence should reflect his audience size, not treat him as equal to other Tier 1 candidates.

**Two.** The "unfilmable canon" angle (Dunsany, Blackwood, Hodgson) is artistically interesting but commercially smaller than expected. The audience is real but it's primarily a "fantasy history" audience, not an "adaptation" audience. Save these works for after the channel has built its own audience that can be redirected.

**Three.** Educational-syllabus inclusion inflates view counts for some works (*Trifles* in Nepal, *Open Window* in India, *A Doll's House* in international curricula). Worth understanding which audience is which when reading raw view numbers. *Trifles* genuinely has lower entertainment-adaptation demand than its raw numbers suggest.

**Four.** The dominant existing format (audiobook narration with static imagery) is the actual competitive landscape. Channel 3 doesn't need to displace existing AI cinematic literary adaptation channels (there are none). It needs to be the cinematic *upgrade* to audiobook channels — a clearly visible step up in production quality that the existing audiobook audience will recognize and migrate toward.

**Five.** The audiobook channels themselves are useful audience indicators. Bitesized Audio Classics, HorrorBabble, Classic Ghost Stories Podcast (Tony Walker), Mike Bennett — these are the active operators in the lane. Watching what *they* publish and which of their videos perform best is the highest-signal market research for Channel 3's source material selection. Worth a single afternoon of analysing their top-20 lists when the channel approaches launch.

## What to do with this document

This calibration sharpens the strategy without changing the destination. Channel 3 still launches with *Sredni Vashtar*. The dramatic-writing-corpus document still describes the full canon. But the sequencing and audience expectations are now grounded in actual demand signal rather than literary judgment alone.

Pin this document alongside the strategy doc. Re-read both when Channel 3 launch approaches. Update the audience demand numbers periodically — these are May 2026 snapshots; by launch they'll have shifted, and some sleeper authors may have emerged.

The genuine encouraging takeaway: **the audience is real, demonstrable, and currently underserved.** M.R. James alone generates 2.59 million YouTube results, with the top performers being audiobook narrations from one-person operators earning real money. The audience is there, the existing format ceiling is low, and the gap Channel 3 is designed to fill is precisely defined. The demand evidence supports the lane choice.
