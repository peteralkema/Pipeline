## The brand framing

Lazarus Films adapts public-domain dramatic writing — novels, plays, screenplays, short stories — into new audiovisual works. The name signals what the channel does: raises dramatic writing thought to belong to someone else and gives it a second life.

The three-channel arc, taken together:

- **Final Hours** — the end
- **Success Coach** — the journey
- **Lazarus Films** — the return

That's not a brand exercise. That's a worldview rendered as three channels: death, life, resurrection. Viewers feel the coherence even when they can't articulate it.

The strategic implication is that Lazarus Films is *not* trying to be the AI-slop answer to public-domain horror reboots ("Betty Boop slasher film," as one PD commentator put it). It's the dignified-register counter-position. The same craft discipline that gives Final Hours its moat — face-never-resolved, scene canons, object-substitution, named humans treated with weight — applies directly to literary adaptation. The brand promise is *resurrection of writing thought to belong to someone else, done with respect.*

## Festival target: Astana AI Film Festival 2026

The inaugural Astana AI Film Festival (AAIFF) is the strategic target for Lazarus Films' first submission. Several reasons converge to make it the right first festival:

**It's free.** Submissions for the 2026 edition are free — single YouTube link, no FilmFreeway fee. Most other festivals charge $20–$80 per submission. Free removes the cost-of-experimentation barrier completely.

**It's inaugural.** First-edition festivals have no incumbent prestige hierarchy. There's no roster of past winners to compete against. Curatorial process is still finding its level. The first edition is the moment to be on the slate, before the festival becomes harder to crack.

**The thematic fits.** The 2026 Thematic Competition theme is *"The Future Worth Living In."* Lazarus Films' premise — that great dramatic writing shouldn't get lost in the AI-cinema slop wave — is literally an answer to that question. A future where AI cinema produces dignified adaptations of public-domain literature rather than another shovelware horror reboot is a future worth living in. The thematic fit is direct, not stretched.

**The prize pool is real.** $1M USD combined across Thematic and Open competitions. Largest prize pool ever for AI-native cinema. Even semi-finalist recognition has career-leverage value.

**The timing works.** Submissions open May 25, 2026. Deadline August 15, 2026. Festival held October 26 — November 1 2026 in Astana, Kazakhstan. Works backwards to a clean production timeline (see Timeline section below).

**It's globally accessible.** "Open to participants worldwide, regardless of experience or access to professional studios." Explicitly welcomes individual creators. No prior festival credits required.

URL: https://aaiff.ai/

## Public domain inventory — what's available

The US public domain operates on a 95-year copyright term for works published 1928 onwards. Each January 1, a new year's worth of works enters PD.

### Already available (entered Jan 1, 2026 — works from 1930)

These are usable *now*. The Astana August deadline can be hit with any of these as source material.

**The Maltese Falcon** by Dashiell Hammett — *the strongest candidate*. The novel (not the 1941 Bogart film, which remains copyrighted as a separate work). Standard Ebooks describes it as "perhaps the best-known noir book — and film — of all time." Sam Spade. Iconic dialogue. Universal title recognition. Almost nobody outside the IP-law community has registered that the novel is now PD.

The "wait, how did he get the rights to that?" reaction is at maximum strength with this title. It's also a brilliantly adaptable text — hardboiled prose translates cleanly into dignified-register visual treatment. The challenge would be to make a *new* Maltese Falcon adaptation that doesn't try to compete with the Bogart film but instead returns to Hammett's prose voice.

**As I Lay Dying** by William Faulkner — Nobel-laureate literary classic. Fifteen-narrator structure built for dramatic monologue adaptation. The most prestigious available title. Less "wait, how?" recognition than Maltese Falcon but more literary credibility. A safer prestige play.

**The Murder at the Vicarage** by Agatha Christie — first Miss Marple novel. The Christie estate is famously protective of her work, which means for 95 years the assumption has been "you can't touch Christie." Now you can. Strong "wait, how?" reaction. Cosy crime register might not fit Lazarus Films' interior darker brand, but worth holding in reserve.

**The Secret of the Old Clock** by Carolyn Keene — first Nancy Drew novel. Less aligned with the dignified-interior brand, but historically important as the launch of one of the most enduring detective series.

**All Quiet on the Western Front** (1930 film) — Best Picture, Lewis Milestone direction. Already PD. Could become reference material rather than direct adaptation.

### Coming Jan 1, 2027 — works from 1931 (seven months away)

These can't be used for the August 2026 Astana submission, but matter for Lazarus Films' second and third films.

**Frankenstein** (1931 Universal film, Boris Karloff). The Mary Shelley novel has been PD since the 1800s, but the specific 1931 *film* — Karloff's monster design, the lab equipment, James Whale's direction, the cinematography — enters PD January 1, 2027. Peak "wait, how?" reaction available in the next two years. Universal has guarded the monster's image for decades; that protection ends.

**Dracula** (1931 Universal film, Bela Lugosi). Same situation.

**City Lights** (Chaplin) and **M** (Fritz Lang, Peter Lorre). Cinematic masterpieces entering PD as full films.

**The Waves** by Virginia Woolf — experimental modernist masterpiece. Six characters in interior monologue across a lifetime. Built for the dramatic-monologue adaptation form Lazarus Films can specialize in.

**The Good Earth** by Pearl S. Buck — Pulitzer Prize winner. Major literary work, less recognition than the headliners but serious adaptation material.

**Sanctuary** by William Faulkner — darker Faulkner. Available alongside *As I Lay Dying* in 2026, *Sanctuary* in 2027, *Light in August* in 2028, *Absalom Absalom* in 2032.

**The Loving Spirit** by Daphne du Maurier — *her debut novel*. This is the discovery from the conversation. Du Maurier's interior-gothic register (the same writer who later wrote *Rebecca*) becomes accessible January 1, 2027. The dark interior moral weight that connects to early McEwan (Cement Garden, First Love Last Rites, Comfort of Strangers) finds its public-domain equivalent in du Maurier's catalogue starting now and progressing year by year through the 1930s and 1940s. *Rebecca* itself (1938) enters PD January 1, 2034.

**As Time Goes By** — Herman Hupfeld's song, made famous by Casablanca. Soundtrack/music-bed possibilities.

## The McEwan / du Maurier interior-register thread

A surfaced insight worth banking: the writing register that gives Final Hours its moat — interior, controlled prose, moral weight, willing to sit with dread — exists as a tradition in English literature that Lazarus Films can directly inherit.

Living writers in this tradition (NOT available — alive, in copyright): Ian McEwan, especially his "Ian Macabre" early period (1975-1981 — *First Love Last Rites*, *In Between the Sheets*, *The Cement Garden*, *The Comfort of Strangers*). Cormac McCarthy. Kazuo Ishiguro.

Dead writers in this tradition entering PD on a rolling basis:

- **Daphne du Maurier** — *The Loving Spirit* (1931 debut) → PD January 2027. *I'll Never Be Young Again* (1932) → Jan 2028. *The Progress of Julius* (1933) → Jan 2029. *Jamaica Inn* (1936) → Jan 2032. *Rebecca* (1938) → Jan 2034.
- **Patricia Highsmith** — *Strangers on a Train* (1950) → Jan 2046. Later for the major work.
- **Shirley Jackson** — *The Lottery* (1948) → Jan 2044. *The Haunting of Hill House* (1959) → Jan 2055.
- **Graham Greene** — early novels from late 1920s entering PD now and over next few years.
- **Elizabeth Bowen** — entering PD soon.

The du Maurier inheritance is the cleanest near-term thread. A Lazarus Films channel that establishes the dignified-interior-register adaptation brand in 2026 (with Maltese Falcon as opener), then moves to du Maurier from January 2027 onwards, has a *coherent multi-year content runway* of natural source material. Each January 1 unlocks more of the catalogue.

This is the kind of long-arc thinking that compounds. The brand identity built around Maltese Falcon (interior, controlled, dignified) sets the expectation that pays off when du Maurier enters PD seven months later.

## Suggested first-three-films sequence

Working sequence for Lazarus Films' first six months:

**Film 1: *Sredni Vashtar* by Saki** — already in PD (Saki died 1916, all his work long since PD). Short story, controlled length, single setting, naturally adaptable to a 12-15 minute film. The protagonist's name (Conradin, 10 years old) and the central object (the ferret-god in the shed) are unforgettable. Already named in your multi-genre-script-architecture doc as the proof-of-concept.

This is the *technical proof* — does the Lazarus Films pipeline work? Does the dignified register translate from Final Hours' historical mode to literary mode? Does Phase 2 of your architecture (hierarchical scenes + variable shot duration) hold up?

**Film 2: *The Maltese Falcon* (selected scenes)** — Hammett, PD as of Jan 2026. Probably not the full novel — that's a feature film. A 15-20 minute Lazarus Films treatment of selected scenes: the office, the Spade-and-Cairo encounter, the closing exchange with Brigid. Dignified register, hardboiled prose returned to its source-text voice.

This is the *brand statement* — Lazarus Films can do hardboiled, can do iconic, can do "wait, how did he get this?" This is the film submitted to Astana.

**Film 3: *The Loving Spirit* by Daphne du Maurier** — opens immediately upon January 1, 2027 PD drop. Du Maurier's debut. Cornish coast, generational family saga, romantic-gothic register. The brand statement film for the 2027 PD cohort.

That sequence — Saki proof-of-concept, Maltese Falcon brand statement, Du Maurier on the day the cohort drops — establishes Lazarus Films as the *fastest* dignified-register adapter of major PD works. First mover on each year's calendar.
