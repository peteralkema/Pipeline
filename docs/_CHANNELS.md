### 0.2 · The packaging law (evidentiary — this is the highest-value section in the document)

*Derived from Scripture's four-cut Elijah natural experiment (05 Jul) and independently confirmed by Sacred Dawn's own before/after (17 Jul). **Two channels, two methods, same answer.***

**The Elijah four cuts — same story, four operators, ranked by outcome:**

| cut | thumbnail strategy | result |
|---|---|---|
| Power of the Word | **prestige portrait** — one beautiful hero face at an emotional peak, raven, blood-tear | **3.0M** |
| Unraveling | **stacked-spectacle poster** — hero + fire column + chariot in storm sky + ranked ravens | **2.7M from a TINY channel** |
| Black and White | **shock-claim text** — worst image of the four, but "HE KILLED 450 MEN" | **763K** |
| The Bible Journey | **inert portrait** — competent photoreal man, raven, canyon, nothing happening | **99K — THE LOSER** |

**There is no single winning composition. Poster-vs-portrait is the wrong axis.** Three strategies win — spectacle-stack, shock-claim text, prestige-face — and each beats an inert portrait decisively.

> **THE ONE LOCKED PRINCIPLE (negative, high-confidence): NEVER SHIP THE INERT PORTRAIT.**
> A figure doing nothing, with a label. No claim, no stacked event, no prestige grade. It is the single composition in the set that LOST.
> The dividing line: does the thumbnail contain **an unanswered question, a claim of scale, OR an exceptional emotionally-loaded face** — or does it just show a competent figure doing nothing? A face at a genuine emotional PEAK (terror, resolve, grief) is prestige-face and wins; a calm portrait is the inert loser.

**The confirming experiment (Sacred Dawn, 16–17 Jul):**

| | thumbnail | CTR |
|---|---|---|
| before | flat gold, no cool anchor, sky brightest, human dwarfed to invisibility | **1.4–1.9%** |
| after | warm/cool split, cobalt lightning, face brightest object, human legible | **6.3%** |

Same film. Same day. The only variables were a title and an image. **No pipeline work will ever beat that ratio.**

**The four laws that follow:**

1. **LIGHTING IS THE #1 CTR LEVER — warm/cool split.** The eye stops on the brightest, warmest point in a field of dark thumbnails. Make the hero's lit face and the fire the brightest, warmest things in frame; let the storm go cooler and deeper (blue/violet) around them. A hot **rim-light** forces separation from the dark sky. This is the §0.1 scrub at the frame level: bright hero against cooler storm, **never uniformly dark, never uniformly muddy** — and never uniformly *warm* either, which was Sacred Dawn's failure: all gold, no cool anchor, nothing to separate against.
2. **HERO SCALE IS A FEED-SIZE DECISION, NOT A BIG-SCREEN ONE.** "Man dwarfed by spectacle" is a big-screen virtue that HURTS legibility at 120px. A commanding mid-frame hero reads where a tiny one vanishes. **Dwarf-by-scale in the WIDE composition; keep the hero large enough to read at 120px.** Judge every candidate shrunk to ~120px — pick the one that survives the feed, never the prettiest at full size.
3. **TEXT GETS ITS OWN DEAD SPACE — never overlapping the face or the central action.** Reserve the zone; composite the hero to avoid it; keep the bottom ~15% clear for the mobile duration stamp.
4. **IDENTICAL TYPE ACROSS EVERY VIDEO *IS* THE SERIES BRANDING.** A generative model cannot give you identical twice — **six generations produced six different type treatments** (17 Jul, measured). Build the wordmark once as a transparent PNG and composite it. Deterministic Pillow compositing, not a prompt.

*Practical note (17 Jul): gpt-image renders type well enough to prompt with text baked in, and it fixed a tracking fault the compositing path had. But it drifts on face, outline weight and fill across generations. **Prompting type is acceptable for a one-off; the layer is what makes it a series.***

**Complement, never echo.** The image carries the *what*; the title carries the *why-click*. Different nouns. **The frame must promise the TITLE's video** — a Lincoln-speech frame under a battle title is a CTR–AVD mismatch.

**Banked frame-selection laws:** a reaction/rally beat beats a calm portrait (emotion stops scrolls) · high-arousal beats art-house grief (beauty loses the impression auction on a cold channel) · weather is free drama (storm, rain, lightning, god-rays) — cinematic runs stormy/blazing, never flat daylight.

### 0.3 · The title law

**Two clauses. Em-dash. Concrete noun in each. The second clause withholds.**

*Measured against the Enoch cohort, 17 Jul:*
```
"The Book of Enoch Describes What's Beneath The Ocean Floor — And Why God Sealed It There"   889K
"…the Terrifying Journey of the Soul After Death"                                            613K
"…What's Frozen Under Antarctica — And Why It Was Buried There"                              403K
"…What's Inside The Moon — And the Beings Who Operate It"                                    242K
```
**The stronger second clause names an OBJECT, not a reason.** "— And the Gates Hidden at Its Edge" beats "— And Why It Was Never Ours to Know."

**A colon is a label, not a hook.** "The Book of Enoch: The Oath They Swore on Mount Hermon" got 105 impressions in 18 hours. YouTube had nothing to match against demand.

Channel variants: **Scripture** leads with the named figure (recognition-first, drama-second — the name is the search/suggest floor). **Synthetic** uses famous proper noun + time/number anchor + stated catastrophe. **Sacred Dawn** uses the source + the withheld object.

**Names are not automatically hooks.** Sacred Dawn's four name-led posters ran 3.7–5.1% — Azazel, the most on-thesis subject, was the *worst* at 3.7%. The best (4.7% at ~2,700 impressions, the only figure above noise) was **Michael vs Lucifer — a conflict, not a portrait.**

### 0.4 · The attribution moat (biblical channels; the analogue binds Synthetic)

**Every supernatural claim stays attributed: *the text says · Enoch describes · the book claims*. Never assert in our own voice.**

> **The frame flirts with the claim; the narration never crosses into asserting it.**
> "Enoch describes the moon riding a chariot of wind, its light poured in through gates" — magnetic, true, shippable.
> "Enoch proves aliens built the moon" — banned.

**The attribution is what makes the aggression safe.** Be as magnetic as you want, as long as the sentence structure never leaves the text. This protects YPP and it is non-negotiable — **reject any retention advice that asks for a bald assertion.**

**Never fabricate scholarship.** The competitors' authority layer is partly invented; their researchers and measurements do not survive checking. **Verify every name, date and figure against a source before it enters narration.** A fabricated citation in a 24-minute film is a permanent liability. The real scholarship is better material anyway.

**Superlatives must be true.** Casualties stated as casualties, never inflated to deaths. Never claim resolutions the render doesn't have (no false "4K").

**Fidelity-of-intent, not fidelity-of-detail.** Convey the horror and its meaning faithfully; imply rather than depict. No gore, no gratuitous specifics. **If a story can't be told without graphic depiction, soften the frame, never the truth.**

### 0.5 · Lane discipline

**One-line stance test per video: *which crowd is this for, and does the framing stay in lane?*** Bleed kills the split.

```
AUDIENCE A — faithful / canon-affirming     → SCRIPTURE ON SCREEN
AUDIENCE B — origins / mystery / apocryphal → SACRED DAWN
SECULAR    — catastrophe / spectacle        → SYNTHETIC PRESS
```

**The Sacred Dawn boundary is the most important operational rule across these docs.** Scripture never touches the apocryphal/forbidden register — no "BANNED," no "the book they hid," no "NOT human," no sepia mystery wash. Those belong to Sacred Dawn. **Sacred Dawn and Scripture are sister channels: one pipeline, stance is the only divergence — a config flag, not a code fork.**

**The Enoch routing decision is on record and settled:** the Enoch/Nephilim/Watchers cluster is the hottest demand in the biblical lane (2.5M/5.6M/1.4M plus small-channel outliers at 19–51×) and it **collides with a faithful-canonical charter.** It goes to Sacred Dawn. Scripture does not chase it.

**The grades must be visually distinct in a feed.** That is the point of three palettes, and it is why no channel may borrow another's.

### 0.6 · The config law (rewritten 17 Jul — the rule had a hole in it)

> **NOTHING WRITES TO THE BOX. Not a human, not a script.**
> All config and code flows **laptop → git → box**. The box is pull-only, always.

**Why this is a rewrite, not a restatement.** The old rule said *never hand-edit the box* — and was followed literally. Then `_Scripture-On-Screen.md §9a` instructed: *"Config change = `python3 -c` one-liner on the BOX, never a hand-edit,"* and explicitly blessed *"a `git`-free box config edit."* A program did the writing, so nobody broke the rule as written.

**The cost, measured 17 Jul:** Synthetic's teal-orange grade — the entire de-Final-Hours cleanse for that channel — existed **only** on the box, uncommitted, from 13 Jul. Alongside it: eight days of shared engine work (wordless leg dispatch, canon token expansion, an ElevenLabs duration floor). All of it one disk failure from gone. The tell was unicode normalisation (`\u2014` → `—`) on lines whose meaning never changed — a human doesn't do that; `json.dump` does.

**The correct pattern:** patch scripts run on the **laptop**, against the laptop tree → commit → push → pull on box → **verify at the artifact on the box.** The verification stays on the box. The writing never does.

**If the box pull aborts:** `git diff` **first**, read it, then `git checkout --` only what you understand.

### Length doctrine — length is an output of retention, not an input
**Do not open with 30-minute long-form from a cold start.** The algorithm reads AVD as a **percentage**, not raw minutes. **A 12-min video held to 60% sends a far stronger signal than a 30-min video that dies at 35%.** Every early video auditions with zero behavioural history.

There's a production-risk leg too: a 30-min script is 180+ beats — more places to sag, and a long loop before you learn anything. **At 10–14 min you get 3–4× the at-bats per unit of compute and review attention. On a new channel, learning velocity beats episode grandeur.**

- **Phase 1 (videos 1–~15): 10–14 min.** The goal isn't views — it's a **retention baseline**: where the curve sits and *where it drops*. **The drops are the data.**
- **Phase 2 (once a video holds ≥50% AVD): extend deliberately.** Long-form is a Phase 2 weapon, not a Phase 1 bet.

**The counter-pressure, on record:** the niche skews long (Immersive runs 60–85 min from near-cold; long-form stacks ad breaks in a 5–11 RPM niche). But Immersive is documentary narration over loose visuals — cheap per minute, easy to pad. **We cannot out-length a documentary channel on a per-minute-expensive format; we out-execute it on hold rate. Play our game, not theirs.**

**As executed (18 Jun):** Esther is the 30-min flagship (the one deliberate exception). The ten-video batch ran 7–13 min. **The lean back half was shipped as-is rather than padded** — consistent with length-is-earned. **Beat-floor runtime ≈ beat count × ~14s, which exceeds the words/153-wpm estimate — count beats, not words.**

> **Instrumentation is non-negotiable, or Phase 1 teaches nothing.** Read the retention graph per video, log *where* the drops happen, feed it back. **The length decision and the retention-logging discipline are the same decision.**
