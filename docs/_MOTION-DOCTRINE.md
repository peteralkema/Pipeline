# _MOTION-DOCTRINE.md
### How to assign camera motion to each shot in an image-to-video montage
**Scope:** the `motion_prompt` on each beat when animating a *picked still* via Kling image-to-video. This is the exact procedure used to motion the Revelation and Catastrophes montages. It is deterministic — motion is *derived* from the shot, not invented per beat.

---

## THE ONE PRINCIPLE
**Motion is a function of the shot, not a choice.** Every clip's camera move is *read off* two things already true about the beat: (1) what the phenomenon is doing in space, and (2) the emotional register of the beat. You never sit and "think of a nice move" — you classify the shot, and the classification names the move. That is why it feels art-directed: it is not taste applied 40 times, it is one rule applied 40 times.

Motion has exactly one job in a music-driven montage: **give a still a slow living drift so it reads as a *shot*, not a slideshow — and let the move quietly amplify what the frame already means.** It is NOT there to add action. No characters running, no complex choreography — that is where AI drift lives. One primary motion, slow, subject locked.

---

## THE DECISION PROCEDURE (run this per beat, in order)

**Step 1 — Read the phenomenon's axis of energy.** Look at the picked frame and ask: *which way does the force in this image move, or want to move?*
- Force comes DOWN from above, or the subject rises/toweers vertically → **CRANE-UP** (camera rises with it).
- Force is a single overwhelming thing the viewer should be pulled *into* → **PUSH-IN**.
- The meaning is *how big / how many / how far it spreads* → **PULL-BACK** (reveal the scale).

**Step 2 — Override with emotional register if the beat is QUIET.** If the beat's job is stillness, grief, reverence, or aftermath, the phenomenon-axis rule is overridden:
- Eerie stillness / held breath (empty plague street, abandoned ruin, dead sea) → **NEAR-LOCKED** (almost no camera move; only ambient smoke/dust/water drifts).
- Reflection / aftermath / recovery / grief → **GENTLE SETTLE** (very slow downward drift and settle — a visual exhale).

**Step 3 — Default.** If a beat is neither vertical, nor a single overwhelming subject, nor a scale-reveal, nor quiet → **PUSH-IN**. Push-in is the safe, powerful default; it increases pressure on almost anything.

That is the whole procedure. Three questions, in order, and the first one that fires names the move.

---

## THE FIVE MOVES (the entire vocabulary — nothing else)

| Move | Use it when… | Why it works | Example beats |
|------|--------------|--------------|---------------|
| **PUSH-IN** (slow ease forward) | one overwhelming subject; awe, dread, divine encounter, a face | quietly increases pressure; pulls the viewer *into* the thing | the throne, the beast rising, the asteroid, a burning city |
| **PULL-BACK** (slow ease outward) | the meaning is scale/consequence/number | reveals "it's bigger than I thought" as the shot plays — the scale gag *in motion* | armies to the horizon, Babylon burning, a war cemetery, a ruined city |
| **CRANE-UP** (slow rise) | vertical phenomena; fire from sky, an eruption column, a descending city, a towering figure | the camera rising *with* the thing amplifies its height and majesty | the sky torn open, the woman in the heavens, New Jerusalem descending, a volcano |
| **SETTLE** (very slow drift down, near-locked) | reflection, aftermath, recovery, grief | reads as an exhale; closes a section down | dawn over ruins, ruins reclaimed by nature, a frozen retreat, a famine line |
| **NEAR-LOCKED** (static, ambient only) | eerie stillness, held breath, the quiet-is-a-weapon beat | stillness is a weapon; a push here would *break* the silence | empty plague streets, abandoned Pripyat, the dead Aral Sea, a silent pithead |

---

## THE HARD RULES (what makes it look intentional, not AI)

1. **One primary motion per beat.** Never two moves. One, slow, plus incidental ambient motion (cloth, smoke, dust, water). Simultaneous complex motion is the #1 hallucination cause.
2. **Slow is fast.** Every move is *slow* — eases in, never abrupt. Slow reads as expensive, gives the eye time, and reduces AI drift. A weighted 40kg camera, never a snap.
3. **Lock the subject; move the camera.** The subject barely moves; the camera and the environment carry the motion. A subject drifting through frame reads as error.
4. **Never push-in on silence.** The single most important override. The quiet beats (near-locked / settle) are *ruined* by a push-in — the held breath is the point. This is the rule that separates a doom-reel from something with soul.
5. **Match the move to the meaning, never to a timer.** Crane because the fire *descended*, pull-back because the story *widened* — motivated, never mechanical. Timer-driven variety is the metronome problem in disguise.
6. **The still is locked first.** Because this is image-to-video off a *picked* frame, the composition can never drift — Kling only adds the motion. So the framing you chose is safe; only the move is variable. (Pick the still first, always.)

---

## WHY IT WORKS AS A WHOLE (the montage-level effect)
Because the move is *derived from the shot*, the motion **rotates automatically** across the film — push, pull, crane, settle, locked — exactly as the shots rotate. That rotation is what keeps a montage alive: the eye never sits in one kind of motion, the quiet beats become breaths between the loud ones, and the whole thing *breathes*. You did not choreograph 40 moves; you applied one rule 40 times, and the rule's variety came from the shots' variety. That is the crux: **stunning per-shot motion is a deterministic read of the shot, not a per-shot decision.**

---

## THE PROCEDURE IN ONE LINE
> For each beat: is it vertical? → crane. One overwhelming subject? → push. About scale? → pull. Quiet/reflective? → near-locked or settle. Else → push. Slow, one move, subject locked, never push on silence.
