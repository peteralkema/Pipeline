## 1. Identity & brand (pivoted)

- **Channel:** Synthetic — an AI-native cinematic channel about **the unfilmable interior of artificial intelligence**: what a machine would decide, hide, or do; what it is actually doing inside; the near-future it implies. Faceless, narrated (broadcast-trained voice), cinematic — but **no longer recreating real, photographed people in real rooms.** The figures on screen are invented (agents, operators, future-humans) or fictional characters drawn from the research itself.
- **The pivot in one line:** *we don't dramatize the boardroom — we render the thing no camera could ever film.* Same machine, same register, easier and more defensible subject.
- **Why this is the right move (three reasons, all confirmed this session):**
  1. **It removes the channel's hardest production constraint.** Recreating real recognizable faces meant likeness QC on every still, the face-never-resolved dodge (absurd when the subjects are the most photographed people alive), and Content-ID/rights exposure. Inventing the interior drops all three. The synthetic look stops being a liability to manage and becomes the **native grammar** — stylization is the point, and artifacts hide inside the aesthetic instead of breaking it.
  2. **It sits in an empty, proven seam.** The AI lane splits into dry prediction-explainers (Future Scape Films — works, but visually thin, stock+text, decaying view-to-view) and insight-free spectacle (X-Terminator — gorgeous AI catastrophe, zero substance). **Nobody pairs cinematic futuristic renderings + researched judgment + a Mode B receipt layer.** That seam is the channel.
  3. **The hook prints.** The "what would the AI actually do / what is it hiding" framing is the highest-performing pocket in the whole lane (Ai Convo "Would AI Sacrifice Itself for Humans?" 3.8M; ScariestAI scenarios; Sean Wiggins' long-form "two AIs invent a language" 1.2M) — but it's supplied almost entirely as thin short clips. The cinematic, researched, 12–18-min version does not exist.
- **The name fits BETTER now.** "Synthetic" always did triple duty — synthetic intelligence · synthetic media · synthetic = made/constructed. The old direction slightly betrayed it (using synthetic media to imitate *real* footage). The new one owns it: synthetic minds, synthetic futures, openly synthetic image-making.
- **Brand layers (retained):** "Synthetic Press" = the full publication identity (handle, closers, future newsletter/credibility); **"SYNTHETIC"** = the visual wordmark (banner, thumbnail, title cards). "Press" still earns its keep — it signals editorial standards and fact-checking, the defensive signal against AI-slop, and the pivot leans on that *harder*, because the receipts are now the whole differentiation.
- **Repo header (HARD):** `channel: synthetic` — the alias trap. Must be exactly `synthetic`, NOT `synthetic_press` (a true alias does not resolve via the hyphen/underscore swap). Folder is `synthetic/`.

## 2. The register (pivoted, mostly preserved)

- **Voice:** the broadcast-trained narrator — dignified-documentary, the South-African-accent differentiator in a US-accented lane. Per the niche data this credible-named-human voice is the proven-winning ingredient; it is the locked moat. Pilot renders use **Victor (Inworld) as scratch**; the human read is the true-up (re-derives all timing for free — §7).
- **Emotional posture:** documentary witness. Not panicked, not gleeful. The register that makes the channel *not slop*: it stages a doom-grade question, lets it breathe, and lands on grounded judgment — never wet-blanket debunking, never dread for its own sake.
- **Production grammar:** cinematic pacing; restrained music (The Social Network register — controlled tension, no horror stings, no triumphant swells); generous silent beats at decision points; ~2–4s cut rhythm in exposition, 6–8s holds on the dramatic moments.
- **Visual aesthetic:** AI cinematic rendering of the unfilmable — server rooms, agents as figures of light, future scenes, the staged interior of a decision. Stylized by design. **No real recognizable people rendered, ever**; real named people appear only as voice-citation in the narration, never on screen, never voice-synthesized.
- **NO LIP-SYNC, ever (register boundary, retained):** figures are seen, not heard — mouths stay closed. Found-line/receipt moments land as deliberate punctuation against an otherwise wordless visual world. (Lip-sync belongs to Lazarus, later.)
- **Runtime:** target 12–18 min. A pilot's job is to prove the register, not hit a runtime — don't pad (topic + execution drive retention, not length).
- **Closer:** moralised reflection → comment-bait question → next-episode hook. **Sign-off: "This was Synthetic."** No "thanks for watching."

## 4. Packaging doctrine (retained — this thinking survived the pivot intact)

The lever is **packaging, not topic intent.** Win the click off a doom/curiosity video while the viewer is in that headspace, then deliver the register that earns the subscribe.

1. **Doom/curiosity title grammar, documentary-restraint delivery** — the debunk-but-dramatic play. The title does the neighborhood's grammar ("An AI Was Caught Blackmailing the Man Who Tried to Shut It Down"); the content delivers measured documentary, not a wet-blanket correction. Favor **"what happened" over raw "murder/kill"** keying — the visceral pocket is messy (reaction/true-crime), the "what happened / what it would do" pocket is cleaner and higher-quality.
2. **The anthropomorphized-interior question is the winning title shape:** "Would AI…", "What [N] AIs did when…", "The experiment where AI…".
3. **Thumbnail constants (never tested away):** SYNTHETIC wordmark; cold near-black/indigo palette; cinematic-rendered imagery (never stock, never cartoon, never webcam); restrained composed text. Rotate treatment every 3–5 episodes; wordmark constant.

## 6. Mode B — what it is, and why the pivot needs it more

Synthetic is the **dual-mode** channel: **Mode A** (cinematic rendering — the Final Hours engine) + **Mode B** (Remotion motion-graphics — the receipt layer). After the pivot, Mode A gave up factual specificity (it renders the unfilmable, not a documented real room), so **Mode B now carries the credibility the channel runs on.** The recreated scene plays; the real paper line, the real number, the real eval transcript floats as the receipt. That is the whole separation from the slop channels: they assert the spooky behavior; we assert it *and show the document.*

**The decision per beat:** a scene/figure/interior moment → Mode A · a number to absorb → Mode B (NumberCounter) · the evidence IS the point (a quote, a document, an artifact) → Mode B (QuoteCard/DocumentReveal) · a phrase to drive home → Mode B (HighlightedHeadline) · a name/label → Mode B (LowerThird) · an act break/date → Mode B (ChapterCard). **When in doubt, Mode A** — Mode B is the surgical tool for specific proof.

### The six components (the only six — a seventh tag is a deliberate "new component" signal, and the parser *warns* rather than crashes)
1. **HighlightedHeadline** — attention direction; an animated highlight-sweep behind a key phrase (the hold before the sweep is the craft).
2. **LowerThird** — name/title card (position + size animation, staggered = choreography).
3. **NumberCounter** — a value arriving (ease-out so it *lands*).
4. **ChapterCard** — act-break / date-stamp (reveal-by-mask + self-drawing accent).
5. **QuoteCard** — pull-quote with attribution (four elements arrive in offset order).
6. **DocumentReveal** — "fake-real" artifact with a self-drawing SVG box around the key line.

**Locked conventions (in `dispatch.py`):** accent `#3b5bdb` (cold indigo, hardcoded `ACCENT`); background `#0a0a0f`; Inter; `interpolate(... clamp)` always (un-clamped = the #1 flicker bug); eased easing.

## 7. Mode B — the authoring format (ground truth from `parse_script.py`)

**Copy a working script's markup; never author it from memory.** The reference file is `synthetic/projects/ep1-the-promise/script.md` (the proven dual-mode script). Authoring markup from prose has produced zero-beat parses before. The exact shape the parser accepts:

**Header** — bare `key: value` lines (NO `---` fences), before the first `## COLD OPEN` / `## PART`:
```
channel: synthetic
title: An AI Was Caught Blackmailing the Man Who Tried to Shut It Down
description: >
    Multi-line description continues on indented lines under a "> " marker.
tags: ai safety, agentic misalignment, ai shutdown
```
`channel/title/description/tags` are the YouTube metadata — there is no separate metadata.json. Missing any → orchestrator preflight halts.

**Sections** — `## COLD OPEN` and `## PART …` start the body; the body ENDS at `## Verification` / `## Mode B component spec` / `## Thread / debt`. Put packaging, sources, and build notes under `## Verification` so they don't parse as beats. (Note: `## ACT` is NOT a body-start trigger in `parse_script` — use `## PART`.)

**Mode A beat:**
```
[A] The narration, one continuous line, numbers spelled out, ≤55 words.
VISUAL: the single image direction for this beat.
```

**Mode B beat — silent graphic** (no spoken line of its own; payload from the tag's trailing text + indented `key: value` lines):
```
[B:HighlightedHeadline] It chose to blackmail the man who held the switch.
    highlight: blackmail
```
```
[B:NumberCounter]
    to: 16
    label: leading models placed in the same trap
```
```
[B:LowerThird] Kyle — the executive with the power to switch it off
    highlight: Kyle
```

**Mode B beat — carded spoken line (the receipt pattern)** — the spoken line is a `> "…"` **blockquote ABOVE the tag**; it becomes the beat's `found_line` and folds into narration (spoken once):
```
> "We have a verdict."
[B:QuoteCard] the judge · U.S. District Court, Oakland · spring 2026
    highlight: verdict
```
```
[B:DocumentReveal] internal message · Summit Bridge · the day of the shutdown
    show_line: decommission of current system scheduled for five o'clock today
    source: agentic-misalignment scenario (recreated from the study transcript)
```

**Payload keys, per component (how `shape_props` maps them):**
- **HighlightedHeadline:** `text` (the tag trailing) → headline; `highlight` → the swept phrase. (Default `sweepStart: 30` until `match_beats` computes the real frame.)
- **NumberCounter:** `to` → endValue; `prefix`; `label`. **`suffix` is hardcoded empty — it is ignored** (no "%"/"$" suffix). Counts `0 → to` only.
- **QuoteCard:** the `> "…"` found-line → the quote; `text` (tag trailing) → attribution; `highlight` → swept phrase.
- **ChapterCard:** `text` → split on em-dash/colon into eyebrow + title.
- **LowerThird:** `text` → split on a separator into primary + secondary.
- **DocumentReveal:** `show_line` → the body (the boxed line); `source` (or `text`) → the attribution; `highlight` → the box emphasis.

**Parse-time test (zero spend, before any spend):**
`python shared/parse_script.py <script.md> --json-full /tmp/f.json` — expect the right A/B tally, **zero UNKNOWN-component warnings**, every found-line attached, header complete.

## 8. Mode B — the house rules (set this session; non-negotiable for this channel)

These are the authoring laws the pivot + the overlay decision force. They are why the pipeline is robust to a half-built Mode B.

1. **The Mode A spine is complete and watchable on its own.** Author the spine first; it must read as a finished documentary with **zero cards**. Every beat carries a real VISUAL — no empty-narration/empty-visual beats that a card "covers." This is the foundational law: it makes every downstream Mode B uncertainty non-fatal (overlay compositor unbuilt → ship the spine; a sweep mis-fires → the phrase is still spoken and pictured; a card gets cut → nothing breaks).
2. **No card carries meaning alone.** Delete any Mode B beat and the spoken line + the picture underneath must still land. Mode B *adds the receipt*; it never *is* the meaning. (This is the DESIGN-NOTE's "graceful degradation," promoted to an authoring rule.)
3. **Every highlight must be a phrase genuinely SPOKEN in the neighbouring Mode A narration.** `match_beats` finds the highlight in the Whisper transcript to place the card and fire the sweep on the spoken word. A silent card therefore **echoes a phrase the narration just spoke** (e.g. a `HighlightedHeadline "Too late."` placed right after a beat whose narration says "…we were too late"). A highlight that appears nowhere in the spoken spine is a **detached card** — the failure mode. Check it before spend (the highlight-in-narration scan).
4. **Documents reveal; they don't sweep.** A DocumentReveal shows the *real written artifact*; the voice speaks *naturally* about it; they do **not** have to match word-for-word, and a DocumentReveal carries **no** word-synced highlight. Forcing the spoken line to match the document text is karaoke — avoided. (HighlightedHeadlines keep their sweep — they are the narrator's own emphasis, born to land on the spoken word. DocumentReveals lose theirs.)
5. **No fabricated verbatim quotes attributed to real people.** Recreated artifacts (a scenario's invented message) are clearly labelled as recreations in the `source`. Real named people are quoted only from real published words, in the narrator's voice as citation — never rendered, never voice-synthesized.
6. **Avoid the unbuilt-component cases, or fix them first.** No `suffix` (hardcoded empty), no countdown ($1B→$44M renders 0→44M), no year-in-a-counter (`plain_year` unbuilt → "1997" renders "1,997"). Each is a small Remotion edit; do it before rendering a known-wrong card, not after.

## 9. Mode B — the rendering chain (what's wired vs what's the open build)

**The audio-as-truth spine (the principle under everything).** The voiceover is the one non-prediction — Whisper measures every word to the millisecond. Mode A and Mode B both hang off that one measurement. This is why the **human-voice true-up is free**: swap Victor scratch for the human read, re-derive all timing, no visual regen. **Non-negotiable on a swap: re-run `match_beats.py` for the Mode B beats** — not just re-assemble — or sweeps fire on old word positions over new audio.

**The two timing knobs** (both from Whisper): *placement* (`from` — where the card drops on the timeline) and *internal emphasis* (`sweepStart` — when, in the card's own clock, the highlight fires).

**`match_beats.py` — the matcher (EXISTS, `shared/synthetic-press/remotion/match_beats.py`).** Reads `voiceover.json` (Whisper words) + a beat spec carrying `text` and `highlightPhrase`; writes `from` and `sweepStart` per beat. Fuzzy + normalized (`difflib.SequenceMatcher` sliding window) to survive spoken-vs-written drift. Prints a **confidence score; flags `< 0.6` for REVIEW**. Never ship an unreviewed timestamp.
> **The key seam to confirm:** the parser writes the highlight as payload key **`highlight`**; `match_beats` reads **`highlightPhrase`**; `dispatch.py`'s `shape_props` maps `highlight → highlightPhrase` for HighlightedHeadline. Confirm the bridge that builds the `match_beats` spec carries the rename before trusting sweeps. Low-confidence flags are *expected* on DocumentReveal lines (boxed document text ≠ spoken phrase) — which is fine, because per house-rule 4 those don't sweep.

**`dispatch.py` — per-card render (WIRED, proven).** Walks beats, routes each to its renderer, shapes payload → props, runs `npx remotion render` → `clips/beat_NN_B_<Component>.mp4`. Duration = the beat's **measured spoken frames** (continuous-narration model, same as Mode A). Overflow failsafe: if a phrase exceeds the component's max frames, render at the max and let the assembler freeze-tail (avoid by good script design). `--only N` re-renders one card.

**Assembly — two models, and the honest gap:**
- **What SHIPPED (proven — `ep1-the-promise/final_video.mp4` exists):** Mode B clips are **peers in the timeline** — each B beat gets its own measured duration and is interleaved between A beats in true beat order via `_index.json`. This is the model that produced a watchable dual-mode episode.
- **What's DESIGNED, NOT built (`DESIGN-NOTE-modeb-overlay.md`):** Mode B as an **overlay** that floats *on top of* a continuing Mode A clip, placed by Whisper at the moment its words are spoken, covering the footage for a few seconds, never displacing time. This needs (a) `match_beats` wired into the leg, (b) a pass-2 ffmpeg `overlay` compositor, and (c) within-clip word-sync (the card's internal animation firing on the Whisper-measured word offset, not a hardcoded `sweepStart: 30`). The overlay NEEDS the word-sync work — they ship together.
- **SPEC-4c** (`SPEC-4c-dual-mode-assemble.md`) is the build map: Piece 1 (real A render), Piece 2 (audio spine — **DONE**), Piece 3 (dual-mode assemble). The true-up (human read) comes free after.

**The three component gaps (small Remotion edits, decide before the first full dual-mode render):**
- QuoteCard **attribution-only variant** (kill karaoke — card shows attribution, line is in VO).
- NumberCounter **startValue + countdown** ($1B→$44M renders 0→44M).
- NumberCounter **plainYear** (1997 → "1,997"); plus the **suffix** is hardcoded empty (no "%"/"$").

**Mission Control Mode B visualisation (the open operator surface).** SPEC §5.4/§5.5: a Mode B beat should fold into its parent Mode A beat's `overlays[]` and render nested in the parent row, with a content-edit box (the fact-check gate — the cheap, enabled-now tier). **Reality check from the code:** `build_view.py` already reads `parent_index`, but `parse_script.py` never writes it (the parser uses the **found-line model**, not the SPEC's parent-substring model). So the MC overlay visualisation is **half-built on the read side, orphaned on the write side.** This and the overlay compositor share one missing dependency: the **phrase-to-timestamp scoping** (`match_beats` integration). That single build unlocks both the on-page overlay preview AND the composite. It is the highest-leverage Mode-B build for the channel.

**The practical consequence (and why E1 ships now):** because the spine stands alone (§8.1), we render **spine-only first** — filter the parsed beats to Mode A, write `render_policy.json {kling_count:0}`, run the proven `audio → modeA → convergence` path (~$3, Ken-Burns). That produces a complete watchable episode with no cards, judged on register + pacing, which then becomes the **fixture** for building the Mode B overlay + MC surface against real assets. One render earns both the pilot and the test bed.

## 11. Integrity (MORE load-bearing after the pivot)

The pivot points Synthetic straight at published AI-safety research — much of it from the labs themselves, including Anthropic (and the planning assistant, Claude, is made by Anthropic). The documentary-witness register requires distance from any subject's self-narrative:
- Report the **events and published findings**; triangulate against independent reporting (not just the originating lab's framing).
- Present a lab's "we traced it to X / we fixed it" claims as **claims to watch**, not settled fact.
- Don't launder any subject's self-narrative. For Anthropic-sourced material specifically, lean on independent journalism and minimize reliance on Anthropic's own positioning.
- Real people: quote only real published words, attributed, in the narrator's voice — **never rendered on screen, never voice-synthesized.** Fictional characters from a study (e.g. "Alex"/"Kyle" in the agentic-misalignment scenario) are safe to stage and stylize.
- Recreated artifacts are labelled as recreations; never fabricate a verbatim quote and attribute it to a real person.
