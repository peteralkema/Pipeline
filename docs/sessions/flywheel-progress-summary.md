*4 July 2026 · Peter Alkema, The Hague*

## The operation in one line

One person, one channel-agnostic Python pipeline that turns a script into a finished narrated, scored, packaged video — and the same machine serves twelve channels across different niches. The bet is that **the production system is the moat**, not any single channel. A new channel costs one config file, not new code.

The system is now proven at two scales: a **55-video batch across six channels ran in one unattended sweep** (54/55 clean), and the same pipeline rendered its **first two feature-length films** (75 and 70 minutes) end to end with no format-level break. Marginal cost per video is a few dollars; the real constraint is craft attention, not compute.

Topic and niche selection is not guesswork — **Leo's criteria are applied diligently to every channel**: under 100K subscribers, started one to four months ago, at least one viral video past 200K views, a visibly growing trajectory, and a format the pipeline can recreate. The edge is best-execution in evergreen, already-served lanes, not spike-chasing; NexLev and Google Trends are treated as lagging detail signals, never as the topic-picker.

## What the numbers actually say

**The "no distribution" worry is dead.** Sacred Dawn pulled 60k impressions, You Had To Be There 43k, Woodworking 32k. YouTube is *serving* these channels heavily — reach was never the problem. That collapses the whole question down to CTR and retention doing their job once impressions are handed out, which is exactly where a packaging-first operation should want its bottleneck. The algorithm is giving me at-bats.

**Two opposite failure modes across the portfolio.** Sacred Dawn and Scripture On Screen have elite retention (5:10 and 7:29 AVD on long-form is genuinely strong) but mediocre CTR (3.4%, 2.8%) — thumbnails and titles are under-converting content that clearly satisfies once clicked. That's the *good* problem: the expensive half (retention) is solved, the cheap-to-iterate half (packaging) is the lever, and packaging is near-zero marginal cost to keep testing. You Had To Be There is the mirror image — strong CTR (4.8%) but weak-for-the-view AVD (3:25), so packaging pulls clicks the content doesn't fully pay off, which is why its spike didn't convert to sustained suggested traffic (14% suggested vs Sacred Dawn's 67%). Opposite fixes: rework thumbnails on the first pair; tighten the open and mid-retention on the second.

**Final Hours is the one genuinely concerning channel.** 2.1% CTR, 2:22 AVD, impressions down 20%, views down 33%, subs down 67% — the sole channel where both levers underperform *and* YouTube is throttling impressions in response. It's also the designated flagship with the most craft attention, which makes it the clearest signal in the set: effort isn't the missing input. Working hypothesis from the retention gap is that the cold open runs too long before the hook lands — but that's a "pull the intro-retention curve" question, not a guess to act on.

**Success Coach's 6.4% CTR is the highest in the portfolio and means nothing for the flywheel** — a legacy channel with a subscriber base clicking out of habit, 2% suggested, flat. Now demonetized; its real value is as the external ebook-funnel testbed, not a content signal.
