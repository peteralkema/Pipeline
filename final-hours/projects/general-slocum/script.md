description: On a bright June morning in 1904, more than a thousand people — almost all women and children from a German immigrant church in New York — boarded the steamboat General Slocum for a day's outing. A fire broke out minutes into the trip. The life preservers were rotten, the fire hoses useless, the crew untrained, and the captain steered for an island instead of the nearest shore. It became the deadliest disaster in New York City until the September 11th attacks. This is the documented story of the General Slocum, and how a single morning destroyed an entire community. ## PART ONE — The Failures

[A] When the fire took hold, the crew reached for the fire hoses. They came apart in their hands. The hoses had rotted, never tested, never maintained. They burst and fell to pieces under the water pressure. The boat's first line of defence simply did not exist.

[A] So the people reached for the life preservers. And here was the cruellest failure of all. The cork inside them had rotted to powder over the years. [A] The scale and the sheer preventability of it forced change. ## CLOSE

[A] The General Slocum is a lesson about the danger that hides in plain sight — in the things we assume are safe because someone, surely, must have checked them.
