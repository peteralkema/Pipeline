## PART ONE — The Abandonment

[A] When the second relief ship failed to arrive, the rules the expedition had been given left them no choice. If no ship came, they were to abandon their base and retreat south by boat and sledge, to a point where supplies were supposed to be waiting and a ship might find them. So, as the brief summer ended, they left.

[A] It was a desperate journey. They worked their boats south through the breaking ice, dragging and rowing and hauling, racing the freeze. They reached the area where supplies were meant to be cached — and found almost nothing. The depots that should have sustained them were far smaller than promised, or simply not there.

[A] So they were trapped. Winter was coming down, the sea was freezing solid, and they were stranded on a barren cape with food for a fraction of the months they would have to wait. They built a low shelter of stone and an overturned boat and canvas, and they prepared to endure the dark.

## PART TWO — The Starving Winter

[A] The food ran lower and lower. The men cut their rations, and cut them again, until each was living on a few ounces a day — then less. They ate everything they could find. ## PART THREE — Rescue

## PART FOUR — Naming Them

[A] Nineteen men of the Greely Expedition died on that frozen cape, of cold and hunger and the failure of the ships that should have brought them home. They were scientists and soldiers who had gone north to expand human knowledge, and were left to starve at the top of the world.

[A] We name the absence — nineteen men, each with a name, a home, a reason for going, who held their discipline and their dignity through a winter-long death that almost no one could imagine enduring. They were not lost to a single storm. They were lost, slowly, to being forgotten just long enough.
