## Structural skeleton (5 beats, ~12-15 min)

### Beat 1 — Cold open: the empty ship (0:00–1:30)
*Mystery anchor. Object-substitution. No humans visible.*

December 4, 1872. The Dei Gratia, working its way across the North Atlantic, spots a brigantine moving strangely. Captain David Morehouse orders a boarding party.

Beat content: The boarding party climbs aboard. The wheel turning to nothing. Ropes uncoiled but not tangled. Sails partly set, partly torn. Below: water in the hold but not enough to sink. Food and water stocked for weeks. Cargo (1,701 barrels of industrial alcohol) untouched. Captain's cabin: clothes still folded, charts in place, pipes on the desk. The chronometer disturbed. Navigation tools missing. The yawl gone from the davits, neat tackle behind it.

Closing beat 1 line: *"The last entry in the logbook was dated ten days earlier. After that, the writing simply stopped."*

### Beat 2 — The pivot (1:30–2:00)
*The reframe. Cold-open contract gets a new question.*

This is the load-bearing moment for retention. The Hindenburg lesson says the 1:30–2:00 window decides whether viewers stay.

Pivot content: *"The story everyone tells about the Mary Celeste is the story of an empty ship. But it leaves someone out — the one person who lived the longest with what happened on board. He wasn't on the ship. He was seven years old, in Massachusetts, waiting for letters that never came."*

Cut to: a small house in Marion, Massachusetts. A boy at a desk. School books. A grandmother in the kitchen.

### Beat 3 — Who they were (2:00–6:00)
*The biographical engine. Named humans. Sensory writing distributed across locations.*

Captain Benjamin Spooner Briggs, 37. Long line of sea captains. Religious. Did not drink (carrying a hold full of alcohol). Had invested his savings in the ship.

Sarah Elizabeth Briggs, his wife. Minister's daughter. Had honeymooned at sea. Sailed before, capable, calm.

Their two-year-old daughter Sophia. On board for what was meant to be a family passage to Genoa.

Their seven-year-old son Arthur. Left in Massachusetts with his grandmother — so he could go to school.

Sarah's last letter to her mother-in-law (preserved): *"Tell Arthur I shall depend on his writing me. I shall try to remember things to tell him."*

The voyage they expected to have. The harbor at Staten Island. Bad weather delaying their departure. The final letter sealed and sent ashore. The ship sailing on November 7, 1872.

Beat 3 sensory distribution: Massachusetts schoolroom (Arthur), the Marion grandmother's kitchen (the letters arriving), Staten Island harbor (the departure), the Mary Celeste's cabin (Sarah preparing for the voyage), the deck (Sophia, small hand in her mother's).

### Beat 4 — The discoveries become belongings (6:00–10:00)
*Return to the boarding party — but now every object is somebody's.*

Re-enter the boarding party sequence, but this time the objects are reframed. The folded clothes are Sarah's. The toy on the cabin floor is Sophia's. The captain's pipes are Benjamin's. The chronometer disturbed by a man who knew the calculation was suddenly urgent. The missing yawl rowed by seven sailors with names — the Lorenzen brothers Volkert and Boy, Arian Martens, Gottlieb Goodschaad. The first mate Albert G. Richardson, 28, newly trusted by Briggs. The second mate Andrew Gilling. The steward Edward William Head, newly married.

The Gibraltar inquiry: Frederick Solly-Flood's suspicion. No bodies. No blood. No answer.

Then: the empty ship docked at Gibraltar. The clothes still folded. The toy still on the floor. Arthur's school letters in Massachusetts that no one was reading.

### Beat 5 — Closer: Arthur's life (10:00–end)
*The ocean takes him too. End on image.*

Arthur Briggs grows up under the mystery. The newspaper rumors. The Conan Doyle fictional story in 1884 that pollutes the record. The films and pulp magazines that turn his mother and sister into characters.

He becomes a sailor himself. In the early 1900s, decades after his family disappeared, Arthur Briggs dies in a shipwreck off the east coast of the United States.

**Closing image:** the ocean at dawn, empty. Voiceover: *"The ocean took his mother. It took his father. It took his sister. And then, when he was old enough to follow them, it took him too."*

Silent beat. Title card with dates: *Captain Benjamin Spooner Briggs (1835–1872 disappeared). Sarah Elizabeth Briggs (1841–1872 disappeared). Sophia Matilda Briggs (1870–1872 disappeared). Arthur Stanley Briggs (1865–1931 lost at sea).*

End.
