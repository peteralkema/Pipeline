## 1. Identity & premise (CORRECTED — demand-first, not supply-first)

**Lazarus Films makes cinematic recreations of public-domain literature, in a single demand-validated lane: literary psychological dread — modernised, present-day, and fully exposed.** The name does double duty: resurrection (reviving works thought to belong to the past) and the uncanny (the thing that returns from the dead, changed and wrong). Let the name *imply* the dread; never announce it — the quiet is the strength.

**The original design was supply-first and it was wrong.** "Unlimited public-domain material" tells you what you *can* make, not what anyone wants to watch. Faithful, reverent, scene-by-scene adaptation is the LOWEST-demand corner of the literary space (the Tony Walker floor: 99.7K subs over fourteen years — the failure mode to AVOID, not the benchmark). What survived the correction: the public-domain SUPPLY (bottomless, legal-clean, press-defensible) wrapped in ONE algorithm-legible lane.

**The counter-position holds, modernised:** Lazarus is NOT AI-slop public-domain horror. It is the dignified-CRAFT counter-position — but "dignified" means *craft discipline*, NOT period costume. The dread is literary and serious; the rendering is modern, bright, and alive (§6).

**Tagline (banner):** LAZARUS — public domAIn revival. Single-pass legible; the one capitalised AI the only wink, quiet not smirking. "Revival" carries the darker sense (something brought back), not merely celebrated.

## 2. The lane (CORRECTED — single, modern, McEwan)

**Literary psychological dread, in the Ian McEwan / *Cement Garden* register: interior unease, moral transgression, the quiet wrongness inside ordinary people — NOT slasher, NOT jump-scare, NOT supernatural-monster horror.** The data backs it: the "disturbing psychological stories that question humanity" cluster pulls 400–600K a video. The operator's favourite author is the centre of a proven demand pocket, not a niche taste.

McEwan is in copyright; Lazarus draws on his public-domain ancestors — **Gilman** (*The Yellow Wallpaper*), **Maupassant** (*The Horla*), **Poe** (*Tell-Tale Heart*, *Cask of Amontillado*), **Bierce** (*Owl Creek Bridge*, *The Boarded Window*), **Stevenson** (*Markheim*), **Le Fanu** (*Green Tea*). Adjacent expansion lane (month 6+): dark-folklore origins (Grimm unedited). Earmark, needs a data pull: classic sci-fi/dystopia (Wells).

**The operating principle: reverent interior, ruthless exterior.** McEwan's register for choosing the source and writing the inside; demand-grammar for the packaging (the title sells the wrongness — "She Was Left Alone in That Room to Rest" — not the literary pedigree). Nobody in this lane does BOTH: the explainer channels do the hook without the cinema; the cinematic-shorts people do the visuals without the literary spine. Lazarus is the one channel doing both.

**The genre-legibility LOCK:** YouTube classifies by genre and audience co-watch, NOT by "register." A multi-genre anthology (Saki→Hammett→du Maurier) is unservable — it retrains the algorithm every upload and craters the first-48h signal. **Lazarus is single-lane.** "Public domain" is a SUPPLY strategy, not a lane. Vary author/era/sub-theme freely; never vary the genre. (Crime/romance/comedy texts, if ever wanted, are *different channels*.)

## 3. The manifesto — DREAD THAT GRIPS, NOT DREAD THAT DRIFTS

The governing test, asked of every beat, cut, thumbnail, motion prompt: *does this grip, or does this drift?* Drift is the Tony Walker failure mode — restraint with no engine, atmosphere with no question, a slow bleed dressed as mood. Grip is restraint with the screws turning: the camera holds, the tension never stops climbing.

**Slack ≠ restraint.** Slack (nothing escalating, no live question) is fatal, killed everywhere. Restraint (camera withholds, menace implied) is the edge — kept, but TAUT, never sleepy. Tighten the screws; don't shake the camera.

Operational rules (each is the manifesto specified for one stage): a live question always (cold open poses one in the first thirty seconds; plant a second early); the first thirty seconds carry the launch (most-engineered passage); tighten as it climbs (beats shorten toward the end); the cut tracks the heartbeat (cut frequency accelerates with the dread); restraint is punctuation, tension is default; under-show but spike (image withholds; 2–3 visceral beats per film).

Enforcement (three layers, or it rots to decoration): doctrine (this file); code (`check_script.py` Lazarus profile flags a non-tightening back third at zero spend); data (the read-side verdict is retention CURVE SHAPE — grip = shallow curve carrying into the climax; drift = steady bleed from the open).

## 4. The human-anguish face-open (LOCKED — the hook, hold, drift-fix, thumbnail, unified)

Every video's cold open opens on the FACE of the character who suffers most / carries the dread and the climax — full, held, lit per §6.5. That same face IS the thumbnail. **Thereafter that character is rendered FACELESSLY** (hands, back of head, over-shoulder, POV, their world) so the face is never resolved again.

One rule, four jobs: the hook (a stranger's suppressed-anguish face is the most retention-dense image possible — wins the open and the can't-scroll-past); the hold (bonding to the sufferer's interiority in beat one is what makes them stay); the **drift-fix by design** (AI face-drift only happens across SUCCESSIVE stills of one face — spend the face ONCE, never resolve it again, and there's no consistency to break — the model's weakness becomes the discipline); the thumbnail (one face render serves both thumbnail and opening beat — consistent by construction, keeps the open's promise, no AVD-punishing bait-and-switch).

Selector — whose face: whoever's interiority the story inhabits / carries the dread (usually narrator/protagonist; occasionally the victim). Decided per video. Caveat: face is the strong DEFAULT, not absolute — dread-of-place/object texts may open on the space/object that carries the trauma (the sufferer rendered by absence). Lead with the single most retention-dense image of the central trauma.

## 5. The brutal YouTube filter & PD selection (retained, reframed)

Reading PD for pleasure ≠ reading it for adaptation; most beloved works fail the YouTube test. Score candidates on: a 30-second event-promise; one-sentence plot compression; a peak inside the runtime window; visualisable specifics; an articulable protagonist stake; first-mover lane open (famous marquee titles fail on saturation — adapt a recognised AUTHOR's lesser-known TITLE); current flow vs legacy stock; jurisdiction-clean.

**SEO/recognition as a TIEBREAKER, never the picker:** ride famous-AUTHOR equity, adapt lesser-known TITLES (first-mover lane open); separately harvest detached-title recognition (the "had to read it in school" reflex — *Yellow Wallpaper*, *Tell-Tale Heart*, *Monkey's Paw*); CLUSTER by author early to convert rented author-equity into owned channel-equity. Recognition breaks ties between register-fit candidates; it never drags toward a work that fails register/runtime/visual-feasibility.

**Supply is deep:** Cohort-A psychological-dread core (Poe, Bierce, Maupassant, Le Fanu, Gilman, Stevenson, the Russians in pre-1926 translation — watch the translation copyright gate) is years of weekly output, roughly doubled by the dark-folklore adjacency, plus the annual Jan-1 US PD cohort (render-ahead, scheduled-private, but gate marquee calendar plays on GLOBAL clearance, not just US). Supply was always the strong part; the bottleneck is craft-attention, which is why we narrowed to one lane.

**The Dahl lineage → the TWO dread modes (28 June).** Tracing Roald Dahl's documented PD ancestors (Dahl retained as a touchstone): Poe is a confirmed direct influence ("The Landlady"), and Joyce Carol Oates places Dahl in "the select society of Saki." **Saki (H.H. ## 6. VISUAL & DREAD-CRAFT DOCTRINE (LOCKED — the heart of this rebuild)

*Bought with a wasted candlelit render and a long correction. Do not let the model's priors or Claude's defaults quietly re-introduce darkness, period, prettiness, or stock-cleanliness.*

### 6.0 The McEwan thesis
McEwan dragged literary dread OUT of candles/curtains/creaking-Victorian-floorboards and INTO the modern household — bright sunlight, the kettle boiling, horror from NORMALITY. Lazarus renders PD dread the same way: stories are timeless, so we render **present-day, fully lit, ordinary**, dread from the wrongness inside a normal bright frame. Darkness is a cheap crutch that hides the horror and wastes half the frame. **The dread is in the detail, fully exposed.** Lazarus brings the dead works ALIVE in the modern age; it does not embalm them in gaslight.

### 6.1 Anti-bias / anti-prior / anti-stock protocol (LOCKED)
Biases are invisible from inside — they arrive dressed as good reasoning. Intention can't fix them; mechanism can.
- **PERIOD prior:** "literary/dread/classic/Poe" defaults to candlelit Victorian gothic. Beat it with OBJECTS, not adjectives — a visible in-frame modern object (phone, laptop, radiator, kettle, joggers, vape, charger). Adjectives are too weak. *Especially in tight crops where the room can't anchor.*
- **DARKNESS prior (the model's AND Claude's):** "atmospheric dread" defaults to dark/backlit/silhouette; it serves the cliche and hides flaws. Fix: BRIGHTNESS-BIASED DEFAULT — when uncertain, always err brighter/more-exposed.
- **PRETTINESS/stock prior:** defaults to attractive symmetrical faces, soft flattering light, performed emotion. Strongest on young/female faces. Fight hardest there.
- **Claude does NOT pick the look from description.** Every look decision goes through a cheap STILLS-ONLY test where the OPERATOR'S eye is the gate ("I know it when I see it"). Claude generates options; the operator rejects the biased ones. ~$1, no audio/Kling, run liberally BEFORE spend. Standing enforcement.
- **BANNED WORDS** (style_suffix + VISUAL lines): candle, candlelit, lantern, gaslight, chiaroscuro, deep shadow, darkness, dark, dim, gloom, murky, shadowy, Victorian, gothic, period, 1840s, crypt, flickering, cosy, warm glow, golden hour, soft flattering light. (Per-beat night-scene exceptions only as a deliberate named override.)

### 6.2 The look (modern-exposed base)
> cinematic psychological realism, present-day contemporary setting, modern domestic life, fully and evenly lit, bright but COLD clinical daylight, cool desaturated colour grade, drained warmth, slightly sickly green-grey cast, clean ordinary modern interiors, banal everyday detail, off-centre composition with unsettling negative space, observed from a detached clinical distance, too still and too empty, high detail edge to edge, shallow depth of field, photoreal, fine cinematic grain, the uncanny emerging from bright everyday normality, quiet dread, no warmth, no cosy, no period, no Victorian, no candles, no darkness, no text, 16:9

Non-negotiable pairing: **fully lit BUT cold.** Bright-and-warm is a cereal advert; bright-and-cold is dread.

### 6.3 The dread-grade signature & THREE POLES
What makes a frame feel something-bad-is-coming before the content registers. Levers: cool desaturated drained colour (skin too); off-centre negative space OR rigid wrong symmetry; detached/too-far/slightly-high camera (the witness that never judges); too-still/too-empty; long lens + shallow focus isolating subject from a softened wrong background. A grade is a QUALITY (safe channel-wide), not a prop.

The three poles — one signature, tuned per film via `look.json`:
- **Cement Garden** (McEwan, atmosphere only — in copyright, never the text): bright cold domestic summer-stillness; sealed isolated house; ordinary life over a buried secret. → confinement, family-wrong, sealed worlds.
- **Prisoners** (Villeneuve/Deakins): bleak wet grey-November; near-monochrome but fully legible; flat hard light on worn faces; huge negative space. → grief, the worn protagonist, bleak exteriors.
- **Mayor of Easttown:** grubby fluorescent working-class lived-in realism; anti-glamour, specific grubby texture, real depleted people. → gritty crime-realism; the named fix when a face comes back too pretty.

Channel owns the signature; film owns the pole. (This is the channel-owns-the-frame / film-owns-the-interior architecture, now with three named presets.)

### 6.4 The anti-stock anguish-face recipe (the most important frame on the channel)
Stock looks stock because: soft flattering light, attractive symmetrical face, performed emotion, comfortable framing — engineered unobjectionable, the opposite of dread. The levers, all toward uncomfortable and specific:
1. **Light:** harsh, flat, frontal, UNFORGIVING — every pore/line/bag visible. No soft flattering light, no glamour.
2. **Face:** ordinary, worn, REAL, asymmetrical, not a model, no makeup, real texture, a face you'd pass on the street; specify age/wear.
3. **Expression:** SUPPRESSED, not emoting (the whole game) — blank hollow thousand-yard stare, shut down, holding something back, dissociated, numb; NOT crying, NOT screaming, NOT fearful, NOT performing. The negatives matter as much as the positives.
4. **Framing:** uncomfortable — too close, or off-centre with a void beside them, or too-high detached.
5. **Grade:** cold, desaturated, warmth drained from the skin → unwell, sleepless pallor.
6. **Context:** a SPECIFIC banal modern place, softened (shallow focus) — hospital waiting room, kitchen at 4am, parked car, fluorescent corridor. Specific banality = real person in a real bad moment (period + stock anchor in one).

**Prettiness-prior note:** younger/more-female = stronger resistance; push uglier/plainer/flatter explicitly. When a face returns too pretty, the named correction is "shoot it like Mayor of Easttown" (de-glamoured, puffy, unwashed, fluorescent; Winslet kept the under-eye bags — the refusal to flatter IS the technique). Proven: the gaunt sleepless fifties man (modern Tell-Tale narrator) landed first try; the teenage girl came back too pretty (the predicted prior).

### 6.5 Anti-stock generally
Stock-ness in any frame = too clean, too composed, too generic, no POV, no specific wrongness. Cure = SPECIFICITY: every frame carries ONE specific unsettling detail and a specific composition. Solved at the SCRIPT stage (write specific dread into every VISUAL), not the look stage. Grubby/specific/banal/lived-in beats clean/generic every time.

## 7. Production grammar (LOCKED carry-overs)

- **Motion gears:** CREEP (default `default_motion`) / TIGHTEN (escalation) / HOLD (detonation) / one rationed SWING. Name BEHAVIOUR not prop in motion prompts (a "candle" token summons candles; "flickering flame" — and even that is now banned-list-adjacent, prefer "faint movement of light"). ≥2/3 of any script carries specific varied motion prompts; flagship runs higher. (Requires the parser `MOTION:` patch — applied this session.)
- **Beat length governed by the ~5s Kling clip clock:** ~10–18 spoken words / ~4–6s per beat so motion reads at natural speed (the first Tell-Tale render was SLUGGISH because long beats stretched 5s clips 3.5×). DELIBERATELY keep a few long HOLD beats where heavy-time slowness is the intent — stretch is a bug on a TIGHTEN, a feature on a HOLD. **NB the slow-fill stretch recurred on Yellow Wallpaper (3–4.7× across most beats) — acceptable on a near-frozen slow-burn film where held motion IS the register, but it means the motion is a slowed 5s clip, not Kling across the whole beat; the real fix is longer clips or shorter-narration beats so clip-length ≈ beat-length.**
- **LENGTH DISCIPLINE — count WORDS, not beats (the 2-minute miss, 28 June).** Runtime comes from word count, NOT beat count. An 18-beat / 355-word Yellow Wallpaper draft rendered at ~2 min because each beat was one sentence — a near-fatal cold-channel debut. **Target words = runtime (min) × ~120** (Rosalind at 0.85 ≈ 120 wpm; faster reverent voices run 165–178, see ante-machinam §6 — calibrate per voice). Count words BEFORE rendering (`grep -E '^\[A\]' script.md | sed 's/^\[A\] //' | wc -w`); never infer length from beat count. ~10 min ≈ ~1,200 words. A weak watch-time signal on a cold channel, set exactly when the algorithm samples what the channel is, is the cost of getting this wrong.
- **Music:** curated per-channel beds (`<channel>/music/`, random-N crossfaded, low under voice at 0.040). **CONFIRMED BUG, three heads (28 June):** the batch/orchestrator path generates a fal ghost by default; the Mission Control auto-assemble AND "Re-assemble (latest clips)" button BOTH drop music entirely (never pass `--music-dir`) → a voiceover-only final with no warning. **INTERIM (locked until patched): finalize every Lazarus video FROM THE BOX** — `assemble_episode.py --project <proj> --durations <proj>/durations.json --index <proj>/_index.json --voiceover <proj>/voiceover.mp3 --clips <proj>/clips --music-dir <channel>/music` (proven 28 June; re-muxes existing approved clips with a crossfaded bed, no re-render). Real fixes: patch Mission Control's assemble handler to always pass `--music-dir`; AND make `assemble_episode.py` FAIL LOUD on a missing bed unless `--no-music` is explicit (the fix that makes the bug class impossible). Bed test: "could I hum it?" → too musical, cut it. ("Who Lives up That Hill" culled for being a song.)
- **Voice:** **Lazarus is multi-voice, cast to CHARACTER, not to a house narrator** (`voice_id` snake_case-exact — wrong casing silently falls back to Victor). First-person texts take a narrator matching the protagonist's interiority. **Yellow Wallpaper → Rosalind (British, mature/warm/measured) at 0.85 — CAST 28 June.** The decisive audition lesson: for a first-person unreliable narrator, choose the VULNERABLE / part-of-the-story voice over the abstracted-narrator voice — the listener must be bonded TO her as she disintegrates, not read to ABOUT her. Rate-tuning is narrow: 0.8 drags (depressive, telegraphs the ending), 0.9 is too in-control, 0.85 is the seam (measured + present + vulnerable). Screen every candidate for BREATH CONTROL first — any breathy/ASMR voice is out (it turns dread into a sleep-aid, doubly so at slow rates). British modern-not-posh (literary signal + restraint + differentiates from the American true-horror slop field; RP/costume-drama is the audio version of the candle mistake). **Beatrice** (rich/authoritative/graceful) banked as the twist-in-the-tail (§5) narrator — that mode wants the knowing, detached register Yellow Wallpaper rejected.

## 12. CRAFT-EXECUTION DOCTRINE (LOCKED — the prompt-and-panel layer, harvested 28 June)

Where §6 is *what the look is*, §12 is *how to make the model produce it without wrestling every frame*. Every rule here was paid for in re-renders on the Yellow Wallpaper launch.

### 12.1 Detailed-prompt-by-default (Lazarus only)
Lazarus prompts are fully specified by default. The "happy accident" of a loose prompt is a luxury for lower-craft channels (Prehistoric, the volume plays); here the look IS the moat, and a wrong default costs a re-still → re-animate → re-assemble. Every Lazarus beat prompt carries three things:
- **Material** — name the substance, not just the subject. "dry matte printed paper," not "wallpaper." "weathered hands," not "hands."
- **Composition** — name the framing and what is / isn't shown. "his hands, her face turned away, no frontal face," not "a couple."
- **Negation** — forbid the model's known wrong default explicitly, often redundantly. "no gloss, no wet, no rounded cells"; "no void, no black, not a flat wall."

The discipline is NOT maximum words. It is: **specify what the model gets wrong; leave genuinely free choices free.** A wrong default (solid-yellow wall, frontal face, glossy macro) gets locked hard; a neutral choice (exact shadow position) breathes. Over-specifying everything produces mush.

### 12.2 The hard-shot library (repeat offenders + proven overrides)
These compositions reliably fight the model. Pre-load the override from the start on any beat of this type — do not wait for the failure.
- **Faces touching (two faces, four hands):** forces frontal faces + drift. FIX: render one person as hands only ("his hands enter from the frame edge, body out of frame"), the other from a three-quarter rear angle ("face turned down and away, never her eyes, never frontal"), negate the frontal face redundantly. If still fighting → change the shot to hands-on-shoulders from behind (no face at all). *(On-doctrine: this beat is SUPPOSED to be faceless — a full frontal face here breaks §6.4's drift-fix.)*
- **Back-of-head against a wall:** renders flat/even = "Zoom virtual background." FIX: "uneven diagonal daylight across the wall, pattern visible and textured, shallow depth of field, wall softly out of focus, photographic depth, not a flat backdrop."
- **Macro / extreme close-up of a surface:** defaults to glossy/wet/organic (jelly, honeycomb, bubbles). FIX: name the material + negate gloss hard — "dry matte printed paper, visible grain, completely matte, no shine, no wet, no rounded cells," + raking side-light to reveal dry texture. Pull back from "extreme" to "close" if it persists.
- **Wall-pattern presence:** "yellow wallpaper" renders a SOLID yellow wall. FIX: "the printed pattern clearly visible across the whole wall," "not a flat solid-colour wall," stated twice.
- **Almost-a-figure-in-the-pattern:** renders a literal dark blob. FIX: "the faint suggestion of a shape woven into the print itself, formed by the pattern, never a literal figure." A literal figure resolves the mystery and kills the dread.
- **Pattern deforming (something pressing behind it):** renders either flat-decorative OR a torn edge against black. FIX: "the printed pattern stretches and bulges outward as if a shoulder presses from behind, leaves distorted over the swell of a hidden shape, the rest flat so the bulge stands out," raking light to catch relief. Negate BOTH "flat sample" and "tear/void."

**Named-pattern continuity lever:** to hold a wallpaper across beats WITHOUT an image reference, name a specific real pattern ("William Morris willow-leaf, sage-green on sickly mustard-yellow ground"), not "a pattern." This holds the *family* reliably across beats in one render (proven on Yellow Wallpaper). It does NOT hold an *identical* print — that needs image reference (§12.4). **When the detailed override is a known repeat-offender (the list above), use it from the START — the "fix only what fails" rule applies to NOVEL failures; known offenders get pre-loaded.**

### 12.3 Mission Control is the Lazarus render path (NOT the batch)
Lazarus renders through Mission Control's stills-review gate, frame by frame, NEVER the fire-and-forget batch. The review gate belongs wherever the craft bar is high enough that one frame can break the piece — Lazarus, and any future cinematic-register channel. Batch + Studio-review (post-hoc) is correct for volume channels; Mission Control (pre-spend, frame-by-frame) is correct where craft-attention is the moat. They operate at different points: Studio review judges what already rendered (money spent); Mission Control judges stills BEFORE any Kling. **The panel + a second-eye override is the core workflow: the panel surfaces WHICH beat failed, doctrine NAMES the failure, the library (§12.2) REMEMBERS it.** Consequence: the daemonization fix (`systemd-run --scope` / double-fork) graduates from minor backlog to a real reliability need now the panel is Lazarus's primary path — until fixed, never restart the service mid-animate.
**Standalone-stills quirk:** the standalone `stills` command needs the engine schema (`image_prompt`), but `parse_script.py` emits `visual`. Run `modea_beats.py` between them: **parse → modea_beats → stills**, not parse → stills. (The batch path does this internally; the standalone path does not.) Stills live under `<project>/modea/stills/`.
**Re-assembly discipline:** Mission Control auto-assembles BEFORE later edits, so the first `final_video.mp4` is stale. After any beat change: fix still → Accept → render clip (assembly pools CLIPS, not stills — a new still with an old clip ships the old clip) → re-assemble → confirm runtime matches expectation (a stale short cut means old clips were pooled). And music is dropped on every panel assemble (§7) — finalize from the box.

### 12.4 Image reference — style-not-pattern (roadmap, not built)
fal `flux-pro/v1.1/redux` tested end-to-end (28 June): it holds STYLE / family, NOT an exact pattern, and under-weights the prompt (echoes the source). So redux does NOT solve wallpaper-identity — and named-pattern prompting (§12.2) already gets same-family for free. Image-reference's real, reserved jobs are the two things prompts CANNOT hold: **character-face consistency** (lock the opening anguish face, reference it for later glimpses) and **per-film look-lock** (one reference frame holds a whole film's register). Build it deliberately with STRUCTURAL / identity conditioning (depth/canny or an identity adapter), NOT redux, prioritising the uploaded-reference use case. ### 12.5 Continuity variance is an asset only when the narration absorbs it
