title: Drowned Worlds: The Stone Age Country the North Sea Swallowed
description: Beneath the cold grey waters of the North Sea lies a lost country. Ten thousand years ago it was dry land — a vast lowland of rivers, marshes, and forests, richer than almost anywhere in Europe, and home to thousands of Stone Age people. We call it Doggerland. This is the story of how the melting of the ice age raised the seas and drowned it, valley by valley and coast by coast; how its people watched their homeland shrink for generations and were driven back before the rising water; how a single catastrophic wave may have delivered the final blow; and how, all across the world, the meltwater swallowed coastlines, land bridges, and human worlds that we are only now rediscovering beneath the waves. Drawn from the bones, tools, and forests that fishing boats still drag up from the seabed. What other worlds are waiting under the water? Tell us in the comments.

## PART ONE — The Lost Lowland

## PART TWO — The Rising Sea

## PART THREE — The Wave

[A] Around eight thousand years ago, that enormous slope gave way. In a single catastrophic event, a mass of seabed the size of a country slid into the deep ocean.

[A] We call it the Storegga Slide. It was one of the largest known landslides in the Earth's recent history — and when that much earth moves into the sea at once, the sea answers with a wave.

[A] Britain was an island at last, cut off from Europe by a new sea. And between the two, where a whole country had lived and breathed, there was only the grey water of the North Sea.
