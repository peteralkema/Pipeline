title: The Last Mammoths: Still Alive When the Pyramids Were Built
description: When the Great Pyramid of Giza was rising, woolly mammoths were still alive. This is the story of the last mammoths on Earth — how a species that once ruled a frozen world stretching across three continents dwindled, herd by herd, as the ice age ended and the mammoth steppe died; how a final population was marooned on a single Arctic island, Wrangel, cut off by the rising sea; and how those last few thousand animals lived on, alone and slowly failing, for thousands of years after the mainland mammoths were gone — until they too vanished, around the time the pharaohs ruled Egypt. Drawn from the bones and frozen carcasses preserved in the permafrost, and from the DNA of the very last of their kind. A story of the long, quiet end of a species. ## PART FOUR — The Slow Unravelling

[A] A few hundred animals, alone on an island, with no way for new blood to ever reach them. That is a precarious way for a species to live.

[A] With so few animals, every mammoth on Wrangel was related to every other. Generation after generation, close kin bred with close kin, because there was no one else.
VISUAL: A small mammoth herd gathered on island grassland, the animals close and few, the same handful of shapes repeated across the thin population.

[A] And when a population breeds within itself for long enough, harmful flaws in the blood, which a larger population would wash away, instead build up, and are passed down, and cannot be escaped.
