title: The Messinian Salinity Crisis: When the Mediterranean Dried Into a Desert of Salt
description: Around six million years ago, one of the world's great seas vanished. The Mediterranean — today a deep, blue body of water ringed by three continents — was cut off from the Atlantic Ocean, and under the relentless sun it began to evaporate. Over thousands of years it shrank, and shrank, until almost the entire sea was gone, leaving behind a vast, blistering salt desert kilometres below the level of the world's oceans, where rivers carved canyons into the dry seabed and brutal brine lakes baked under an unbearable sun. This is the story of the Messinian Salinity Crisis — how a whole sea dried up, what the dead salt basin was like, and the staggering flood that finally brought the waters roaring back. Drawn from the kilometres-thick beds of salt still buried beneath the Mediterranean floor. How could an entire sea simply disappear? Tell us in the comments.

## PART ONE — The Sea on a Knife's Edge

[A] That narrow strait is the sea's lifeline. Through it, the Atlantic constantly pours fresh ocean water into the Mediterranean, replacing what the fierce sun burns away.
