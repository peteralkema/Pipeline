## PART ONE - THE SKY AS A SIGN

[A] To feel Baraqiel's crime, begin with what the sky was meant to be. The heavens, the scriptures say, do declare something - the glory of the One who made them. The storm and the lightning were meant to speak of a power beyond us, and to lift the eye toward it.

[A] And there is a true reading of the sky, a good one. The farmer who watches the clouds and knows the rain is coming. The traveler who reads the wind and seeks shelter. To understand the weather is wisdom, and the old text does not condemn that. It condemns the turn past it.

[A] For there is a line between reading the sky and worshipping it. Between knowing rain is coming, and believing the lightning is a god's verdict on your life. Baraqiel led mankind across that line - from understanding the weather to fearing it, from wisdom to superstition.

[A] And once across it, the sky changes. It stops being a gift you read for your safety and becomes a power you dread for your fate. Every storm is now a judgement. Every flash a finger pointed at you. The heavens, meant to lift the eye, now press it down in fear.

## PART TWO - THE OMEN IN THE FLASH

[A] What Baraqiel taught, precisely, was to find messages in the sudden sky. A bolt of lightning was no longer just lightning. It was a sign - of coming doom, of a god's anger, of a fate to be feared. He taught mankind to read the storm as a language aimed at them.

[A] And you can feel why it took hold. The storm is frightening. The lightning is sudden and violent and beyond control. To believe it carries a message is, strangely, less terrifying than to believe it is meaningless - for a message can at least be read, and perhaps appeased.

[A] But the comfort is a trap. For if the storm is a message, then someone is always sending it - and you are always its target, always under its judgement, always searching the next dark sky for the next verdict. The reading that promised meaning delivers only endless dread.

[A] And so the gift that seemed like wisdom becomes a chain. The one who reads omens in the lightning can never simply watch a storm again. Every flash demands interpretation. Every thunderhead carries a threat. The sky he learned to read now rules him.

## PART THREE - FEAR INSTEAD OF TRUST

[A] But the deepest harm, as with Kokabiel and the stars, is a turning away. To read your fate in the storm is to hand the sky a power over your life that belongs to the One who made the sky. It is to fear the weather more than its Maker.

[A] The scriptures show the true order plainly. The storm is the servant of its Maker, not a god in itself. The lightning goes out at his word; the thunder is his, not its own. To dread the sky as if it ruled you is to bow to a servant and forget the King.

[A] And this is Baraqiel's crime, though no blood is spilled. He taught mankind to give the sky its fear and its trust - to search the storm for a verdict, and forget the One whose word the storm obeys. He turned the servant-weather into a false and dreaded god.
