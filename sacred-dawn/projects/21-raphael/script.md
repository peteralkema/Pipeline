## PART ONE - THE ANGEL OF HEALING

[A] To understand the binding, first understand the binder. Raphael is one of the great archangels - named alongside Michael, Gabriel, and Uriel as one of the chief princes who stand before the throne. And his particular office, across all the old texts, is healing.

[A] His name declares it. Raphael - God heals. In the old understanding, he is the angel set over the mending of what is broken, the closing of wounds, the restoration of the sick. Where there is hurt, the tradition says, Raphael is the one heaven sends to mend it.

[A] And the Book of Tobit, in the canon of many traditions, shows him at exactly this work. There, Raphael walks the earth in disguise, guiding a young man on a journey, and heals a blind man's eyes and frees a household from a tormenting spirit. The angel of healing, mending what is broken.

[A] So Raphael is, above all, the restorer. Which makes his role in the story of the watchers so striking. When the earth lay poisoned and sickened by the fallen, the angel heaven sent was fittingly the angel of healing. But the way he healed was not what anyone would expect.

## PART FIVE - THE MERCY OF THE CHAIN

[A] Step back, because Raphael's story overturns a comfortable assumption. We tend to think of binding and healing as opposites - that mercy means release, and love means letting free. But the old text says something harder and truer. Sometimes the most healing thing is to bind.

[A] For a poison left loose does not become less poisonous through kindness. A source of harm left free only spreads. [A] And this reframes the binding of Azazel. It was not vengeance. It was not even punishment, in the first place. It was healing - the necessary sealing of a poison so that the wounded earth could finally mend. Raphael bound Azazel because he was the angel of healing, not in spite of it.
