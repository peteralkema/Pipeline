[A] The giants were waiting when Mahaway came down out of the sky. The whole grim assembly gathered close on the high plateau as the winged one landed and set the two stone tablets down upon the rock. And then Enoch's words were read aloud to the monsters of the earth.
VISUAL: A vast ring of colossal faceless silhouettes closing in around the winged figure on the plateau as he lays two pale tablets on the dark stone; their immense shadows converging inward; storm light behind, cold grey-blue, the tablets small and bright at the centre.

[A] And as the verdict was read — the drowned garden, the opened books, the closing door, the eight souls carried through and the whole world that would not — a sound went through the assembly that the earth had never heard before. The sound of giants afraid.
VISUAL: The ring of colossal silhouettes recoiling, heads turning away, vast forms leaning back from the tablets at the centre as if from heat; their shadows breaking apart across the rock; cold grey light, the great shapes flinching, dust shivering off them.

[A] All but one. Ohyah. The largest, the proudest, the one who had himself seen the throne and the opened books in his own dream — Ohyah heard the verdict, and laughed.
VISUAL: The broad scarred giant-silhouette risen to its full colossal height above the others on the plateau, head thrown back, one huge arm flung out in derision; the other giants small and bowed around its feet; storm light behind it, the figure black and immense.

[A] Tell me another vision, he said — to the messenger, to the tablets, to the absent scribe and the heaven that sent him. Tell me another dream. I know a great deal.
VISUAL: Close on the scarred shoulder and raised arm of the largest giant from behind, the vast hand open and dismissive against the storm sky; below and beyond, the small bright tablets on the rock; cold ember light along the giant's edge, contempt in the posture.

[A] He had seen the courtroom at the end of the world with his own eyes, and his pride would still not let him bow to it. This is the oldest pride there is. Not the pride that does not know — Ohyah knew. He had been shown.
VISUAL: The colossal scarred silhouette turned half away, head high, against a sky boiling toward storm; faint and immense behind it, the after-image of the throne it had seen, ignored; deep grey and red-black, the figure refusing to turn toward the light.

[A] It was the pride that knows, and is told, and still will not kneel. He would rather be the strongest thing in a drowning world than a humbled thing in a saved one.
VISUAL: The colossal scarred silhouette standing alone and defiant on a high rock against the storm, fists clenched at its sides; the first hard spits of rain beginning to streak past it; deep grey and red-black, the figure unmoved, the sky descending.

[A] But Mahaway, the winged one, the one who had listened — Mahaway believed. He went among the giants and pleaded with them in the words he had heard Enoch preach. He told them the dreams were true.

[A] He begged them to do the one thing the second tablet had left open to their fathers. To turn. To pray. And they would not. The strong do not pray. The proud do not turn.
VISUAL: The winged figure with both hands raised toward the assembled giants, who have begun to turn their vast backs to him one by one; his wings sagging; cold blue and grey, the lone lit figure among the turning dark shapes.

[A] They had eaten the world, and ruled it, and they could not believe that anything — even a flood, even a throne, even the opened books — could unmake what they were. So they did the only thing left to a creature that will not bow. They turned on each other.

[A] It began, the texts hint, in quarrels over the last of everything — the last grain, the last beasts, the last untouched ground. It became something older and worse. Across the whole face of the earth, the sons of the Watchers fell upon one another.

VISUAL: Faceless human women's silhouettes huddled at a cave mouth in deep shadow, looking out; in the bright violent distance beyond them two giants falling together in a vast cloud of dust; the women's hands pressed to their mouths, motionless; cold foreground, burning distance.

## PART FIVE — What They Became

[A] To understand what happened next, you have to understand what the giants were. They were not only flesh. Their mothers were of the earth — but their fathers had fallen out of heaven.
VISUAL: A single fallen giant's vast silhouette lying still on a drowned ridge at storm-dusk, half its bulk in cold blue earth-shadow and half lit by a thin break of pale heaven-light through the clouds; the two lights meeting along the still form; blue-grey and pale gold.

[A] And so the giants were made of two worlds at once. Half mortal. Half spirit. Belonging, fully, to neither. And that single fact is the whole of what they became.
VISUAL: Close on the fallen giant-form half in cold blue shadow and half in pale gold light, the dividing line running down the centre of the great still body; mist rising from it; the two irreconcilable lights meeting; blue and gold.

[A] When a man dies, his body returns to the earth and his spirit goes on to be judged. When one of the faithful spirits of heaven has its dwelling, it is in heaven. But the giants fit nowhere in that order. They were a thing that should never have existed.
VISUAL: A wide, desolate flooded plain at first grey light after the rain, vast still giant-forms half-submerged across it; a faint sickly luminescence beginning to rise from them into the cold air; grey water, ash-coloured sky, a thin unnatural blue-green light gathering.

[A] And when their bodies died, their spirits had nowhere to go. Not heaven — they were half of earth. Not the prison of fire and ice prepared for their fathers — that cell was kept for the Watchers, not their sons. Not the grave of mankind — they were not, fully, men.
VISUAL: A sickly blue-green vapour lifting slowly out of a vast fallen giant's submerged form, formless and coiling, spreading low across the dark water toward the distant drying land; cold luminous green against the grey flood; the great body motionless beneath it.

[A] Every door in creation was shut to them. The gate of heaven, barred. The prison kept for their fathers, not theirs. The quiet grave of mankind, not for them. Made of two worlds, they were turned away by both — and left standing outside in the only place that remained. Here. With us.
VISUAL: A single coil of green spirit-vapour drifting against a vast dark expanse where three faint doorways of differing light stand shut and receding — pale gold, deep fire-red, soft grey — none of them opening; the green thing turned away from all three; cold green against three closed lights.

[A] And so the spirits of the giants rose up out of their drowned bodies — and they stayed. This is what the Book of Enoch says, in the words heaven itself spoke to the scribe.
VISUAL: An immense wall of faintly glowing ancient script rising out of frame in the cold dawn light, one line catching a thin gold gleam; a small robed silhouette standing far below it, head tilted back, reading; deep grey-blue, the script's light pale and cold.

[A] The giants, born of the spirits and the flesh, shall be called evil spirits upon the earth, and on the earth shall be their dwelling. Their beginning was in the holy Watchers, and their flesh was of men, and so evil spirits they would be called.
VISUAL: The sickly green spirit-vapour drifting low over a drying, ruined landscape at dawn, taking and losing half-formed shapes as it moves, seeping toward the first signs of returning human life; cold green luminescence against brown ruin and pale sky.

[A] And then comes the line that is the whole sorrow of it. The spirits of the giants, the old book says, will afflict, and oppress, and destroy. They will attack, and do battle, and work ruin across the whole face of the earth.
VISUAL: The coiling green spirit-vapour gathered thick over a barren waste at dusk, restless and writhing, never settling, with a terrible churning hunger to its motion; nothing to consume anywhere in the dead landscape; cold green against deep violet dark.

[A] And they will take no food — but they will hunger. They will need no water — but they will thirst. The appetite of the giants, with no body left to feed it. Forever.
VISUAL: The green vapour stretched thin and grasping over cracked dry ground at last light, reaching and closing on nothing, nothing anywhere for it to take; cold sickly green against bone-dry violet earth, starving and endless.

[A] Do you remember what the giants were, in life? Always hungry. Devouring the harvests, the herds, at last the people, and never filled. And now, in death, that is exactly what they became.
VISUAL: A slow dissolve holding two images at once — an enormous hand tearing through a grain field, and over it the same churning green spirit-vapour reaching and grasping at empty air; the living hunger and the dead hunger laid over each other; bronze beneath, cold green above.

[A] These restless, homeless, starving spirits, the old tradition says, are the things every later age would learn to fear under a single short word. Not giants any longer. Not Nephilim. The texts give them the name we still flinch from now. Demons.
VISUAL: The churning green spirit-vapour drawing slowly together over a dark ridge into one dense, watchful, faceless mass, hanging in the night air above a sleeping human valley far below; cold malevolent green against deep blue-black, the small warm lights beneath unaware.

[A] A hunger with no body to feed. An appetite that can never again be satisfied, loosed upon the world without end. The thing they had done to the world in life, they would now do to it forever in spirit.
VISUAL: The green vapour sweeping low and ravenous across an empty dusk plain, reaching toward distant warm lights and recoiling, reaching again; never filled, never still; cold green motion against a darkening violet land.

[A] And worst of all — the texts are precise about this — these spirits would rise up against the children of men, and against the women. Against humanity itself. Because it was from humanity, through their mothers, that they had partly come.
VISUAL: The sickly green vapour seeping toward a small, newly rebuilt human settlement at dusk, thinning and curling hungrily at the edges of its warm amber windows; the human lights small and unaware; cold green creeping against warm gold, the dark pressing close.

[A] They would turn on the very race that bore them. Children of two worlds, at home in neither, hating the half of themselves that was human and unable to escape the half that was not.

[A] The Book of Giants is, in the end, a tragedy. The monsters were warned. The monsters refused. And the monsters became the homeless hunger that has haunted the world ever since.

[A] The demonology of the Gospels and the sorrow at the end of the Book of Giants describe, the old tradition holds, the very same beings — the spirits of the giants, still loose, still seeking, still unable to come home.

[A] That is the whole of it. A world of giants who ate everything and were never filled. Two dreams, sent in mercy to monsters. A winged messenger who listened, and a proud brother who would not.
