## 1. THE FRAME — event, not biographical

**Methuselah's root failure was a biographical frame.** "The life of X" made every supernatural
event a cameo and produced 88% terrestrial on a blockbuster channel.

**This film is an EVENT FRAME.** It is not *the life of Enoch*. It is **one journey** — a man taken
out of the world and carried west to the end of it, shown things in order, and returned. Enoch is
the thread; the impossible places are the film. Every block is an arrival somewhere that cannot be
photographed on this earth.

**Scope: 1 Enoch 17–22, the journey to the ends of the earth**, with the chambers as the payoff.
The title sells the chambers (the click); the film delivers the whole road to them (the watch).
Chapter 22 alone cannot carry 320 beats — it would be four caves and a taxonomy, which is exactly
what the 3-view and 36-view versions of this topic are.

**From the Methuselah scorecard, "what was available and unused":** *the throne of fire · the
storehouses of the stars · the prison of the fallen stars · the chambers of the dead · the seven
mountains.* This film spends four of the five.

## 2. THE SPINE OBJECT — the tablet

**One physical thing, photographed, tended, threatened, and finally handed over.**

Enoch is the scribe. He is taken with a blank tablet and a stylus; he writes what he is shown; the
tablet fills as the journey deepens; at the end he carries it down to a world that has months to
live and puts it in Methuselah's hands.

**It escalates visually** — blank → cut → dense → full — which is the audience-readable escalation
meter the doctrine asks for, and it needs no narration to read.

**THE TEN-FRAMES TEST (mandatory — the stones gave three):**

1. blank, tucked under one arm as hands take him
2. the first line cut by stylus, lit from below
3. held up against the river of fire, light through the edge
4. wet with spray where fire meets cold sea
5. pressed flat to his chest in the wind at the earth's corner
6. dropped, caught one-handed over the abyss
7. held out toward the bound stars, the hand shaking
8. laid on bare rock while he kneels at the first hollow
9. carried down the mountain, dense with cut lines
10. passed into an older man's hands

**Ten distinct pictures. PASS.**

**⚠ HARD CAP: 24 beats (7.5%).** Methuselah's stones took 26% of the film. This is the one declared
spine exception to the span law; it is not an exception to the cap. If authoring wants a
twenty-fifth tablet beat, the answer is no — write the thing he is looking at instead.

**Fallback if the cap fights the film:** demote the tablet to a motif and promote **the guide** to
the spine. Decide before block 3, never after.

## 3. THE ANTAGONIST — Cain, named and placed

**"The world" is not an antagonist.** Cain is named in the source (Gen 4; 1 En 22:7), and the film
does not have to invent him — an invented antagonist costs attribution, a named one is free.

| block | appearance |
|---|---|

| **b7** | Abel's voice rises against him from the third chamber; the crime is named |

**The mirror.** Methuselah's antagonist was *Lamech of Cain's line*. This film's is Cain himself.
Same house, one generation up — deliberate series continuity, and it makes the two films a pair
without either depending on the other.

## 9. WHAT COULD STILL KILL THIS — recorded before authoring

1. **The tablet becomes the stones.** Highest-risk Step-1 decision in the file. Cap is 24 beats;
   check it at Step 3 and demote to motif if it is over.
2. **The chambers render as four caves.** Four tokens that are all "smooth dark rock" will smear
   under the near-duplicate scan. Give each chamber a distinct material and light signature at the
   token, not at the beat.
3. **The authority material turns terrestrial.** Dead Sea Scrolls, the councils, Ethiopia — the lane
   opens with this and it is all container. **Keep it in the narration and off the screen.** Never
   render the scroll when the narration names it.
4. **The base rate.** One in ten in this lane. This film is the ticket, not the win. Read
   IMPRESSIONS at 48h before CTR — served-and-not-clicked and not-served-at-all are different
   failures with different fixes, and Sacred Dawn currently cannot tell them apart.
