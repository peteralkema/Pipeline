## PART ONE - THE TREE WE FORGOT

[A] Go back to the planting of the garden. Genesis describes Eden in a few unhurried lines, and in the very middle of it, the text is careful to set not one significant tree, but two, growing side by side.
VISUAL: A lush primeval garden at golden hour, rivers threading through deep green, and at its centre two great trees rising together above the canopy, distinct and deliberate, bathed in warm light

[A] One is the Tree of the Knowledge of good and evil. That one we know. The other, named in the same breath, is the Tree of Life. And of that tree, in the beginning, nothing is forbidden at all.
VISUAL: Two great trees in a sunlit clearing, one ringed faintly with a sense of warning shadow, the other open and radiant and unguarded, its low branches heavy with glowing fruit

[A] That is the detail that slips past every retelling. At first, the Tree of Life was free. The humans could have eaten from it any day they wished. Immortality was not hidden. It was simply standing there, offered, in the open garden.
VISUAL: A radiant fruit-laden branch hanging low over a green path in warm light, well within reach, no barrier near it, the garden quiet and generous around it

[A] Only one tree carried a warning. Only one fruit was withheld. And of all the trees in all that garden, that is the one the story turns on - the one taken, the one that should have been left.
VISUAL: A single piece of fruit half in shadow on a darker tree, a faceless hand reaching toward it through dim green light, the gesture of the oldest mistake, the rest of the frame falling dark

[A] We know what the fruit of knowledge cost. The eyes opened, the shame, the hiding, the voice walking in the garden in the cool of the day. But watch what happens next - because it is not the punishment we expect.

## PART TWO - WHY HE SEALED IT

[A] After the fall, you might expect the garden to be destroyed. Burned, buried, struck from the earth. Instead, Genesis records something far stranger. God speaks a few quiet words to himself - and they are words of concern.
VISUAL: A soft overwhelming radiance standing among the great trees at dusk, no form within it, only presence and light, the garden hushed and listening around the brightness, no face shown

[A] Behold, the voice says, the man has become like one of us, to know good and evil. And now - and here the sentence turns - lest he reach out his hand, and take also of the Tree of Life, and eat, and live forever.
VISUAL: The radiant Tree of Life glowing in its clearing, and at the edge of the frame two small shadowed human figures, the distance between them and the tree suddenly charged and significant

[A] There it is. The reason for everything that follows. Heaven's concern was not that fallen man might know too much. It was that fallen man might never die. That the broken creature might eat from the second tree and become broken forever.
VISUAL: A faceless hand reaching slowly toward a glowing low branch of the Tree of Life, almost touching the radiant fruit, the whole frame holding its breath at the nearness of it

[A] For death, in that terrible moment, becomes a mercy. To live forever in the fallen state - never healing, never ending, the wound made permanent - would be the worse fate. So the road to the endless tree had to be closed.
VISUAL: A long radiant path leading toward the Tree of Life, and across its mouth a deepening shadow falling, the warm light beyond it slowly being sealed away behind the encroaching dark

[A] And so the same hand that planted the garden now drives its children out of it - not in cruelty, the text implies, but to keep them from a worse immortality. The gate closes behind them. And then the guard is set.

## PART THREE - THE GUARD AT THE GATE

[A] Genesis names what was placed at the entrance, and the words are heavy with old fire. He placed at the east of the garden cherubim, it says, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the Tree of Life.

[A] Cherubim. Not the soft winged children of later art. In the old texts they are vast and terrible - many-winged, burning, the living guardians that stand closest to the throne itself. These are what was set at Eden's gate.

[A] And the sword that turns every way is the detail that should stop us cold. A blade that closes every angle. A guard that never sleeps and never tires. The way to the Tree of Life was not merely shut. It was made unenterable.

[A] Notice what is being guarded, and what is not. Heaven set no guard on the Tree of Knowledge. That tree had done its work; there was nothing left to protect. The whole weight of the watch falls on the other tree - the tree of endless life.

[A] One tree we were warned away from, and we went anyway. The other tree we were never warned about at all - and now we cannot reach it. The fruit that could end death is real, the text insists, and it is behind a wall of fire.

## PART FOUR - THE GATE THAT WAS NEVER REOPENED

[A] Now follow the thread forward, because this is where the account becomes genuinely haunting. Read all the way to the end of Genesis, and the rest of the book, and the next, and the next. The sword is never called off.
VISUAL: A long dim corridor of ages rendered as receding green-gold light, the burning gateway holding steady at the far end of it, unmoved across the passing generations, still turning, still barring

[A] No later verse comes back to open the gate. No prophet is sent to lower the blade. The garden simply drops out of the story, sealed, with its guardians still standing and its sword still turning, somewhere east of everything that follows.
VISUAL: A walled and glowing garden seen from far off at dusk, small on a distant horizon, a faint point of turning fire at its gate, the human story moving away from it across a darkening plain

[A] Where it went, the texts do not plainly say. Some of the old writings imagine it lifted away, kept somewhere apart, waiting. Genesis only closes the door and leaves the guard in place. The road to endless life goes quiet, but it is never said to end.

[A] And here honesty must keep its footing. The exact fate of the garden, the where and the how of it, Genesis withholds. It tells us the tree was real, the guard was set, the gate was closed - and then it lets the matter go silent.

[A] But the silence is not the same as a denial. The sword that turned every way was set to keep one road, and no page in all the rest of scripture says the road was ever opened again. The guard, as far as the text tells us, is still there.

## PART FIVE - THE TREE AT THE END OF THE BOOK

[A] And then, on the very last pages of the Bible, in the book of Revelation, the tree comes back. After all the ages, after the long silence, the Tree of Life is named again - and this time the gate is open.

[A] In that final vision, the river of life runs through a new city, and on either side of it grows the Tree of Life, bearing its fruit, and its leaves, the text says, are for the healing of the nations. The forbidden tree, freely given at last.

[A] Read the whole book as one story and the shape is staggering. It opens with the Tree of Life sealed behind a flaming sword, and it closes with the same tree thrown open, the sword nowhere, the road clear. The guard, at the very end, has stood down.

## PART SIX - WHAT THE SWORD IS STILL KEEPING

[A] We cannot find the gate today. Eden is gone from the map, the garden sealed or lifted away, the tree named only at the start of the book and the end. We have the account, and the long silence in the middle, and the guard the text never dismisses.
VISUAL: A vast quiet wilderness at dusk with no garden anywhere in it, only bare hills and a deep sky, and somewhere beyond the horizon an implied faint glow, unreachable, unmarked

[A] And honesty holds to the last. Where the sword turns now, whether the gate still stands east of some forgotten place or waits folded away until the end - Genesis does not tell us. It gives us the guard, and lets the centuries keep their secret.
VISUAL: The turning blade of pale fire alone in deep shadow, no surroundings visible, only the fire and the dark, suspended somewhere the text will not name, still keeping its watch

[A] But weigh what the book does state plainly. There was a second tree, and its fruit ended death. The way to it was closed with fire. And no verse between Eden and the new city ever says the fire was put out. These the text gives, and holds.
