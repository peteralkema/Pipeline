## PART ONE - WHAT THE FLOOD WAS FOR

[A] To feel the weight of those four words, you have to remember what the Flood was actually for. We tell it now as a story about an ark and animals. The texts tell it as a story about giants.

[A] Genesis says the earth was filled with violence, and that every thought of the human heart had turned to evil. But it places that ruin right beside the giants - the Nephilim, the offspring of the sons of God and the daughters of men.
VISUAL: Enormous shadowed forms moving across a ravaged landscape at dusk, fields trampled, small settlements beneath them, the horror conveyed by scale and shadow alone, no detail shown

[A] The old text of First Enoch is blunter still. It lays the corruption of the whole world at the feet of the watchers and the giants they fathered - the violence, the bloodshed, the cry of the earth going up to heaven.

[A] And heaven's answer was water. Wash it clean. Every living thing that had been corrupted, the texts say, and the giants above all. The Flood was the cure, and the giants were the disease.

## PART TWO - THE FOUR WORDS

[A] And then the family steps out onto a cleaned earth, and the human story begins again from a handful of survivors. The disease is gone. The world is washed. This is the promise the Flood was supposed to keep.
VISUAL: A small group of distant robed figures descending a muddy mountainside under a pale clearing sky, a faint rainbow in the high cloud, the washed land bare and shining below them

[A] Except Genesis has already told us otherwise. Before the Flood even begins, in the very verse that names the giants, the book has quietly added its warning. The Nephilim were on the earth in those days - and also after that.
VISUAL: The ancient scroll once more, the final four words of the line glowing faintly while the rest stays dark, set apart as if underlined by the light itself, deliberate and unsettling

[A] The writer knew. Whoever set down that verse already knew how the story would go, and chose to plant the contradiction early, on purpose. He tells you the giants are coming back before he has even drowned them.

[A] And the rest of scripture proves him right. Generations later, the spies sent into the promised land come back pale and shaking. We saw the Nephilim there, they say - the same word, the same giants. After the Flood.

[A] So the four words are not a slip. They are a thread the book ties off and then picks up again hundreds of pages later. The giants were before the Flood - and, scripture insists with a straight face, after it. The thread does not break.

## PART THREE - THE ANSWERS THAT DON'T QUITE CLOSE

[A] So how? If the cure worked, the disease should be gone. Honest telling admits the texts do not hand us one clean answer. But the old writings circle the gap, and the explanations they offer are each, in their own way, unnerving.

[A] The first answer is the simplest. Perhaps the corruption did not need the watchers to come down a second time. Perhaps it was already carried in the blood - and one strand of that blood walked onto the ark.

[A] Not in the family itself - the texts call them blameless in their generation. But the old writings whisper of a wife, a line, a trace of the old blood riding through the Flood in a body no one thought to suspect. The cure had a passenger.

## PART FOUR - THE RESTLESS DEAD

[A] That third answer is the strangest, and the Book of Enoch states it almost plainly. When the giants died, it says, their bodies returned to the earth - but the spirit in them, born of heaven and flesh together, had nowhere to go.

[A] Too earthly for heaven. Too unnatural for the grave. So they remained, the text says - spirits without bodies, wandering the world, and they would trouble mankind from that day forward without ever being seen.

## PART FIVE - THE GIANTS AT THE BORDER
