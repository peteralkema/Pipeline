## PART ONE — Let My People Go

[A] For four hundred years, the people of Israel had lived in Egypt — and for much of it, they had lived as slaves. They built the cities of Pharaoh with their own hands, under the whip, crying out under a burden that never lifted. And the old text says God heard them.

## PART TWO — The First Blows

[A] So it began. Moses met Pharaoh at the river at dawn, and stretched out his staff over the water. And the Nile — the lifeblood of Egypt, the river they worshipped as divine — turned to blood. The fish died. The water stank. And a god of Egypt lay bleeding in its own bed.

[A] Seven days passed. And then came the second blow. Frogs — swarming up out of the bloodied river in their countless thousands, covering the land, filling the houses, the beds, the ovens, until there was nowhere in all of Egypt a person could stand without them underfoot.

[A] Pharaoh wavered — and hardened again. So the eighth plague came, on a wind out of the east that blew all day and all night. And in the morning, the locusts came. They covered the face of the whole land until it was black with them, and ate every green thing the hail had left.

## CLOSE — The Lamb and the Sea

[A] And so, that very night, Israel walked out of Egypt. A nation of slaves, four hundred years in bondage, walking free into the desert under the stars — carrying their kneaded dough on their backs, leaving in such haste there was no time even to let the bread rise.
