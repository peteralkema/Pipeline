## PART ONE - WHAT A WATCHER WAS

[A] To feel the weight of the oath, you have to understand what was standing on that mountain. Genesis calls them the sons of God. The old texts call them watchers. Either way, these were not low or lesser beings.

[A] A watcher's task was in the name. To watch. To stand sleepless over the young world, to observe it, to guard its order from above. They were trusted with the earth the way a sentinel is trusted with a sleeping city.

## PART TWO - THE FEAR OF DOING IT ALONE

[A] Here the old text gives a detail so human it is almost unbearable. The watchers wanted the daughters of men. But they were afraid. Not of the act. Of doing it alone - and bearing the punishment alone.

[A] Their leader puts the fear into words. I fear, he says, that you will not actually go through with this - that you will leave me to pay the price for this great sin by myself. One being, afraid of being abandoned by the rest.

[A] Think about what that fear admits. They all knew it was a great sin. Not a grey area. Not a misunderstanding. A great sin, named as such, in advance, by the very ones about to commit it. They went in with their eyes open.

## PART THREE - THE SWEARING

[A] So picture the summit on that night. Two hundred of them, ranged in a circle on the cold stone, the world sleeping far below, the stars of heaven they were about to forfeit burning hard overhead. And the leader lifts his hand.

## PART FOUR - THE WEIGHT OF A SWORN THING

[A] Step back from the mountain for a moment, because the oath is doing something the rest of the story depends on. In the old understanding, a sworn thing has weight. A word, once bound, is real. An oath is not undone by simply changing your mind.

[A] That is why the leader reached for an oath and not merely an agreement. An agreement can be abandoned. An oath, in that world, binds the soul to the deed. By swearing, the watchers did not just plan the fall. They made it owe them nothing but its completion.

[A] And so the tragedy is locked in before a single watcher ever touches the earth. The descent, the teaching, the giants, the ruin, the Flood - all of it flows from a choice already made unchangeable on the summit. The oath is the hinge the whole disaster turns on.

[A] This is why the old texts dwell on the oath at all. It would have been enough to say the watchers came down. But the texts insist on the swearing first - because the swearing is where the will turns, where guardians decide, together, to stop being guardians.

## PART SIX - WHAT THEY SWORE AWAY

[A] We cannot stand on the summit as it was. The watchers are long since fallen and bound; the night of the oath is three thousand years past telling. We have the mountain, still bearing its name, and the old text that says what was sworn upon it.

[A] And honesty holds. The swearing itself, the circle, the cord of binding - that is the old text speaking, filling the silence Genesis leaves. Genesis gives the fall in a handful of lines; First Enoch gives the night that made it sure. Weigh them each as you will.

[A] But weigh what both of them agree upon. Beings of heaven looked down at the earth, and crossed a line that was never meant to be crossed, and the world was ruined for it. That much the canon and the old text tell together, without flinching.
