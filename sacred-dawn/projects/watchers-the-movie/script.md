VISUAL: Time wheeling over the ark and the small ageing figure at its side, seasons turning, the hull slowly closing; jewel-toned, patient decades.

[A] And all the while, Noah warned them. The old text calls him a herald of righteousness — a man who preached, year upon year, to anyone who would listen, that the end was coming, and that there was still a door standing open.
VISUAL: The faceless figure of Noah raised on the half-built hull, calling out to a gathered crowd below, arm lifted in warning; jewel-toned, unheeded.

[A] And year upon year, they laughed. Where is your flood, old man, they called. The sky is clear. It has always been clear. You have wasted your life on a boat in a field, and we have wasted enough breath laughing at it.
VISUAL: A jeering faceless crowd at the foot of the ark, turning away, mocking gestures, a giant-shadow among them; storm-grey, derision.

[A] That is the loneliest thing faith ever asks — to keep building when every voice you know is certain you are a fool, and the sky gives you not one single sign that you are right. Noah built anyway. For a hundred years and more, he built anyway.
VISUAL: The lone figure working on the vast hull under an empty, clear, pitiless sky, no cloud anywhere; jewel-toned, the long test of faith.

[A] And then, at last, the animals came — and the laughter caught in the throats of the watching world. For whatever called the beasts out of the forests two by two was plainly not Noah. Something the mockers could not see had begun to move.
VISUAL: The jeering crowd falling silent as the long columns of paired animals stream past them into the ark; storm-grey, dawning fear.

[A] Noah took aboard food for every kind, and stores for the long dark of the water, and when the last of the remnant was within, the eight souls went up into the ark, and waited. And the world, uneasy now, waited with them.
VISUAL: The last stores carried up the ramp and the eight small faceless figures going into the vast ark, the uneasy world watching; jewel-toned, a held breath.

[A] For seven days, nothing happened. The ark sat sealed on the dry ground, and the world went on mocking, and the giants went on warring, and the cities went on singing. And then, on the seventh day, the sky began to change.
VISUAL: The sealed ark on dry ground under a sky going from gold to iron, the first vast clouds stacking on the horizon; storm-grey overtaking jewel tones; ominous stillness.

## PART SEVEN — THE DELUGE

[A] It did not begin with rain. The old text says the fountains of the great deep burst open — that the water came first from below, the sea breaking up through the floor of the world, the ground itself splitting apart and pouring.
VISUAL: The earth cracking open across a wide plain, columns of seawater erupting upward from the broken ground, faceless figures scattering; storm-grey, violent, immense.

[A] And then the windows of heaven were opened. The sky, which had never broken in all the history of the world, broke at last — and the rain came down, not as weather, but as judgment, in a weight of water no living thing had ever seen.
VISUAL: The whole sky opening into a grey wall of falling water, the land vanishing behind it, the ark beginning to lift on the rising flood; storm-grey, overwhelming.

[A] At first the people only ran for higher ground, the way men run from any flood, certain it would crest and fall. It did not crest. It did not fall. It rose, and rose, and kept rising, past every mark that any flood had ever left.
VISUAL: Faceless crowds fleeing uphill from rising grey water, looking back as it follows them faster than they can climb; storm-grey, mounting panic.

[A] The valleys filled first. The rivers swallowed their banks, then their cities, then the hills the cities had climbed for safety. The forges drowned. The terraces drowned. The riverbank where the daughters of men once walked in the cool of the evening went under without a sound.
VISUAL: Floodwater swallowing terraced fields and stone cities, the warm places of the opening now grey and drowning, faceless rooftops going under; storm-grey, mournful.

[A] And the giants ran. The lords of the earth, the unkillable kings, the sons of heaven and earth — they fled the water like everything else that lived, climbing for the high places, roaring up at a sky that would not stop.
VISUAL: Colossal faceless giant-shapes wading and clambering up flooding mountainsides, the water rising faster than they can climb; storm-grey, the mighty made small.

[A] The men who had worshipped them, and feared them, and starved to feed them, now drowned beside them in the same grey water — master and victim, giant and slave, all of it levelled at last by the rising of the sea.
VISUAL: Faceless figures of every size struggling together in the same churning grey flood, all distinction gone; storm-grey, a terrible equality.

[A] But there is no high enough ground when heaven empties itself. The water took the foothills, and then the mountains, and then the peaks the giants had climbed to. One by one, the things that had terrorised the whole earth slipped beneath the same grey flood as everyone else.
VISUAL: The last mountain peaks going under, colossal faceless forms sinking into a world-covering grey sea, the surface closing flat above them; storm-grey, vast and final.

[A] The strength that had crushed armies meant nothing against the flood. The hunger that had eaten the world could not eat the water. The giants who could not be killed by any blade of man were unmade, every one, by the rising of the sea.
VISUAL: A wide grey ocean where a mountain range used to be, nothing breaking the surface, the giants gone beneath it; storm-grey, an emptied world.

[A] And the corruption went down with them. The forbidden metalwork rusting on the seabed. The cities of pride dissolved to silt. The blood that had soaked into every field, lifted and carried and washed at last out of the wounded ground.
VISUAL: Underwater — drowned cities and scattered weapons settling into grey silt, the wound of the world quietly closing beneath the flood; storm-grey, strangely peaceful.

[A] Even Azazel's teaching drowned. The swords he had taught men to forge sank into the dark. The bright stones he had taught the women to covet rolled away across the seabed. Everything the watchers had given the world, the world carried down with it into the deep.
VISUAL: Blades, ornaments, and carved symbols tumbling slowly through grey water into the silt, the watchers' gifts returning to the ground; storm-grey, an undoing.

[A] Above it all, one wooden vessel rode the grey waste. Sealed, and small, and alone on a sea with no shore — carrying the last clean seed of the living through the unmaking of everything else.
VISUAL: The ark alone on an endless grey ocean under a falling sky, no land in any direction, a single dark shape on the face of the deep; storm-grey, solitary, hopeful.

[A] And there were small human moments in the great drowning, the kind the old text does not record but we cannot help but imagine. Families climbing to the highest roofs. Mothers lifting children above the rising water for as long as their arms would hold.
VISUAL: Faceless figures crowded on a last rooftop above the grey flood, small forms held up against the water; storm-grey, heartbreak.

[A] The mockers who had laughed at the boat on dry land now stood in the flood they had been warned of, and understood, far too late, that the madman with the hammer had been the only sane man in the world.
VISUAL: Faceless figures waist-deep in rising grey water, faces turned toward the distant ark, dawning comprehension too late; storm-grey, bitter.

[A] Some, the old traditions say, swam out to the ark itself, and beat upon its sealed sides, and begged to be let in. But the door had been shut by a hand no man could open, and it did not open now. The time to enter had been the long years of the building.
VISUAL: Faceless hands beating on the sealed dark hull of the ark amid the flood, the door shut fast against them; storm-grey, anguish.

[A] And the ark did not answer them. It could not. It only rode the grey water, sealed and silent, carrying its remnant over the heads of the drowning, while the world that had laughed went under it without a trace.
VISUAL: The sealed ark riding high on the flood, dark shapes failing in the water around and beneath it; storm-grey, terrible and serene.

[A] One by one, the last high places vanished. The last rooftops. The last hands above the water. Until there was nothing left to see in any direction but the grey, unbroken face of a sea that now covered the whole earth.
VISUAL: The final rooftops and reaching hands slipping under, until only a flat grey sea remains to every horizon; storm-grey, a drowned world.

[A] For forty days the rain did not stop. And when at last it did, there was no world left to see. Only water, from edge to edge, where a whole corrupted age had been. The earth had been washed back down to nothing, and given the chance to begin again.
VISUAL: A still grey sea under a clearing sky, the ark a tiny silhouette at the centre of an empty drowned world; storm-grey lifting toward pale gold; vast quiet.

## PART EIGHT — THE SPIRITS THAT REMAIN

[A] So the watchers were bound, and the giants were drowned, and the corrupted world was washed away. The story should end there, clean. But it does not. Because something survived the flood that the water could not touch.
VISUAL: The still grey sea under a pale sky, calm and empty — and beneath the calm, a faint dark shimmer moving just under the surface; storm-grey, an unease that does not belong.

[A] When the giants died, their bodies sank. But the giants were never only bodies. They were the children of two worlds — flesh from their mothers of the earth, and spirit from their fathers out of heaven. And spirit does not drown.
VISUAL: Beneath the grey water, colossal faceless bodies settling into the deep — and rising up out of each one, a faint pale shadow the water does not hold down; storm-grey, eerie.

[A] The old text is terribly precise about this. The bodies of the giants returned to the earth. But the spirits that came out of them — the spirits of the mighty, born of heaven and earth — those did not die. Those rose. And those remained.
VISUAL: Pale faceless shapes lifting off the surface of the drowned world, drifting upward into the empty air over the water; storm-grey, weightless and wrong.

[A] They could not go up. Heaven was closed to them. And they could not go down to rest, the way the dead of men go down. They were a thing that had never been meant to exist, and the world had nowhere to put them.
VISUAL: The pale spirit-shapes hanging between a closed bright sky above and the grey drowned earth below, belonging to neither; storm-grey, suspended, homeless.

[A] So they stayed. Here. In the air of the world, in the empty places and the ruined places, in the spaces between the living. Homeless, restless, and full of a hunger they could no longer feed with their hands. The flood had taken their bodies. It had not taken their appetite.
VISUAL: Pale faceless wisps drifting through a fog-grey ruined landscape, threading between broken stones, searching; storm-grey, a haunted emptiness.

[A] And as the waters fell and the new world dried, they spread out across it — thin, and patient, and waiting. Waiting for the earth to fill again with people. Waiting for new hands they could not have, new lives they could not live, new flesh to envy and torment.
VISUAL: The pale wisps spreading thin over a recovering grey-green landscape, drifting toward the first new shoots and the first returning birds; storm-grey and faint green.

[A] And the old text gives them the name the world has feared ever since. The spirits of the giants, it says, shall afflict, oppress, destroy, and assault. They shall rise up against the children of men, because they have come out of them. These are the evil spirits. These are the demons.
VISUAL: A pale faceless shape drawing close behind a small unsuspecting human figure in a grey waste, leaning toward them, a hunger in the lean; storm-grey, intimate dread.

[A] This is the part the storybooks leave out. The demons of scripture, the unclean spirits cast out in every age — the old text says they are not strangers here. They are the ghosts of the giants. The orphaned hunger of the watchers' sin, still walking the earth their fathers ruined.
VISUAL: A field of pale faceless wisps spread thin across a recovering world, drifting among the first new trees, watching the first new people; storm-grey and faint green.

[A] They cannot create. They cannot build. They can only get inside what others have built, and twist it. They whisper at the ear. They sour the heart. They drive the mind against itself. Every torment the old world knew from the outside, the new world would learn from within.
VISUAL: A pale wisp sinking into the shadow behind a small human figure's shoulder, the figure unaware; storm-grey, the war moving indoors.

[A] And so the world that came after the flood was never quite as empty as it looked. Behind the new green, behind the rebuilt walls, behind the faces of the living, the orphaned hunger of the old world drifted, and waited, and watched.
VISUAL: A recovering green-grey world, faint pale wisps threaded invisibly through its quiet places, watching the living; storm-grey and faint green, unease.

[A] This is why, in every age and every land, men have felt it — the sense of something unseen at the edge of the dark, something that wants in, something that hates the warmth of a living body because it remembers having one, and losing it.
VISUAL: A small human figure by a low fire at night, a pale shape gathering just beyond the firelight's edge; storm-grey, intimate dread.

[A] The old text names them without flinching. Evil spirits. Unclean spirits. The spirits of the giants, who afflict and oppress and assault the children of men — because they came out of men, and cannot forgive the living for being alive.
VISUAL: Pale faceless shapes pressing close around the edges of a lit human dwelling, kept out only by the warmth within; storm-grey, siege.

[A] They are not the watchers, bound in the deep. They are not the devil of the later books. They are something smaller, and nearer, and more personal — the ghosts of a drowned and monstrous race, still hungry, still here, still waiting for the only thing that will finally still them.
VISUAL: Pale faceless wisps drifting close around the edges of a small lit human dwelling in a recovering grey-green world, hungry and near; storm-grey, intimate dread.

[A] That is why the flood did not end the war. The watchers were chained and the giants drowned, but the hunger they made was loosed into every generation that followed — including this one. The bodies are four thousand years gone. The spirits, the old text says, remain until the day of judgment.
VISUAL: The pale wisps thinning into near-invisibility over a world growing green again, never quite gone, woven into the air the living breathe; storm-grey bleeding into pale jewel tones.

## PART NINE — THE COVENANT

[A] But the story does not leave us in the haunted dark. For the same patience that had withheld the flood for the long years of Noah's building now turned, at last, toward mercy. The rain had stopped. The waters began, slowly, to fall.
VISUAL: The grey sea lowering, the first dark shapes of drowned hilltops breaking the surface again, a paler light in the sky; storm-grey lifting toward gold.

[A] And the ark, which had ridden the grey waste for so long, came at last to rest. It grounded upon the mountains of a high country, and sat still, and waited, while the world beneath it slowly drained away toward the sea.
VISUAL: The ark settling onto a bare wet mountain peak rising from the falling flood, the first solid ground in a drowned world; storm-grey and dawn-gold, stillness.

[A] Noah sent out a raven, and it did not return. Then he sent a dove, and it found no place to rest, and came back to his hand. He waited, and sent it again. And this time it returned with a fresh olive leaf in its beak.
VISUAL: A pale dove returning to a faceless outstretched hand at the ark's window, a single green olive leaf in its beak, grey water beyond; jewel-toned hope against storm-grey.

[A] A green leaf, plucked from a living tree. Somewhere out in the drowned world, something had begun to grow again. The judgment was over. The earth was healing. And the small family in the ark wept, the old text lets us imagine, at the sight of one green leaf.
VISUAL: Close on the single green olive leaf in the pale hand, the first living green in a world of grey water; jewel-toned, tender, a world reborn in a leaf.

[A] And when the ground was dry, the door that heaven had shut was opened. Noah and his family stepped down onto a cleaned and quiet earth, and the remnant of the living went out with them, into a world washed back to its beginning.
VISUAL: Faceless figures and animals descending from the grounded ark onto wet new earth under a clearing sky, the first footprints on a remade world; jewel-toned, hushed.

[A] And the first thing Noah did, on the new earth, was build an altar. Not a fortress, not a forge, not a throne. An altar — turning the very first act of the new world back toward the heaven that had spared him.
VISUAL: A small simple altar of stones on a bare hillside, a thin column of smoke rising from it toward a clearing sky, a faceless figure bowed before it; jewel-toned, reverent.

[A] And heaven answered with a promise, and hung the sign of it in the sky. A bow of light, bent across the clearing clouds, the first rainbow the world had ever seen. Never again, the promise said, would the waters be sent to drown the whole earth.
VISUAL: A great rainbow arcing across clearing storm-clouds over the new green-grey world, the ark small beneath it, faceless figures looking up; jewel-toned, the first covenant.

[A] It was a true mercy, and a real beginning. The blood was washed from the ground. The giants were gone. The cities of pride were silt on the seabed. The world had been given, against all its deserving, a second morning.
VISUAL: A wide view of a cleaned green-grey world under a rainbow, small new beginnings of smoke and life scattered across it; jewel-toned, dawn, fragile hope.

[A] And yet. The watchers still lay bound beneath the hills. Azazel still lay chained in his desert pit. And the pale spirits of the giants still drifted, thin and hungry, through the very air the new world would breathe. The flood had cleaned the earth. It had not finished the war.
VISUAL: The new world under its rainbow — and woven faint through it, the bound dark beneath the ground and the pale wisps in the air, the shadow inside the second morning; jewel-toned shot through with grey.

[A] So the new world began, exactly as the first one had — beautiful, and forgiven, and already shadowed. The mercy was real. But so was the hunger that survived it. And both of them were handed down, together, to every generation that has lived since. Including ours.
VISUAL: The green-grey world settling into its second age, the rainbow fading, the faint shadows remaining; jewel-toned with a persistent thread of storm-grey.

## CODA — THE WAR THAT WAITS

[A] And so the Book of Enoch leaves the world in a strange and unfinished peace. The flood is over. The remnant walks out onto cleaned ground. The bow is in the sky. But nothing has truly ended. Everything has only been delayed.
VISUAL: Faceless figures stepping down from the grounded ark onto wet new earth under a fading rainbow, the long story not quite closed; jewel-toned hope shot through with grey.

[A] The watchers lie bound in the dark beneath the hills, exactly where the sentence left them — not destroyed, only kept. Waiting out the seventy generations until the day they are brought up to be judged at last.
VISUAL: Deep underground, faceless colossal forms bound in chains of cold light in absolute dark, utterly still, waiting; storm-grey black, the patience of the condemned.

[A] Azazel lies bound in the desert, in the place called Dudael, in the darkness the very first scene of this story showed you — the first thing you saw, now the last thing left of him, until the great judgment opens that pit one final time.
VISUAL: The shattered desert from the cold open, the colossal bound figure half-buried and motionless under a bruised sky, the scattered weapons rusting around him; storm-grey, the circle closed.

[A] And the spirits of the giants still move through the air of the world, restless, hungry, and unhoused — the demons of every age since, the war the flood could not drown, waiting like their fathers for the day it is all finally answered.
VISUAL: A modern-feeling grey dusk, faint pale wisps threading between the lights of a living world, the ancient hunger still moving among the unsuspecting; storm-grey, quietly chilling.

[A] One man walked out of this story without dying. Enoch, the scribe who carried heaven's sentence to the watchers, did not go down to the grave like other men. The old text says he walked with God — and then he was not, for God took him.
VISUAL: A single faceless figure walking upward into a column of high gold light and simply ceasing to be there, the ground empty where he stood; jewel-toned, luminous, mysterious.

[A] He is the one clean thread out of the whole dark age — the man who saw the watchers fall, wrote down their judgment, walked among them unstained, and was lifted out of the world before the water came. And what heaven showed him on the way up is its own story.
VISUAL: The empty place where Enoch stood, the high light closing, a faint impression of vast crystal-and-fire halls glimpsed in the brightness above; jewel-toned, a door left open.

[A] So let me leave it with you. The watchers thought a sworn oath could make a forbidden thing safe — that if all were guilty, none would answer. Tell me below: when everyone around you is doing what you know is wrong, does the crowd make it right, or only make it easier?
VISUAL: A single small faceless human figure standing between the bright closed sky and the dark waiting earth, the pale spirits faint in the air around them; storm-grey and gold in balance.

[A] Two hundred angels broke the world rather than keep their watch. The flood washed the earth, but not the hunger they left in it. Next, on Sacred Dawn: the scribe who was taken — Enoch, and the heavens he was shown. Until then, walk in the light while it still calls you by name.
VISUAL: The faceless figure of Enoch ascending into high gold light over a cleaned and quiet world, the pale spirits below and the bound dark beneath, the whole story held in one frame; jewel-toned and storm-grey.
