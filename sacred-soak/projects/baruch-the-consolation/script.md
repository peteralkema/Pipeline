## PART ONE - The Grief Over the City

[A] Be still. We open a new book tonight, the Apocalypse of Baruch, an old book of comfort, and come to the prophet in his sorrow, sitting in grief over a fallen city, weeping for what was lost - and to the gentle voice of the Lord, which came to him in his grief, to console him, and to lift his eyes to a greater city, that cannot fall.

[A] And Baruch sat, says the book, and wept, and mourned over the city that had fallen, and said: blessed are they who were not born, or who, being born, have died; but for us who are left, woe, for we have seen the sorrows that are come. And he sat in his grief, by night, and could find no comfort, and lifted his heart to the Lord.

There is no shame in grief, nor in finding no comfort for a while. But the lifting of the heart, even in sorrow, is the beginning of the comfort. Rest, and lift your heart gently, and let the comfort come.

## PART TWO - The Voice That Answers

[A] And the voice of the Lord came to Baruch, says the book, in the night, gently, and said: why do you weep, Baruch, and mourn so deeply? Do you think that this is the city of which I said, on the palms of my hands I have graven you? This building that is fallen is not the city I meant.

[A] It is not this that I had in mind, says the Lord, when I spoke of the city, for the city of stone is but a shadow of the true one. The true Jerusalem is not built by hands, nor thrown down by hands, but is kept with me, prepared from of old, from the time I resolved to make paradise, and it cannot fall, nor be taken, nor be lost.
VISUAL: A faint vast city of soft white-gold light gathered high in a luminous heaven, gentle and shining and whole, a deep and steady glory, awe-filled and reverent, no figures.

[A] Let the voice that answers be gentle in your sleep, said the night - the Lord coming to the grief, and saying: the thing you mourn was only a shadow; the true city cannot fall. The things we grieve are often shadows of a truer thing that cannot be lost. Rest in that gentle answer: the true thing is kept, and whole, and cannot be taken, however much the shadow falls.

## PART THREE - The City That Is Kept

[A] For I showed it to Adam, says the Lord, before he sinned, the true city, and the garden; and I showed it to Abraham, in the night, among the stars; and I showed it to Moses, on the mountain, the pattern of it, with all its measures; and it has been kept, with me, ever since, prepared, and waiting, and it shall be revealed at the last.

[A] And it shall not be hidden for ever, says the Lord, but in the end of days it shall be revealed, and come down, and be given to my people, and they shall dwell in it, and there shall be no more sorrow in it, nor weeping, nor any falling, but only light, and gladness, and a peace that has no end.

[A] Let the city that is kept be the thought you carry down, said the night - the true Jerusalem, shown to Adam and Abraham and Moses, kept with the Lord from of old, prepared, and waiting, to be revealed at the last, where there is no more sorrow, nor weeping, nor falling. There is a city kept for you, that cannot fall. Rest toward it, the kept city, where there is no more weeping, gently, while the night turns over you.

## PART FOUR - The Sorrow Is Short

[A] And the Lord said to Baruch: do not let your heart be troubled overmuch by the present time, for the present is short, and full of sorrow, but the time that is coming is long, and full of glory, and the one is but a passing shadow, and the other is the true and lasting day.

[A] For the youth of this world is past, says the Lord, and the strength of creation is already spent, and the coming of the times is very near, and the harvest of the age is at hand. Therefore do not weep for the passing things, but lift your eyes to the things that are coming, which are good, and lasting, and full of light.
VISUAL: A soft dawn breaking gently over a still quiet land, a warm light spreading slowly across it, a deep promise in the calm, painterly and reverent, no figures.

[A] Let the shortness of the sorrow be gentle in your sleep, said the night - the present time short, and full of sorrow, but passing, a shadow; the coming time long, and full of glory, and lasting. Whatever sorrow you carried today is a passing shadow, and the glory that is coming is the true and lasting day. Rest in that, that the sorrow is short, and the light is long, and the day is nearly here.

[A] Therefore be comforted, Baruch, says the Lord, and do not mourn overmuch, and do not let the sorrow of the present overwhelm you, for I have not forgotten my people, nor the city that is kept, nor the promise that is sure, but I am keeping all of it, and bringing it to pass, in its own time, which is near.

Lift your eyes too, from whatever has fallen, to what is kept, and be comforted, and be at peace. Rest in the comfort, your eyes on the city that is kept.

## PART EIGHT - Closing Reflection

[A] So tonight we have heard the comfort spoken to Baruch - the grief over the fallen city, the gentle voice that answered, the true city that is kept and cannot fall, the shortness of the sorrow, the rising of the righteous, the wiping of every tear, and the lifting of the eyes from what falls to what is kept.

[A] Hold none of it too tightly. You do not need to carry the sorrow, or the city, or the promise. It was set down long ago, that it might be a comfort to a generation far off. Perhaps tonight, that generation is you.

[A] So let the old comfort rest where it has fallen, and let the night be still. The things you grieve are often shadows of a truer thing that cannot be lost; the present sorrow is short, and the coming glory is long; and there is a day when every tear is wiped away. Lift your eyes from what falls to what is kept. Rest in that comfort, while the night turns slowly over you.

[A] So breathe out. Let the day go from you. Another night we will sit with Ezra, and hear his hard questions gently answered. But for now, be comforted, and rest. Until then - rest.
