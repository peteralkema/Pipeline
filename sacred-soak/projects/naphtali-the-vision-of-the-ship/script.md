## PART TWENTY - Closing Reflection

[A] So tonight we have kept company with Naphtali - the vision of the ship upon the sea, the tossing and the keeping, the scattering and the gathering, the lights set in the heaven to guide, the gentle order of all things, the seasons and their turning, and the ship coming, at last, to harbour, and the family gathered there, at rest.
VISUAL: The single oil lamp again, burning low now in the dark stone chamber, the scroll closed beside it, the amber light soft and fading, dust settling slowly in the still air, intimate and quiet, the reading done.

[A] Hold none of it too tightly. You do not need to carry the ship, or the order, or the harbour. It was set down long ago, that it might be a comfort to a generation far off. Perhaps tonight, that generation is you.
VISUAL: A wide calm horizon at the very end of dusk, a thin warm line of gold beneath a deepening blue, the first stars appearing above it, calm and vast, the day letting go, no figures, only the turning sky.

[A] So let the old vision rest where it has fallen, and let the night be still. You do not steer your own voyage, but are carried by a pilot who does not fail; though the sea toss you, the ship is kept; though you be scattered, you shall be gathered; and every voyage has its harbour, and its rest. It is the season of rest now. Keep it gently. Rest in that, while the night turns slowly over you.
VISUAL: The vast still night sky once more, the faint band of stars turning slowly overhead, deeper now and calmer, the dark soft and complete, endless and peaceful, the long quiet of the heavens.

[A] So breathe out. Let the day go from you. You are carried by a faithful pilot upon the sea of the world, kept through every tossing, and bound, at the last, for a quiet harbour where all the scattered are gathered and made whole. This is the season of rest. Another night we will draw again from the deep quarry of the old books. But for now, keep the season of rest, and sail on gently. Until then - rest.
VISUAL: A single faint star high in a deep and gentle dark, holding steady, the night calm and boundless around it, utterly still, the last soft light, nothing else.
