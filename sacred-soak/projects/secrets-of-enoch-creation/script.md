VISUAL: A soft dim dawn over a still quiet earth, a faint warm light breaking gently at the far horizon, a deep and tender promise in the calm, painterly and reverent, no figures.

[A] Let the word be gentle in your sleep, said the night — you are earth, and to the earth you shall return, and even that is not the end, for the One who took you up from the dust can take you up again. The lying-down in the ground is a sleep, not a finishing. Rest in the promise that lies underneath it, sure and quiet, while the night turns over you.
VISUAL: A soft dim dawn over a still earth, a faint warm light breaking gently at the horizon, a deep and tender peace, deeply restful, painterly and serene, no figures.

## PART TWENTY-TWO — The Times Appointed

[A] And I appointed the times, said the Lord, and the seasons, and the years, and the months, and the days, and the hours, that man might number them, and know the turning of the years, and the courses of the sun and the moon, and the ordering of his life upon the earth.
VISUAL: A vast deep heaven of slow-turning stars over a still earth, a soft sun and pale moon set in their gentle order, the seasons turning calm and unhurried, serene and reverent, no figures.

[A] And I set the seven days of the week, said the Lord, and appointed the seventh for rest, that on it should be no labour, but stillness, and peace, and the remembering of the making. And I blessed the seventh day above all the days, and made it holy, a day of rest for all the world.
VISUAL: A wide calm land at golden hour under a soft sky, the light low and warm and even, a deep and settled stillness over the whole of it, deeply peaceful, painterly and serene, no figures.

[A] Let the seventh day's rest be gentle over your sleep, said the night — the day the Lord blessed above all others, set apart for stillness, and peace, and the laying-down of labour. The rest you take tonight is a small keeping of that first blessed rest. You are allowed to stop. You were made to stop. Rest now, in the holy stillness set for you from the beginning.
VISUAL: A wide calm land at golden hour, soft warm light lying even over the whole of it, a deep settled stillness, deeply restful, painterly and serene, no figures.

## PART TWENTY-THREE — The Day Without Count

[A] And after the seven thousand years, said the Lord, I appointed an eighth time, a day without count, a great rest without end, a day that does not number itself in weeks or in years, but goes on, in stillness, and in light, for ever.
VISUAL: A vast and gentle heaven of soft endless gold and white light beyond the horizon, a deep and boundless peace lying over it, faint and serene, painterly and reverent, no figures.

[A] For all the days of the world will be counted, said the Lord, and all the years will be numbered, and then the counting itself will cease, and there will come a day that is not a day among days, but a rest that holds all the rest within it, and never turns again to evening.
VISUAL: A vast soft endless light beyond a still horizon, gentle and boundless, a deep peace with no edge and no ending, painterly and reverent, no figures.

[A] Let the day without count be the far horizon of your sleep, said the night — beyond all the weeks of the world, an eighth day that never numbers itself, a rest that does not end, a morning that does not turn again to evening. The little rest you take tonight is a small share of that great endless one. Rest now, in the shadow of the rest that has no end.
VISUAL: A wide calm horizon at the edge of a soft endless dawn, a gentle gold light rising over a still world, a deep boundless peace, deeply restful, painterly and serene, no figures.

## PART TWENTY-FOUR — None Counselled Me

[A] And all this I made known to you, Enoch, said the Lord, and there was no counsellor in my making, nor any helper, but I myself, and my wisdom, and my word. I am self-existent, and without beginning, and without end, and there is none beside me.
VISUAL: A vast soft white-gold radiance filling a still heaven, gentle and boundless, deep and without edge, awe-filled and serene, no figures, only the light.

[A] My thought is without change, said the Lord, and my wisdom is my counsellor, and my word is my deed. If I look upon all things, they stand and are kept, and tremble; and there is nothing hidden from me, and nothing too small for my care, and nothing too far for my eye.
VISUAL: A vast soft radiance over a still world, a gentle and even light holding everything quietly in being, calm and boundless, awe-filled and serene, no figures.

[A] Let the steadiness of the Lord hold you in your sleep, said the night — the one who made all things without a counsellor, whose word is his deed, whose eye is on all things, and on you. Nothing is too small for his care, and nothing too far for his gaze. He has not looked away. Rest in that holding, sure and unchanging, while the night turns slowly over you.
VISUAL: A vast soft white-gold radiance over a still and peaceful world, a gentle even light holding all things calmly in being, deeply restful, painterly and serene, no figures.

## PART TWENTY-FIVE — The Books Given

[A] But apply your mind, Enoch, said the Lord, and know him who speaks to you, and take the books which you yourself have written, and go down to the earth, and tell your sons all that I have told you, and all that you have seen, from the lowest heaven to my throne.
VISUAL: A faint shining figure seated within a great soft light, a soft pale book open before him, a gentle radiance over the calm heaven, reverent, no faces shown.

[A] For these things must not be lost, said the Lord, but written, and kept, and given to your children, and to their children after them, that the generations far off might know how the world was made, and by whom, and might walk in the light, and find their way home.
VISUAL: A soft pale book of gentle light held open in a great radiance, faint and luminous, a sense of words being kept for a far generation, reverent and serene, no figures.

[A] Let the giving of the books be the last gentle thought of the telling, said the night — the secrets of the making, set down, and kept, and handed on, all the long way down the generations, until they reached this night, and you. They were written so that you might know you were made, and wanted, and loved. Rest now, holding that knowing, gently, as you fall asleep.
VISUAL: A soft pale book of gentle light in a great calm radiance, faint and luminous, a deep peace over the still heaven, deeply restful, painterly and serene, no figures.

## PART TWENTY-SIX — Closing Reflection

[A] So tonight we have heard the oldest secret of all — how the Lord, alone in the invisible, conceived a world, and called forth the first light and the first darkness, and laid the foundations, and made the seven days, and the lights, and the living things, and the first man from seven things, and set the times, and the great rest beyond all counting.
VISUAL: The single oil lamp again, burning lower now in the dark stone chamber, the scroll closed beside it, the amber light soft and fading, dust settling slowly in the still air, intimate and quiet, the reading done.

[A] Hold none of it too tightly. You do not need to carry the seven days, or the making of man, or the ordering of the times. It was all set down long ago by a scribe who walked with God, that it might be a comfort to a generation far off. Perhaps tonight, that generation is you.
VISUAL: A wide calm horizon at the very end of dusk, a thin warm line of gold beneath a deepening blue, the first stars appearing above it, calm and vast, the day letting go, no figures, only the turning sky.

[A] So let the making of the world rest where it has fallen, and let the night be still. You were gathered from the dust and the dew and the starlight and the breath of God, a little of the whole creation, made free, and crowned, and loved. The same hand that made the light still holds you. Rest in that, while the night turns slowly over you.
VISUAL: The vast still night sky once more, the faint band of stars turning slowly overhead, deeper now and calmer, the dark soft and complete, endless and peaceful, the long quiet of the heavens.

[A] So breathe out. Let the day go from you. The world was made gently, and made good, and you were made within it, of earth and of heaven, to be free, and to be loved. Another night the scribe will go down to the earth again, to tell his children all of this. But for now, rest in the making, held by the hand that made the light. Until then — rest.
VISUAL: A single faint star high in a deep and gentle dark, holding steady, the night calm and boundless around it, utterly still, the last soft light, nothing else.
