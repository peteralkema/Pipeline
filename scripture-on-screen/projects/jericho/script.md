## ACT 4 - THE STRANGEST BATTLE PLAN

[A] Then the Lord gave Joshua the plan for taking the city. And it was unlike any battle plan in history. There was no siege. No ladders, no battering rams, no assault on the gates. Instead, God said: march around the city once, with all the armed men. Do this for six days.

[A] Seven priests were to carry seven trumpets of rams' horns in front of the ark of the covenant. Each day, for six days, the army would march once around the city in silence, with only the priests sounding the trumpets. No shouting. No war cries. Not a single word from the people. Just the tramp of feet and the wail of the horns, circling the walls, once a day.

[A] And then, on the seventh day, the plan changed. On that day, they were to march around the city not once, but seven times. And on the seventh time around, when the priests sounded a long blast on the trumpets, the whole army was to give a great shout. And the Lord promised: the wall of the city will collapse, and the people will charge straight in.

[A] Stop and think about how this looked to the soldiers of Israel. They were trained, armed, ready for war. Even humiliating. It required them to trust that obedience itself - not their swords - was the weapon.

[A] But they obeyed. On the first day, the armed guard went ahead, then the seven priests with the trumpets, then the ark of the covenant, then the rest of the people. [A] For six days it went on. Once around, in silence, every single day. Nothing happened. The walls still stood. To any watching eye, the whole exercise was achieving absolutely nothing.
