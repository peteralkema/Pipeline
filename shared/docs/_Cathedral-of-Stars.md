## 2. The thesis — why this channel exists

### The strategic chain

Cathedral applies the operation's banked filter — **un-filmable vs. re-watchable**, and *is there a reference the audience can catch us failing against?* The cosmic-scale lane passes cleanly: **no one has footage of TON 618, the surface of Betelgeuse, IC 1101, or the cosmic web.** These are the *only* images that could ever exist for these subjects. Flux's painterly, impossibly-lit quality — the thing that reads as *fake* in modern history — here reads as **awe**. The model's weakness is the genre's requirement. This is the same open-water logic that made Sacred Dawn the highest-fit use of the machine; Cathedral is the secular-cosmic sibling of that insight.

### The two swamps we deliberately avoid

The cosmic lane has two crowded, defensible-by-others swamps. Cathedral sits in neither:

1. **JWST-news-reactive churn** — "James Webb Just Saw X — And It's Horrifying." This is where the **money** is (Cosmicus, §3) but it is a treadmill: the hook burns, the clone swarm is brutal, and it ties content to a news cycle. Cathedral's topics are **evergreen** (the size of a star doesn't expire), so there is no crest to fall off — the same anti-graveyard logic as Sacred Dawn's fixed canon.
2. **Conspiracy / terror framing** — "The Scariest Planet," "Terrifying things that should not exist." Durable demand, but the wrong register for the brand and a YPP/quality risk.

### The wedge: the reverent-cinematic layer

Against the Space-Engine-flythrough and terror-doc competitors, Cathedral's differentiator is the **emotional-cinematic-reverent layer** — Elliot's liturgical read, the faceless-silhouette-for-scale motif, the curated music bed, transformation-not-termination endings. **The Quiet Observatory (§3) is the proof this works**: a real human voice actor doing contemplative cosmic docs at 24–28 min, hitting 549K on Sagittarius A* and 261K on TON 618 — the *exact* topics and register Cathedral runs, validated at long length. Cathedral's variation: the same reverence at **tight 8–12 min**, on the cheap pipeline, at scale.

### Why it's durable

Cosmic scale is a fixed, finite, non-seasonal, non-trend subject with permanent demand. You cannot saturate "the biggest thing in the universe." The only saturation risk is **packaging** (the terror treadmill), which we opt out of by anchoring on reverent awe. The moat is the lived judgment layer (niche selection, packaging discipline, banked failure principles), not the pipeline code.

## 4. The moat — reverence vs. terror packaging

The money-leaders print on a **terror treadmill**: "And It's Horrifying," "The Scariest Planet," "Should Not Exist." That demand is durable; those *framings* are not — the hooks burn, the clone swarm is relentless, and there is real YPP/demonetization risk in the register.

Cathedral keeps the **wonder** and drops the **fear**:

- **Awe, not dread.** Every topic that could be framed as terror (the Sun dying, a black hole, Betelgeuse exploding) is rendered as **transformation and wonder**. The supernova is a gift, not a threat; the Sun's death is "a cathedral of light."
- **The faceless silhouette is the brand and the craft.** A tiny lone human figure low in frame gives overwhelming scale at a glance — it is reverent, it dodges Flux's face/hand weakness, and it is the channel's visual signature across stills and thumbnails. Reverence and the production win are the same move (the Sacred Dawn lesson, applied to cosmic scale).
- **Attribution discipline.** Claims are tagged to what astronomy has measured ("a reverent recreation, drawn from what astronomy has measured of…" in every description). This separates Cathedral from the slop crowd and protects monetization.
- **The name disciplines packaging.** Read a terror title under "Cathedral of Stars" and it jars; read a reverent-awe title and it sings. Anything that clashes with the name is probably a title we shouldn't run (see the §11 packaging test).

The audience is **AI-indifferent but not reverence-indifferent.** It won't punish the AI look; it *will* reward genuine awe and punish cheap scares. Both cut toward us.

## 5. The content model

- **Format:** cinematic cosmic recreation, **~8–12 min** (flagship Stephenson 11:30; batch range 8:22–11:15). **Length is retention-earned, not padded** — the richest-ladder topics (Stephenson, Betelgeuse) run 11+; leaner single-concept topics (TON 618, death-of-Sun) run honestly at ~8:20. The lane floor is ~8 min (validated band; competitors win at 8:03). See §6 for the calibration discipline that produced this.
- **Mode:** **Mode A only** (cinematic recreation; no Mode B graphics). Cleanest pipeline path: `audio → modeA → convergence`.
- **Motion:** **front-two-Kling** — `channel.json` carries `kling_count: 0` as the floor, but the batch is **always run with `--kling-count 2`** (Kling on beats 1–2 to land motion on the cold-open kill zone — the "superglue" cold open; everything else Ken Burns). The runner defaults to 0 without the flag — passing `--kling-count 2` is mandatory.
- **Voice:** **Elliot** (`voice_id: "Elliot"`, `speaking_rate: 0.9`). British, deep, liturgical. The slow grand read is the register.
- **Render rate (calibration constant):** **~15.7 s/beat** at Elliot 0.9 (measured from the flagship: 44 beats → 11:30). Target ~11 min ≈ **~43 beats**. Use this to estimate runtime from beat count: `runtime ≈ beats × 15.7s`. (Note: higher than the ~14s/beat earlier rough estimate — Elliot at 0.9 runs slow and grand, which is the asset.)
- **Look:** cosmic awe — a colossal glowing object (star/galaxy/nebula) in the right two-thirds, deep gold-amber cooling to blue-white, a tiny faceless silhouette low for scale, dark negative space across the left third, painterly photoreal, no text. Awe-not-garish.
- **Music — curated Artlist.** Eight cinematic-fantasy-uplifting tracks normalized `track_01.mp3`…`track_08.mp3`, scp'd to `cathedral_of_stars/music/` on the box (gitignored — `*.mp3`; only the `channel.json` block is committed). `music` block: `{dir: "music", tracks: 3, crossfade_seconds: 2, level: 0.06}`. (Cathedral runs level **0.06**, a touch under the 0.07 proven elsewhere — validated by ear on the flagship.)
- **Cost:** **under $20 in fal spend per finished video**, regardless of beat count — Ken Burns is free, Kling is the only paid leg, front-two-Kling caps it at two calls. Beat count does **not** drive cost.
- **Upload:** channel-agnostic OAuth upload step **is wired** (token minted this session). Category = **Entertainment** (`category_id: "24"`), `privacy_status: private` with `publishAt` scheduling — the scheduler flips private→public at the stamped time automatically.

## 6. The craft spine (retention)

*Inherits the two-lever model from Sacred Dawn / You Had To Be There, in a cosmic-awe key. Sharpened this session by the length-calibration work that took the batch from short first drafts to the final 8–11 min band.*

### The two-lever model

1. **The cold open (universal, topic-independent).** The channel bleeds audience in the first ~75 seconds regardless of topic. The fix is the **"superglue" cold open**: the two most kinetic visuals married to the two most arresting hook lines, **front-loaded into the Kling window** (beats 1–2 animated, so motion lands on the kill zone). Open inside the wonder, not a thesis; deliver the awe immediately; every one of the first ~4 beats escalates; plant the spine image (the colossal object, the silhouette for scale). Date/place braided in, never an opening brief.
2. **The body (the topic/execution filter).** Past the open, retention is decided by whether the piece is an **escalating climb of scale**, not a flat concept-explainer. The signature structure is a **ladder**: climb rung by rung (Sun → planets → asteroid belt; galaxy → group → cluster → supergiant; black hole → bigger → supermassive → titan), each rung a named comparison that re-earns attention. One human silhouette present through the entire runtime, including the explanatory acts (defuses the mid-video cliff). Sensation the still can't supply (the cold of space, the silence near the event horizon). Transcendent close + comment-bait.

### The fixed script-authoring rules (every script verified against these)

- Beats paired **1:1 with VISUAL** lines; one VISUAL per beat; every beat has narration with an animatable foreground subject.
- **Zero digits in narration** — all numbers spelled out (numerals OK in metadata/headers).
- **No beat over ~55 words.**
- **Faceless, drift-safe:** the tiny silhouette-for-scale motif; no resolved faces.
- **Recurring spine object/motif** planted early (the silhouette, the colossal object), harvested at the climax.
- **The ladder** — escalating named rungs of scale or time, not a single jump to the payoff.
- **Awe not dread** — NO "scariest / terrifying / should not exist (as fear)." Endings as transformation.
- **Un-referenced topics only** — the emotional-cinematic frame, never a Space-Engine flythrough.
- **Close accelerates:** image → moralised closer ("we are small, but we are the part of the universe that has learned to measure the giants") → **answerable comment-bait question** → **sequel hook** ("stay with us, and we will keep climbing…").
- **The brand refrain** — "stand beneath the cathedral of stars" kept as the spine of the close, wording varied per script.
- **Script locked before any pipeline spend.**

### The length-calibration discipline (banked this session — the hard lesson)

First drafts **consistently land short** (~25–32 beats / ~6–8 min when aiming for ~43 / ~11). Expanding "by feel" repeatedly **undershot** — adding 2–3 beats per act *feels* like a lot but doesn't reach target. The reliable method, proven on Betelgeuse (30 → 43, the proof-of-method):

1. **Expand to an explicit per-act budget**, not by feel: cold open 4 (untouched — never disturb the superglue beats), five acts of ~7, close 6 ≈ 43.
2. **Deepen, don't pad** — add real rungs to the ladder (more named comparisons), a "how we know" act (how astronomy measures the thing — the same move that gives credibility), more sensory/scale beats. Restating an existing beat in new words is the **padding tell** — when expansion starts producing near-duplicates, **stop**.
3. **Report the section distribution**, not just the total, to confirm balance.
4. **Accept ≥35 beats where pushing to 43 would pad.** "Length is retention-earned, not padded to a benchmark." Some topics (a black-hole *fall* is one event, not a ladder of many objects) genuinely carry less material and run honestly at ~9 min. Forcing them to 11 makes them *worse*. Variation 8–11 min is how a real catalog looks, not a defect.

**Banked caution:** re-editing the same files repeatedly across a long session causes beat-count drift and accidental near-duplicate beats. Re-count from ground truth (`grep -c '^\[A\]'`) after each pass; verify added beats landed and don't restate neighbors.

## 7. Title & thumbnail doctrine

**Complement, never echo.** The image carries the *what*; the title carries the *why-click*. The thumbnail headline and the video title must not repeat the same nouns.

### The thumbnail headline rule (banked, hard-won)

- **Two lines, each ~2–3 short words / ≤~13 characters.** The compositor shrinks the font to fit the widest line into the left column, so *character width per line* — not word count — is what matters. "DYING / TONIGHT" and "OUR GALAXY / IS A SPECK" both work; a single long word ("EXTRAORDINARY") shrinks just as badly as four short ones. Keep each line short and let the **subtitle carry the specifics**.
- **The subtitle is the recognition noun** (the object's name: "Stephenson 2-18," "TON 618," "Betelgeuse"); the **title is the dread/wonder hook** ("SHOULD / NOT EXIST," "IT SWALLOWS / SOLAR SYSTEMS").
- **No repetition across the slate.** "OUR GALAXY" opening three different headlines reads as formulaic when binged — vary the opening, not just the length. (Caught and fixed this session.)

### The `low_silhouette` thumbnail composition (the channel signature)

Cathedral uses **`composition: "low_silhouette"`** — the cosmic object massed right, a tiny silhouette low for scale, headline anchored **top-left over a dark scrim**. rembg OFF (the silhouette is part of the Flux render, not a paste; `segment_foreground: false`). Each script ships a paired **`<slug>.thumb.json`** (dot-named, exact base-name match or the runner silently skips it) with `title` (UPPERCASE two-line hook) and `subtitle` (recognition noun).

### The thumbnail bugs caught + fixed this session (do not regress)

The flagship test render surfaced **three batch-killers**, all now fixed in committed `channel.json`:

1. **Colors must be RGB arrays, not hex strings.** `make_thumbnail.py` does `tuple(color) + (255,)` — `[244,246,255]` works, `"#F4F6FF"` throws `TypeError: color must be int, or tuple…`. *This shipped the flagship with no thumbnail before it was caught.*
2. **`low_silhouette` needs the full layout-key set** or it falls to centered DEFAULTS (`text_anchor: "top-center"`, `text_align: "center"`). The required keys, mirrored from the proven Scripture/YHTBT blocks: `text_anchor: "top-left"`, `text_align: "left"`, the sizing stack (`title_area_pct: 0.52`, `title_max_height_pct: 0.34`, `title_start_size: 150`, subtitle equivalents), `stroke_width: 12`, `shadow`, and a soft scrim (`{side: left, width: 0.42, opacity: 0.5, feather: 0.7}` — *not* a hard `feather: 0.18`). `margin_x: 40` (not 70).
3. **Still composition** — the Flux prompt suffix now explicitly pushes the object into the right two-thirds and **reserves a dark left third** for the headline, so freshly-generated stills have a genuine dark column (not just a scrim over a bright star). The selection step picks the candidate with the cleanest left negative space.

**Mechanic to remember:** the pipeline writes `thumbnail_still.png` (selected) then overlays the headline → `thumbnail.png` (what the uploader attaches). To re-overlay an existing still without fal re-spend: `rm thumbnail.png` then run `make_thumbnail.py --project … --still …thumbnail_still.png --channel cathedral_of_stars --title "…" --subtitle "…"`.
