## 1. Identity & brand premise

**Lazarus Films adapts public-domain dramatic writing — novels, plays, screenplays, short stories — into new cinematic audiovisual works.** The name signals the act: it raises dramatic writing thought to belong to someone else and gives it a second life. The brand statement, locked: *Lazarus Films adapts public-domain dramatic writing into new audiovisual works that honour the source text rather than spectacularising it.*

The three-channel worldview (taken together): **Final Hours = the end · Success Coach = the journey · Lazarus Films = the return.** Death, life, resurrection — a worldview rendered as channels; viewers feel the coherence even when they can't articulate it.

**The counter-position:** Lazarus is NOT the AI-slop answer to public-domain horror reboots ("Betty Boop slasher film"). It is the **dignified-register** counter-position — the same craft discipline that gives Final Hours its moat (face-never-resolved, scene canons, object-substitution, named humans treated with weight) applied to literary adaptation. *Resurrection of writing thought to belong to someone else, done with respect.*

**Tagline (banner frontrunner, frozen pending mock-up):** **LAZARUS: public domAIn revival** — single-pass legible; "revival" is the right verb (biblical resonance + mission match); the single capitalised AI is the only joke, quiet not smirking. (Deprecated: "Putting the AI in Public DomAIn" — needs clause-parsing; fine for social bio / end-card.)

**Mission statement candidate:** "Lazarus Films makes works in the public domain visible again through cinematic adaptation. We treat the public domain as cultural heritage that belongs to everyone, and use new tools to widen access to the dramatic literature that shaped Western imagination."

## 2. The deeper thesis — Lazarus is a five-year writing curriculum disguised as a channel

The genuine moat in AI cinematic recreation is not the pipeline (commodity), the visual style (commodity), or the format (widely copied). **The moat is screenplay-craft applied to YouTube format.** Lazarus is a **five-year creative-writing curriculum** that simultaneously (1) produces channel content, (2) develops Peter's dramatic-writing capability, (3) builds capability transferable to the eventual viral tier, (4) feeds Final Hours' continuing improvement, and (5) creates a craft moat no YouTube competitor can shortcut.

**Why master writers are the best teachers** — every PD author adapted teaches a different dramatic technique:

| Author | Technique |
|---|---|
| Saki | cruel endings, last-line discipline |
| Hammett | compression, dialogue rhythm, refusal to over-explain |
| du Maurier | interior dread, landscape as character, Cornish register |
| Christie | misdirection, reveal pacing, withheld information |
| Faulkner | temporal manipulation, voice as structure |
| Conrad | moral ambiguity, witness perspective |
| Wharton | class observation, social cruelty in drawing rooms |
| Bierce | psychological compression, perfect endings, war-horror |
| Stevenson | moral collapse, atmospheric novella, period dignity |
| Jacobs | cruel-ending discipline at popular-fiction tier |
| M.R. James | antiquarian-horror atmosphere, restrained dread |
| Blackwood | landscape-as-character, sustained natural dread |
| Highsmith (when PD) | sustained dread, sympathy with the wrong character |
| Greene | moral compromise, faith under pressure |

By 2030: 50+ adaptations, 15+ master writers internalised, a capability moat impossible to replicate by anyone working only in YouTube. **The sequence matters:** launching the viral tier *before* this apprenticeship produces format-right / architecture-missing work; launching Lazarus first means months of craft development first.

**Reading IS work.** Three filters run during every PD read: (1) *technique catalogue* — what craft pattern does this teach; (2) *adaptation scoring* — is this the next video (length, dialogue density, character count, AI-cinematic feasibility); (3) *capability spec* — which pipeline capability would this unlock/demand. The output is a curated PD shortlist no other operator can reproduce — the intersection of public availability + craft pattern + pipeline feasibility + register + demand timing. **The PD catalogue is public; the taste curve through it is not — the taste curve is the uncopyable moat.** Protected reading time IS pipeline-building time.

## 3. Register discipline (the most important operating distinction)

**Register** = the consistent emotional/tonal/structural posture a channel holds across all videos (voice character, emotional posture, address mode, production grammar, visual aesthetic, title/thumbnail language, closer discipline). **A coherent register supports vast topic diversity; a varying register breaks the channel signal regardless of topic quality.** (Proof: Esme Time Travels — 101 videos/60 days across Pompeii→Atlantis→Chernobyl; topic varies wildly, register stays locked. Arthur Revives the Past — 20 videos, 4.36M views/4 months, same scholarly-companion register throughout.)

**The Lazarus register (proposed, refine during proof-of-concept):**
- **Voice:** dignified-literary narrator, period-aware, restrained.
- **Emotional posture:** reverent toward the source, attentive to craft, never camp.
- **Address mode:** third-person narrator with optional companion phrasing ("we step into…"). Named-narrator pattern deferred to Final Hours first.
- **Production grammar:** cinematic pacing, classical/period-appropriate music, restrained closer.
- **Visual aesthetic:** AI cinematic recreation, period-accurate, dignified palette, atmospheric over sensational.
- **Title language:** *[Work title] — A [Author] adaptation (AI Cinematic Recreation)*, optional evocative subtitle.
- **Closer:** moralised reflection at the present-day viewer; no "thanks for watching."

**Topics that DON'T fit the register (belong to future channels):** multi-character drawing-room dialogue (Austen, late Wharton) — needs the dialogue/lip-sync capability; theatrical declamation (Shakespeare, Marlowe) — multi-character avatar work; comedic register (early Wodehouse, Twain) — different emotional posture; action-heavy adventure (Burroughs, pulp) — different production grammar. Not permanent bans — recognitions that they need capabilities/registers not yet built.

## 4. The brutal eight-question YouTube filter (apply BEFORE the romantic-literary instinct)

Reading PD for pleasure and reading it for adaptation are different activities. Most beloved works fail the YouTube test. Score each candidate 1–5 on:

1. **30-second promise** — does the opening promise an *event* the viewer wants resolved in 30s? Tone isn't enough. (Sredni Vashtar: sick boy told he won't grow up — yes. Ethan Frome: frame narrator wondering about a man — dead on YouTube.)
2. **One-sentence plot compression** — can a stranger be made to want it in one sentence? (Sredni Vashtar — abused boy's pet ferret kills his abuser. Most Henry James doesn't compress.)
3. **Peak inside the runtime window** — 10–20 min optimum; the source needs one articulable peak that survives compression.
4. **Visualisable specifics** — AI cinematography needs concrete images (Saki's shed/ferret; Hammett's office/falcon/fog). James gives interiority — an order of magnitude harder.
5. **Articulable protagonist stake** — what do they want, what do they lose. (Modernist literature deliberately undermines this → loses on YouTube.)
6. **First-mover positioning available** — is the AI-cinematic-adaptation lane for this work empty or saturated? Famous classics (Hamlet, P&P, Dracula, Jekyll & Hyde) fail on stock saturation; lesser-known works in the same catalogues (Sredni Vashtar, Owl Creek Bridge, The Body Snatcher) pass.
7. **Current viral demand vs SEO legacy stock** — recent (6-month) outlier momentum vs accumulated all-time school-curriculum views. NexLev outlier scores measure *flow*; cumulative view counts measure *stock*. Front-load works where flow exists.
8. **Jurisdiction-clean across hosting regions** — final due-diligence on trademark (Sherlock Holmes residual issues), estate-administered rights (Peter Pan → Great Ormond Street), jurisdiction-specific exceptions.

The eight filters narrow ~50 beloved works to ~12; channel-fit + visual-pipeline tests cut that to ~6–7 — the actual two-year shortlist. Works that fail still earn reading time because they teach transferable craft (James's consciousness compression sharpens Hammett adaptation; Joyce's epiphanies sharpen Saki).

## 5. Public-domain cohort taxonomy (the legal-strategic frame)

US PD operates on a 95-year term for works published 1928+; each Jan 1 a new year enters PD.

- **Cohort A — Globally PD (author died pre-1926):** zero territorial risk anywhere. Saki (d.1916), Bierce (d.1914), Stevenson (d.1894), Wilde (d.1900), M.R. James (globally PD 2007), Stoker, Conan Doyle (globally PD 2001), Poe, Maupassant, Chekhov, Le Fanu, Blackwood (globally PD 2022). **Year one front-loads Cohort A entirely** — a takedown on video 1–2 destroys account-level trust regardless of merits; Cohort A removes that risk.
- **Cohort B — US-only PD, UK/EU still protected:** published pre-1930 = US PD now, but author died later so UK/EU life+70 extends. Maltese Falcon (US PD 2026, UK to 2031), Faulkner's earliest, du Maurier's Loving Spirit (US PD 1 Jan 2027, UK to 2059). **Festival-safe; YouTube-exposure scales with growth** (UK/EU holders can file takedowns; rare for small channels). Maltese Falcon is fine for Astana; YouTube release may wait for global PD or accept exposure.
- **Cohort C — Never copyrighted** (Shakespeare, the Bible, Homer): legally safest, strategically weakest (centuries of adaptations, fails first-mover hard). Not a Lazarus hunting ground (→ future channels / one-off marquee).
- **Cohort D — Procedurally PD** (copyright-formality failures: Night of the Living Dead, It's a Wonderful Life): legally safe, strategically irrelevant (already widely circulated *because* PD).

**Lazarus operates as a Cohort-A operator with selective Cohort B for marquee events.** "Every adaptation we ship is verifiably in the public domain" is a credible trust signal worth foregrounding in About-page copy. **The CC0 question — answered NO:** keep Lazarus adaptations as standard copyrighted works (preserve licensing optionality; Romero lost millions to the Night-of-the-Living-Dead copyright failure). What CAN be opened (CC-BY) without giving away rights: the *source materials* (storyboard.json, canon, script.md) — demonstrates the method, supports the cultural-heritage framing, doesn't compromise the finished-video rights.

## 6. Demand calibration — Tony Walker & the near-empty lane

The dignified-literary-faceless niche is currently warmest in **atmospheric horror & supernatural**, not literary drama broadly. **Tony Walker's Classic Ghost Stories:** 99.7K subs, 682 videos/14 years, 23.7K avg, no video above 1.5× average (stable subscribed base, not viral lottery). NexLev's similarity engine, given Tony Walker, found only ONE channel above 60 similarity (Vitor Machado, Brazilian/Portuguese, 5.68K subs after 14 years — the cautionary data point: decade-long commitment alone is insufficient; you need register + English market + craft floor). **Dignified English-language literary horror narration is essentially a one-operator niche.** The lane has room for a cinematic-tier entry — Tony Walker's 99.7K over 14 years is the floor for a serious entry, not the ceiling; the cinematic differentiator may compress the timeline but the framing is **decade-plus accumulation, not viral breakout.**

**What his audience watches most (calibration):** Lovecraft, Wharton's "A Bottle of Perrier," A.J. Alan, M.R. James, Russell Wakefield, Dickens ghost stories. **Pattern: atmospheric horror & supernatural dread dominate — not drawing-room drama.** So the first 3–5 Lazarus videos land in the **atmospheric-horror + dramatic-cinematic** intersection (algorithmically warm soil), then broaden.

**Format-discipline lessons from his channel trailer:** (1) **cold open is dramatic dialogue, not exposition** — hook first, host second; for Sredni Vashtar, open on the boy's prayer, then "Welcome to Lazarus Films." (2) **Author list structured for discovery AND retention** — famous works as SEO surface, lesser-known as retention payoff. (3) **Twin-upload format** — story video + a short post-story craft-analysis companion (5–8 min, narrator + stills + screen text, low production cost; second weekly upload; education-mission proof; long-tail SEO). Worth piloting at video 5–7.

## 7. Register-expansion roadmap (the corner-risk defence is timing, not the absence of horror)

- **Videos 1–5 (months 0–6) — atmospheric-horror anchor.** Saki, Bierce, Stevenson's horror novellas, M.R. James, Jacobs. Establish register, train the audience, build trust. **Do NOT broaden in this window** — the algorithm needs unambiguous channel-signal.
- **Videos 6–10 (months 6–12) — atmospheric expansion within the register.** Wilde's darker non-horror (Lord Arthur Savile's Crime), Saki's non-horror (Tobermory), compressed Conrad, Wharton's pre-1926 ghost stories (Afterward, The Eyes), Nesbit's grown-up supernatural.
- **Videos 11–20 (year two) — psychological drama.** Wharton's non-supernatural short fiction, Bierce's military-realism, Stevenson's character studies, selected Henry James (The Real Thing).
- **Videos 21–30 (year three) — crime/mystery + historical.** Conan Doyle's Holmes (globally PD since 2001), earliest Chandler/Hammett, period detective fiction.
- **Videos 30+ (years four–five) — broad dignified-literary register** from a position of audience trust.

## 10. The "channel owns the frame, film owns the interior" principle (pipeline-relevant)

Lazarus is **the channel built on the per-film look override.** The *channel* has a fixed brand identity, but each *film* may need a vastly different look (Saki's Edwardian irony vs a Shakespearean tragedy). Resolution: a **third resolution layer** falling out of the existing canon-override pattern — `channel.json` provides defaults; a per-film `look.json` (or script-header block) overrides them. Machinery unchanged (still reads a resolved config); it resolves channel-then-project.

- **Constant — channel identity (connective tissue):** the Lazarus wordmark + "public domAIn revival" lockup, and *where/how it appears* (title-card frame, end-card, corner bug). The thread that makes ten different films unmistakably *Lazarus*. The channel owns **the frame and the signature.**
- **Variable — per-film look (the interior):** palette, in-content type, colour grade, recreation `style_suffix`, Mode B card styling. The film owns **the interior.**

This is the prestige-anthology pattern (Black Mirror, Criterion): interiors vary wildly, connective tissue is rigorously constant. **Decision to make before building:** hold at least one constant element beyond the logo (a framing device, a title-card grammar) as the spine — total per-film freedom risks an anthology with no spine. The channel-designer's deliverable is therefore the *constant system* (wordmark, lockup, title-card frame that holds any film's art while staying Lazarus), not per-film art.

**Audio contract:** Lazarus is multi-voice. v1 (heard, not seen speaking) rides the existing machine — needs only a `speaker` field + voices map, no new leg (swappable audio). v2 (lip-synced dialogue) is **locked audio** — voice final before render, a voice swap forces a re-render — and needs the **lip-sync leg** (~2027–28, depends on the Hetzner avatar capability). **Channel boundary banked:** *Synthetic = narrated witness, mouths closed; Lazarus = performed dialogue, lip-sync, later.*
