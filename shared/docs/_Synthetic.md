## 3. The format gap (the moat, confirmed in both neighborhoods)

Across every co-viewership map the high performers share a tell: **none is faceless cinematic recreation.** They're talking-head interviews (DOAC, Novara), host-driven investigative explainers (More Perfect Union — the closest comp, crushing it at 1–2.6M), animation (Kurzgesagt), legacy-media documentary (BBC, NOVA), or video essays (Philosophy Tube). **Nobody is *dramatizing* the events — the boardroom, the firing, the 2am calls — as cinema.** That slot is empty in the single largest, highest-performing AI-content space on YouTube. **Format is the differentiation, not POV.** The *take* is saturated (everyone has Hao's "empire" thesis across a dozen interview uploads); the *cinematic dramatization* is unoccupied. Synthetic doesn't win with a hotter take than More Perfect Union — it wins by being the only one rendering the story as documentary cinema.

## 4. Packaging doctrine (the operational lever)

The adjacency lever is **packaging, not topic intent** — the co-viewership graph only learns the association once your videos are already watched by that audience, so the lever is the **title + thumbnail that wins the click off a doom/exposé video while the viewer is still in that headspace.**

1. **Doom-or-exposé-curiosity title grammar, documentary-restraint delivery.** The title does enough of the neighborhood's grammar to win the click; the content delivers the register that earns the subscribe. (The Infographics Show proved the exact seam: Drew's literal "AI tried to murder a human" headline → measured "here's the real experiment" payoff, faceless → 708K.) Favor **"what happened" over raw "murder"** keying — the visceral-event title lands in a messy pocket (reaction streamers, true-crime); the capability/"what happened" framing sits in a cleaner, higher-quality pocket.
2. **Credible named human + curiosity title = the ceiling in BOTH neighborhoods** (Yampolskiy 18.9M, Hinton 6.8M, BBC AI2027 11.3M). **Synthetic already owns the rare half** — broadcast-trained, named, human-voiced narration is the locked moat; the lever to dial is title grammar.
3. **Consistency-vs-legibility tension, resolved by format.** The exposé default is accusatory ("they're lying to you"); Synthetic's register is calmer ("documentary witness — not panicked, not gleeful"). Don't resolve by getting angrier and betraying the register — resolve by leaning on the empty format slot: doom/exposé-legible *packaging* to catch the wave, documentary-cinematic *delivery* to be unmistakably Synthetic. Promise legible to the neighborhood; pay off something distinctly yours.

**Thumbnail constants (never tested away):** SYNTHETIC wordmark; cold near-black / indigo palette; **cinematic-recreation imagery** (recreated rooms/faces, never stock, never cartoon, never webcam — the one signal separating us from both neighborhoods); restrained composed text. Rotate treatment every 3–5 episodes to avoid spam-suppression; the wordmark stays constant.

- **Voice:** Peter's own — radio voice-over training + youth-TV presenting → dignified-documentary register; South African accent as a strategic differentiator in a US-accented lane. **The proven-winning ingredient per the data** (credible named human). Calibration session before E1: test natural / BBC-documentary / newscaster-authoritative on the cold open, lock one.
- **Emotional posture:** documentary witness. Not panicked, not gleeful. Curious about what happened and what it means; treats the people inside the story as actors worth understanding.
- **Address mode:** third-person omniscient, occasional companion phrasing ("we step into the boardroom"). Faceless visually, human-voiced.
- **Production grammar:** cinematic pacing, named-scene structure, restrained music (closer to *The Social Network* than Final Hours' mournful drones — controlled tension, no horror cues, no triumphant swells), generous silent beats at decision-points, 2–4s cut rhythm in exposition, 6–8s holds on dramatic moments.
- **Visual aesthetic:** AI cinematic recreation of real people in real rooms (boardrooms, offices, trading floors, server rooms). Period-accurate. **Stylized, never photographic deepfake** — recognizably cinematic recreation, legally defensible. Reference documented events, never invent dialogue. Credit primary sources generously.
- **Runtime:** 15–20 min. **Closer:** moralised reflection; name the journalist whose research made it possible; preview the next episode's question. **Sign-off: "This was Synthetic Press."** No "thanks for watching."
- **NO LIP-SYNC, ever (register boundary):** the people on screen are *seen, not heard* — mouths stay closed. This keeps the recreation behind the deepfake line AND is load-bearing for the evidence register (because recreated figures never speak, the found-line moments land as deliberate punctuation against an otherwise wordless visual world). *Lip-sync belongs to Lazarus, later.*

## 6. The two-mode visual architecture (Synthetic's defining pipeline feature)

Synthetic is the **dual-mode** channel: **Mode A** (cinematic recreation, the Final Hours engine) + **Mode B** (Remotion motion-graphics, Vox/Johnny Harris/Dhruv Rathee register). **The documentary effect comes from MOVEMENT between the modes, not either mode alone** — stay too long in either and the form collapses (pure A = a drama, pure B = a Vox explainer). The interleaving IS Synthetic.

**Ratio: Mode A 60–70%, Mode B 30–40%** of runtime (the inverse of Vox). Mode B moments are MORE FREQUENT but SHORTER than Mode A scenes (e.g. 5 long A scenes ~90s + 10 short B moments ~25s). **Anti-patterns:** Mode B chunks >60s (you've written a Vox script); Mode A scenes >2 min without a Mode B return (the audience forgets it's a documentary and judges it as a film).

**Mode-decision framework (per beat):** human face/room/scene to land → A · evidence IS the point (the quote/document is the artifact) → B · a number to absorb → B · tweet/email/born-digital artifact → always B · geographic/temporal/org context → B · interior moment (thought, realization, decision) → A. **When in doubt, Mode A is the default** — Synthetic is a documentary about HUMANS, with Mode B the surgical tool for specific proof/data.

### The six Mode B components (and there is no seventh fundamental)
Each teaches one core technique; everything else (bullet builds, bar charts, diagrams, maps) is a recombination.
1. **HighlightedHeadline** — attention direction (animated highlight-sweep behind a key phrase; *the hold before the sweep is the craft*).
2. **LowerThird** — name/title card (animating position AND size, staggered = choreography).
3. **NumberCounter** — value counting up ($0→$13B; *the frame drives the data*; ease-out so the number *arrives*).
4. **ChapterCard** — act-break/date-stamp (reveal-by-mask + self-drawing accent rule).
5. **QuoteCard** — pull-quote with attribution (sequencing-as-meaning — four elements arrive in a deliberate offset order).
6. **DocumentReveal** — "fake real" artifact with a self-drawing SVG box around the key line (`strokeDasharray`/`strokeDashoffset`).

**Component conventions:** background `#0a0a0f`, accent `#3b5bdb` (cold indigo, deliberately not Vox yellow), Inter font, `interpolate(... clamp)` always (un-clamped = the #1 flicker bug), eased easing (linear feels clinical), defensive text-prop guards. *(Note: an earlier visual-architecture draft proposed a navy/amber/bone/rust "press" palette and a 9-component Phase-1/2/3 library with richer props — superseded by the six-component indigo prototype actually built. The 9-component list — NewspaperArticle, AnimatedBarChart, TimelineEvents, EmailReveal, etc. — remains a useful menu of recombinations to build as needed.)*

### Known component gaps (small Remotion tweaks, not pipeline work)
QuoteCard renders the full quote on screen (need an **attribution-only/highlight-only variant** — the card should show attribution, the line is in VO); NumberCounter has no **startValue+countdown** ($1B→$44M renders 0→44M) and no **plainYear** (1997 renders "1,997"). None block 4c; each makes one beat render exactly as scripted.

## 7. The timing architecture & the audio-as-truth spine

**Two timing layers:** (1) **placement** via `<Sequence from={X}>` (drops a component on the master timeline at frame X, resets its internal clock to 0); (2) **internal emphasis** via a prop-driven frame (e.g. `sweepStart`) relative to the beat's own clock. Placement and emphasis are independent knobs — exactly what sync needs, because Whisper supplies both numbers.

**THE PRINCIPLE — the audio is the source of truth.** Everything else is a prediction until the audio exists (script estimates duration, storyboard estimates shots, typed frames are guesses; guesses drift). The voiceover is the one non-prediction — measured to the millisecond by Whisper. **Visuals follow measured audio, never the reverse.** Mode A and Mode B share ONE spine: generate the voice → measure it (Whisper) → hang everything off the measurement. This is why the **true-up IS the human-voice swap** — swapping Inworld scratch (Victor) for Peter's read re-derives every visual timing for free (Mode A holds + Mode B counts alike), no visual regen, no fal spend. *Non-negotiable on a swap: re-run `match_beats.py` for Mode B beats, not just re-assemble, or visuals play at old emphasis frames over new audio.*

**The Whisper matcher (`match_beats.py`):** reads Whisper word-timestamps + a beat spec (which phrase to emphasize) → writes `beats.json` with computed `from` + `sweepStart`. The only hard part is fuzzy matching (spoken ≠ written: "thirteen billion dollars" vs "$13 billion") — solved by normalizing + a `difflib.SequenceMatcher` sliding window. **Computed-default + human-confirmable:** emits a confidence score, flags low-confidence matches for REVIEW (never ship an unreviewed timestamp — a sweep firing on "the" instead of "deceive").

## 8. "The spoken line and its receipt" (the flagship quoted-moment pattern)

The standard for every quoted moment (and there are many). When the narrator voices a real line for impact, **Mode B shows it as a sourced QuoteCard/DocumentReveal, voice-synced, so the words appear as they're spoken — the visual carries the attribution so the voice never has to.** Three layers, no redundancy:
- **Voice** = the words. The narrator just *says the line* ("I deeply regret my participation") — never the leaden citation scaffolding ("as Ilya Sutskever wrote, quote… end quote").
- **Card** = the words *plus* what the voice omits — name, source, date.
- **Highlight** = the emphasis. The key phrase highlight-sweeps as it's spoken.

A narrator *claiming* a quote is an assertion; saying it *while the sourced card builds* is the assertion plus its receipt, in one beat — the documentary-witness credibility move, mechanical. **No-karaoke rule:** the card never duplicates the full sentence as a text wall the narrator also reads verbatim (eyes + ears doing the identical thing is redundant and faintly insulting). Voice = words; card = words + proof; highlight = emphasis.

**The evidence register (free from the trial):** the Musk v. OpenAI trial produced real public exhibits (2017 internal emails admitting they weren't committed to staying nonprofit; Musk's $38M donations; testimony he once pushed to fold OpenAI into Tesla; his "calendar technicality" post). These become Mode B DocumentReveals/QuoteCards — *recreate the experience (A), show the evidence (real exhibits, B), and only show evidence you can prove you're allowed to show* (court exhibits are public record — clean). The strongest possible footing for the documentary-witness register: not editorializing, showing the record.

**Archival as a privileged interruption (not a "Mode C"):** used rarely, for evidentiary weight the generated image can't carry (the iconic real artifact; a real recent named face — you can't generate Sam Altman, the big divergence from Final Hours' face-never-resolved; a document where its realness matters). **Rights reality:** "fair use" is a narrow legal *defence*, not permission, and YouTube adjudicates via Content ID (automated, owner-friendly) — a claim silently diverts the breakout video's revenue, the exact catastrophe to avoid for a power-law channel. **Preference order (control + moat, high→low): Mode A (generate) → Mode B (Remotion) → licensed/public-domain archival → claimed/fair-use archival.** Go down the list, stop at the first that can honestly do the job. Mostly 1–2; occasionally 3; almost never 4. If built, archival needs a provenance/clearance step (`sources.json` recording URL + licence + rights basis) — the step with no Mode A equivalent.

## 9. The launch: the OpenAI / *Empire of AI* series (6 parts)

The launch is the OpenAI story told as a 6-part dual-mode series. **Primary source: Karen Hao's *Empire of AI*,** triangulated against Cade Metz/NYT, Stephanie Palazzolo/The Information, Steven Levy/Wired, Parmy Olson/Bloomberg, and the public court filings. Generous on-screen attribution to Hao throughout.

**Why a series:** builds *internal* recommendation gravity (each episode suggests the next); amortizes one research substrate across 6 outputs; hardens the dual-mode pipeline on a controlled, consistent topic. **The discipline:** treat the *research* as one investment but each episode's *packaging* as an independent experiment — six surfaces to catch the click off one spend = more shots on goal, *provided* you don't pre-render all six before reading the first two or three episodes' signal. Launch 2–3, read retention + co-viewership, finish tuned to what caught.

**Arc logic:** wide → narrow → widen (the idea → money/capability → the 96-hour coup, the tightest point → the reckoning). **Three threads stitch the six:** Musk (cofounder→walks→plaintiff), Altman (dealmaker→deposed→vindicated-on-a-technicality), Ilya Sutskever (true believer→the mind behind the breakthrough→moves against Altman then publicly regrets it). **Thematic spine:** *Can you build a god "for humanity" without becoming an empire?* — posed in E1, left deliberately unresolved in E6. **Standalone-value rule:** every episode delivers a complete arc to a viewer who's seen none of the others.

**The six (each rides a neighborhood; the series spans both):**
1. **The Promise** *(exposé-origin)* — 2015, the founding dinner; a charity chartered to build AGI "for all of humanity" with the defining contradiction baked in night one. Mode B: the charter as DocumentReveal, founding cast as lower-thirds.
2. **The Money** *(exposé/power)* — 2018–19; the capped-profit structure, Microsoft's first billion, Musk's exit. Mode B earns its keep (the structure animated, the valuation counter, the Microsoft deal as document-reveal).
3. **The Unleashing** *(doom/capability — closest to Drew)* — 2020–22; the scaling bet, ChatGPT's escape. Documentary restraint instead of dread; title-keyed to the clean "capability/what happened" pocket, never "murder."
4. **The Coup, Part I: The Fall** *(drama/thriller, doom-curiosity packaging)* — Nov 2023 Thu–Fri; the board fires Altman with "not consistently candid." Cliffhanger ending = the mid-series recommendation-gravity driver.
5. **The Coup, Part II: The Return** *(drama, the climax — the heart of the series)* — Nov 2023 Sat–Tue; Murati interim, Nadella's open door, ~700/770 threatening to walk, Ilya signing against his own coup then "I deeply regret my participation." Mode B: the employee-letter signature count climbing in real time (literally NumberCounter).
6. **The Reckoning** *(exposé + current-search)* — 2024–26; the for-profit conversion at ~$500B, Musk's suit, the **May 2026 Oakland verdict** (jury threw it out on statute-of-limitations — found Musk knew as early as 2021 — and dismissed the aiding-and-abetting claim against Microsoft; the jury never ruled the conversion *right*, only that Musk was too late; OpenAI showed Musk himself once pushed for a for-profit he'd control; Musk called it a "calendar technicality," vowed to appeal). Returns to E1's question and refuses to answer it, because the jury didn't either.

**Structural decisions:** the coup split into two (E4/E5) is decided (highest-demand material, two genuine acts, the strongest binge driver). Anthropic-defection and Suchir Balaji are OUT of the launch six (both planned as standalone episodes; Balaji especially — a death — deserves its own restrained treatment, and the Anthropic episode carries the integrity-note discipline). **Open:** "The Empire's Cost" (Hao's actual central thesis — hidden labor, data-center extraction, the people outside Silicon Valley who pay; the most Hao-faithful, least-covered, most differentiated material, doable as named-protagonist recreation) — recommendation: keep the six for launch reach, make "The Empire's Cost" the immediate next episode / S2 opener.

**The trial as frame story:** the Musk v. OpenAI trial frames the whole series — open E1 on the verdict, flash back to the founding, close E6 on the verdict's full meaning. The trial *was itself a retrospective* (under oath, over three weeks, the principals re-told the whole history), so the frame is intrinsic, not bolted on. Heaviest at the two ends, light in the middle (don't collapse the series into "Musk vs Altman" — that feud is the hook that gets the stranger in the door; Ilya, Murati, Nadella are the soul of the middle). **Release in arc order, E1 first** — the trial-frame cold open gives E1 the current-news, name-heavy hook it otherwise lacked, so no cold-start reach is sacrificed to series integrity.

**E1 cold open (in medias res):** the narrator voices the found line, then steps out to narrate — same voice, two registers, joined by an attribution hinge.
> **"We have a verdict."** *(courtroom ambience under the voice; Mode B QuoteCard sources it — court, Oakland, May 2026)*

Did Sam Altman steal a charity — to build the most powerful company on earth? *(A: the jury filing in; Altman at the defense table.)* They cleared him. But they never said he was right. They said only that Elon Musk had waited too long to ask. … To understand how two men who set out to save humanity from artificial intelligence ended up here, we have to go back to the beginning. *(Title: SYNTHETIC — "The Promise.")*

**E1 packaging spectrum (the test):** lead with **E (the Musk–Altman character/bridge variant)** — "The Dinner Where Elon Musk and Sam Altman Built OpenAI" (two megastar names = pure cold-start reach, bridges both neighborhoods, honest to the episode) and/or a **verdict-framed variant** ("Elon Musk Just Lost to Sam Altman.
