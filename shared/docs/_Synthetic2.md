## 2. COMPETITIVE POSITION (banked from the Gettysburg NexLev read, 03 Jul)

- **The graph is the audience.** A topic like Gettysburg sits inside a suggested graph with millions of accumulated viewers on OTHER channels (map giants at 786K–5M subs; 1993-movie-clip uploads at 760–800K views). A new channel borrows that audience by parking against the graph — subscribers were never the ceiling, only the floor.
- **Clone graveyards mark saturated angles.** Five clones of one viral Armistead video: 37–2.8K views each; the original stands alone at 94K. Never clone a specific hit — take the LANE the hit proves and execute a tier above.
- **The empty lane test:** demand proven (big numbers exist in the graph) + reconstruction supply absent (no cinematic operator in it) + anniversary/search spike available = the slot ships.
- **Cold-start physics (bank this against launch anxiety):** the algorithm's amplification decision is per-video, made on CTR + early-retention from tiny impression batches; a first video CAN break out by winning that auction against a deep graph — and equally, a quiet first week means nothing. One video proves nothing in either direction. The floor is built by volume on a coherent cluster; the answer to variance is at-bats, and this machine prints at-bats at ~$75/film.
- **48h decision levers:** CTR and AVD only. Impressions are outputs. The single needle that says the graph is opening: **Browse/Suggested % in traffic sources** climbing off zero. Ignore dashboard-AI narration on n<1000 impressions; triage its suggestions against doctrine (packaging churn on tiny n = decline; funnel wiring = accept).
- **First readouts (trailer, launch night):** AVD 94.8% of length — the tiny-beat format holds attention wall to wall. That is the retention lever confirmed on first contact.
- **The lane's validation receipts (carry the evidence, never just the conclusion):** small-channel breakouts in the AI-reconstruction disaster/battle lane, 90-day window at validation: Galveston 1900 "(AI Reconstruction)" **720K views from an 11.3K-sub channel** (same channel's Halifax 224K — repeatable, not fluke); 345K from a 1,020-sub channel; Accidental Scholar — dormant 2021 account whose FIRST reconstruction did **558K**, catalog of 18–23-min "(AI Reconstruction)" films at 200–700K, **monetized (verified)**. Every calendar slot must clear the four demand gates + the operator screen (monetized comparables · sub-100K precedent · recent virality · reproducible · goals-aligned · US/Western high-RPM audience) BEFORE its script is written.
- **A third competitive situation exists** beyond empty-lane and weak-incumbent: the **rising format wave with multi-winner tolerance** — e.g. the biblical AI-movie lane, where the same story from competing operators wins millions simultaneously (two Elijah movies both >2.7M; two Enochs; two Revelations). Pre-sold IP with retelling tolerance means the graph feeds the FORMAT, not one winner: entrant N wins on tier-above craft + cadence, not on incumbent weakness. Assess which of the three situations a lane is IN before choosing the thesis.
- **Sweep-width honesty:** one narrow sweep (single keyword, one collection, one sub-cap, 90 days) cannot declare a lane empty — hour-long movies on six-figure channels live outside that net. State conclusions no wider than the net that caught them; a first-hand "I watched it" outranks a narrow sweep.

## 4. PACKAGING

- **Title formula:** famous proper noun + time/number anchor + stated catastrophe. "GETTYSBURG: The Three Days That Cost America 51,000 Men (AI Reconstruction)". Casualty figures stated as CASUALTIES, never inflated to deaths. Superlatives must be true ("deadliest battle", not "deadliest day" where another event holds it).
- **Thumbnail:** composite from the strongest rendered still via the panel's Generate (title / subtitle / still #). Two-tier headline upper-left: WHITE gap-line over ORANGE anchor-line, black stroke. Grand-plus-hook copy beats grand-alone: a superlative or number that opens a question ("AMERICA'S / DEADLIEST DAYS", "51,000 FELL / IN THREE DAYS"). The image and the text must complement, never echo — the gap is the click. Funnel pairs escalate: the film's thumbnail advances the trailer's, never repeats it ("ONE MILE. / HALF RETURNED").
- **Description:** hook line + premiere/link pointer in the visible fold; history-rich narrative below (the significance angle — for Gettysburg, the July-4th/"four score" thread); sign-off line; 3–5 hashtags. Anniversary facts go in the PINNED COMMENT, not buried mid-description.
- **Tags:** topic + lane + seasonal ("july 4, independence day") + "ai reconstruction, synthetic press".
- **Two legal packaging grammars, chosen per video and A/B'd in Studio:** (1) **gap grammar** — two-tier hook copy that opens a question only the video answers; (2) **poster grammar** — ONE massive proper noun (GETTYSBURG → HIROSHIMA → EASTLAND), the channel's series signature, trading per-video gap for catalog-level brand recognition. Title then carries the stakes; thumbnail carries the noun (division of labor, not echo). The film shipped poster grammar over the rally frame.

## 5. SCRIPT AUTHORING DOCTRINE (the core craft)

### 5a. Structure
- **Open on a paradox, not a place or a date.** The cold open states the impossible fact and drops the viewer inside it ("decided in fifty minutes, on a single mile… This is that mile").
- **Scale needs a face at the bottom of the frame.** Every act alternates spectacle beats with human-instrument beats.
- **Quiet is a rhythm choice, never a brightness choice.** Quiet passages are built from consecutive calm shots of the same scene (cutting within it), rendered at full chromatic richness — magic-hour, rim-light, glowing windows against vivid twilight. Long single holds do not exist in this format.
- **Length earned, never padded — and earn at least 8:01.** The narration's natural length IS the film length, and every film script clears the 8-minute mid-roll threshold by construction (~one more act, ~$10–13 marginal, permanent ad inventory on every future view).

### 5b. Beat mechanics (the all-trim contract)
- **Word ceiling 11 per beat, target 5–10.** At the measured voice pace every beat lands under the ~5s animation atom → every clip plays NATIVE and TRIMS. Zero stretch, zero pan-scan fills, by construction.
- **Numbers are the hidden weight.** Spelled compound numbers ("sixty-two") and mid-beat full stops read far slower than their word count (worst measured: 0.61 s/word vs 0.36 normal). Any beat that is number-dense AND multi-sentence gets SPLIT. Rough model for flagging: 0.36×words + 0.28×(number tokens) + 0.4×(extra sentences) > 4.7s ⇒ split.
- **Narration is locked once written.** Visual passes never touch narration — beat boundaries are timing slices; the voice reads one continuous document, so prosody is unaffected by slicing.
- **Numbers spelled out in narration.** No stage directions. Deliberate slow-motion moments are rationed (≈5 per film) and live in the MOTION FIELD at the gate, never in the still prompt.

### 5c. The six-slot VISUAL grammar (every Mode A beat; terseness only where a slot is genuinely irrelevant)
1. **Camera — angle and position only.** "(low angle from directly behind)". NO speed words in still prompts; velocity belongs to the motion prompt. Stills must be sharp.
2. **Period anchor, always.** The year plus era-correct nouns for whatever is in frame (for 1863: slouch hats, kepis, bedrolls, muskets, bronze Napoleons, horse-drawn, dirt roads). Every human beat, every street beat, no exceptions. An unanchored human renders as the model's median human — the anchor IS the continuity mechanism, and repetition across 185 prompts costs nothing because each prompt is the model's entire universe.
3. **Geometry stated, never implied.** Firing axes, facing directions, the camera's side of the line ("barrels parallel, aimed downrange away from camera"). "A line of X" reads as a queue until told otherwise. Multi-weapon compositions ALWAYS state the axis.
4. **Three-plane depth, named.** A named foreground object, mid-ground action, background scale.
5. **Light and grade — one clause.** The suffix carries the house look; the beat adds only scene-specific light, and scene light is specified BRIGHT (clear gold dawn, blazing afterglow, vivid twilight) — the model defaults dark when light is unspecified.
6. **Negations only for classes that have failed.** "no cars, no modern objects", "no steel helmets, no modern gear", "no gore" on anguish-adjacent beats, "full frame, no letterboxing" on wide-vista classes. The banned-list grows from evidence, never speculation — speculative negation vetoes the model's best output.

### 5d. Faces (the emotional instrument)
- **Anonymous faces are REQUIRED, not permitted.** Close-up fear, exhaustion, resolve, grief — specified explicitly (~1 face beat per 8–10 beats; the cold open leads with one). One-shot faces cannot drift; there is no consistency cost.
- **Named historical figures render backlit / silhouette-forward** — a recurring figure across multiple beats is a drift surface; silhouette holds identity. The most famous figures stay distant.
- **No gore, ever.** Anguish on a face, dark shapes on a field, violence by implication. This protects the register, the platform, and the animation model's content filter simultaneously.
- **Youth-adjacent nouns trip the image filter** ("drummer boys" → black-frame rejection). Render the object, not the minor: "a field drum slung at a marching soldier's hip". Same for crowds: "a child on his father's shoulders" is the limit; when a frame dies black, de-youth the noun first.

### 5e. Environment
- **Atmospheric effects are per-beat and diegetic only.** Battle beats name their smoke; dawn streets, roll calls and kitchens render on clean air. Murk never lives in the channel identity.
- **No legible text demanded anywhere** — no readable signs, inscriptions, clock faces, instrument scales. Narration carries the words; visuals show the object weathered/indistinct or show the phenomenon instead (heat shimmer, not the thermometer).
- **Props carry their motion source.** A vehicle in frame has its horse; anything that implies motion should include what moves it, or the animator will invent it.

### 5f. Markup (exemplar law — copy the shape, never author from memory)
Header: bare `key: value` lines — `channel:` (exact folder name), `title:`, single-line `description:`, single-line `tags:`. `## SECTION` flush against its first beat. Each beat: `[A] narration` with `VISUAL:` on the next line, no blank between; blank line between beats. The file ends at the last beat — no trailing notes or verification sections. Verification lives in chat/worklog, never in the ingest file.

## 7. MOTION DOCTRINE

- **Every beat animates.** 100% animation is the format floor, not a luxury — the audience's nervous system knows film vs slideshow within seconds, and AVD follows. Kling 2.5 Turbo Pro at $0.07/s makes a 185-beat film ≈ $65 of animation (~$75–78 all-in). At that price the marginal film is noise; the constraint is craft attention, not compute.
- **The channel default motion string carries the house energy on every beat** ("dynamic cinematic camera movement, powerful momentum…"). Per-beat motion fields OVERRIDE it at the stills gate:
  - **Slow motion:** typed on the ~5 rationed beats (first stride, colors falling, hero gesture, final sting ×2).
  - **Pinning on quiet clusters:** "slow drift, almost still, only mist and breath and fabric moving, everything else stays still" — roll calls, kitchens, crowds, aftermath streets. **Tell the animator what moves AND what stays**, or a dynamic default invents drama (a parked wagon will drive itself away).
  - **The still can fix the motion:** when the animator invents motion, adding the motion source to the FRAME (the horse in harness) often beats fighting it with words — design WITH the model's story-instinct.
- **Rhythm floor and ceiling:** ~150 beats of house-dynamic + ~10 pinned-quiet + ~5 slow-motion = the film breathes. Uniform energy across 185 cuts flattens.
- Animation is sequential (~2–4 min/clip → 6–11h per film) — run overnight in tmux. Parallel semaphore (5–10 concurrent) is the first post-launch build.
- **Measured economics (Gettysburg):** ~$10/min raw; **$12–13/min fully loaded** with first-film regen tax; ~92% of spend is Kling; all-trim generates ~2.2× the footage kept (the price of the never-stretch guarantee — accepted). Expect $13/min on a channel's FIRST film in a new register, trending $9–10 by film three as failure classes bank into the suffix/negations — the learning loop visible as unit cost. Regen a still ($0.07) beats re-animating a clip ($0.35): the cheapest-first gate order is a 5× save per catch. The binding constraint is gate attention, not dollars.
- **Cost reduction is a Kling-count problem (2 MC controls scoped — both DELETE spend, not discount it):** ~92% of cost is animation-seconds = clip count × 5s atom, and all-trim generates ~2.2× the footage kept. Two per-beat review-page toggles recover it without touching sync — the Whisper-aligned beat timeline is immutable; only the pixel SOURCE for a slot changes. **(1) Ken-Burns override:** flip a genuinely static beat (wide crowd, held landscape, de-textured object) from Kling to a free programmatic push — on a truly still beat it's also SAFER (no invented motion = no hallucinated artifacts). **(2) "Previous clip covers this":** merge two short same-scene beats into one 5s clip cut at the A+B boundary; renders nothing for beat B (marginal still waste), recovers the footage the trim discards, and cannot black-frame/mis-dimension (nothing generates = no re-render tax). Both are visually safe ONLY on the same clusters already tagged for motion-pinning — same eyeball, same moment, three controls (motion field · KB toggle · merge toggle). The panel is where craft-judgment and cash meet per beat: "first N Kling / rest Ken Burns" was a global guess wrong on both tails; the real gate is narrative-function — does the MEANING live in the camera move or in the stillness — which only an editor's eye can call. Prerequisite before building: read the assembler's beat→shot→audio binding; merge lives or dies on whether clips timestamp from Whisper-aligned data or from file length.

## 8. ASSEMBLY

- **Never stretch, only trim.** With the §5b ceiling every clip is longer than its beat → native playback + end-trim on every beat. Time manipulation is directorial and lives in the motion prompt.
- **`--kb-tail` stays available as the dormant safety net** at final assembly: a rare over-atom beat clone-pads invisibly (<0.5s remainder) instead of visibly filling. If `durations.json` shows zero beats over the atom (~5.04s), the panel's Re-assemble is byte-equivalent and fully legal.
- **Music lives in the edit suite (Filmora), not the pipeline** — assemble clean/dry, scp down, lay the bed (documentary-level under narration, swelling on the set pieces), optional SFX hits on the slow-motion beats, 1s fade-to-black tail, loudness pass, export. Licensed-track per-video clearing after upload.

## 9. THE RENDER WORKFLOW (Mission Control end-to-end)

Create project (paste script → slug check → the banner must show the expected beat count and `{"A":N}`) → **verify on disk**:

```
python -c "import json; d=json.load(open('synthetic/projects/<slug>/beats_full.json'));
b=d['beats'] if isinstance(d,dict) and 'beats' in d else d;
bad=[x['index'] for x in b if not (x.get('narration') or '').strip() or not (x.get('visual') or '').strip()];
print(len(b),'beats |',set(x['mode'] for x in b),'| bad:',bad or 'NONE')"
```

→ **fingerprint check** (verify one distinctive line of the INTENDED script version on the panel — a stale paste renders the wrong film) → Mode OFF dry-run → Launch → **audio gate** (ear-check cold open + one late passage; duration ÷ word count sanity: 150–190 wpm — variance between renders is pause-distribution, favorable when faster) → **durations proof** (zero beats over 5.04s) → **stills gate**, in cheapest-first order:

1. **Dimension sweep:** every still `1280×704` (or `1280×720`) — anything else stops the line.
2. **Bar-strip pass:** automated top/bottom black-bar detector, crop + rescale in place.
3. **Black-frame check** on any suspect: mean brightness < 10 = safety rejection → de-youth/reword.
4. **Eyeball pass, four questions per frame:** period · geometry · faces landed · grade bright-and-detailed in the quiet clusters. Budget 10–15 judgment regens per 185 (measured trailer rate ~25%, mostly automated-fixable classes). "Fix this image" preserves a 90%-right frame; "Regenerate" re-rolls a broken skeleton; a regenerated still requires re-animating its clip.
5. **Motion fields typed** (§7) → Kling count set to cover ALL beats → animate (tmux, overnight).

Morning: **fallback grep** on the animate log (`held_clip|fallback|content_policy`) → re-prompt any held-frame beats → clip spot-check → Re-assemble (or standalone with `--kb-tail`) → Filmora → upload.

**Operating laws:** verified-by-paste — never assume a command ran; evidence over inference. Config changes travel LAPTOP → git → BOX pull with a printed verify. Service environment is captured at service start — new env keys need a service restart, which is legal ONLY while nothing renders; never restart mid-render. The panel caches — when it disagrees with expectations, check the artifact on disk. The engine composes prompts at generation time, so config changes only reach stills generated by processes started after the pull.

## 14. THE SHORTS LEG (first run 04 Jul — film → ~30 verticals)

The film is a Shorts quarry: each high-impact beat is an independent lottery ticket into the Shorts feed graph (distinct from the long-form suggested graph), turning one film into ~30 at-bats. First run cut from GETTYSBURG.

- **Cut on first-spoken-word impact; TITLE = the opening line verbatim** ("12,500 of them are standing in a treeline"; "50 minutes after 12,500 men stepped out of the trees") — the scroller reads the exact words about to hit their ears; text and audio reinforce, never compete. Highest-leverage Shorts packaging lever. Append topic + channel hashtags.
- **Vertical format = square-centre-blur (the honest fit for cinematic wides):** 1080×1080 of the frame sharp and centred (crops only far-left/right edges — usually smoke/sky, not subject), a blurred zoomed copy filling top/bottom. Preserves composition where a 9:16 crop would gut it; no hard bars. Per-clip judgment like motion: **subject-centred beats (a face, a hero frame) may crop to full-bleed 9:16; wide multi-subject compositions get square-blur.** ffmpeg: `[0:v]scale=1080:1920:force_original_aspect_ratio=increase,crop=1080:1920,gblur=sigma=28[bg];[0:v]scale=-1:1080,crop=1080:1080[fg];[bg][fg]overlay=(W-w)/2:(H-h)/2` with `-c:a copy` (audio byte-identical); lift overlay `y` to `H*0.18` for a bottom caption zone. zsh needs `setopt null_glob` or the batch loop dies on empty extension globs.
- **First-phrase extraction:** local faster-whisper (`base.en`, `vad_filter=True` skips a music head, `condition_on_previous_text=False` stops invented lead-ins), first sentence to the first terminator — the auto-caption/title source.
- **Cadence caution (bank for run 2):** ~30 near-identical teal-orange Shorts firing hourly into one feed inside 48h risks the algorithm reading them as a single repetitive block and throttling after the first few. Fix is not unscheduling — it's reading the shape: if the first 5–6 live Shorts show later ones getting progressively fewer impressions, that's saturation → thin the NEXT film's cadence (every 2–3h, or across more days). Watch for double-booked timestamps (two Shorts on one slot waste it).
- **The read is the DISTRIBUTION, not any single Short:** 30 Shorts on one topic in 24h is ONE experiment — do 2–3 break out and route taps to the film (Shorts-as-funnel proven; breakouts name which MOMENTS travel → feeds ideation), or do all 30 flatline (footage doesn't short / feed saturated). That answer gates whether this becomes `make_shorts.py` (Whisper first-phrase → burn caption → square-blur vertical → output folder, straight from the final render).
