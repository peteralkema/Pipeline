## 2. The honest framing (the spine — read first)

Peter is a real woodworker. He knows the legitimate full-time creators who are genuinely on-camera in their own studios, hands on the timber. **This channel is not an attempt to fake that or compete with them on their turf** — that would be dishonest and would lose, because their whole value is the authentic human craft on screen. Peter is no longer in the workshop daily; the channel does not pretend he is.

The proposition is different and must be stated plainly so the doctrine never drifts into impersonation:

> **This channel delivers what the pipeline genuinely makes well — clear, faithful, instructional explainer content for woodworkers — as honest faceless educational visualisation. It is "reference and explanation," not "watch a master at his bench."** It adds value to woodworkers who want clear answers to specific questions, delivered visually, on demand.

**The legitimacy test for every video:** would a woodworker find this genuinely useful and accurate, and does it stand on its own as honest instructional content without pretending to be live workshop footage? If yes, it's in bounds. If it would only "work" by tricking viewers into thinking they're watching a real person build in a real shop, it's out of bounds. This is encoded into the renderer itself (§3, §9): the `style_suffix` forbids people, faces, and hands, so the channel *cannot* accidentally generate fake-workshop imagery.

## 3. Thesis

Woodworking for Everyone is **not a "woodworking channel" in the on-camera sense.** It is a **search-fed evergreen education engine** — a system for taking a durable woodworking question (which wood, which joint, which finish, which tool) and rendering a clear, accurate, honestly-faceless visual answer for a few dollars a video. The frame is consistent: **answer the exact question a woodworker typed into the search box, faithfully, with clean illustrative visuals and a calm instructional voice.**

The win condition is a **slowly compounding annuity** of videos that each answer a searched question and keep earning for years — structurally the **Success Coach game, not the Sacred Dawn game.** Variance/breakouts are a bonus, not the plan. (One sub-lane — the cinematic wood-portraits of §8B — deliberately plays the *other*, browse/emotional game; it is flagged as distinct precisely because it breaks this thesis.)

Three things make it defensible:

**The honesty moat.** Trust is the one thing separating this channel from the clickbait farms (the "65-year-old carpenter's secret SHOCKED engineers" sub-lane, §6). Peter is a real woodworker; **every script's technical claims survive Peter's accuracy review before publish.** A wrong joint-strength or a bad finishing claim damages the only durable asset. This is the woodworking-specific form of the flywheel's "nothing publishes a human didn't review" rule, and it is non-negotiable here because Peter's name is on the channel.

**The search-asset compounding.** Each video targets a specific evergreen query and accretes search traffic over months (the Window Seat shape — ramped for years, still pulling 6.3% search CTR on a 2014 thumbnail). Library breadth across angles (§5) compounds: more answered queries = more search surface = more internal suggested cross-traffic.

**The owned-demand flywheel.** Site (woodworkingforeveryone.net) ↔ videos ↔ book (10,000 copies) ↔ plans. JEPLANS proves the "animate + sell the plans" loop (§4). Window Seat already pulls ~12% external traffic from the site. This is a monetisation edge the pure-AdSense faceless farms lack.

Tools (fal Flux, Inworld, Whisper, ffmpeg) are commodity. The moat is the three layers above + Peter's domain accuracy + concentrated packaging craft-attention.

## 5. Topic principles & the angle system

Three filters for any video:
1. **A durable, searched woodworking question** — not "woodworking tips," but "is pine good for furniture", "what is the strongest joint", "Danish oil vs polyurethane". The exact phrase a woodworker types. This is a *search* channel (Window Seat traffic: 44% search) — the query is the topic.
2. **Honest instructional register** — calm, competent, plain-spoken; authority through clarity, not drama. No fake-shock framing. If a topic can only be made to "work" with clickbait or faked footage, it's wrong regardless of how clickable.
3. **Render-friendly within current capability** — deliverable on the Ken-Burns + stills stack TODAY, or honestly flagged as needing a new lane. Angle availability gates the queue (§4).

**The title grammar (search-query-first):** lead with the exact phrase typed into search. Brand prefix ("Woodworking for Everyone:") is **BANNED from the front of titles** — it buries the searchable phrase, the exact mistake the legacy Window Seat title makes. Honest curiosity gaps allowed; fake-shock ("SHOCKED engineers") banned. *(The cinematic §8B sub-lane uses a DIFFERENT, emotional title grammar — that contrast is itself the signal of which game a video plays.)*

## 8. Packaging system (the thumbnail & title specifics)

### 8A. Thumbnail doctrine
- **Honest curiosity gap, no fake faces.** Thumbnails use the channel's own rendered/illustrated imagery + a curiosity-gap text pair + (optionally) a graphic arrow — never a live-action person or pretend craftsman. Matched text+image pairs (caption restates the picture) are banned: the gap must force a question only the video answers.
- **The channel `thumbnail` block** (in `woodworking/channel.json`) inherits the proven Anton / top-left-scrim machinery but with `candidate_prompt_suffix` + `selection_rules` enforcing **no people / no faces / no hands** and a clean subject (wood sample / joint / tool / finished piece) in the right two-thirds, dark negative space top-left for the headline. The honesty boundary is thus encoded into the vision-picker — it cannot select a fake-face composition.
- **Thumbnail text spec** (flywheel-standard): two lines, ~2–3 short words each, ~13 chars/line max, Anton, high contrast. (Proven on the Window Seat **HIDDEN / STORAGE** test.)
- **Headline ≠ title.** The thumbnail headline is a SHORT punchy curiosity string; the title carries the SEO query. Image = the "what," title = the search phrase. They complement, never echo.
- **Window Seat refresh (the worked example, banked this session):**
  - **New title:** *How to Build a Window Seat with Storage (Step-by-Step)* — leads with the exact query; "with Storage" = high-intent modifier; brand prefix dropped.
  - **Thumbnail text:** **HIDDEN / STORAGE**; best paired with the frame showing the lids open / storage cavity visible; honest (no person) version preferred for the evergreen search video.
  - **Description:** first sentence leads with "How to build a window seat with hidden storage…"; repeats head terms naturally; links site + book + subscribe; signature; hashtags.
  - *Already-uploaded → thumbnail must be set by hand in Studio / via `thumbnails.set` (local generation does not attach to a live video).*
