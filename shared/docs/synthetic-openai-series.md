## What this series is

The launch series for Synthetic Press: the OpenAI / *Empire of AI* story, told as a 6-part cinematic documentary series, dual-mode (Mode A cinematic recreation + Mode B Remotion motion-graphics), Peter-narrated. Primary source: Karen Hao's *Empire of AI*, triangulated against independent reporting (Cade Metz/NYT, Stephanie Palazzolo/The Information, Steven Levy/Wired, Parmy Olson/Bloomberg, the now-public court filings). Generous on-screen attribution to Hao throughout; active promotion of her book.

It is the correct launch because the OpenAI story is the one topic that sits at the **confluence of both target neighborhoods** — the doom/capability neighborhood (Drew Spartz / Species) and the exposé/power neighborhood (Hao's circuit, More Perfect Union, DOAC). See the strategy doc for the co-viewership evidence.

## The arc logic (three jobs at once)

**1. Wide → narrow → widen.** Open at the widest aperture (the idea, the institution, the founding contradiction), progressively tighten through money and capability into the most intimate, highest-intensity human moment (the 96-hour coup, split across two episodes — the series' tightest, most cinematic point), then pull back at the end to meaning (the reckoning). Documentary breathing.

**2. Three threads stitch the six together** so each episode stands alone yet the arc holds:
- **Musk** — recurring foil: cofounder (E1) → walks (E2) → returns as plaintiff (E6).
- **Altman** — the throughline: dealmaker → deposed king → vindicated-on-a-technicality.
- **Ilya Sutskever** — the tragic conscience: true believer (E1) → the mind behind the breakthrough (E3) → the man who moves against Altman and then publicly regrets it (E4–5).

**3. Each episode rides a neighborhood; the series spans both.** This is the confluence made operational. Packaging differs episode to episode *because the neighborhood does* — packaging is the per-episode differentiation lever (per strategy doc: doom/exposé-curiosity title grammar, documentary-restraint delivery).

**Thematic spine:** *Can you build a god "for humanity" without becoming an empire?* Posed in E1, answered — ambiguously, deliberately unresolved — in E6.

**Standalone-value rule:** every episode delivers a complete arc and payoff to a viewer who has seen none of the others. The series rewards bingeing but never requires it.

## The six episodes

### Episode 1 — "The Promise"
**Neighborhood:** exposé-origin (broad). **Packaging angle:** the contradiction hook — a charity built to save us from the thing it then built.
2015. A private dinner, a billionaire, and a handful of researchers who believed they were the only thing standing between humanity and a dangerous AI future controlled by Google. The widest aperture: how OpenAI began as a literal nonprofit chartered to build AGI "for all of humanity" — and how the defining contradiction was baked in from night one (you cannot build the most powerful, most lucrative technology in history as a charity). Standalone payoff: the origin myth of the company now worth half a trillion dollars, told straight, idealism real and flaw visible. **Mode A:** the dinner, the early Mission-District office, the whiteboard dreams. **Mode B:** the actual charter language as DocumentReveal; founding cast as lower-thirds. Ends on the question the whole series answers.

### Episode 2 — "The Money"
**Neighborhood:** exposé (power/money). **Packaging angle:** "How do you fund a god?" / how OpenAI stopped being a charity without quite saying so.
2018–2019. The dream meets the bill. Frontier training costs more than any nonprofit can raise, so OpenAI invents one of the strangest structures in corporate history — the "capped-profit" entity bolted onto the charity — and takes Microsoft's first billion. Meanwhile the first schism: Musk wants control, floats folding OpenAI into Tesla, is refused, and walks, taking his money and his faith. Standalone payoff: the clearest explanation anywhere of the weird machine at OpenAI's core and why it was built — the structure that contained the contradiction until it couldn't. **Mode B earns its keep here:** the capped-profit structure animated as a diagram, the valuation counter ticking up, the Microsoft deal as document-reveal. **Mode A:** the negotiating rooms, Musk's exit. The exposé neighborhood's native territory.

### Episode 3 — "The Unleashing"
**Neighborhood:** doom (capability) — closest to Drew's audience. **Packaging angle:** "the day they lost control of their own creation."
2020–2022. The scaling bet pays off beyond anyone's model, and on a November night in 2022 a "low-key research preview" called ChatGPT escapes into the world and becomes the fastest-adopted product in history — catching even its makers off guard. The doom-wave episode: the capability moment, the thing-is-loose energy Drew's neighborhood runs on, delivered with documentary restraint instead of dread. Standalone payoff: what scaling laws actually are and why the people who built it were both thrilled and frightened. Ilya's thread deepens — the scientist watching his creation wake up. **Mode B:** compute/scaling numbers, the adoption curve. **Mode A:** the quiet lab the night it shipped. Title-keyed to the cleaner "capability/what happened" pocket, never the messy "murder" pocket.

### Episode 4 — "The Coup, Part I: The Fall"
**Neighborhood:** drama/thriller, doom-curiosity packaging. **Packaging angle:** "why OpenAI's own board tried to destroy it."
November 2023, Thursday–Friday. The aperture narrows hard. The board — including Ilya — fires Sam Altman with four words that detonate the company: not "consistently candid." No warning, no clear public explanation, the world's hottest startup leaderless overnight. The palace-intrigue episode — tightest and most cinematic: closed rooms, encrypted messages, a board convinced it's doing the right thing and unable to say why. Standalone payoff: the most-discussed corporate firing of the decade, finally laid out clearly. **The cliffhanger ending** — Altman out, company visibly collapsing, employees in revolt — is the engine that pulls viewers straight into Part II (the mid-series recommendation-gravity driver). **Mode A** almost throughout (boardroom, the calls); **Mode B** for the timeline and the org-chart of who-moved-against-whom.

### Episode 5 — "The Coup, Part II: The Return"
**Neighborhood:** drama (the climax). **Packaging angle:** "96 hours: how Sam Altman took back the most important company on earth."
November 2023, Saturday–Tuesday. The narrowest, most intense episode — the heart of the series. Mira Murati as interim CEO for a weekend; Microsoft's Nadella opening a door for Altman and the whole team; ~700 of ~770 employees threatening to walk; Ilya signing the letter against his own coup, then the public anguish — "I deeply regret my participation." Altman returns; the board that fired him is gone instead. Standalone payoff: the full 96-hour thriller, every beat in place. Arc payoff: the E1 contradiction erupts into the open — mission vs money, safety vs speed, fought hand-to-hand and decided in a weekend. **Mode A** at full cinematic stretch; **Mode B** for the hour-by-hour timeline and the employee-letter signature count climbing in real time (the NumberCounter component, literally).

### Episode 6 — "The Reckoning"
**Neighborhood:** exposé + current-search. **Packaging angle:** "Elon Musk took OpenAI to court. Here's what really happened."
2024–2026, pulling back to the widest aperture again. OpenAI completes its turn to a for-profit-controlled structure at a ~$500B valuation; Musk sues, claiming the charity he funded was stolen; and in May 2026, after a three-week trial in Oakland (Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers, advisory jury), the jury throws out the suit — finding Altman, Brockman, and OpenAI not liable on all claims because Musk waited beyond the statute of limitations (the jury found he was aware of the conduct as early as 2021), and dismissing the aiding-and-abetting claim against Microsoft on the same grounds. **The register-critical nuance:** the jury never ruled the conversion was *right* — only that Musk was too late — and OpenAI's lawyers had shown Musk himself once pushed for a for-profit structure on condition he control it. Musk called it a "calendar technicality" and vowed to appeal. Standalone payoff: the freshest, most search-relevant beat in the saga, explained cleanly (rides the live verdict search traffic). Arc payoff: return to E1's question — promise betrayed or fulfilled? — and refuse to answer it for the viewer, because the jury didn't either. **Mode A:** the courtroom. **Mode B:** the claims-vs-verdict breakdown; Musk's "calendar technicality" post as DocumentReveal. Closes: "This was Synthetic Press."

## Structural decisions (and the open one)

**Decided — the coup is split into two episodes (E4/E5).** It is the highest-demand, most-viral material, it genuinely has two acts (the fall Thu–Fri; the chaos and return Sat–Tue), and the mid-series cliffhanger is the strongest possible driver of internal recommendation gravity (binge + next-episode clicks + subscription).

**Decided — Anthropic defection and Suchir Balaji are OUT of the launch six.** Both are already planned as standalone episodes in the wider pipeline. Balaji especially (a death) is the wrong tone for a launch and deserves its own careful, restrained treatment, not a beat in a finale. The Anthropic defection also carries the integrity-note source-diversity discipline (Claude is made by Anthropic) and is better handled with that care in a dedicated episode.

**Open — "The Empire's Cost."** This six is weighted toward the *internal corporate saga* and lighter on Hao's actual central thesis: the empire's cost — the hidden labor (e.g. data-labelers in the Global South), the data-center extraction, the people outside Silicon Valley who pay. That is the most Hao-faithful, most differentiated, least-covered material and a real exposé-neighborhood asset — and it can be done as named-protagonist cinematic recreation (Hao's book names specific workers).
- **Recommendation:** keep the six as drafted for launch reach; make "The Empire's Cost" the immediate next episode (season finale slot or Season 2 opener).
- **Alternative if you want fuller exposé coverage + maximum good-faith-with-Hao baked into the launch:** make it E6 and fold the Musk verdict into the back half of E5 or push the verdict to a standalone.
- *Status: Peter's call. Recommendation stands pending his decision.*

## Hao good-faith checklist (applies to every episode)

- Report the **events** (free to retell); write the script entirely in **Peter's own words and structure**; never paraphrase-track her prose or mirror her chapter sequence.
- Triangulate every factual beat against independent reporting — protects legally *and* makes better documentary (the multi-source-authority moat).
- **On-screen, by name, prominently and repeatedly:** "This account draws on the investigative reporting of Karen Hao in *Empire of AI*." Named again at the close.
- **Active book promotion** with a link (affiliate fine — it sends her sales): "the definitive account — read the book."
- **Transparency** that it's AI-native production; disclosed, not discovered.
- **Never synthesize her voice or render an AI 'Karen Hao' speaking words she didn't say.** Quote only real published words, attributed, on screen, read in Peter's narrator voice as a citation — never performed as her. (Same deepfake line as the visual register.)
- **Don't out-angle her.** The exposé neighborhood leans accusatory; Synthetic stays documentary-neutral. Let the events carry the weight; credit her framing as hers; keep Peter's posture as the neutral narrator. Borrowing her conclusions as a hot take is worse documentary and worse faith.
- Outreach after 3–5 episodes live (show, don't ask): invite her to tell you if attribution/promotion feels right.
