## PART ONE - WHAT IS NOT NEW

[A] Start honestly. Losing a job to a machine is not new. Looms, tractors, telephone exchanges, spreadsheets. Every one displaced skilled people who had done nothing wrong.
VISUAL: A bright modern museum of tools and machines in clean daylight, orderly and well lit, historical.

[A] And economies did not collapse. New work appeared, often better work. The person displaced, however, frequently never saw that new work at all.
VISUAL: A bright open modern workplace in clear daylight, new roles alive and busy, sunlit and thriving.

[A] So the honest starting point is that automation itself is not a moral crime. Refusing to improve, so that jobs stay unchanged, has its own victims. The unborn business. The unaffordable service.
VISUAL: A bright clean balance in daylight, both pans holding real human consequences, evenly weighted.
[CARD: automation itself is not the wrong - how it is done can be]

[A] The moral question is not whether efficiency may cost jobs. It always has. The question is how the loss is decided, and what is owed to the person who bears it.
VISUAL: A bright dignified ordinary worker standing in clear daylight, calm and worthy of consideration.

## PART TWO - WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGES

[A] Now the genuinely new part. When a manager makes the decision, three things are quietly present, and we barely notice them until they vanish.
VISUAL: A bright clean set of three simple forms on a pale surface in daylight, distinct and present.

[A] First, a reason that can be stated. A person can be asked why, and must answer in words. The reason may be poor, but it exists, and it can be challenged.
VISUAL: A bright honest conversation across a clean sunlit desk in daylight, two people, plain words.

[A] Second, someone who bears the weight. A human being who must look at you, and live afterwards with what they chose. That discomfort is doing real moral work.
VISUAL: A bright figure carrying a visible weight of responsibility in clear daylight, sober and present.
[CARD: a reason you can hear. a person who bears it. a door to appeal.]

[A] Third, the possibility of appeal. Circumstances can be raised. A judgment can be revisited. A mistake can be found and undone.
VISUAL: A bright open modern door in clean daylight, an appeal welcomed, sunlit and accessible.

[A] An automated dismissal can remove all three at once. The reason becomes a score. The responsibility diffuses into a process. The appeal has no one to appeal to.
VISUAL: A bright clean closed system in daylight, orderly, efficient, and entirely without a doorway.

## PART THREE - THE RESPONSIBILITY GAP

[A] This is what philosophers call the responsibility gap, and it is the true ethical wound here. Not the job loss. The evaporation of anyone answerable.
VISUAL: A bright clean gap between two sunlit surfaces in daylight, an empty space where a person should be.

[A] Ask who decided, and every answer points elsewhere. The executive says the system recommended. The engineer says the executive set the goal. The vendor says the client configured it.
VISUAL: A bright modern office in clean daylight, several capable people, each gesturing courteously elsewhere.
[CARD: everyone points somewhere else - nobody is answerable]

[A] Each is telling the truth. And so a life is altered, and no human being anywhere has to say, I did this, and here is why.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary person standing outside a clean modern building in daylight, holding a plain letter.

[A] That is not a technical problem. It is a moral vacuum. And the people who build these systems did not create it on purpose. It simply fell out of the design.
VISUAL: A bright clean flowchart on a sunlit wall in daylight, complete and efficient, with no name upon it.

## PART FOUR - WHAT IS ACTUALLY OWED

[A] So what does a company owe someone whose job it automates? Not a promise of employment forever. That was never realistic, and pretending otherwise helps nobody.
VISUAL: A bright honest modern workplace in clean daylight, capable and changing, alive and unsentimental.

[A] But three things follow from the dignity of the person, and they are not expensive.
VISUAL: A bright clean set of three simple gifts arranged on a pale surface in daylight, modest and clear.

[A] An explanation a human being will stand behind and repeat aloud. Not a score. A reason, given face to face, by someone who accepts it as their own.
VISUAL: A bright honest manager speaking plainly across a clean sunlit desk, present and accountable.

[A] A genuine route of appeal, staffed by a person with the authority to overturn the machine. If nobody can reverse it, the appeal is theatre.
VISUAL: A bright open doorway in clean daylight, a real person waiting behind it, attentive and able.
[CARD: an explanation. a real appeal. a share of the gain.]

[A] And a share in what the efficiency created. If the machine's value comes partly from the work those people built, they have a claim on some of it. Notice. Retraining. Time.
VISUAL: A bright clean portion being deliberately set aside and offered in daylight, fair and unforced.

## PART FIVE - THE DEEPER TRADE

[A] Now the thing that should trouble even the most hard-headed executive. A company that automates dismissal is teaching its whole workforce something.
VISUAL: A bright modern workplace in clean daylight, people watching quietly, learning something true.

[A] It teaches that no reasoning will save you. That your relationships are not currency. That the way to survive here is to look good to the system, not to do good work.
VISUAL: A bright office in daylight where people work carefully toward a screen rather than toward each other.
[CARD: people optimise for the metric they can be fired by]

[A] And you will get exactly that. Work shaped to what the metric can see. Effort withdrawn from everything it cannot. This is not sentiment. It is prediction.
VISUAL: A bright clean dashboard rising steadily on a sunlit wall while the room around it thins, daylight.

[A] The company that keeps a human being in the loop is not being soft. It is protecting the one thing an automated firm cannot generate. Trust, and the discretionary effort that follows it.
VISUAL: A bright team genuinely working together in clean daylight, engaged, trusting, and productive.

## PART SIX - THE WEIGHT YOU CARRY OUT

[A] So is it wrong to let an artificial intelligence fire someone? The honest answer is that the machine is not the wrong. The vanishing is.
VISUAL: The balance-scale mark, clean and level, bright petrol and amber, steady and serious.

[A] Use the model to inform. Never to absolve. Somewhere at the end of every decision that changes a life, a person must be standing, willing to say, this was mine.

[A] And if your organisation cannot name that person, you have not built an efficient process. You have built a way for everyone to do something none of them would do alone.
VISUAL: A bright clean system in daylight with one chair placed firmly and visibly at its end, occupied.

[A] The test is simple, and you can apply it tomorrow. Could you look the person in the eye and explain? If not, do not let a machine do it in your place.
