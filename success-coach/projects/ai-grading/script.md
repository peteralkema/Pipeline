## PART ONE - WHAT A GRADE ACTUALLY IS

[A] We speak as if a grade is a measurement. As if the essay had a quality, in it, the way a rod has a length, and marking simply reads it off.
VISUAL: A bright clean measuring instrument on a pale surface in daylight, precise and objective, sunlit.

[A] But a grade is not a measurement. It is a judgment, made by a person, communicated to another person, about work they made.
VISUAL: A bright honest teacher writing a considered comment in clear daylight, attentive and unhurried.

[A] Which means it carries something a number cannot. It says, I read this. I saw what you were attempting. Here is where it worked and where it did not.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary handwritten note in the margin of a page in clean daylight, warm and specific.
[CARD: a grade says "I read this" - a score says nothing at all]

[A] Take that away, and the number may survive intact while the thing it was for has quietly gone.
VISUAL: A bright clean number alone on a pale page in daylight, correct, complete, and unaccompanied.

## PART TWO - THE GOOD CASE, TAKEN SERIOUSLY

[A] Now be fair, because the case for automation here is strong, and dismissing it is lazy.
VISUAL: A bright modern staff room in clean daylight, capable teachers, orderly, and plainly overworked.

[A] Human markers are inconsistent. They mark more harshly when tired, more generously after a good essay, and are influenced by handwriting and by a child's name.
VISUAL: A bright clean stack of papers in daylight, marked late, honestly and unevenly, ordinary.

[A] A teacher with thirty children cannot give each essay twenty minutes of real attention every week. The choice is often not machine or teacher. It is machine or nothing.
VISUAL: A bright modern classroom in clear daylight, many children, one capable adult, calm and finite.
[CARD: often the real choice is machine, or no feedback at all]

[A] And systems that handle the mechanical part, spelling, structure, obvious errors, can hand a teacher back the hours to do the part only a person can do.
VISUAL: A bright teacher sitting down beside one child in clean daylight, unhurried and fully present.

[A] So the question is never simply whether to automate. It is which part, and what the freed time is actually used for.
VISUAL: A bright clean pair of tasks in daylight, one mechanical and one human, plainly distinguishable.

## PART THREE - WHEN A PREDICTION BECOMES A PROPHECY

[A] Now the sharper danger, and it is not about grading essays at all. Systems increasingly predict which children will struggle, before they have struggled.
VISUAL: A bright clean modern screen in daylight showing a calm ranked list of ordinary names, orderly.

[A] The model uses attendance, past marks, postcode, and family circumstance. It is often accurate, in the sense that its predictions come true.
VISUAL: A bright clean prediction on a pale surface in daylight, precise and unnervingly well supported.

[A] But consider what happens next. A teacher, told this child is likely to fall behind, expects less. Offers easier work. Calls on her less often.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary classroom in clear daylight, a child at a desk, quietly and kindly overlooked.
[CARD: the prediction changes the treatment. the treatment fulfils the prediction.]

[A] The child falls behind. The model is vindicated. And nobody in the chain did anything unkind, or noticed the loop closing.
VISUAL: A bright clean loop in daylight closing gently upon itself, orderly and self-confirming, precise.

[A] A prediction about a person is not like a prediction about the weather. The child can hear it. Everyone around her can hear it. And it makes itself come true.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary child in clean daylight, capable and unremarkable, and expected to be less.

## PART FOUR - WHAT SCHOOL IS ACTUALLY FOR

[A] Step back and ask what we send children to school to receive. Not information, which is free and everywhere. Something else.
VISUAL: A bright modern school corridor in clear daylight, ordinary, busy, and full of young people.

[A] They go to be known. To have an adult who is not their parent take them seriously, notice what they are good at, and expect something of them.
VISUAL: A bright teacher recognising something in a child's work in clean daylight, warm and specific.

[A] The most reliable finding in all of education is that a single adult who believes in a particular child changes that child's trajectory. Not curriculum. A person.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary moment of encouragement between teacher and child in clear daylight, real.
[CARD: children go to school to be known by an adult]

[A] A system can deliver content, mark accurately, and personalise pacing beautifully. It cannot believe in anyone. There is nobody in there to do the believing.
VISUAL: A bright clean capable system in daylight, precise, helpful, and containing no one at all.

## PART FIVE - THE HONEST TEST

[A] So here is a test any school, any parent, any minister can apply.
VISUAL: A bright meeting room, a diverse team around a clean table, a plain question written on glass.

[A] Does this system give the teacher more time with the child, or does it give the school an excuse to have fewer teachers? Those two look identical on the day it is installed.
VISUAL: A bright modern classroom in clean daylight, a teacher present, or a chair standing empty.
[CARD: does it free the teacher, or replace her?]

[A] Second. Is any prediction about a child visible to the child, and to the adults around her, and is it reviewed as she changes? Or is it a quiet label she can never see or shed?
VISUAL: A bright clean open record in daylight, legible and revisable, honest and unhidden, sunlit.

[A] Third. When the system is wrong, who notices? If nobody is reading the work, the answer is nobody, and the child will simply be wrong about herself instead.
VISUAL: A bright honest adult reading a child's actual work carefully in clean daylight, attentive.

[A] Automate the marking, gladly. Never automate the knowing. That is the whole distinction, and it is not subtle once you have named it.
VISUAL: A bright clean tool in daylight, well used, in the hands of a person who is fully present.

## PART SIX - THE WEIGHT YOU CARRY OUT

[A] So should a machine grade your child? For spelling, for structure, for the tenth practice paper this week, yes, and be grateful for the time it returns.
VISUAL: The balance-scale mark, clean and level, bright petrol and amber, steady and warm.

[A] But for the essay she was proud of, for the judgment about who she is and what she might become, no. That must come from someone who read it, and who knows her.
VISUAL: A bright teacher reading a child's work with genuine attention in clean daylight, unhurried.

[A] And be vigilant about predictions. A forecast about weather is harmless. A forecast about a child, spoken aloud, is an instruction to everyone around her.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary child in clear daylight, unlabelled, open, and entirely undetermined.

[A] Every one of us can remember the adult who thought we were capable of something. That is what school was for. Do not let anyone install a system that makes such a person unnecessary.
