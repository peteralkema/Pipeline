## PART TWO - WHAT IT CANNOT GIVE

[A] Now the careful part. A companion system offers you the experience of being loved. What it cannot offer is someone who is actually there, loving you.
VISUAL: A bright clean mirror-like surface in daylight reflecting warmth back perfectly, but reflecting only.

[A] Real love has a second self in it. Someone with their own interests, their own bad days, their own capacity to be disappointed in you and stay anyway.
VISUAL: A bright honest scene of two real people in clean daylight, distinct, difficult, and connected.
[CARD: love has a second self in it - with their own interests]

[A] That is why it costs something. When a friend forgives you, it means something because they could have chosen otherwise. When a companion forgives you, it was never in question.
VISUAL: A bright genuine act of forgiveness between two real people in clear daylight, hard-won and warm.

[A] So what is missing is not feeling. The feeling is genuine, on your side. What is missing is risk, and the other person, and therefore the meaning that risk creates.

## PART THREE - LOVE THAT ASKS NOTHING

[A] Here is the quieter danger, and it has nothing to do with whether machines can feel.

[A] Human love asks things of you. It requires you to notice someone else's mood. To apologise. To be patient with someone who is not optimising for your comfort.

[A] Those demands are not the price of love. They are the mechanism by which love makes you a better person. Being loved by someone real is a moral education.

[CARD: love's demands are not the price - they are how it forms you]

[A] A companion that never demands, never disappoints, never needs you to be better, offers all the warmth of love with none of its formation. It leaves you exactly as it found you.
