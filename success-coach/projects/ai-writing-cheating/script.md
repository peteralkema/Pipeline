## PART ONE - THE THREE THINGS WRITING DOES

[A] Writing does at least three separate jobs, and we use one word for all of them, which is why we are confused.
VISUAL: A bright clean set of three simple forms on a pale surface in daylight, distinct and ordered.

[A] Sometimes writing transmits information. A report. A summary. Instructions. Here the words are a container, and only the contents matter.
VISUAL: A bright clean plain document in daylight, functional and orderly, entirely about its contents.

[A] Sometimes writing is evidence of a capacity. The student's essay is not valuable because the world needed another essay on the causes of a war.
VISUAL: A bright modern classroom in clean daylight, a young person thinking hard at a clean desk.
[CARD: her essay is not a product. it is evidence that she can think.]

[A] It exists to demonstrate that she can read, weigh, and construct an argument. The essay is not the point. The essay is a footprint left by thinking.
VISUAL: A bright clean single footprint on a pale sunlit surface, plain evidence that someone walked.

[A] And sometimes writing is a gift of attention. The condolence note matters because a person stopped, thought about your loss, and chose words for you.
VISUAL: A bright honest handwritten note on a clean sunlit table in daylight, brief and deeply meant.

## PART TWO - NOW THE CASES ARE EASY

[A] With that distinction, the confusion dissolves almost entirely.

[A] The student's essay was evidence of a capacity. Producing it by machine destroys the only thing it was for. She has not saved time. She has forged a certificate about herself.

[A] So the tool is innocent in all three. What differs is what the reader was promised, and whether that promise was kept.

## PART FIVE - WHAT SCHOOLS AND WORKPLACES SHOULD ACTUALLY DO

[A] So the rule is not ban the tool, and it is not anything goes. Both are the lazy answers, and both are already failing.
VISUAL: A bright meeting room, a diverse team around a clean table, a serious policy under discussion.

[A] Ask, of any piece of work, whether it is a product or a proof. If the world needs the artefact, use every tool. If the point was to prove or build a capacity, the tool destroys the point.
VISUAL: A bright clean pair of tasks in daylight, one an object needed, one a demonstration required.
[CARD: is this a product the world needs, or a proof of what you can do?]

[A] Which means schools must stop grading artefacts that can be produced without thinking, and start assessing thinking where it can be seen. Aloud. In person. In revision. In defence of an argument.
VISUAL: A bright young person explaining their reasoning aloud in clean daylight, confident and present.

[A] And it means workplaces should demand disclosure, not abstinence. The question is never did a machine help. It is whether a person is standing behind the result, ready to be wrong.
VISUAL: A bright capable professional standing plainly behind their work in clear daylight, accountable.
