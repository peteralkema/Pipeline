## PART ONE - WHAT A PRICE IS

[A] A price looks like a fact about an object. The chair costs this much. As though the number lived in the chair.
VISUAL: A bright clean modern chair on a pale surface in daylight, ordinary and plainly priced, sunlit.

[A] It is not. A price is a meeting point. It is where what a seller will accept overlaps with what a buyer will pay. Everything above the seller's floor is negotiation.
VISUAL: A bright clean overlap between two ranges on a pale surface in daylight, precise and simple.

[A] And crucially, a public posted price does something quietly democratic. It gives every buyer the same information, and it means the shy pay the same as the shrewd.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary shop window in clear daylight, one price, visible to everyone passing, warm.
[CARD: a public price means the shy pay what the shrewd pay]

[A] Personalised pricing does not change what a thing costs to make. It transfers the entire negotiating range to whoever has the better information.
VISUAL: A bright clean balance in daylight, information gathered heavily on one side only, uneven.

## PART TWO - THE HONEST DEFENCE

[A] Now take the case for it seriously, because economists make it and it is not stupid.
VISUAL: A bright modern lecture room in clean daylight, orderly and rigorous, a clear argument advanced.

[A] Charging different people different prices can expand access. A cinema ticket cheaper for students. A medicine priced lower in a poorer country. Fewer people are priced out.
VISUAL: A bright modern cinema entrance in clear daylight, ordinary people entering, various and welcome.

[A] Airlines have done this for decades. The person beside you paid something quite different, and nobody considers it a scandal.
VISUAL: A bright modern aircraft cabin in clean daylight, orderly rows, ordinary travellers, calm.
[CARD: discounts for students are price discrimination too]

[A] And in principle, a perfectly personalised price could charge the wealthy more and the poor less, which sounds rather like fairness.
VISUAL: A bright clean balance in daylight, adjusted thoughtfully toward those with less, honest.

[A] So the practice is not automatically wicked. Which means we need to find precisely where it turns, because it plainly does turn.
VISUAL: A bright clean line drawn carefully on a pale surface in daylight, exact and deliberate.

## PART THREE - WHERE IT TURNS

[A] Here is the turn. Traditional discounts price by category, and the categories are declared. Student. Senior. Off-peak. Everyone knows the rule, and anyone in the category may claim it.
VISUAL: A bright clean posted list of published categories in daylight, plain and openly available.

[A] Personalised pricing prices by prediction, and the prediction is hidden. Not what group are you in, but how desperate do you seem, right now.
VISUAL: A bright clean hidden estimate on a modern screen in daylight, precise, quiet, and unpublished.
[CARD: not "which group are you in" but "how desperate do you seem"]

[A] The student discount charges you less because society decided students should have access. The desperation price charges you more because you appeared to have no alternative.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary person in clean daylight, urgently needing something, entirely unaware of being read.

[A] One is a policy anyone can inspect. The other is a private judgment about your weakness, made in a moment you did not know you were being assessed.
VISUAL: A bright clean assessment being made silently in daylight, orderly, accurate, and unannounced.

[A] And notice who bears it. The person searching urgently at midnight for a flight home is, more often than not, the one who can least afford it.
VISUAL: A bright modern airport in clear daylight, an ordinary anxious traveller, tired and unremarkable.

## PART FOUR - THE THING BEING SOLD IS ASYMMETRY

[A] So what is actually being purchased by the company doing this? Not better products. Not lower costs. Information asymmetry.
VISUAL: A bright clean balance in daylight, knowledge stacked entirely on one pan, honest and stark.

[A] A market is supposed to work because both parties know what they are trading, and either may walk away. That is the whole moral basis of a free exchange.
VISUAL: A bright honest marketplace in clean daylight, two people trading openly, informed and equal.

[A] When one party knows the other's tolerance precisely, and the other party knows nothing at all, the exchange still looks free. But the freedom has moved entirely to one side.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary transaction in clear daylight, voluntary, legal, and quietly unbalanced.
[CARD: it still looks like a free exchange. the freedom moved to one side.]

[A] And this is why disclosure matters so much. A price you know is personalised is a price you can refuse, compare, or complain about. A hidden one is simply what things cost.
VISUAL: A bright clean label stating plainly that a price was personalised in daylight, honest and open.

## PART FIVE - WHERE THIS IS GOING

[A] Now extend the logic a few years, because the mechanism does not stop at retail.
VISUAL: A bright modern connected world in clean daylight, orderly, capable, and increasingly predictive.

[A] Insurance premiums set by inferred behaviour. Loan terms adjusted by inferred desperation. Rents proposed by systems that know exactly how many alternatives you have.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary residential street in clear daylight, homes, ordinary lives, quietly assessed.

[A] Each is defensible individually as accurate pricing of risk. Together, they mean the people with the fewest options are charged the most for everything, automatically, everywhere.
VISUAL: A bright clean set of separate systems in daylight, each reasonable, all pointing the same way.
[CARD: individually defensible. collectively, a machine that charges the trapped.]

[A] Nobody designed that outcome. It falls out of many rational systems, each optimising honestly, each blind to the person standing at the intersection of all of them.

## PART SIX - THE WEIGHT YOU CARRY OUT

[A] So why do you pay more than your neighbour? Because a system estimated that you would. That is the entire answer, and it is worth sitting with.
VISUAL: The balance-scale mark, clean and level, bright petrol and amber, steady and serious.

[A] The test is not whether prices differ. They always have. It is whether the rule is published, whether you can see it, and whether it prices your circumstances or your helplessness.
VISUAL: A bright clean published rule in daylight, legible to anyone who cares to read it, honest.

[A] Charging what a person can bear is not the same as charging what a thing is worth. The first has a name older than economics, and it is not a flattering one.
VISUAL: A bright honest marketplace in clear daylight, plain prices, open dealing, ordinary and fair.

[A] So ask, of every price you are shown. Is this what it costs, or is this what they think I will tolerate? You are entitled to know. Increasingly, you are not told.
