## PART ONE - WHAT THE LAWS OF WAR ACTUALLY DEMAND

[A] War has rules, and they are not decoration. Three of them matter here, and each requires something a machine may not be able to do.

[A] Distinction. You must separate combatants from civilians. That is not a visual classification problem. A person lowering a weapon, a person surrendering, a person too frightened to move.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary person raising empty hands in clear daylight, plainly surrendering, dignified.

[A] Proportionality. Any harm to civilians must not be excessive relative to the military advantage. This requires weighing incommensurable things. There is no formula, and there never was.

[CARD: proportionality has no formula. it was always a judgment.]

[A] And precaution. You must take feasible steps to avoid harm, including waiting, verifying, and choosing not to strike when unsure.

[A] Each of these is a judgment, not a calculation. They were written on the assumption that a person, capable of doubt, stands at the decision.
