## COLD OPEN

[A] The question is no longer whether artificial intelligence will change work. It already has.
VISUAL: A vast open-plan office at dusk, half the desks lit and occupied, half already dark and empty.

[A] The real question, the one that decides your next ten years, is more specific. Is your particular job on the early list, or the late one.
VISUAL: A sharp line down the middle of that office where the light ends and the shadow begins.

[A] Because the difference is not about how skilled you are. It is about the shape of what you do.
VISUAL: A single occupied desk right at the edge of the lit half, the dark pressing close beside it.

[A] Most predictions about this get it wrong in the same way. They assume the lowest-paid jobs go first. That is not what the data shows.
VISUAL: A balance scale in dim light, a hard hat on one pan and a laptop on the other.

[A] Some highly paid knowledge jobs are more exposed than manual ones, for a reason we will unpack. So let us be precise instead of fearful.
VISUAL: The laptop side of that scale dipping lower, tilting unexpectedly.

[A] We will name seven kinds of work where the ground is already moving, and three where it is remarkably solid.
VISUAL: Two columns of light projected onto a dark wall, one with a downward arrow, one with a steady line.

[A] And critically, we will look at why, so that even if your exact job is not on these lists, you can place yourself correctly.
VISUAL: Faint figures moving between those two columns of light.

[A] This is not a video designed to scare you. Fear makes people freeze, and freezing is the one fatal move. This is a map.

## PART ONE - THE PRINCIPLE

[A] Before the lists, the principle that generates them. Artificial intelligence is, at its core, a pattern machine.
VISUAL: A flowing river of glowing data symbols pouring through a dark channel.

[A] It is extraordinary at tasks where the inputs are digital, the patterns are abundant, and the answer can be checked against a vast record.
VISUAL: That river sorting itself into clean ordered streams as it moves.

[A] The more your work looks like that, the more exposed you are. So the dividing line is not skilled versus unskilled.
VISUAL: A sorting machine dropping objects into two bins under hard light.

[A] It is predictable versus genuinely novel, digital versus physical, and solitary versus deeply human.
VISUAL: One stream from that machine smooth and identical, the other tumbling and irregular.

[A] A job made of repeatable digital tasks is exposed even if it took a degree to learn. A job of unpredictable physical moments is protected even if it pays little.
VISUAL: The irregular objects refusing to be sorted, scattering off the belt.

[A] Hold that principle and the lists almost write themselves. Now let us walk them.
VISUAL: A hand turning the page of a large open ledger under warm light.

## PART TWO - THE SEVEN BEING ERASED FIRST

[A] One. Routine data entry and processing. Any job whose core is moving structured information from one place to another, checking it against rules.
VISUAL: An endless wall of filing cabinets in a dim archive, the drawers slowly emptying.

[A] The machine does it faster, cheaper, and without fatigue. This work is not threatened. It is already dissolving.
VISUAL: Papers rising from those drawers and dissolving into points of light.

[A] Two. Basic content production at scale. The routine article, the templated copy, the standard product description.
VISUAL: A printing press running at impossible speed in a dark hall, pages flying off in a blur.

[A] Not the rare brilliant piece, but the ocean of competent, forgettable text the economy runs on. That ocean is now produced in seconds.
VISUAL: The human operator stations beside that press empty and still.

[A] Three. Entry-level coding and routine software tasks. The repetitive, well-documented work that once trained junior developers.
VISUAL: A tall ladder against a dark wall with its lowest rungs missing.

[A] It is exactly what the machine does best, because code is digital, checkable, and richly recorded. The bottom rung of the ladder is being sawn off.
VISUAL: A climber forced to leap to reach the first remaining step on that ladder.

[A] Four. First-line customer support. The scripted query, the password reset, the order status, the standard complaint.
VISUAL: A wall of headsets hanging on hooks in an empty call center, the chairs pushed in.

[A] Work that follows a decision tree is work a machine can walk faster than a person. The volume of simple cases is collapsing into automation.
VISUAL: A single screen in that center glowing with an automated chat scrolling itself.

[A] Five. Routine analysis and reporting. The analyst who pulls standard numbers and builds the same weekly report.
VISUAL: A spreadsheet of glowing figures assembling itself on a dark screen.

[A] Gathering and summarizing the predictable is precisely what these systems excel at. The mechanical assembly of the numbers does not survive.
VISUAL: Charts forming and labeling themselves above an empty analyst's chair.

[A] Six. Translation and transcription of the straightforward kind. The routine document, the standard meeting, the common language pair.
VISUAL: Streams of text in different scripts flowing across a dark surface.

[A] The machine now does this at a speed and cost no human can match. The everyday middle of this field is washing away.
VISUAL: Those streams merging into a single clear language, the conversion instant and silent.

[A] Seven. Routine paralegal and administrative research. Searching documents, pulling precedents, drafting standard forms.
VISUAL: A vast law library at night, the shelves stretching into darkness.

[A] Anywhere a human was hired mainly to search and assemble information, the machine is now the cheaper searcher.
VISUAL: A single beam of light scanning the spines faster than any eye could read.

## PART THREE - THE THREE IT CANNOT TOUCH

[A] Now breathe, because the other list matters more. There are kinds of work that are remarkably safe, and not for sentimental reasons.
VISUAL: An ancient oak tree standing alone in a storm, the deep roots holding it still.

[A] They are safe because of what they require, which is exactly what a pattern machine cannot supply. One. Skilled, unpredictable physical work.
VISUAL: A pair of skilled hands working inside an open wall, fitting wires by feel in tight space.

[A] The electrician in an old building where no two walls are the same. The plumber, the mechanic, the nurse on her feet, the line cook in the rush.
VISUAL: A work light clipped nearby in that dim interior, the hands steady.

[A] Work that happens in the messy physical world, where every situation is slightly novel and a body must be present. This work is a fortress.
VISUAL: The wall around those hands cluttered and irregular, no two parts alike.

[A] Two. Deep human connection and trust. The therapist, the great teacher, the nurse who calms a frightened patient.
VISUAL: Two figures sitting across a small table in warm light, leaning toward each other.

[A] The leader people will follow through hard times, the salesperson closing the deal that turns on rapport.
VISUAL: One of those figures listening closely as the other speaks, fully present.

[A] Work whose entire value is one human being trusting and being changed by another. A machine can imitate the words. It cannot be the person you trust.
VISUAL: A handshake between the two held in that warm light, real and firm.

[A] Three. Genuine creativity and complex judgment under uncertainty. Not the templated content the machine floods, but the original strategy.
VISUAL: A single figure standing before a vast blank canvas in a bright studio, brush raised.

[A] The novel idea, the hard call made with incomplete information and real stakes. Where there is no pattern yet, the pattern machine has nothing to copy.
VISUAL: The white surface of that canvas waiting, no template, no guide.

## PART FOUR - WHAT TO DO NOW

[A] So what do you do with this map. Three moves, starting today, whatever list your job is on.
VISUAL: A compass resting on an open map under a lamp, three routes drawn outward from one point.

[A] First, honestly locate yourself. Take your actual daily tasks, not your title, and sort them.
VISUAL: A hand sorting a stack of cards into two piles on a desk under the light.

[A] How much is predictable and digital, and how much is novel, physical, or deeply human. Your exposure is the ratio.
VISUAL: One pile of cards growing taller, the other holding steady.

[A] Second, move up the value, not away from the machine. The winners climb to the part of their work it cannot do.
VISUAL: A figure climbing from a flooding lower floor up a staircase to a higher, dry room.

[A] Let it handle the routine. Spend your saved hours on judgment, relationships, and originality. Become the human the machine needs.
VISUAL: A wide window in that high dry room, the rising water far below and behind.

[A] Third, build at least one capability from the safe list, deliberately. A physical skill, a deeply human one, or a creative one.
VISUAL: A craftsman's hands and a glowing screen working side by side at one bench.

[A] You are not abandoning your career. You are buying insurance in a currency the machine cannot devalue.
VISUAL: The human and the machine each doing what it does best in the warm light.

[A] The flood is real. But floods reward the people with maps and ruin the ones who freeze. You now have the map.
VISUAL: A lone figure standing on high ground at dawn looking out over a wide valley.

[A] Locate yourself honestly, climb toward the work only a human can do, and the most disruptive technology in a century becomes the most powerful tool you have ever held.
VISUAL: The rising water far below catching the first gold light, the path ahead clear.
