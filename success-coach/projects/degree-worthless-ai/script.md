## PART ONE - WHAT A DEGREE ACTUALLY SOLD

[A] To understand the collapse, you have to understand what you were actually buying. A degree sold three things at once, bundled together.
VISUAL: Three glowing threads braided into a single rope under a lamp.

[A] And we mistook the bundle for the diploma itself. The first thing it sold was knowledge. The actual information and skills taught in the courses.
VISUAL: The individual strands of that rope barely distinguishable, woven tight.

[A] For a long time, this knowledge was scarce and hard to reach. You needed the library, the professor, the institution.
VISUAL: A single locked door in a stone wall with a long line of people waiting before it.

[A] The degree was the gate to information you could not easily get any other way. The second thing it sold was a signal.
VISUAL: A faint golden light spilling from the gap at the base of that locked door.

[A] To an employer who could not test every applicant, the degree was a shortcut. It said, this person was smart enough to get in.
VISUAL: A funnel filtering a stream of identical tokens, most falling away to the sides.

[A] And disciplined enough to finish. The employer was not always buying your knowledge. They were buying a filter that had already sorted you.
VISUAL: A few of those tokens passing through the narrow neck of the funnel into light.

[A] And the third thing it sold was access. The network, the alumni, the recruiters who only visited certain campuses.
VISUAL: A members-only doorway with a velvet rope, warm light and movement within.

[A] For many, this was quietly the most valuable product of all, and the one least related to anything learned in a classroom.
VISUAL: A steady stream of figures passing through that doorway while others stand outside.

## PART TWO - WHY EACH PART IS FAILING

[A] Now watch what artificial intelligence and the open internet do to each of those three products. They dismantle them one by one.
VISUAL: The three-strand rope from before, each strand being cut separately by an unseen blade.

[A] Take knowledge first. The information that was once locked behind the institution is now nearly free and instantly available.
VISUAL: The locked stone door from before now standing wide open with no line.

[A] And the application of that knowledge is increasingly done with you by a machine that has read more than any professor alive.
VISUAL: The golden light spilling freely from that open door into an empty courtyard.

[A] The scarce thing became abundant. Abundant things lose their price. Now the signal. This one is failing more slowly, but it is failing.
VISUAL: A row of identical filtered tokens, now all the same on the far side of the funnel.

[A] When everyone has a degree, the degree stops sorting anyone. And employers, burned by graduates who cannot do the work, are learning to test the work directly.
VISUAL: A hand reaching past those identical tokens for something else entirely.

[A] Skills assessments, portfolios, trial projects. The filter is being replaced by a measurement of the real thing.
VISUAL: A finished working object set down beside the discarded tokens, examined closely.

[A] The access product survives the longest, and it is the most unfair. The network and the prestige door still open for the few.
VISUAL: A grand columned building seen across a wide gulf, a narrow bridge to its door.

[A] But here is the shift. As the knowledge and signal value collapse, the access becomes nearly the only thing left an expensive degree buys.
VISUAL: Only a few figures using that narrow bridge, most turning to other paths.

[A] Which means we are paying four years and a fortune for a contact list. More people are noticing that math.
VISUAL: The wide gulf below the bridge, the far building distant and exclusive.

## PART THREE - WHAT REPLACES IT

[A] So if the bundled degree is unwinding, what does a person actually need now. The three products are being sold separately.
VISUAL: Three separate market stalls in a bright open square, each offering one good.

[A] And you can assemble a better bundle yourself, often faster and cheaper than the institution. For knowledge, the new currency is demonstrated skill.
VISUAL: A figure moving between those stalls, choosing deliberately.

[A] Not certified attendance. The world increasingly does not care where you learned it. It cares whether you can do it, on demand.
VISUAL: A workbench displaying finished, working objects under a clean light.

[A] A portfolio that proves you can build the thing now outranks a transcript that proves you sat in a room four years ago.
VISUAL: A small unread certificate set aside in shadow beside those finished objects.

[A] For the signal, the new filter is a track record. Things you have actually shipped. Problems you have actually solved, visible to anyone who looks.
VISUAL: A wall covered in real finished projects and their results, lit and labeled.

[A] Your work, done in public, becomes its own credential, and a far more honest one, because no one can fake having built the thing that obviously works.
VISUAL: A living gallery of proof rather than a single framed paper.

[A] And for access, the network is no longer locked to a campus. It is built by being useful and visible to the right communities.
VISUAL: A web of connections forming across an open map, lines reaching between distant points.

[A] The access that once came automatically with the prestigious degree must now be earned directly. Harder for some, but open to far more.
VISUAL: The network growing across the map by contribution rather than by gate.

[A] None of this means stop learning. The opposite. It means learning matters more than ever, but the diploma is no longer the proof of it.
VISUAL: A figure at a desk surrounded by tools and an open glowing screen, building something.

[A] In the AI economy the valuable person is not the one who holds the credential. It is the one who can actually do the thing, prove it, and keep learning.
VISUAL: That figure focused, the room warm and alive around them.

[A] So ask the sharper question. Not which degree, but which capabilities, which proof, and which network. Buy the three products directly.
VISUAL: An open hand holding a single small bright seed in the light.

[A] The diploma was only ever a wrapper, and the wrapper is the part that lost its value. The thing inside it never will.
VISUAL: The discarded diploma wrapper on the table, the focus entirely on the seed.
