## PART ONE - THE ARGUMENT, PLAINLY

[A] Singer's argument is unusually simple. It has only two steps, and both are hard to deny.
VISUAL: A bright clean pair of simple stepping forms on a pale surface in daylight, ordered and precise.

[A] Step one. Suffering and death from lack of food, shelter, and medical care are bad. Almost nobody disputes this.
VISUAL: A clean bright card on a pale surface holding one plain line of text, editorial and modern.

[A] Step two. If it is in your power to prevent something bad, without sacrificing anything of comparable moral importance, you ought to do it.
VISUAL: A second bright card beside the first in clean daylight, equally plain, equally difficult to refuse.
[CARD: prevent great harm at small cost - and you ought to]

[A] That is the pond principle. It is exactly what makes you wade in. And it says nothing whatever about distance, or nationality, or whether you can see the child.
VISUAL: A bright clear line drawn from the pond outward across a sunlit map, uninterrupted and steady.

[A] So if you accept both steps, you seem committed to something dramatic. That your obligation to the distant child is much the same as to the one in front of you.
VISUAL: Two bright figures in clear daylight, one near, one far, evenly and equally lit, sunlit.

## PART TWO - THE ESCAPE ROUTES

[A] Naturally, we look for the flaw. And there are several places we reach for. Each one is worth examining honestly.
VISUAL: A bright clean set of doorways in daylight, each promising a way out, sunlit and inviting.

[A] Distance, we say. She is far away. But why should distance matter morally? If the pond were a mile off, or in another town, your duty would not vanish.
VISUAL: A bright clean landscape stretching far under daylight, the same clear light falling everywhere.

[A] Then we say, but she is a stranger. So was the child in the pond. You did not ask her name before you waded in.
VISUAL: A bright anonymous small figure at the sunlit water's edge, a stranger, saved without question.
[CARD: distance and strangeness didn't matter in the pond]

[A] Then we say, but others could help too. True. Yet if a dozen people stood by the pond doing nothing, would that release you? It would not. It would shame you all.
VISUAL: A bright group of figures standing still around a clean sunlit pond in daylight, none of them moving.

[A] Then we say, but my money might not reach her. That is a real and serious objection. Notice, though, that it is a practical problem, not a moral escape.
VISUAL: A bright clean channel of water running clear from one point to another in daylight, direct and open.

## PART THREE - THE HARDEST PART

[A] What makes this argument so uncomfortable is not that it is complicated. It is that it is simple, and that we cannot honestly break it.
VISUAL: A bright clean unbroken chain of two links on a pale surface in daylight, plain and strong.

[A] And its conclusion is demanding. If you should give until giving costs you something of comparable importance, then most of us should give far more than we do.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary comfortable life in clean daylight, pleasant and unremarkable, sunlit.

[A] That is a heavy thought. It suggests the ordinary decent life, the one nearly all of us live, involves a quiet and continuous failure.
VISUAL: A bright honest figure pausing in clear daylight, confronted by something uncomfortable, calm.
[CARD: the argument's cost - an ordinary life may not be enough]

[A] Most people, when they meet this argument, do not refute it. They simply set it down and walk away. Which is itself worth noticing.

## PART FOUR - THE REPLIES WORTH TAKING SERIOUSLY

[A] But there are real replies, and honesty demands we hear them. The first is about what a human being can bear.
VISUAL: A bright figure carrying a reasonable, sustainable load in clear daylight, steady and upright.

[A] A morality that demands you strip your life bare may be self-defeating. People burn out, resent it, and give up entirely. A duty no one can keep is a duty that saves nobody.
VISUAL: A bright clean structure built to last under daylight, strong because it is not overstrained.

[A] The second is about special relationships. We owe our children, our friends, our neighbours a particular care. A world of pure impartiality might lose something precious.

[CARD: impartiality is not the whole of a good life]

[A] Singer himself softened the demand over time, proposing a modest, livable percentage rather than the last penny. The argument keeps its force. It just stops being crushing.

## PART FIVE - THE POND IS NOW EVERYWHERE

[A] Here is what has changed in our century. We used to be ignorant of the distant child. Now, we are not. We hold the pond in our hands.

[A] Our systems show us suffering anywhere on earth, instantly, and offer to transfer help with a tap. The old excuse of distance has technically dissolved.

[A] But those same systems flood us with so much need that we go numb. The feed makes every pond visible, and thereby makes none of them feel real.

[A] Which is a genuinely new moral condition. We are the first people with the power to act at a distance, and the first to be numbed by seeing too much.
