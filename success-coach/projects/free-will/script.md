## PART ONE - THE CASE FOR DETERMINISM

[A] The argument is short, and it is very hard to break. Everything that happens has a cause. Your brain is part of the physical world. So your choices have causes too.
VISUAL: A bright clean sequence of causes on a pale surface in daylight, orderly and complete, sunlit.

[A] Nothing mysterious is being claimed. A person who was abused, exhausted, and desperate did not select those conditions from a menu before acting.
VISUAL: A bright honest ordinary life in clean daylight, shaped by circumstances nobody chose, dignified.

[A] Add randomness, and it does not help. A choice that happens by quantum chance is not more yours. It is simply less predictable. Neither cause nor accident gives you authorship.

[CARD: caused or random - neither one makes the choice truly yours]

[A] And so the hard determinist concludes something startling. Nobody, ever, in the deepest sense, could have done otherwise. Which means nobody truly deserves blame.

## PART THREE - THE COMPATIBILIST REPLY

[A] Here is the reply that most philosophers now accept, and it is subtler than it first sounds. Freedom was never about escaping causation.
VISUAL: A bright open modern space in clean daylight, spacious and free, and entirely physical, sunlit.

[A] To act freely is to act from your own reasons, without compulsion, with the capacity to respond to argument. Not to be uncaused. To be the kind of cause that reasons.
VISUAL: A bright person genuinely reasoning through a choice in clear daylight, deliberate and attentive.
[CARD: freedom = acting from your own reasons, not escaping cause]

[A] The man who was pushed cannot be reasoned with about falling. The man who jumped can. That is the whole difference, and it is a real one.
VISUAL: A bright honest conversation across a clean sunlit table in daylight, reasons offered and received.

[A] So blame is not vengeance against an uncaused soul. It is the way we address a creature who can hear reasons, and be changed by them.
