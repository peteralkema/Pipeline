[A] Right up until the morning they could not get out of the car. Burnout in high performers does not look like falling apart.

[A] For a long time it looks exactly like excellence. That is what makes it so dangerous.

## PART ONE - THE MECHANISM

[A] Here is the engine. High performers do not run on energy. They run on meaning.
VISUAL: An oil lamp burning bright in a dark room, the flame strong and steady.

[A] As long as the work feels significant, they can pour out effort far past the point where an ordinary person would stop.
VISUAL: The reservoir beneath that flame, quietly running low and unseen.

[A] Meaning is the fuel that hides the cost. The problem is that meaning is borrowed against the future.
VISUAL: A ledger open on a desk, columns of neat figures under a lamp.

[A] Every late night is a small loan taken against tomorrow's reserves, repaid with interest.
VISUAL: The most recent entries in that ledger written in a fainter, more uncertain hand.

[A] For a while the books balance. The wins keep coming. And then, slowly, the meaning starts to thin.
VISUAL: A long straight road across a flat plain stretching to the horizon, heat shimmering above it.

[A] It thins for a specific reason. The reward for good work is always more work.
VISUAL: A single signpost on that road pointing only forward, no destination named.

[A] Finish the hard project and you are handed the next hard project. Over time the finish line keeps moving.
VISUAL: The horizon ahead receding, the road never arriving anywhere.

[A] And the part of you that was working toward something begins to suspect there is no something. Only more.
VISUAL: Tire tracks in dust curving back on themselves, going nowhere.

[A] This is the first crack. Not exhaustion. Disillusionment. The quiet realization that the effort no longer buys what it used to.
VISUAL: A furnace door open in a dark space, orange light spilling out, the coal pile beside it nearly gone.

[A] The work that once felt like building now feels like feeding a furnace that is never satisfied.
VISUAL: A shovel resting against the wall by that furnace, the embers low.

[A] And here is the cruel twist. A high performer's response to disillusionment is to work harder.
VISUAL: A hamster wheel turning faster and faster in a dim cage.

[A] If the meaning is fading, they reason, I must not be doing enough. The cure they reach for is the poison.
VISUAL: The blur of that wheel building speed, going precisely nowhere.

[A] Underneath it all sits the deepest engine of the trap. For many high performers, performance is who they are.
VISUAL: A figure made of light in a dark room, surrounded by glowing screens.

[A] Their worth and their output have become the same thing. So to stop, even to slow, feels like ceasing to exist.
VISUAL: Those screens going dark one by one, the figure of light dimming with each.

[A] That is why you cannot just tell a high performer to rest. Rest, to them, is not relief. It is a threat.
VISUAL: An empty beach chair at the edge of a calm sea, a closed laptop on the sand beside it.

[A] Sit them on a beach and within an hour they are anxious, because the thing that tells them they matter has been switched off.
VISUAL: The water perfectly still, the chair untouched, no one in sight.

## PART TWO - THE DISGUISE

[A] Now we can see why burnout hides so well in the capable. Each symptom wears the mask of a strength.
VISUAL: A row of theatre masks on a dark wall, each composed and confident, faintly lit from below.

[A] Cynicism arrives disguised as wisdom. The once-eager employee becomes sharp, detached, quick to see why ideas will fail.
VISUAL: A frost pattern spreading slowly across a window pane from the edges inward.

[A] It reads as experience. It is the heart pulling back to protect itself from caring about something that hurt.
VISUAL: The view beyond that frosted window growing dim and blue.

[A] Over-control arrives disguised as high standards. As reserves shrink, the burning-out performer grips tighter, trusts the team less.
VISUAL: A single hand gripping a steering wheel so tightly the knuckles whiten.

[A] Takes on more because no one else can be trusted to do it right. It is fear wearing excellence as a coat.
VISUAL: The dark interior of that car soft and out of focus around the gripping hand.

[A] Numbness arrives disguised as calm. The person stops getting upset, stops getting excited, moves through the days with a flat steadiness.
VISUAL: A flatlined audio waveform on a dark screen, a single unbroken line.

[A] Everyone says how unflappable they have become. The volume has not gone down. The wiring has gone quiet.
VISUAL: That flat line stretching across the screen where peaks once moved.

[A] And the most dangerous disguise of all. Reliability. The burning-out performer keeps delivering, on time, to standard.
VISUAL: A row of dominoes standing upright in perfect order, all still.

[A] From the outside nothing is wrong. Which is why no one intervenes, until the output stops all at once, with no warning.
VISUAL: The very first domino in that row beginning to lean past the point of return.

## PART THREE - INTERRUPTING THE SLIDE

[A] So how do you interrupt it. Not with a holiday. A holiday is a pause in the same pattern.
VISUAL: A train switching tracks at a junction, the points shifting beneath it.

[A] And the pattern resumes the moment you return. You interrupt burnout by changing the relationship between your worth and your work.
VISUAL: The new line curving away from the old toward open country.

[A] First, separate the two. This is harder than it sounds and more important than anything else here. You are not your output.
VISUAL: Two ropes braided tightly together being gently separated by hand into two strands.

[A] Practice saying it until it stops feeling like a lie. Your value as a person does not rise and fall with your last result.
VISUAL: The two separated strands laid side by side in the light, distinct.

[A] Second, learn to feel the fuel gauge. High performers override their own signals so habitually that they no longer notice them.
VISUAL: A fuel gauge needle resting just above empty, glowing faintly on a dark dashboard.

[A] Start naming the small ones. The Sunday dread. The shortened temper. The joy that has drained out of work you used to love.
VISUAL: A small warning light on that dashboard beginning to pulse.

[A] These are not weakness. They are the gauge. Third, reintroduce the word no, in small doses, deliberately, before you need it.
VISUAL: A hand calmly closing a heavy door, the gap of bright light narrowing.

[A] Not the dramatic no of the breaking point, but the ordinary no of a person with boundaries.
VISUAL: That door easing shut to a sliver, the hand steady on the frame.

[A] Every no you survive teaches your nervous system that the world does not end. That lesson is medicine.
VISUAL: The door fully closed now, calm light glowing softly around its edges.

[A] Fourth, rebuild a source of meaning the job cannot revoke. A craft, a relationship, a body that moves.
VISUAL: A potter's hands shaping wet clay on a wheel, the form rising between them.

[A] A thing you do badly and love anyway. The trap closes when work becomes your only mirror.
VISUAL: The warm out-of-focus workshop around those hands, unhurried.

[A] The escape is to be more than one thing on purpose. And finally, the reframe that saves people.
VISUAL: A long-distance runner walking slowly on an empty track at dusk, hands on hips.

[A] Slowing down is not the end of high performance. It is what makes high performance survivable.
VISUAL: That runner breathing, the finish line behind them rather than ahead.

[A] The athletes who last are not the ones who never rest. They treat rest as part of the training, not a failure of it.
VISUAL: A campfire banked low for the night, glowing embers under a layer of ash.

[A] You were never meant to be a furnace. You were meant to be a fire that can be banked at night.
VISUAL: A dark forest around that banked fire, the embers holding warm and patient.

[A] And built up again at dawn, for years. The goal is not to burn brightest. It is to still be burning when it counts. Look after the gauge.
VISUAL: The first grey light at the horizon beyond the embers, the fire ready to rise again.
