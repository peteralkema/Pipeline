## PART ONE - THE FIRST ANSWER, NATURE

[A] The oldest answer says rights are written into the nature of things. Discovered, like continents, not invented.
VISUAL: A bright clean modern globe on a pale surface in daylight, real, discoverable, and solid.

[A] The great declarations speak this way. We hold these truths to be self-evident. Not agreed. Not voted for. Simply true, whether you accept them or not.

[A] The strength of this view is its spine. If rights are discovered rather than granted, no parliament can repeal them, and no tyrant can argue them away.

[CARD: if rights are discovered, no government can repeal them]

[A] The weakness is the question that will not go away. Discovered where, exactly? Every attempt to point at them ends up pointing at us.

## PART THREE - THE THIRD ANSWER, AND THE BEST ONE

[A] There is a middle answer, and it is stronger than it sounds. Rights are not found in nature. Nor are they arbitrary. They are what respect for persons requires.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary person framed with evident dignity in clear daylight, whole and unmistakable.

[A] Look at a human being honestly. Someone who can suffer. Who has a life that is theirs. Who can be humiliated, and who knows it.
VISUAL: A bright honest portrait of an ordinary modern person in clean daylight, present and real, warm.

[A] Given such a creature, certain treatments are simply incompatible with taking them seriously at all. Torture is not forbidden by decree. It is what taking a person seriously rules out.

[CARD: rights are what taking a person seriously rules out]

[A] So rights are neither floating in nature nor invented at whim. They are the shape that respect takes, once you concede that this creature is a someone.
VISUAL: A bright clean shape forming naturally around a human figure in clear daylight, fitted and sure.

[A] Which explains the history. Rights expanded, painfully, exactly as the circle of who counted as a someone expanded. The rights were always implied. The recognition was what was missing.
