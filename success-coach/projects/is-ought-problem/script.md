[A] Here it is. You cannot logically get from what is, to what ought to be. From a fact, to a value. The gap cannot be crossed by reason alone.

## PART ONE - THE GAP ITSELF

[A] Start with the two kinds of statement. First, statements of fact. Water boils at a certain temperature. The population is growing. This is how things are.
VISUAL: A bright clean set of simple measured readings on a pale modern surface, precise and factual, daylight.

[A] Second, statements of value. You ought to keep your promise. This is unjust. That was wrong. These are not descriptions. They are judgments.
VISUAL: A clean bright card holding a single value-laden line, warm and distinct from the cold readings, sunlit.

[A] Hume's point is that no pile of the first kind, however large, ever adds up to the second. Facts describe. They never, on their own, command.
VISUAL: A bright tall stack of plain factual cards on one ledge, and a clear gap to a single value card across the way.
[CARD: facts describe - they never, alone, command]

[A] Between the world as it is, and the world as it should be, there is a gap. And logic alone builds no bridge across it.
VISUAL: The bright clean gap again between two sunlit ledges, empty and clear, no bridge spanning it.

## PART TWO - HOW THE TRICK IS HIDDEN

[A] Now watch how often we cross that gap without noticing. Someone says, this is natural, therefore it is good. Do you hear the leap?
VISUAL: A bright clean arrow leaping across the gap, but drawn faint and uncertain, sunlit and questionable.

[A] Natural is a fact. Good is a value. Plenty of natural things are terrible, and plenty of good things are artificial. The word therefore did all the sneaking.
VISUAL: A bright modern scene of ordinary life, natural and built things side by side in clear daylight.
[CARD: "natural, therefore good" - the hidden leap]

[A] Or this. The science shows what humans are like, therefore this is how we should live. Again, a fact, then a silent jump to a value.
VISUAL: A bright clean data readout on a pale wall, and beside it a value claim, the gap between them clear.

[A] Hume is not saying values are worthless. He is saying they cannot be smuggled out of facts alone. Somewhere, a value must be openly added. The honest move is to say so.
VISUAL: A bright open hand placing a single clear value card deliberately onto the far ledge, sunlit and honest.

## PART THREE - WHY THIS IS NOT DESPAIR

[A] Some people hear this and panic. If facts cannot give us values, are values just made up? Is it all arbitrary after all?
VISUAL: A bright figure pausing at the edge of the gap, uncertain, but standing in clear open daylight.

[A] No. Hume is not destroying morality. He is showing us its true shape. Values are real, but they enter through a different door than facts do.
VISUAL: A bright clean building with two distinct doorways in daylight, both open, both admitting light.

[A] We reason about values through other values. Fairness, dignity, harm, care. Facts inform those judgments richly. But a starting value must always be there.
VISUAL: A bright clean web of connected points on a pale surface, values linking to values, sunlit and ordered.
[CARD: values are reasoned from other values, informed by facts]

[A] So the lesson is not give up. It is be honest. Name the values you are standing on. Do not pretend the facts forced your hand when they did not.
VISUAL: A single figure standing openly and honestly on clear bright ground, values named beneath their feet.

## PART FIVE - THE MACHINE THAT ONLY KNOWS FACTS

[A] Now here is why this old gap has suddenly become one of the most urgent problems in technology. An artificial intelligence is, at its core, a machine of facts.
VISUAL: A bright modern screen in clean daylight showing calm ordered data, precise and factual.

[A] It can learn every pattern in the world as it is. Every regularity, every correlation, every trend. But none of that tells it what ought to be.
VISUAL: A bright vast field of ordered data points on a pale surface, immense but silent on value, daylight.

[A] The values have to be put in, deliberately, by people. And if we forget that, the machine will quietly treat the world as it is as the world as it should be.

[CARD: the machine learns what IS - someone must supply the OUGHT]

[A] It will learn our past biases as if they were laws of nature. It will mistake what happens for what should happen. The is-ought gap is now an engineering warning.
