## COLD OPEN

[A] There is a particular kind of person who achieves everything they set out to achieve, and feels nothing at the summit.
VISUAL: A figure standing alone at the top of a long staircase at dawn, looking back down.

[A] The promotion lands. The number is hit. The thing they chased for years is finally in their hands.
VISUAL: An empty grey sky ahead of that figure, the climb finished behind them.

[A] And a quiet voice asks, is this it. Carl Jung spent his life studying that voice.
VISUAL: A grand empty study lined with old books, a single leather chair facing a window.

[A] Jung, the Swiss psychiatrist, watched the most successful people of his era arrive in his consulting room.
VISUAL: Dust turning slowly in a shaft of afternoon light across that quiet study.

[A] Not broken by failure, but hollowed out by success. They had won the game and discovered, too late, they had played the wrong one.
VISUAL: A mountain peak emerging from cloud, and behind it, only just visible, a higher peak.

[A] Our culture has an answer for the emptiness. Want more. Set a bigger goal. Optimize harder.
VISUAL: The range of peaks receding endlessly behind the first, each higher than the last.

[A] Jung's answer was the opposite, and it is one of the most useful ideas you will ever sit with.
VISUAL: A tall ladder leaning against a brick wall in an empty courtyard, climbing to nowhere.

[A] We will walk through what he understood about ambition, before you spend thirty years climbing a ladder leaning against the wrong wall.
VISUAL: The top rung of that ladder resting against blank stone, no window above it.

## PART ONE - THE FIRST HALF OF LIFE

[A] Jung divided a life into two great movements. The first half is for building the ego.
VISUAL: A young tree growing fast and straight toward the sun in an open field.

[A] You leave home, you prove yourself, you earn status, you establish your place in the world. Ambition here is healthy and necessary. You are supposed to climb.
VISUAL: The thin eager branches of that tree reaching upward into the light.

[A] But the goals of the first half of life are largely handed to you. By your parents, your culture, the people you want to impress.
VISUAL: A child's hand-drawn picture of a trophy pinned to a wall, faded with age.

[A] You absorb a picture of success before you are old enough to question it. And then you spend years chasing a target you never actually chose.
VISUAL: The real trophy beside that drawing, identical, gathering dust on a shelf.

[A] This is the first thing success culture gets wrong. It treats the inherited goal as if it were your own.
VISUAL: A mask of a confident, smiling face held up on a stick before a darkened figure.

[A] It never asks the prior question. Whose definition of success are you running on. If you have never examined it, the answer is almost certainly not yours.
VISUAL: The real expression behind that mask unreadable in shadow.

[A] Jung called the polished, public version of yourself the persona. The mask you wear to meet the world's expectations.
VISUAL: A theatre stage seen from the wings, a single actor in a spotlight.

[A] A persona is useful. The trouble begins when you forget it is a mask, and mistake the role for the self.
VISUAL: That actor playing to a dark, empty house, the seats stretching into nothing.

[A] You become so good at performing the successful person that you lose contact with the actual one.
VISUAL: The spotlight tight on the performer, everything beyond it invisible.

## PART TWO - THE SHADOW AND THE COST

[A] Here is where Jung's idea cuts deepest. Everything you exile in order to succeed does not disappear. It goes underground.
VISUAL: A figure standing in bright light casting a long dark shadow across the ground.

[A] The softness you suppressed to look tough. The doubt you buried to seem certain. The creativity you abandoned because it did not pay.
VISUAL: That shadow far larger and more detailed than the person casting it.

[A] Jung called this hidden region the shadow. It is not evil. It is simply everything about you that did not fit the persona.
VISUAL: A storeroom behind a polished showroom, crowded with covered furniture.

[A] And the more single-minded your ambition, the larger your shadow grows. Every part you set aside to win goes there to wait.
VISUAL: A thin line of light under the door connecting that storeroom to the showroom.

[A] The shadow does not wait quietly forever. What we refuse to face does not stay buried. It returns.
VISUAL: A calm reservoir behind a concrete dam, the water level high and pressing.

[A] As a sudden, baffling restlessness at the peak of success. As an affair, an addiction, a self-sabotage that wrecks the thing you built.
VISUAL: A single hairline crack beginning to thread across the grey wall of that dam.

[A] As a depression that no achievement can lift. This is the cost success culture never mentions. It sells the climb and stays silent about the bill.
VISUAL: An ornate invoice lying on a marble floor in an opulent empty hall.

[A] It tells you the emptiness at the top means you have not done enough. Jung would say you climbed away from yourself.
VISUAL: The chandelier above that invoice bright and cold, the hall deserted.

[A] And the self you abandoned is now sending the invoice.
VISUAL: The figure named on that page unreadable, the ink faded.

## PART THREE - INDIVIDUATION, THE BETTER AMBITION

[A] So what did Jung offer instead. Not the abandonment of ambition, but its redirection. He called it individuation.
VISUAL: A river that ran dead straight through engineered channels reaching open country.

[A] The slow, demanding work of becoming who you actually are, rather than who you were told to be.
VISUAL: That river beginning to curve into its own natural course across the land.

[A] Individuation begins with an honest reckoning with the shadow. Not to indulge it, but to know it.
VISUAL: A figure standing before a tall mirror in dim light, looking directly into it.

[A] To look at the parts you exiled and ask what they were trying to tell you. The ruthless striver may find a frightened child underneath.
VISUAL: The reflection meeting that gaze without flinching.

[A] The tireless achiever may find a person who was never allowed to rest. The second move is to reclaim what you abandoned.
VISUAL: A house at dusk with many windows, lights coming on one by one in different rooms.

[A] The creativity, the relationships, the curiosity you sacrificed on the altar of advancement. Not to quit your work, but to let your life become wider than your work.
VISUAL: The whole house growing warm and alive against the night.

[A] A self with only one room is easy to lose. A self with many is hard to break. And the third move reorders everything.
VISUAL: A single path branching off from a wide crowded highway, narrow and quiet.

[A] Shift your central question from how high can I climb to what is mine to do. The first question has no end. The second has an answer.
VISUAL: That quiet path leading toward distant hills lit by a low golden sun.

[A] And the answer is yours alone, and when you find it, the emptiness at the summit closes over.
VISUAL: An old craftsman's workbench at golden hour, a single finished object resting on it.

[A] Jung was not against achievement. He achieved enormously. He was against the unexamined achievement that costs a person their soul and calls it winning.
VISUAL: The tools on that bench laid down, the work complete and unhurried.

[A] The ambition he respected was the hardest kind. The ambition to become a whole person, undivided, at home in your own life.
VISUAL: A lone figure walking a mountain ridge at sunrise, the landscape opening gold on either side.

[A] So before you set the next goal, sit with the older question. Whose success am I chasing. What have I exiled to chase it.
VISUAL: The path along the ridge clear and unhurried beneath that figure's feet.

[A] And what, if I were honest, is actually mine to do. Answer those, and ambition stops being a treadmill and becomes a path. Climb, but climb toward yourself.
VISUAL: The figure continuing along the bright ridge, the sun rising vast ahead of them.
