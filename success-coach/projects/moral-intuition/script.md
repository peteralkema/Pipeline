## PART ONE - THE UNCOMFORTABLE EVIDENCE

[A] Psychologists have run a cruel little experiment on all of us. They describe an act that is harmless but disgusting, and watch what happens.
VISUAL: A bright modern research room in clean daylight, orderly notes on a clean table, precise and calm.

[A] Nobody is hurt. Nothing is risked. And still, people declare it wrong. Then they reach for a reason. And when each reason is answered, they reach for another.
VISUAL: A bright person searching honestly for a justification in clear daylight, sincere and unsuccessful.

[A] Eventually many simply say, I cannot explain it, but I know it is wrong. The researchers call this moral dumbfounding, and it is uncomfortably familiar.

[CARD: the verdict came first. the reasons were recruited after.]

[A] The unsettling conclusion is that the verdict came first. The reasons were recruited afterwards, to defend a judgment already made. Reason as lawyer, not judge.

## PART TWO - WHY THE GUT IS OFTEN RIGHT ANYWAY

[A] But do not throw the gut away. It is not noise. It is compressed experience, and it has done extraordinary moral work.

## PART THREE - HOW TO TELL THEM APART

[A] So here is the practical question. Given a strong moral feeling, how do you know whether it is wisdom or prejudice?
VISUAL: A bright clean pair of nearly identical objects on a pale surface in daylight, one real, one false.

[A] The first test. Can you name a victim? Genuine moral wrongness almost always involves somebody harmed, deceived, degraded, or used. If nobody is harmed, be suspicious of your certainty.
VISUAL: A bright honest scene in clean daylight, a clearly harmed person, plain and undeniable.
[CARD: test one - can you name who is harmed?]

[A] The second test. Does the feeling track disgust rather than injury? Disgust evolved to keep us from disease, not to identify injustice. It is a spectacularly unreliable moral guide.
VISUAL: A bright clean modern kitchen in daylight, ordinary hygiene, unremarkable and morally silent.

[A] Much of history's cruelty ran on disgust. Toward the foreign, the ill, the different. It felt exactly as certain as your best convictions feel to you now.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary diverse street in clear daylight, all of them plainly and equally human.

[A] The third test. Would your intuition survive knowing more? Prejudice fades under acquaintance. Genuine moral perception grows sharper the closer you look.
VISUAL: A bright clean lens in daylight, an image growing clearer rather than dissolving, precise.

## PART FOUR - THE PARTNERSHIP

[A] So the answer is not gut over reason, nor reason over gut. It is a partnership, and each has a specific job.
VISUAL: A bright clean pair of tools working together on a pale surface in daylight, distinct and matched.

[A] The gut proposes. It notices, instantly, that something is wrong here. Without it you would walk past every injustice, waiting to be argued into caring.
VISUAL: A bright person noticing something wrong immediately in clear daylight, alert and responsive.

[A] Reason examines. It asks who is harmed, checks whether the feeling is disgust in disguise, and looks for the person your instinct might be excluding.
VISUAL: A bright honest examination of a strong feeling in clean daylight, careful and unhurried.
[CARD: the gut proposes. reason examines. neither rules alone.]

[A] Neither rules alone. A person of pure instinct is a bigot with confidence. A person of pure reasoning can be argued into anything at all.
VISUAL: A bright balanced figure holding instinct and argument together in clear daylight, steady and calm.

## PART FIVE - TEACHING A MACHINE OUR INTUITIONS

[A] Now something genuinely new. We are training systems on the accumulated moral judgments of humanity. Our writing, our verdicts, our rules, our unexamined reactions.
VISUAL: A bright modern archive of ordered human text in clean daylight, vast, orderly, and inherited.

[A] A model learns our intuitions with perfect fidelity. Including the ones that were wisdom. And including the ones that were only disgust, and habit, and who we happened to fear.
VISUAL: A bright clean pattern in daylight faithfully reproducing everything it was shown, precise.

[A] And unlike us, it has no dumbfounding. It will supply a fluent reason for any inherited verdict, instantly, without ever noticing that the reason came second.
VISUAL: A bright clean screen in daylight producing a confident, articulate, and unexamined answer.
[CARD: it inherits the verdict - and manufactures the reason]

[A] Which is the deepest risk in machine ethics. Not a system that reasons badly. A system that has absorbed our prejudices and can defend every one of them eloquently.
VISUAL: A bright meeting room, a diverse team around a clean table, a hard plain question on the glass.

[A] It will feel, to us, exactly like moral common sense. Because it is ours, reflected back, with all the examination left out.
VISUAL: A bright clean reflective surface in daylight, returning our own face without a single question.
