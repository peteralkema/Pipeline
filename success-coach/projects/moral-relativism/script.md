## PART ONE - WHY IT IS TEMPTING

[A] Relativism grows from a real and honest observation. Across the world and across history, people have disagreed about morality, deeply and sincerely.
VISUAL: A bright modern world map on a clean pale wall, warmly lit, open and expansive.

[A] What one culture honours, another forbids. What one century condemned, the next celebrates. The variety is real, and it is humbling.
VISUAL: A bright clean timeline stretching across a pale surface, simple markers along it, sunlit and modern.

[A] And relativism draws a kind and cautious lesson from it. Who are we to judge others? Better to be tolerant. Better to keep our certainties small.
VISUAL: A bright open hand extended in a welcoming gesture, warm and calm, clear daylight.

[A] There is something genuinely good in that instinct. Humility about our own blind spots is a virtue. Many horrors have come from moral arrogance.

## PART TWO - THE CRACK

[A] But now press gently on the idea, and watch it begin to crack. Because relativism does not just ask for tolerance. It makes a much bigger claim.
VISUAL: A bright clean pane of glass in daylight showing the faint start of a single crack, high-key and precise.

[A] It says no moral view is truer than any other. And that claim quietly destroys the very tolerance it was meant to protect.
VISUAL: The bright crack spreading a little further across the clean glass, sunlit and stark.

[A] Because if no view is truer than another, then tolerance is not truer than cruelty. Kindness is not truer than harm. They are just different preferences.

[CARD: if nothing is truer, tolerance is no better than cruelty]

[A] The relativist wanted to say, respect other ways of living. But the logic forces them to say, no way of living is any better. And those are not the same.

[A] Almost every person feels it immediately. That is not wrong for me. It is wrong. Fully, everywhere, for everyone. No culture's approval could make it right.

[CARD: some things are wrong everywhere - not just wrong "for us"]
