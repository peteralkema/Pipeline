## PART ONE - THE CHALLENGE, PROPERLY MADE

[A] Glaucon's argument is stronger than it first appears, and he makes it deliberately as strong as he can, before asking Socrates to break it.
VISUAL: A bright modern colonnade in clean daylight, open and spacious, ideal for a serious argument.

[A] He says justice began as a compromise. It is best to do wrong and never suffer it. Worst to suffer wrong and be unable to repay it.
VISUAL: A bright clean balance in daylight, best on one side, worst on the other, honest and stark.

[A] So people who could not reliably win agreed to a bargain. I will not harm you, if you will not harm me. Justice is that treaty, and nothing more.
VISUAL: A bright clean handshake between two ordinary people in clear daylight, practical and unromantic.
[CARD: justice as a treaty between people who might lose]

[A] Which means nobody keeps it willingly. We keep it because we are being watched, and because breaking it costs. Give any of us the ring, and the treaty ends.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary person alone and unobserved in clean daylight, entirely unaccountable, calm.

[A] To sharpen it, Glaucon imagines a perfectly just man believed by everyone to be wicked, and a perfectly wicked man believed by everyone to be good. Which life would you choose?
VISUAL: A bright clean pair of ordinary lives side by side in daylight, one honoured, one despised.

## PART TWO - THE ANSWERS THAT FAIL

[A] Notice first how many familiar answers simply do not survive the ring.
VISUAL: A bright clean set of answers laid out on a pale surface in daylight, orderly and about to fall.

[A] You will be caught eventually. Not with the ring, you will not. That answer concedes the whole argument. It says goodness is caution.
VISUAL: A bright unwatched modern street in clear daylight, empty of observers, ordinary and open.

[A] Society will collapse if everyone did it. Perhaps. But you are not everyone. The ring gives you an exception, and your single exception costs society almost nothing.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary functioning modern city in clean daylight, entirely unharmed by one exception.
[CARD: "if everyone did it" - but the ring makes you the exception]

[A] Your conscience will punish you. Will it? Or would it fade, the way discomfort fades, once the third or fourth invisible theft passed without consequence?
VISUAL: A bright clean smooth surface in daylight, a mark upon it, gradually and quietly weathering away.

[A] Every one of these answers keeps goodness instrumental. Good because it pays. Which means, when it stops paying, goodness has run out of reasons.
VISUAL: A bright clean transaction in daylight, honest, mutual, and entirely conditional, precise.

## PART THREE - PLATO'S REPLY

[A] Plato's answer is unfashionable and, I think, correct. He says the wicked man with the ring has not got away with anything at all. He has already suffered the worst of it.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary person in clean daylight, powerful and successful and visibly unwell within.

[A] Because a person is not a single thing. There is what you want, what you feel, and what you judge to be right. A good life is these three in working order together.
VISUAL: A bright clean set of three parts working in harmony on a pale surface in daylight, ordered.

[A] The unjust man is at war with himself. His appetite rules, his reason serves it as a clever lawyer, and the part of him that knows better is silenced but never removed.
VISUAL: A bright clean mechanism in daylight, powerful and misaligned, running hard and grinding itself.
[CARD: injustice is not a crime you commit - it is a state you enter]

[A] So injustice is not a payment you make and pocket the profit. It is a condition you enter. The tyrant is not a happy man who cheated. He is a divided man who cannot rest.
VISUAL: A bright modern room in clean daylight, comfortable, expensive, and containing no peace.

[A] Justice, on this view, is not a tax you pay for social life. It is what a person in good working order looks like from the outside.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary integrated person in clear daylight, calm, coherent, and quietly at ease.

## PART FOUR - DOES THE REPLY HOLD

[A] Be honest about the weakness. Some deeply unjust people appear entirely at peace. Plato's answer requires that their inner life is worse than it looks, and we cannot verify that.

[A] But test it against something you can check. Yourself. Think of a time you did something small and dishonest that nobody discovered.

[A] Most people find that the memory does not sit comfortably. Not because they fear discovery. Because they became, briefly, someone they would rather not be.

[CARD: the cost was never being caught. it was becoming.]

[A] That discomfort is the evidence. It is the sound of a person who values something beyond consequences, arguing with a story that says they should not.

[A] And now the reason this ancient story has stopped being hypothetical. We have handed a version of the ring to nearly everyone.
