## COLD OPEN

[A] A ship returns from a long voyage. One of its planks is rotten, so the crew replace it. The ship sails on. Obviously still the same ship.
VISUAL: A bright wooden vessel moored in clear sunlit water, clean and well kept, warm daylight.

[A] The next voyage, another plank is replaced. Then a mast. Then the deck. Year after year, piece by piece, until not one original fragment remains.
VISUAL: A bright dockside in clean daylight, fresh timber replacing old, orderly and calm, sunlit.

[A] Is it still the same ship? Every single part is new. And yet it never stopped being itself, not for one moment, not on any single day.
VISUAL: The same bright vessel on sunlit water, entirely renewed, yet identical in form, high-key and clear.
[CARD: every plank replaced - same ship, or a different one?]

[A] Now a harder turn. Suppose someone had quietly kept every old plank, and reassembled them all in a warehouse. Which vessel is the real ship of Theseus?
VISUAL: A bright open warehouse in clean daylight, weathered timbers reassembled into a familiar shape, sunlit.

[A] This is not a game about boats. It is a question about you. Because you are doing exactly the same thing, right now, in this moment.
VISUAL: The channel balance-scale mark, bright petrol and amber, clean and still, generous white space.

## PART ONE - YOU ARE THE SHIP

[A] Almost every cell in your body will be replaced across your lifetime. The atoms that make you are borrowed, briefly, and returned to the world.
VISUAL: A bright modern portrait of an ordinary person in clear daylight, whole and calm, warmly lit.

[A] The person in your oldest photograph shares almost no physical material with you. Not the skin. Not the blood. Barely anything at all.
VISUAL: A bright clean photograph on a pale surface in daylight, a much younger face, sunlit and modern.

[A] And it is not only your body. Your beliefs have shifted. Your tastes have turned over. Some memories have faded past recovery.
VISUAL: A bright clean series of small shapes changing gently across a pale surface, sunlit and shifting.
[CARD: body, beliefs, memories - all replaced over a life]

[A] So in what sense are you the same person who lived that childhood? Every plank has been changed. And yet you feel, unshakeably, that you are still you.
VISUAL: A bright figure standing in clear daylight, certain of themselves, calm and continuous.

## PART TWO - THE FIRST ANSWER, CONTINUITY

[A] Here is one answer, and it is a good one. What makes the ship the same ship is not its material. It is the unbroken continuity of its existence.
VISUAL: A bright unbroken line drawn steadily across a clean pale surface in daylight, continuous and sure.

[A] The ship was always there, being repaired, sailing on, never ceasing to be a ship. The reassembled planks in the warehouse have no such history. They stopped.
VISUAL: A bright vessel sailing on in sunlit water, its wake unbroken behind it, clear and continuous.

[A] By this view, you are you because there is an unbroken thread running from that child to this moment. Not the same stuff. The same continuing story.
VISUAL: A bright clean thread running through many small changing forms, whole and unbroken, sunlit.
[CARD: identity = unbroken continuity, not unchanged material]

[A] It is a beautiful answer. Identity is not a thing you have. It is a process you are. A river is never the same water, and always the same river.
VISUAL: A bright clear river flowing steadily under daylight, always moving, always itself, high-key.

## PART THREE - THE SECOND ANSWER, MEMORY

[A] The philosopher John Locke offered a different thread. What makes you the same person, he said, is memory. The chain of remembering that links you backwards.
VISUAL: A bright clean chain of small linked forms across a pale surface in daylight, each one holding the last.

[A] You are the child in the photograph because you can, however faintly, remember being them. The connection is not physical. It is the reach of your own recollection.
VISUAL: A bright figure looking warmly at an old photograph in clean daylight, connected across the years.

[A] But test it. If a man cannot remember a crime he committed decades ago, is he the same man who committed it? Our courts say yes. Locke's theory hesitates.
VISUAL: A bright modern courtroom in clean daylight, orderly and calm, the question hanging in bright air.
[CARD: if memory is identity - what of what we have forgotten?]

[A] And memory is unreliable. It edits, invents, and quietly rewrites. If it is the thread of the self, the thread is thinner than we would like.
VISUAL: A bright clean thread with faint gaps and knots in daylight, holding, but imperfectly, sunlit.

## PART FOUR - WHY WE ARGUE ABOUT SHIPS

[A] Here is the deep suspicion the puzzle plants. Perhaps there is no single fact about whether it is the same ship. Perhaps the question is not asking anything precise.
VISUAL: A bright open space in clean daylight, two answers coexisting comfortably, calm and unforced.

[A] Ship is a word we invented for a useful pattern. Identity may be the same. Not a hidden essence, but a convenient way of tracking a thing across time.
VISUAL: A bright clean pattern holding its shape while all its pieces shift, sunlit and coherent.
[CARD: identity may be a useful pattern, not a hidden essence]

[A] That sounds deflating. But look again. It means you are not a fixed object slowly wearing out. You are a shape that persists, endlessly renewed. That is remarkable.
VISUAL: A bright flame-like or wave-like form holding its shape while its substance flows through, daylight.

## PART FIVE - THE COPY IN THE MACHINE

[A] Now the puzzle arrives in our century, and it stops being abstract. We are learning to copy patterns. Voices. Faces. Ways of speaking. Perhaps, one day, minds.
VISUAL: A bright modern screen in clean daylight showing a calm familiar pattern, precise and uncluttered.

[A] Suppose a system learned everything about a person, and could speak in their voice, with their memories, their turns of phrase. Is that person still there?
VISUAL: A bright clean space in daylight where a familiar shape is reproduced, faithful yet somehow other.

[A] The old planks are gone. The pattern remains. By the continuity answer, this is not that person. By the memory answer, it unnervingly might be.
VISUAL: Two bright vessels side by side in clean daylight, identical in form, different in history, sunlit.
[CARD: the pattern survives - but is anyone home?]

[A] And a harder question waits beneath it. If we copy a person's pattern, do we owe the copy anything? Have we made a tool, or something with a claim on us?
VISUAL: A bright meeting room, a diverse team around a clean table, a human question sketched on glass, daylight.

## PART SIX - THE WEIGHT YOU CARRY OUT

[A] So when do you stop being you? Perhaps the honest answer is that you never were a fixed thing that could stop. You were always a pattern in motion.
VISUAL: The balance-scale mark, clean and level, bright petrol and amber, steady and calm.

[A] The planks will keep changing. Your body, your certainties, even your memories. And still, something holds. A shape. A direction. A continuing story you are writing.
VISUAL: A bright river flowing steadily under clear daylight, always new, always itself, expansive.

[A] Which means you are not stuck with who you were. You are being rebuilt, plank by plank, by everything you choose. That is not a loss. It is a freedom.
VISUAL: A bright figure walking forward with purpose into clear open daylight, renewed and continuous.

[A] And as we build machines that can copy our patterns, we will need to know, urgently, what we think a person really is. The ship has come back to harbour.
VISUAL: The balance-scale mark, level and firm, bright, the amber dot glowing steady at its centre.

[A] I am glad you thought it through with me. There is always more to weigh. And we will weigh it together, here.
