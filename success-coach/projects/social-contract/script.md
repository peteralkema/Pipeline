## PART ONE - THE BARGAIN

[A] Thomas Hobbes began with a bleak picture. Strip away all authority, and life becomes a war of everyone against everyone. Nasty, brutish, short.
VISUAL: A bright empty landscape in clear daylight, open and lawless, no road, no boundary, sunlit.

[A] So rational people would trade. Give up some freedom to a sovereign power, and receive in return the one thing freedom alone cannot buy. Safety.
VISUAL: A bright clean balance in daylight, liberty on one pan, security on the other, evenly weighed.
[CARD: trade some liberty, receive security - the original bargain]

[A] John Locke softened it, and improved it enormously. The contract is not a surrender. It is a trust. Government holds power on condition it protects life, liberty and property.
VISUAL: A bright modern civic building in clean daylight, open doors, orderly and accountable, warm.

[A] And if it breaks that condition, the trust dissolves. Locke's contract contains, quietly, the right of the people to remove a government that has failed them.
VISUAL: A bright open public square in clear daylight, ordinary citizens present and unafraid, sunlit.

## PART TWO - THE PROBLEM OF THE SIGNATURE

[A] But look closely at the bargain, and something is missing. Nobody ever signed it. Not you. Not your parents. Nobody alive.
VISUAL: A bright clean signature line on a pale document in daylight, entirely empty, precise and telling.

[A] The classic reply is tacit consent. By staying, by using the roads, by accepting the protection, you have consented in practice, if not in words.

[A] David Hume demolished this with a single image. A man carried onto a ship in his sleep, waking at sea, and told he freely consented by not leaping into the ocean.

[CARD: you consented by not leaping into the ocean]

[A] Leaving your country is not a free choice for most people alive. Consent that costs you everything to withhold is not really consent at all.

## PART THREE - WHAT SURVIVES

[A] So does the contract collapse? Not quite. It survives by changing what it claims to be.
VISUAL: A bright clean structure in daylight, rebuilt on a different footing, sound and honest, sunlit.

[A] Modern thinkers stopped saying you actually agreed. They ask instead a sharper question. Would rules like these be agreed to, by reasonable people, if they were choosing fairly?

[CARD: not "did you agree?" but "could this be agreed to, fairly?"]

[A] That is the version with real force. It does not pretend you signed. It asks whether the arrangement could be justified to every person living under it.
VISUAL: A bright clean set of rules laid open on a pale surface in daylight, legible to everyone present.

[A] And it gives you a test. A law you could not possibly justify to the person it burdens most is not a contract. It is simply a command.

## PART FOUR - THE OBLIGATION THAT REMAINS

[A] So why obey? Not because you signed. Because the arrangement, imperfect as it is, holds up something you rely on entirely.

[A] Every promise you keep, every queue you join, every unlocked door depends on nearly everyone else doing the same. Obedience is a contribution to a shared thing.

[CARD: your obedience is a contribution to something that holds us all]

[A] Which also tells you when disobedience is right. Not when the law inconveniences you. When the arrangement itself has stopped being justifiable to those it governs.
