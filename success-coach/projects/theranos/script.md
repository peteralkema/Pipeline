## PART ONE - THE PHRASE THAT MADE IT POSSIBLE

[A] Silicon Valley has a saying. Fake it till you make it. Say the thing is real, and then race to make it real before anyone checks.
VISUAL: A clean bright card on a pale surface holding one line of text, editorial and modern, sunlit.

[A] And here is the awkward truth. Very often, that works. The demo is smoke and mirrors, the team builds furiously, and by launch the promise has become real.
VISUAL: A bright modern workshop in clean daylight, a team building rapidly and honestly, energetic.

[A] Most great products were, at some early moment, a confident claim ahead of a working thing. That gap between promise and reality is where innovation lives.
VISUAL: A bright clean gap between a sketch and a finished object on a pale surface in daylight, sunlit.
[CARD: every startup lives in the gap between promise and reality]

[A] So the ethical question is not whether the gap is permitted. It is what you may put inside it. And that turns out to depend entirely on who is standing underneath.
VISUAL: A bright clean space beneath a promise, and beneath it a real ordinary person, clear daylight.

## PART TWO - WHERE THE LINE ACTUALLY SITS

[A] If your unfinished product disappoints an investor, they lose money they chose to risk. That is the deal they signed up for. The gap is theirs to bear.
VISUAL: A bright modern boardroom in clean daylight, informed adults weighing a known risk, calm.

[A] If your unfinished photo app crashes, a user loses an afternoon. Annoying. Recoverable. Nobody is harmed in any way that lasts.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary person shrugging at a modern screen in daylight, mildly inconvenienced, calm.

[A] But if your unfinished blood test tells a healthy woman she is losing her pregnancy, or tells a sick man he is well, the gap has closed on someone who never consented to stand in it.
VISUAL: A bright clean clinic room in daylight, a patient waiting calmly, trusting entirely, dignified.
[CARD: the line is drawn where the risk lands on someone who never agreed]

[A] That is the line. Not between honesty and hype. Between risk you carry, and risk you silently transfer to somebody who trusts you.
VISUAL: A bright clean balance in daylight, risk shifting from one pan to an unsuspecting other, stark.

[A] Theranos did not merely exaggerate. It moved the risk onto sick people, and told them nothing.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary person holding a plain result in clear daylight, trusting it completely, calm.

## PART THREE - HOW GOOD PEOPLE STAYED

[A] Now the part that should trouble you most. The company was full of capable, decent scientists. Many knew something was badly wrong. Most said nothing for a long time.
VISUAL: A bright modern laboratory in clean daylight, capable people at clean benches, focused and quiet.

[A] Why? Because the story was intoxicating, and because doubt was reframed as disloyalty. To question the device was to reveal that you did not believe in the mission.
VISUAL: A bright inspiring vision on a clean wall in daylight, warm, compelling, hard to argue against.

[A] And because every individual step was small. This demo will be pre-recorded, just this once. This sample will run on a commercial machine, just for now.
VISUAL: A bright clean series of small reasonable compromises on a pale surface in daylight, each modest.
[CARD: no single step was the crime - the sequence was]

[A] No one morning did anyone decide to defraud the sick. They agreed to a series of temporary measures, each defensible, arriving together at something monstrous.
VISUAL: A bright clean path of small even steps arriving somewhere unintended in daylight, sunlit.

[A] Then a young employee, Tyler Shultz, told the truth. He faced lawyers, pressure, and the fury of his own family's connection to the board. He was right, and it cost him dearly.
VISUAL: A bright honest young figure standing alone in clear daylight, calm, principled, and costly.

## PART FOUR - THE CULTURE THAT PRODUCES IT

[A] Theranos is not a story about one unusual person. It is a story about a culture with a missing part.
VISUAL: A bright modern building in clean daylight, impressive, but with a plain gap in its structure.

[A] The culture rewarded conviction and punished doubt. It treated secrecy as protection of genius rather than as a shield against scrutiny.
VISUAL: A bright closed clean door in daylight, quiet and firm, keeping questions politely outside.

[A] And it confused two very different things. The courage to promise something hard. And the honesty to say, out loud, that it does not work yet.
VISUAL: A bright clean pair of distinct objects on a pale surface in daylight, easily confused, precise.
[CARD: courage to promise = honesty about what does not yet work]

[A] A healthy organisation can hold both. Believe fiercely in the vision. And tell the plain truth about the machine on the bench today.
VISUAL: A bright honest team examining a working prototype together in clear daylight, candid and calm.

## PART FIVE - THE SAME GAP, NOW WITH AI

[A] Now look around. We are living through another season of extraordinary promises. Systems described as capable of things they cannot reliably do.
VISUAL: A bright modern office in clean daylight, a confident demonstration on a clean screen, impressive.

[A] A demo is polished. A capability is implied. And somewhere downstream, a real person is relying on the output. A patient. A defendant. A job applicant.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary person receiving a machine's answer in clean daylight, trusting it entirely.

[A] Ask the Theranos question of every such system. Who is standing underneath the gap between what this promises and what it actually does?
VISUAL: A bright clean gap in daylight, and beneath it, plainly, an ordinary trusting human being.
[CARD: who is standing underneath the gap?]

[A] If the answer is an investor, that is a business risk. If the answer is a sick person who was never told, the name for it is not optimism. It has an older name.
VISUAL: A bright meeting room, a diverse team around a clean table, a hard plain question on the glass.

## PART SIX - THE WEIGHT YOU CARRY OUT

[A] So what do you take from Theranos? Not that ambition is dangerous. We need people who promise things that do not exist yet. That is how anything is built.
VISUAL: The balance-scale mark, clean and level, bright petrol and amber, steady and serious.

[A] And take the sequence. Nobody arrives at fraud in one step. They arrive by many small temporary measures, each of which seemed forgivable at the time.

[A] So the question to ask yourself is not, would I commit a great wrong. It is, would I notice, on the morning I took the third small step.
