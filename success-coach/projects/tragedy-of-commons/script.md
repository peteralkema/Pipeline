## PART TWO - THE FAMOUS SOLUTION, AND WHY IT IS HALF WRONG

[A] Garrett Hardin, who popularised the phrase, saw only two exits. Divide the commons into private property, or place it under a coercive central authority.
VISUAL: A bright clean field in daylight, divided by neat modern fences, orderly and precise, sunlit.

[A] Privatise it, and each owner bears the cost of their own overuse. Or regulate it, and let the state limit what each may take.
VISUAL: A bright modern civic building in clean daylight, orderly and official, calm and authoritative.
[CARD: privatise it, or police it - Hardin saw no third way]

[A] Both work, sometimes. Both fail badly, often. Property lines cannot be drawn around a fishery or the sky. And distant regulators frequently do not know what local people know.
VISUAL: A bright open sea under clear daylight, unfenced and unfenceable, wide and honest.

[A] Hardin also assumed something crucial, and it was false. He assumed the herders cannot talk to each other. That there are no rules, no watching, no consequences among neighbours.
VISUAL: A bright village square in clean daylight, ordinary people in easy conversation, warm and real.

[A] But real commons are not full of strangers. They are full of people who see each other every day. And that changes everything.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary community in clear daylight, familiar faces, mutually visible, connected.

## PART THREE - THE ANSWER THAT WON A NOBEL PRIZE

[A] Elinor Ostrom went and looked. Not at models, but at real commons that had survived for centuries. Alpine pastures. Japanese forests. Spanish irrigation systems.
VISUAL: A bright modern research field study in clean daylight, careful observation, orderly notes.

[A] She found communities that had governed shared resources sustainably for hundreds of years, without privatisation and without a central state.
VISUAL: A bright well-tended shared landscape under clear daylight, healthy, productive, and ancient.

[A] And she extracted the conditions. Clear boundaries about who may use it. Rules made by the users themselves, fitted to local conditions.
VISUAL: A bright clean set of locally written rules on a pale surface in daylight, plain and practical.
[CARD: users make the rules. users watch. penalties escalate gently.]

[A] Monitoring by the users, of each other. Graduated sanctions, small at first, so a first offence does not destroy a neighbour. Cheap ways to resolve disputes. And recognition from the outside authority.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary neighbour quietly noticing another's use in clean daylight, calm and unhostile.

[A] It is not romantic. It is careful institutional design. Ostrom's finding was that the tragedy is not inevitable. It is what happens when the institutions are absent.
VISUAL: A bright clean structure standing sound in daylight, unglamorous, deliberate, and working.

## PART FOUR - THE CONDITIONS THAT MATTER MOST

[A] Two of her conditions deserve special attention, because they are the ones we usually get wrong.
VISUAL: A bright clean pair of key components on a pale surface in daylight, plain and essential.

[A] First, the users must make the rules. Rules handed down by distant authorities are obeyed grudgingly and evaded cleverly. Rules you helped write, you enforce on your neighbour.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary meeting of local people writing their own rules in clean daylight, engaged.
[CARD: rules you helped write are rules you will enforce]

[A] Second, monitoring must be visible and mutual. Not surveillance from above. Simply the ordinary condition of being able to see what your neighbour takes.
VISUAL: A bright open shared landscape in clear daylight, everyone's use plainly visible to everyone.

[A] Both conditions collapse at scale, and in anonymity. Which is precisely why global commons, like the atmosphere, are the hardest case we have ever faced.
VISUAL: A bright modern globe of connected light on a clean pale wall in daylight, vast and impersonal.

## PART SIX - THE WEIGHT YOU CARRY OUT

[A] So why do we destroy what we share? Not because we are selfish. Because the gain is concentrated and the cost is spread, and that arithmetic beats good intentions every time.

[A] Which means the answer is never to appeal for restraint. It is to change the arithmetic. Boundaries, visibility, rules the users own, and gentle escalating consequences.
