## PART ONE - THE BIG IDEA

[A] The idea was sharpened by two Englishmen. Jeremy Bentham, and his student John Stuart Mill. They wanted ethics to be useful. Practical. Fair.
VISUAL: A bright modern study with tall sunlit windows, clean shelves, an open notebook on a pale desk.

[A] Before them, morality often meant obeying rules handed down from above. Bentham asked a different question. What actually makes a life better?
VISUAL: A bright clean path forking away from an old closed gate toward open sunlit country.

[A] His answer was happiness. Pleasure over pain. Well-being over suffering. And crucially, everyone's happiness counts the same. The king and the servant. One each.
VISUAL: A wide bright row of diverse ordinary people in clear daylight, each one evenly lit, none above the others.
[CARD: everyone counts equally - one person, one measure]

[A] So to choose well, you add it up. Tally the happiness each option creates, subtract the suffering, and pick the one with the highest total.
VISUAL: A clean bright ledger on a pale surface, simple marks accumulating on one side, high-key and modern.

[A] It is ethics as arithmetic. And for a huge range of decisions, it works beautifully. It is fair, it is impartial, it takes everyone seriously.
VISUAL: A bright balanced scale, both pans full and level, sunlit, calm and satisfying to look at.

## PART TWO - WHY IT IS SO POWERFUL

[A] Do not underestimate this idea. It quietly runs the modern world. When a government decides how to spend a health budget, it is doing utilitarian maths.
VISUAL: A bright modern public building in clean daylight, people moving through it purposefully, open and civic.

[A] It gives us a way to compare things that feel incomparable. To be fair to strangers we will never meet. To think past our own small circle.

[A] And it has a moral courage to it. It tells you that your comfort does not outweigh a stranger's survival. That distant suffering still counts.

## PART FOUR - THE PATCHES

[A] Utilitarians are not fools. They saw this problem coming. And they built a repair. It is called rule utilitarianism.
VISUAL: A bright clean workshop bench in daylight, a single well-made tool resting on it, precise and modern.

[A] Instead of judging each act, judge the rule behind it. A world where doctors harvest healthy visitors is a world of terror. No one would ever go to a hospital.
VISUAL: A bright modern hospital exterior in clean daylight, people walking in freely, calm and trusting.

[A] So the rule that produces the greatest good over time is do not kill your patients. And the arithmetic, done properly, agrees. The recoil was right after all.
VISUAL: A bright balanced scale settling level again, sunlit and steady, the tension resolved.
[CARD: judge the rule, not the single act]

[A] It is a clever fix. But notice what it admits. That raw happiness maths, left alone, points somewhere terrible. We need guardrails it cannot supply on its own.
