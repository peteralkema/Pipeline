## PART ONE - WHY IT WORKS

[A] The philosopher John Rawls built this idea to solve a very old problem. When people argue about justice, they argue from where they already stand.
VISUAL: A bright modern public square in clean daylight, people in animated but calm discussion, open.

[A] The wealthy tend to find low taxes just. The struggling tend to find generous support just. Everyone is sincere. And everyone is quietly serving themselves.
VISUAL: A bright clean balance in daylight, each side subtly weighted by where its holder stands, sunlit.

[A] So Rawls asked. What if we could remove that bias entirely? Not by making people better, but by making self-interest impossible to calculate.
VISUAL: A bright clean lens in daylight, clearing a distorted image into sharp focus, modern and precise.

[A] Behind the veil, you cannot favour your own group, because you do not know which group is yours. Self-interest becomes fairness by force of pure logic.
VISUAL: A bright figure choosing carefully behind a soft veil of light, deliberate and calm, clear daylight.
[CARD: not knowing who you'll be forces you to be fair]

## PART TWO - WHAT YOU WOULD CHOOSE

[A] So what would you actually pick? Rawls thought you would be careful. Because you might arrive as the least fortunate person in the whole society.
VISUAL: A bright ordinary street in clean daylight, people of every circumstance, all evenly and warmly lit.

[A] You would not gamble on being born lucky. You would look hard at the bottom of the society you are designing, and ask if you could bear to live there.
VISUAL: A bright honest view of the humblest home on a sunlit street, dignified and clean, high-key.

[A] From that came his famous principle. Inequalities are only acceptable if they make the worst-off person better off than they would otherwise be.
VISUAL: A clean bright card on a pale surface holding one line of text, editorial and modern.
[CARD: inequality is only fair if it lifts the worst-off]

[A] Not that everyone must be equal. But that no one may be left behind so that others may climb. The floor is what matters most.
VISUAL: A bright clean floor beneath a rising structure in daylight, solid and firm, supporting everything.

[A] And you would insist on liberties. Free speech, fair trial, a vote. Because you cannot know whether you will be in the majority or the despised minority.
VISUAL: A bright modern civic building in clean daylight, open doors, people entering freely, warm and just.

## PART THREE - THE OBJECTIONS

[A] It is a powerful test. But it is not beyond challenge, and the challenges are interesting.
VISUAL: A bright open forum in clean daylight, ideas being weighed carefully, calm and rigorous.

[A] Some say Rawls assumed too much caution. Perhaps behind the veil you would gamble, accepting a harsh floor for the chance at a glorious ceiling.

[A] Others say it strips away too much. Our identities, our loyalties, our communities are not distortions to be removed. They are part of who we are.

[A] Both objections have force. But notice that neither destroys the tool. Even imperfectly applied, the veil still catches special pleading with remarkable reliability.

## PART FOUR - USE IT TODAY

[A] The real power of the veil is that you can apply it right now, to any rule you argue about, and it will tell you something true.
VISUAL: A bright modern desk in clean daylight, a clear question written plainly, uncluttered and calm.

[A] Would you accept this policy if you did not know whether you would be the employer or the employee? The landlord or the tenant? The healthy or the sick?
VISUAL: A bright clean pair of doorways in daylight, two lives behind them, either one yours, sunlit.

[A] If the answer is no, you have learned something uncomfortable. You did not think that rule was just. You only thought it was good for you.
VISUAL: A bright figure pausing in honest self-examination in clear daylight, thoughtful and calm.
[CARD: if you wouldn't accept it from below, it isn't justice]
