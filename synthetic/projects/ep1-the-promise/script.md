## How to read this script

`[A]` = Mode A cinematic recreation. `[B:Component …]` = Mode B Remotion graphic (payload = render spec). **Visual exclusivity:** the screen is only ever one or the other. `[SILENCE]` = held beat, no narration.

**No lip-sync. Faces rationed to two** (⭐ marked): the disciple watching the prophet; the two men as witnesses at the close. Everything else stages interiority *without* faces — hands, backs, windows, empty chairs, the room around a person. Rare face = faces-never-resolved.

**Found lines** use the receipt pattern: narrator says the line; `[B:QuoteCard]` carries name/source/date; highlight sweeps the stressed phrase. No karaoke.

**Educational golden threads (channel device):** ① *what is this thing?* (The Dream — seeds Kasparov/Deep Blue & future explainer episodes) · ② *what are they actually building?* (productisation; pays off E2/E3 as GPT) · ③ *who is really convinced?* (believer thread; pays off E2 with Gates).

⚠VERIFY = confirm before lock. List at the foot.

## PART ONE — THE DREAM
*AI explainer, reframed contemporary. Drama-framed, Mode-B-heavy. Doom as fact, not prophecy. Seeds the channel's educational thread and the Kasparov episode.*

**[B:HighlightedHeadline]** "Can we build a mind?"
  highlight: "a mind"

**[A]** *VISUAL: contemporary — a phone waking, a cursor blinking in a chat box, a voice assistant's glow. The AI we know.*
Most of us met artificial intelligence only in the last few years. A chatbot that writes. A voice that answers. It feels new. It isn't. The dream of building a mind is decades old — and for most of those decades, it lost.

**[A]** *VISUAL: a chessboard, wide and low — a man on one side, a machine on the other. No clear face.*
The first time the world watched it win was across a chessboard. In 1997, a machine beat the greatest chess player alive.

**[B:NumberCounter]** to=1997 plain_year=true label="Deep Blue defeats the world champion"
  *(⚠VERIFY 1997. Render as a plain year — no prefix/separators.)*

**[A]** *VISUAL: the board after the final move. The machine doesn't celebrate. It stops.*
It was a strange victory. The machine hadn't out-thought him. It had out-calculated him — millions of moves a second, feeling nothing. We had built something that could beat us without understanding the game at all. Remember that man at the board. We'll come back to him.

**[A]** *VISUAL: a shift — flowing data, layers, something more organic.*
The real breakthrough was a different idea entirely. Don't program the machine with rules. Let it *learn* — the way a child does. Show it ten thousand pictures of a cat, never once tell it what a cat is, and let it work the pattern out for itself.

**[B:HighlightedHeadline]** "Not programmed. Trained."
  highlight: "Trained"

**[A]** *VISUAL: 2012. A lab, a screen, a result that changes everything.*
In 2012, that idea finally worked. A system taught itself to see — and quietly began the era we are living in now. One of the young men who built it was Ilya Sutskever. Remember him too. He will be in almost every room in this story.

**[B:LowerThird]** Ilya Sutskever — co-author of the 2012 breakthrough
  *(⚠VERIFY AlexNet 2012, Sutskever w/ Krizhevsky & Hinton. Golden thread: the man who lit the fuse is the man OpenAI recruits.)*

**[A]** *VISUAL: the machines larger, the data vaster — scale as a force.*
The lesson of the next decade was simple and brutal. The more data and the more computers you gave these systems, the smarter they got — with no ceiling anyone could find. The future would belong to whoever had the most of both. And we were building minds we couldn't fully explain. Not even their makers always knew why they did what they did.

**[B:HighlightedHeadline]** "We don't fully know how it works."
  highlight: "don't fully know"

**[A]** *VISUAL: warm California light arriving — the present of the story.*
[SILENCE — 2s]
So here was the oldest ambition, suddenly within reach — carrying the oldest fear. Were we building the greatest tool in human history — or its greatest threat? By 2015, a handful of people in California had decided the dream was becoming real, almost urgent. They simply could not agree on how to deal with it. And the loudest of them was a man for whom everything about AI was personal.

## PART TWO — THE FEAR
*Why OpenAI exists. Staged through Musk's private terror — doom present, never given the microphone.*

**[A]** *VISUAL: a Napa hillside, warm evening, years before. Two men apart from the crowd — Musk and Larry Page. At a distance; we read the body language, not the faces. (⚠VERIFY occasion.)*
For years, Elon Musk and Larry Page were close. Two of the most powerful technologists alive, and they ended most nights the same way — arguing, happily, about the future. Until the night the argument stopped being happy.

**[A]** *VISUAL: closer. The ease going out of the space between them. A hand set down too hard.*
They were arguing about AI — about what happens when machines pass us. Page wasn't worried; to him, digital minds were simply the next step in evolution. Musk's insistence that humanity stay in control struck him as sentimental. He had a word for it.

A man he trusted — who would help build some of the most powerful machines on earth — had just called the survival of our species a prejudice. Musk never forgot it. The danger, he decided, wasn't the machine. It was the people building it, who didn't care whether it loved us.

**[A]** *VISUAL: a cool blue server hall. DeepMind. Power, held by someone else.*
The lab that frightened him most was DeepMind. When Google bought it, the most advanced AI in the world passed beyond anyone's reach but Google's. Musk had tried to stop the sale. He failed.

His whole fortune rested on one belief — that a single person, moving fast enough, could bend the future. If the danger was a god built by the wrong hands, the answer was obvious: the right hands would build it first. He found his partner in a younger man.

**[A]** *VISUAL: two silhouettes against the rockets.*
Two men who agreed on the one thing that mattered — that AI was coming, and must not be left to the people who didn't fear it — set out to build the counterweight together. They'd need the best minds alive. And a promise big enough to take them from Google.

## PART THREE — THE PROMISE
*The founding. The mentorship at its height. Mode B carries the vow and the money. Believer + productisation threads planted.*

**[A]** *VISUAL: a private dining room, Sand Hill Road, late 2015. Candlelight, a small group, the hush of people who think they're starting history. (⚠VERIFY venue.)*
In the autumn of 2015, a small group gathered for dinner near Stanford. Around the table sat some of the people who would shape the next decade of human life.

**[A]** *VISUAL: an empty chair — the one they want most.*
Musk and Altman had a recruiting problem money couldn't solve. The researchers they wanted already had Google's money, Google's machines, Google's promise of the future. You couldn't outbid that. You had to out-*mean* it.

**[A]** *VISUAL: the chair filled — staged through the room's attention turning, not a face-hold.*
The one they wanted most was the young man from the 2012 breakthrough — Ilya Sutskever, now one of Google's own. To leave, for him, was a leap of something close to faith. He believed the promise more purely than anyone in the room. These were not colleagues so much as believers — Sutskever would later stand and officiate at Greg Brockman's wedding. Hold onto that closeness. It will cost them more than anyone.

**[A]** *VISUAL: the moment the thing is named. A laptop on the table, a document open.*
The promise was this. They would build artificial intelligence not as a company, but as a gift. A nonprofit, owned by no one, its research shared openly with the world. Its purpose written into its name.

Open — because secrecy was how Google had become something to fear. And to prove they meant it, they announced a sum that stunned the field. A billion dollars.

Except the billion was a banner, not a bank balance — most of it a pledge, not a payment. And the man whose name carried the most weight, who had brought the fear and would one day bring the lawsuit, gave only a fraction of what the world had been told.

## PART FOUR — THE CRACK
*The seed of the war. Productisation thread deepens. Money, and under it, control. End on the fracture, not the break.*

**[A]** *VISUAL: a darkened lab at night, screens glowing, the hum of machines costing more than most companies earn.*
For two years, OpenAI was a lab in search of a purpose — some of the most brilliant people alive, a mission to save humanity, and almost no idea where to begin. So they reached for what had worked before. Games.

**[A]** *VISUAL: a video game arena, a tournament crowd, a screen — a machine playing a game humans take seriously.*
They built a system that mastered a wildly complex video game and beat the best players in the world. A genuine marvel — and not, a benefactor might reasonably ask, an obvious path to saving the species. The question hung over everything. What were they actually building?

**[A]** *VISUAL: money leaving — the cool blue of compute, burning.*
And the longer that went unanswered, the worse the second problem got — money. To build a mind you feed it: computers, by the tens of thousands, for months, burning cash the whole time. A charity can take gifts. It can't print money. The promise to stay a nonprofit and the ambition to build a god had been at war from day one.

**[A]** *VISUAL: Musk, restless — pacing feet, a hand on a doorframe. The benefactor used to command.*
There was a third war, quieter, beneath the other two. Musk had funded the dream, and Musk was used to running what he funded. As OpenAI struggled, he came to believe it needed a single decisive hand. His.

**[B:DocumentReveal]** internal emails, 2017–2018 (court exhibits, Musk v. OpenAI)
  show_line: "the only path … is for me to unequivocally have control"
  source: trial exhibit, later made public
  *(⚠VERIFY exact wording from the public exhibits — use the true text. Evidence register depends on it.)*

**[A]** *VISUAL: a meeting, tense. Two sides of a table — staged through hands, postures, the gap between them. No faces.*
His proposals all led to the same place: fold OpenAI into one of his companies, or let him run it directly — the charity, under the one man who'd paid for it. The others wouldn't do it. Altman wouldn't. The mission couldn't belong to any single person — that was the entire point.

**[A]** *VISUAL: the room after. An empty chair pushed back from the table.*
[SILENCE — 2s]
It was the first time the student had told the teacher no. It would be the last conversation between them that mattered for a very long time.

**[A]** *VISUAL: early 2018. A figure leaving a building — from behind, a door, the back of a coat. He does not turn around.*
In the spring of 2018, Musk left the board of the company he had named. The reason given was a conflict of interest — his own car company was building AI now, and he couldn't sit on both sides. That was true. It wasn't the whole truth. He hadn't been given control. And he'd come to believe they were going to lose.

**[A]** *VISUAL: the door closing. Beyond it, a single figure at a window, back to us. Altman. The promise is now his to keep, or to break.*
[SILENCE — 3s]
The man who brought the warning was gone. The disciple was left holding the mission — and the bill. Everything OpenAI would become, and everything it would be accused of, begins in this room, the moment that door closed. They still didn't know what they were going to build. They were about to find out — and it would change the world and break the promise in the same stroke.

## CLOSE
*Mirror the cold open. Land the internal conflict. Refuse to resolve. Hand the debts forward.*

**[A]** *VISUAL: slow return to the present courtroom — now understood. The empty chair where an accuser once sat.*
Years later, the two of them met again — in front of a jury. By then one had built a company worth more than most nations, and the other had built a rival to destroy it, and each was certain the other was going to doom the world.

**[A]** *VISUAL: ⭐ FACE-HOLD #2. Two faces, brief, intercut — Musk, Altman — neither speaking. Witnesses to each other. Then we let them go.*
Here is the strangest thing — maybe the most human thing about them. Each cast himself as the one who would save us. Each looked at the other and saw, clearly, the danger. They couldn't both be right. They were both, in their own minds, the hero.

**[SILENCE — 2s]**

**[A]** *VISUAL: the protesters again, the signs, the world that wanted an answer.*
We watched, and we waited for someone to tell us which of them to trust. We are like that. We want a saviour — and when we cannot choose one, we want a court to choose for us. Years later, a court would try.

**[B:HighlightedHeadline]** "It would fail."
  highlight: "fail"

**[A]** *VISUAL: the warm founding light, one last time — the table, the word OPEN, the believers who meant it.*
This was the promise. It was real. That is what makes the rest of it a tragedy — and not, as it is so often told, simply a crime.

**[A]** *VISUAL: fade toward black. The single chair at the founding table, empty now.*
What the promise cost — the money that broke it, the partner who would arrive with billions, and the question of what they were truly building — is the next part of this story.
