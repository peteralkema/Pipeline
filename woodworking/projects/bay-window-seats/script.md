## PART ONE - WHAT A BAY WINDOW IS

[A] Start with the shape itself. An ordinary window sits flat in the wall, flush with the surface, looking straight out. A bay window does the opposite - it projects outward from the wall, jutting beyond the line of the house, usually in three panes set at gentle angles to each other.
VISUAL: A bay window shown from outside projecting out from the house wall in three angled panes; clean exterior study, soft daylight, no people.

[A] Those three angled panes are the heart of it. A flat centre pane looks straight ahead, and two side panes angle back to meet the wall. Together they form a shallow three-sided bay - and inside, beneath them, is a pocket of floor that belongs to nothing else. An alcove, waiting.
VISUAL: The inside of a bay window showing the flat centre pane and two angled side panes forming an alcove below; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] This projecting shape is why a bay window changes a room. It doesn't let in light - it adds space, pushing the room outward into a little bay on the flat wall. That extra pocket of floor is a gift, and the most natural thing in the world to fill with a seat.
VISUAL: A room with a bay window creating a small projecting alcove of extra floor space; clean interior, soft daylight, no people.

[A] So before a single cushion is placed, the bay has already done the hard work. It has carved out an alcove, framed it with glass on three sides, and set it apart from the rest of the room. A flat window offers a wall. A bay window offers a room within a room.
VISUAL: A bay window alcove sitting ready and empty, framed by glass on three sides; clean interior, warm daylight, no people, no text.

## PART TWO - WHY THE SHAPE MAKES THE SEAT

[A] Now the seat almost designs itself. In a bay, the bench runs along the base of the three panes, following the shape of the alcove. The angled sides mean the seat gently wraps around you, so you are not perched on a straight edge but cradled within a shallow curve of windows.
VISUAL: A bench following the shape of a bay window, gently wrapping around the alcove; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] That wrapping shape is why a bay seat feels so much cosier than a flat bench. On a straight bench you sit against a window. In a bay you sit within one, held on three sides, the room behind you and the light before you.
VISUAL: A person's-eye view from within a bay window seat, windows curving on three sides, the view all around; clean interior, bright daylight, no people.

[A] The bay also solves the hardest problem of any window seat - depth. A flat window gives you only the thin ledge of the sill. But a bay projects outward, and that projection creates real depth, room enough for a proper bench you can curl up on rather than merely balance against.
VISUAL: A deep bench filling the projecting depth of a bay window alcove, roomy and inviting; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] And beneath that deeper bench there is room for storage. Because the bay pushes out a full pocket of floor, the seat built above it can be a deep chest with a lifting lid. The very shape that makes the seat cosy also makes it useful, hiding storage in the space the bay created.
VISUAL: A bay window bench with its lid lifted to show deep storage in the projecting alcove below; clean interior, warm light, no people.

## PART THREE - THE MAGIC OF THE LIGHT

[A] But the true magic of a bay window seat is the light. A flat window lets light in from one direction only, straight ahead. A bay, with its three angled panes, gathers light from three directions at once - ahead.
VISUAL: Light pouring into a bay window alcove from three angled panes at once, bright and enveloping; clean interior, luminous daylight, no people.

[A] That light from three sides is what makes a bay seat glow. Sit in it and you're surrounded by daylight, not merely lit from the front. It is why a bay window seat is the finest reading spot in a house - the light arrives from every angle, gentle and full.
VISUAL: A softly glowing bay window seat filled with daylight from all sides, warm and shadowless; clean interior, luminous light, no people.

[A] The angled panes also catch the sun as it moves. Through a single flat window the sun crosses quickly and is gone. But a bay, facing three ways, holds the light far longer - catching the morning on one side, the midday ahead, and the evening on the other.
VISUAL: A bay window catching sunlight across its three panes as the light shifts, staying bright; clean interior, warm shifting daylight, no people.

[A] And the view opens wider too. Where a flat window frames one narrow scene, a bay's three panes wrap the view around you - the garden ahead, and a glimpse down the street to each side. You do not look at the world through a bay window. You sit out in it.
VISUAL: A wide wrapping view seen through the three panes of a bay window, the outside all around; clean interior, bright daylight, no people.
