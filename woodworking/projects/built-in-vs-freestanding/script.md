## COLD OPEN

[A] You have decided you want a window seat. But before you choose a cushion or a colour, there is a bigger decision hiding underneath - one that changes the cost, the effort, the look, and even whether you can take the seat with you when you move. Built in, or freestanding?

[A] It sounds like a small choice. It is not. A built-in seat becomes part of the house, framed into the wall as if it always belonged there. A freestanding seat is furniture - a bench you place, move, and one day carry away. Two completely different paths from the same simple wish.

[A] And people choose wrong all the time. They pour effort into building a permanent seat in a home they leave two years later. Or they set a movable bench where a built-in would have transformed the room. The seat was fine. The path was wrong for their life.

## PART ONE - THE BUILT-IN SEAT

[A] Start with the built-in. This is a seat constructed permanently into the wall beneath the window - framed, fixed, and finished so it looks like part of the architecture rather than furniture pushed against a wall. It does not sit in the room. It becomes the room.
VISUAL: A built-in window seat framed seamlessly into the wall, looking like part of the house; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.

[A] The great gift of a built-in is that seamless, permanent look. Because it is fixed to the wall and often runs from side to side of an alcove, it reads as intentional and grand - as though the house were designed around it. Nothing looks more settled or more expensive than a true built-in seat.
VISUAL: A grand built-in window seat filling an alcove wall to wall, settled and architectural; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] A built-in also makes the very best use of an awkward space. Fitted exactly into a bay or a recess, it fills every inch, wasting nothing, turning a difficult nook into a flawless seat. And built permanently, it can hold deep, generous storage beneath, framed right into its base.
VISUAL: A built-in seat fitted perfectly into a recess with deep storage beneath, wasting no space; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] But the built-in asks a price. It takes real skill and effort to build, it must be fixed to the wall and often the floor, and once it's in, it stays. You can't move it, can't take it with you, and can't easily change your mind.
VISUAL: A built-in seat firmly fixed into a wall and floor, permanent and immovable; clean interior, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART TWO - THE FREESTANDING SEAT

[A] Now the freestanding seat - a bench that simply stands beneath the window without being fixed to anything. It is furniture in the truest sense, resting on its own legs or base, placed where you like and moved when you wish. The easiest window seat any home can gain.
VISUAL: A freestanding wooden bench standing on its own beneath a window, movable and independent; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.

[A] Its great gift is freedom. You can build or buy it, set it beneath any window in minutes, and change your mind whenever you like. Rearrange the room, move it to another window, or carry it to your next home entirely. It asks no permanent change to the house at all.
VISUAL: A freestanding bench being enjoyed beneath a window, flexible and free-standing; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] It is also far kinder to a beginner and a budget. A freestanding bench is simpler to build, needs no fixing into walls, and forgives mistakes - if the fit is not perfect, you simply nudge it over. For a first project or a rented home, it is by far the gentler path.
VISUAL: A simple well-made freestanding bench beneath a window, approachable and unfussy; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] But freedom has its cost too. A freestanding seat rarely achieves that seamless built-in look - there's usually a gap at the sides, a hint that it's furniture rather than architecture. It can look placed rather than belonging. And being movable, it may drift out of position or feel less grand than a true built-in.
VISUAL: A freestanding bench with small gaps at its sides beneath a window, clearly furniture; clean interior, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART THREE - MATCHING THE CHOICE TO YOUR LIFE

[A] So how do you choose? The first and biggest question is how long you'll stay. If this is your forever home, a built-in rewards you for years and adds to the house itself. If you rent, or expect to move soon, a freestanding seat you can take with you is almost always the wiser path.
VISUAL: A built-in seat in a settled family home beside a freestanding bench suited to a rented space; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] The second question is the space. A tricky bay, a deep recess, or an odd alcove cries out for a built-in that fills it perfectly. A plain flat wall beneath an ordinary window suits a freestanding bench just as well, with none of the fixing and framing a built-in would demand.
VISUAL: A recessed alcove suiting a built-in beside a flat wall suiting a freestanding bench; clean interior study, warm light, no people.

[A] The third is your skill and your budget. A built-in asks more of both - more woodworking, more materials, more permanence. A freestanding bench is forgiving, cheaper, and achievable for a first-time builder. Be honest about where you are, and let that steer the choice as much as the look you dream of.
VISUAL: A simple freestanding bench beside a more complex built-in, the effort difference clear; clean interior, soft light, no people, no text.

[A] And know that you can blend the two. A freestanding bench can be pushed into an alcove and trimmed with side panels to fake a built-in look, borrowing some of the permanence without the commitment. The line between the two is softer than it seems, and clever building can lean either way.
VISUAL: A freestanding bench dressed with side panels to look built-in within an alcove; clean interior, warm light, no people.

## PART FOUR - THE RIGHT PATH FOR YOU

[A] So weigh it plainly. The built-in gives you a seamless, permanent, architectural seat that makes the very best use of a space - at the cost of effort, skill, and a commitment you cannot undo. It is the seat for a home you will keep and a space that deserves it.
VISUAL: A grand permanent built-in window seat, settled and architectural; clean interior, warm daylight, no people, no text.

[A] The freestanding seat gives you freedom, simplicity, and a gentle path for any budget or any beginner - at the cost of that seamless built-in look and a hint that it is furniture rather than architecture. It is the seat for a rented home, a first project, or a life that may move on.
VISUAL: A flexible well-made freestanding bench beneath a window, free and unfixed; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So ask how long you'll stay, look honestly at your space and your skill, and the path will make itself clear. Neither is better - they're answers to different questions.
