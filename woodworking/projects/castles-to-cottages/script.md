## PART ONE - BORN IN THE CASTLE

[A] It began, as we've seen, with the castle's thick walls. A wall built massive for defence, when pierced by a window, left a deep stone recess - and a bench set into it made the first window seats. They were born of stone, in the homes of the powerful few who could build such walls.

[A] In the castle the window seat was practical and privileged at once. It used the deep recess the thick wall created, and sat in the best light of a dim stone hall - the spot for reading and fine work. Only the grand had walls thick enough to make such a seat.

[A] So at its birth the window seat belonged to the top of society. It required the massive walls only wealth and power could raise, and it graced the halls of lords and kings. The cottage-dweller had thin walls and no such recess. The window seat was, in the beginning, a mark of the mighty.
