## PART ONE - THE COMFORT MISTAKES

[A] The first and most common mistake is wrong height. Build the seat to the windowsill instead of the body, and it ends up too high or too low - feet dangling, or knees rising and back aching. The fix is simple: build to chair height, set by the body, not the sill.
VISUAL: A window seat at wrong height beside one at correct chair height; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] The second is wrong depth. Too shallow and you perch on the edge with no support; too deep and your back never reaches the wall. The fix is to match a good chair - deep enough to support your thighs, shallow enough for your back to rest, unless you want a curl-up nook.
VISUAL: A too-shallow seat beside one of correct chair depth; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] The third comfort mistake is a thin, hard cushion - or worse, none at all. Wood is hard, and a seat you cannot sit on comfortably will not be used. The fix costs only a little thought: plan a generous cushion from the start, and build the bench height to suit it.
VISUAL: A seat with a thin hard cushion beside one with a generous soft cushion; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So the comfort mistakes are the deadliest, because a seat you cannot sit on will never be used however lovely it looks. Get height, depth, and cushion right - all set by the body, not the window - and you have cleared the biggest hurdle a window seat faces.
VISUAL: A comfortable window seat that got the body measurements right; clean interior, warm light, no people, no text.

## PART TWO - THE PLACEMENT MISTAKES

[A] The fourth mistake is building where the light is wrong. A seat in harsh glare is too bright; a seat in a dim corner draws nobody. The fix is to follow the light first - build where the daylight is kind and generous, and the seat pulls people to it.
VISUAL: A window seat in harsh glare beside one in kind gentle light; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] The fifth is forgetting the view. A seat facing a blank wall or a fence a step away gives the eye nothing and no reason to sit. The fix is to weigh the view when choosing the spot - a window onto a garden or sky turns a seat into a place people seek out.
VISUAL: A seat facing a blank wall beside one facing a lovely view; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] The sixth is placing the seat where nobody goes, or in the traffic. A seat in an unused room stays unused; a seat blocking a busy path gets in the way. The fix is a calm spot within a used room - off the main path, but still part of its life.
VISUAL: A well-placed seat in a calm but used part of a room; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] So the placement mistakes waste a good seat by putting it in the wrong spot. Follow the light, weigh the view, and place the seat within the life of the room but out of the rush. Where those three meet is where a window seat belongs - and where it will actually be used.
VISUAL: A window seat placed where light, view, and life all meet; clean interior, warm daylight, no people, no text.

## PART THREE - THE PRACTICAL MISTAKES

[A] The seventh mistake is storage you cannot reach. A lift-up lid buried under a heavy cushion you must clear every time gets opened once and forgotten. The fix is to make storage easy - front drawers, split lids, or lids that stay open on their own, so you actually use the storage you built.
VISUAL: A buried awkward storage lid beside easy front drawers; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] The eighth is ignoring warmth. A seat against cold glass, with no thought for heat, becomes a chilly spot people avoid. The fix is to design for warmth - let heat reach the seat, insulate against the cold window, so the nook stays cosy and used through the winter, not abandoned till spring.
VISUAL: A cold neglected window seat beside a warm cosy one; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] The ninth is the seat that fights the room - wrong proportions, clashing style, a bench that looks bolted on rather than belonging. The fix is to design the seat to suit its room, matching its lines and finish, so it reads as part of the home rather than an awkward afterthought.
VISUAL: A mismatched bolted-on seat beside one that belongs to its room; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] So the practical mistakes - unreachable storage, no warmth, a seat that clashes - each quietly undermine an otherwise good seat. Make storage easy, design for warmth, and suit the seat to its room. These finishing considerations are what separate a seat that merely exists from one that truly works.
VISUAL: A window seat that got the practical details right; clean interior, warm light, no people, no text.

## PART FOUR - BUILDING IT RIGHT

[A] So bring the mistakes together, because avoiding them is a checklist you can run before you build. Right height and depth. A generous cushion. Kind light, a good view, a spot within the life of the room. Easy storage. Warmth. A seat that suits its room. Check each, and the common failures are gone.
VISUAL: A window seat that passed every design check; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.

[A] Notice how many mistakes come from building to the window instead of the person. The sill sets the height wrong; the glass makes it cold; the fixed window blinds you to light, view, and comfort. Design for the person who will sit there, and most of these mistakes never happen at all.
VISUAL: A seat designed for the person rather than the window; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So before you cut a board, run the checklist and picture the person in the seat - warm, comfortable, in good light. Get the design right first. Next time, we will look at how to keep a window seat from feeling cold and hard. Until then, may your seats avoid every common trap.
