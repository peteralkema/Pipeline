## PART ONE - WHAT A BANQUETTE IS

[A] A banquette is simply an upholstered bench built for dining - a padded seat, usually with a cushioned back, set against a wall and paired with a table. Where a window seat is made to sit and rest in, a banquette is made to eat at, sized and shaped for gathering around a table.
VISUAL: An upholstered banquette bench paired with a dining table; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] The magic happens when it turns a corner. A corner banquette wraps around two walls in an L-shape, following the corner, with a table set into the embrace of the bench. This wrapping shape is what creates the beloved breakfast nook - cosy, enclosed, and gathered around the table.
VISUAL: An L-shaped corner banquette wrapping a table in a breakfast nook; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.

[A] It is often built-in and upholstered, which sets it apart from a loose bench. Fixed to the walls, padded and covered in fabric, it reads as a permanent, comfortable, considered feature - part of the room's architecture, softer and more inviting than a hard wooden bench or a set of chairs.
VISUAL: A built-in upholstered banquette, permanent and inviting; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So a banquette is a padded, built-in dining bench, at its best wrapping a corner around a table. It is the window seat's idea - a built-in bench - applied to eating and gathering. And in that role, wrapped around a table in a corner, it becomes something wonderful: the breakfast nook.

## PART TWO - WHY IT WORKS FOR GATHERING

[A] The first reason a corner banquette works so well is that it gathers people. Its wrapping shape sits diners close, facing gently toward one another around the table, drawn into conversation. Where separate chairs scatter people, a banquette pulls them together into a warm, sociable knot.

[A] It also feels cosy and enclosed in a way chairs never do. With a wall at your back and the bench wrapping around, a banquette gives that sheltered, nestled feeling - a defined nook within the room rather than seats floating in open space. People relax and linger in that enclosure.

## PART THREE - HOW IT SAVES SPACE

[A] The second great gift of a banquette is space. A built-in bench against the wall seats far more people in less room than chairs. Chairs need clearance behind them to pull out and move; a banquette tucks flat against the walls, needing none. In a tight kitchen, that efficiency is precious.

[A] Wrapping a corner multiplies the saving. The corner, so often wasted, becomes the fullest seat in the room, using space that chairs would leave awkward and empty. A corner banquette turns the least usable spot into the most useful, seating a whole family where a corner sat idle before.

[A] And beneath the bench lies storage. The hollow base of a banquette holds deep drawers or lift-up compartments for the overflow of a kitchen or dining room. So the banquette seats more, in less space, and swallows storage too - three gifts in a single footprint against the wall.

[A] So for a small or busy kitchen, a corner banquette is a masterstroke of space. It seats more people, uses the wasted corner, and hides storage, all while taking almost no clear floor. Where space is tight and gathering matters, few seating choices give back so much for so little.

## PART FOUR - DESIGNING ONE THAT WORKS

[A] So how do you design a banquette that works? Start with the table relationship. The bench and table heights must agree for comfortable eating, just as a chair and table would - build the bench to seat height and pair it with a table that meets it cleanly, so diners sit at the right level.
VISUAL: A banquette and table at heights that agree for eating; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] Mind the depth and the getting-in. A banquette wants enough seat depth to be comfortable at the table but not so deep you cannot reach your plate, and a plan for how people slide in and out of a wrapping bench, since the inside seats mean climbing past the others.
VISUAL: A well-planned banquette sized for comfort and easy access; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] Upholster it well and durably. A banquette lives where food is eaten and spills happen, so choose comfortable padding and a tough, wipeable fabric that survives daily meals. Comfort makes people linger; durability keeps the nook lovely through years of use. Both matter in a seat that works this hard.
VISUAL: A well-upholstered durable banquette built for daily meals; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] So design the banquette around the table, size it for real bodies, and upholster it to last. Then it becomes the warm, space-saving heart of your kitchen. Next time, we will look at whether window seats add value. Until then, may your nooks gather your family and your corners work hard.
