## PART ONE - WHY THE CORNER IS WASTED

[A] The corner is wasted because ordinary furniture cannot solve it. A room's furniture is built for straight walls - sofas, chairs, and tables all want a flat surface to sit against. But a corner is not a flat wall. It is two walls meeting at a right angle, and nothing straight fits neatly into it.
VISUAL: A corner where two walls meet at a right angle, straight furniture unable to fit the angle; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So the corner becomes a problem to work around rather than a space to use. People fill it with a lamp, a plant, or simply leave it bare, because they have no piece of furniture shaped to belong there. The junction that should be an asset becomes a gap.
VISUAL: A corner filled with a lamp and a plant as afterthoughts, the space not truly used; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] And when the corner holds two windows, the waste is doubled. All that light, arriving from two directions at once, falls on empty floor. It is the brightest, most gathered light in the room, and in most homes nobody ever sits in it. The best light in the house, given to nobody.
VISUAL: Bright light pouring into an empty corner from two windows, falling on unused floor; clean interior, luminous daylight, no people.

[A] The answer is not a straight piece of furniture forced into a bent space. It is a seat shaped like the corner itself - one that turns the angle instead of fighting it. And that is exactly what a corner window seat does. It is made to belong where nothing else can.
VISUAL: An empty corner ready and waiting for an L-shaped seat shaped to fit it; clean interior, warm light, no people, no text.

## PART TWO - HOW THE L-SHAPED SEAT TRANSFORMS IT

[A] A corner window seat is simply a bench bent to follow the corner - an L-shape, one arm along each wall, meeting in the angle. Instead of fighting the corner, it embraces it, turning the very junction that defeated ordinary furniture into the heart of the seat.
VISUAL: An L-shaped bench following two walls and meeting in the corner, embracing the angle; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.

[A] And in doing so it becomes remarkably generous. Two arms of bench give far more seating than a single straight one - room for several people, or for one person to stretch out along a whole side. The corner, once too small for a single chair, now holds a seat big enough for the family.
VISUAL: A generous L-shaped corner bench with room for several to sit along its two arms; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] The bend also creates a natural gathering shape. People seated along an L face gently toward each other, drawn into conversation, the way they never are along a straight bench where everyone stares the same way. A corner seat is sociable by its very form - it turns people to face one another.
VISUAL: An L-shaped corner seat whose bent form naturally turns seating toward a shared centre; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] And beneath both arms of the L there is deep storage waiting. A corner seat has two long benches to hollow out, giving generous hidden chests along each wall. The shape that gathers people and light also gathers storage, tucking it away beneath a seat that already earns its space many times over.
VISUAL: An L-shaped corner bench with storage beneath both arms, the lids raised to show the space; clean interior, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART THREE - THE MAGIC OF LIGHT FROM TWO SIDES

[A] But the true magic of a corner seat is the light. Sitting in the corner where two windows meet, you're lit from two directions at once - daylight arriving along both arms of the L, wrapping around you far more fully than a single window ever could.
VISUAL: A corner window seat lit from two directions at once, daylight wrapping around it; clean interior, luminous daylight, no people.

[A] Light from two sides does something gentle and rare - it softens shadow. A single window throws hard shadows across a face and a page. But light arriving from two directions fills those shadows in, leaving a soft, even glow that is kind to the eyes and perfect for reading, resting, or simply sitting.
VISUAL: A corner seat bathed in soft even light from two windows, shadows filled and gentle; clean interior, warm shadowless light, no people.

[A] The two windows also open two views. Along one arm you look out one way; along the other, another. A corner seat lets you turn your head and change the whole scene - the garden on one side, the street on the other. Two windows, two worlds, from a single seat in the corner.
VISUAL: A corner seat with two windows offering two different views to either side; clean interior, bright daylight, no people.

[A] And the light lasts longer through the day. Facing two directions, a corner seat catches the sun as it moves across the sky, holding daylight on one window as the other fades. Long after a single-window seat has fallen into shadow, the corner seat still glows. The last bright spot in the room.
VISUAL: A corner seat still catching sunlight from one window as the day shifts, staying bright; clean interior, warm shifting light, no people, no text.
