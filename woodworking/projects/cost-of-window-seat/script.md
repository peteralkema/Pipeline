## PART ONE - WHAT DRIVES THE COST

[A] The first great driver of cost is whether the seat is built-in or freestanding. A freestanding bench is furniture - build or buy it, set it down, done. A built-in must be constructed in place, fitted to the walls, fixed and finished, and that labour and skill cost far more than a standalone bench.
VISUAL: A simple freestanding bench beside a fitted built-in, the cost gap clear; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] The second driver is complexity. A plain bench is cheap. Add storage with a lift-up lid, and the cost rises. Add drawers, cubbies, side panels, shelving, and fine trim, and it rises much more. Every feature is more material, more labour, more skill - and the price climbs with each one you add.
VISUAL: A window seat gaining features and cost, from plain bench to elaborate build; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] The third driver is materials. Cheap softwood or basic plywood keeps costs low; fine hardwoods like oak or walnut cost far more. And the finish matters too - a coat of paint is cheap, while fine joinery and hand-finishing add real expense. The wood and the finish you choose swing the price widely.
VISUAL: Cheap softwood beside fine hardwood, the material cost difference clear; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] So three forces set the cost - built-in or freestanding, simple or complex, cheap or fine materials. A freestanding painted pine bench sits at one end of the range; a fitted hardwood built-in with storage, drawers, and custom cushions at the other. Knowing these drivers lets you place your own seat on that scale.

## PART TWO - WHERE THE MONEY GOES

[A] Within any window seat, the money goes to a few main places, and knowing them helps you see where to save. The biggest, in a built-in, is usually labour - the skilled hours to build, fit, and finish it in place. Labour often costs more than all the materials combined.
VISUAL: A built-in window seat under skilled construction, labour the main cost; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] After labour comes the wood and materials - the timber, the plywood, the fixings, the trim. This is where your choice of cheap or fine swings the bill most directly. A seat in basic materials keeps this modest; one in fine hardwood with lots of trim makes it a major part of the cost.
VISUAL: The timber and materials of a window seat, a major cost driver; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] Then the cushions, which surprise people. A good custom cushion, made to fit an odd-shaped seat in quality fabric, can cost a real sum on its own - sometimes rivalling the wood. Cushions are half the comfort and a real slice of the budget, not a cheap afterthought to add at the end.
VISUAL: A quality custom window seat cushion, a real cost in itself; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So the money splits between labour, materials, and cushions, with labour usually the largest in a built-in. Seeing this tells you where to save - do the labour yourself, choose humbler materials, or simplify the cushion - and where not to, since skimping on the wrong one shows in the finished seat.

## PART THREE - BUILDING IT YOURSELF

[A] The single biggest way to change the cost is to build it yourself. Since labour is often the largest expense of a built-in, doing that labour yourself can cut the cost dramatically - sometimes to a fraction of what paying a craftsman would run. Your time and skill replace the biggest line on the bill.
VISUAL: A window seat being built by its owner, saving the labour cost; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] A freestanding bench is the friendliest place to start. It needs no fixing into walls, forgives small errors, and can be built with basic tools and modest skill. For a first-timer, a simple self-built freestanding seat delivers most of the pleasure at a small fraction of a fitted built-in's price.
VISUAL: A simple self-built freestanding window seat, affordable and achievable; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] Building it yourself also lets you spend where it matters and save where it does not. Put your money into a good cushion and a nice finish, and save on hidden structure with cheaper materials no one sees. A self-builder controls every choice, and can get a fine-looking seat for far less.
VISUAL: A self-built seat spending on visible quality and saving on hidden structure; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So if the cost of a built-in gives you pause, the answer is often your own two hands. Build a simple seat yourself, spend wisely on the parts that show, and you can have a lovely window seat for a fraction of the price - trading money for time and the satisfaction of making it.

## PART FOUR - GETTING THE LOOK FOR LESS

[A] So how do you get the window seat you want without overspending? First, match the ambition to the budget. A simple painted bench, fitted well and cushioned nicely, can look wonderful for little. You do not need fine hardwood and elaborate storage to have a beautiful, useful seat.

[A] Spend where the eye and body land. A good cushion and a clean finish are seen and felt every day, so they reward spending. Hidden structure, unseen wood, the inside of a storage box - these can be cheap without anyone knowing. Put money where it shows and save where it does not.

[A] And remember the tricks that fake the expensive look for less - closing gaps, adding trim, painting the seat to match the room, so a simple bench reads as a seamless built-in. The costliest-looking window seats are often not the costliest to build, just the most cleverly finished.
