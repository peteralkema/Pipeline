## PART ONE - WHY THE EYE PREFERS CERTAIN SHAPES

[A] Begin with a strange truth - our eyes have preferences, and they are remarkably consistent. Across centuries and cultures, people have found certain proportions more pleasing than others, certain relationships between length and height that simply feel right. It is not random taste. It is something deep in how we see.
VISUAL: A pleasingly proportioned rectangle shown as a study in what the eye prefers; clean diagrammatic study, warm light, no people.

[A] One famous relationship is a gentle rectangle, noticeably longer than it's tall - a shape the eye finds calm and balanced, neither squat nor stretched. Designers have leaned on this proportion forever, in windows, in doors, in furniture, because it settles the eye the way a square or a long thin sliver never quite does.
VISUAL: A calm balanced rectangle beside a squat square and a stretched sliver, the pleasing one clear; clean diagrammatic study, soft light, no people.

[A] A window seat lives or dies by this. Seen from the front, it's essentially a rectangle - its length against its height. Get that relationship into the range the eye loves, and the seat looks balanced and inevitable.
VISUAL: A window seat whose front rectangle sits in the pleasing proportion the eye loves; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] So the first secret of a perfect window seat is that its overall shape should please the eye as a rectangle - comfortably longer than it is tall, balanced rather than squat or stretched. Before any detail, the big shape must sit right, because the eye judges the whole before it ever notices the parts.
VISUAL: A window seat with a pleasing overall rectangular shape, balanced at a glance; clean interior, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART TWO - THE MEASUREMENTS THAT MATTER

[A] Now the measurements themselves, and how they relate. Three dimensions define a window seat - its height off the floor, its depth from front to back, and its length along the wall. Beauty lives not in any one of them alone, but in how they relate to each other and to the window above.
VISUAL: A window seat with its height, depth, and length shown as three relating dimensions; clean diagrammatic study, warm light, no people.

[A] Height and depth are set mostly by the body - chair height off the floor, thigh depth front to back - and those are fixed by comfort. But length is freer, and length is where proportion sings. The length should relate gracefully to the height, to the width of the window it sits beneath.
VISUAL: A window seat whose length relates gracefully to the window above it; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] The window is the key relationship. A seat that runs exactly the width of the window looks intentional and settled, framed by the glass above. A seat much shorter than its window looks lost beneath it; a seat much longer looks like it wandered past.
VISUAL: A window seat running neatly to the width of the window above, framed and settled; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] And the parts within should relate too. The thickness of the cushion, the height of the base, the depth of the front rail - each should feel proportionate to the whole, none too chunky or too thin. A seat where every part agrees with every other looks calm; one where a part shouts looks awkward.
VISUAL: A window seat where cushion, base, and rail all feel proportionate to the whole; clean interior, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART THREE - THE DETAILS THE EYE READS

[A] Beyond the big measurements, the eye reads finer things, often without knowing. One is the line where the seat meets the wall and window. When the seat aligns cleanly with the window frame above - edges lining up, nothing awkwardly off by a hair - the eye reads harmony. Small misalignments quietly nag.
VISUAL: A window seat whose edges align cleanly with the window frame above; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] Another is balance between solid and open. A seat that is all heavy solid base can look blocky and dense; one lifted on legs or opened with a recess beneath can look lighter and more graceful. The eye enjoys a little breathing room, a sense of lightness even in a solid, sturdy seat.
VISUAL: A window seat balancing solid base with a sense of lightness beneath; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] Symmetry pleases too, especially in a bay or a framed recess. A seat centred beneath a centred window, balanced left and right, settles the eye deeply. We are drawn to symmetry - it reads as calm and intentional, and a window seat that sits square and centred in its space feels profoundly right.
VISUAL: A symmetrical window seat centred beneath a centred window, calm and balanced; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.

[A] And restraint reads as elegance. A seat that tries too hard - too many mouldings, too much fuss, competing details - looks busy and cheap. A seat with clean simple lines and one or two well-judged details looks refined. In proportion as in all design, knowing what to leave out is half the art.
VISUAL: A clean restrained window seat with simple elegant lines, refined and calm; clean interior, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART FOUR - DESIGNING FOR THE EYE

[A] So how do you design for these proportions? Start from the window. Let its width set the length of your seat, so the two agree and frame each other. Then set height and depth by the body, for comfort.
VISUAL: A window seat designed outward from the window's width and the body's needs; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] Then step back and look, honestly, as the eye sees. Does the whole shape please as a rectangle? Do the edges align with the window frame? Does any part shout too loud? Trust your eye - it's been trained by a lifetime of seeing, and it will feel wrongness you can't yet name.
VISUAL: A window seat viewed from across the room, judged by the honest eye; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] And keep it simple. The most beautifully proportioned window seats are rarely the fussiest. They are the ones where every part relates calmly to every other and to the window above, with nothing shouting and nothing lost. Proportion, not decoration, is what makes a seat look perfect - and proportion costs nothing but care.
VISUAL: A perfectly proportioned simple window seat, every part in calm agreement; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.

[A] So design from the window, set comfort by the body, then trust your eye and keep it simple. Your seat will then have the quiet rightness the finest ones share - balanced and beautiful. Next time, we will settle the difference between a window seat and a storage bench.
