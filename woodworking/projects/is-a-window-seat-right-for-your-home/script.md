## PART ONE - QUESTIONS ABOUT YOUR WINDOW

[A] Start with the window itself, because it decides everything. Ask first: does the window have good light? A window seat lives on daylight, so a window with kind, generous light for part of the day is ideal. A window in harsh glare or perpetual gloom will make a seat few want to use.
VISUAL: A window with kind generous light suited to a seat; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.

[A] Ask next: is there a good view? A seat gives the eye somewhere to go, so a window onto a garden, a street, or the sky earns a seat easily. A window facing a blank wall gives nothing to look at, and a seat there will struggle to draw anyone.
VISUAL: A window with a good view worth sitting for; clean interior, soft daylight, no people.

[A] And ask: is the window at a workable height? A window seat sits best beneath a window whose sill is high enough to allow a seat below it. A window that comes right down to the floor, or sits very high, may make a comfortable seat awkward or impossible to fit beneath it.
VISUAL: A window at a height that suits a seat beneath it; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So question the window first - its light, its view, its height. A window with good light, a pleasant view, and a workable height is one that wants a seat. If yours fails these, a seat faces an uphill battle. The window must earn the seat first.
VISUAL: A window that has earned its seat; clean interior, warm light, no people, no text.

## PART TWO - QUESTIONS ABOUT YOUR SPACE

[A] Now turn to the space. Ask: is there room for a seat of proper depth? A window seat needs real depth - chair depth at least. If the spot allows only a shallow ledge, the seat will be a perch nobody wants. Be honest about whether the space can hold one.
VISUAL: A space with room for a seat of proper depth; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] Ask: what is the space doing now, and can you spare it? A window seat claims floor and often wall. If that spot holds storage or a use you need, weigh whether you can give it up. A seat is only a gain if the space it takes was not doing something more valuable.
VISUAL: A space weighed for what a seat would cost it; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] And ask: is anything in the way? Look for a radiator, sockets, pipes, or skirting where the seat must go. These can be moved, but at a cost. Knowing what lurks in the spot before you commit tells you whether the seat is a simple build or a hidden, expensive undertaking.
VISUAL: A space checked for radiators and obstacles; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So question the space - its depth, its current use, its obstacles. A spot with room for a proper seat, no better use, and nothing costly in the way is a spot ready for a window seat. If the space is too shallow, too needed, or too obstructed, the seat may cost beyond it gives.
VISUAL: A space ready and suited for a window seat; clean interior, warm light, no people, no text.

## PART THREE - QUESTIONS ABOUT YOUR LIFE

[A] Now the most important questions, about you. Ask: will you actually use it? Picture yourself a month from now. Do you truly see yourself sitting there - reading, resting, watching the light - again and again? Or are you drawn only to the photograph? Honest habits, not pretty pictures, decide if a seat gets used.
VISUAL: A window seat weighed against honest daily habits; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] Ask: what is it for? A seat with a clear purpose - a reading nook, a breakfast spot, a place to watch the garden - earns its keep. A seat built only because it looks nice risks becoming the pretty ornament nobody sits in. Know what the seat is for.
VISUAL: A window seat with a clear purpose in mind; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] And ask: does it suit your life and home? A cosy nook suits a home that values comfort; a kitchen banquette suits one that gathers to eat. A seat that fits how you live will be used. One that fits an imagined life won't. Match the seat to your true life.
VISUAL: A window seat suited to a real life and home; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So question yourself most honestly of all - whether you will use it, what it is for, and whether it suits your real life. These answers matter more than the window or the space, because a seat you will genuinely use is worth building anywhere, and one you will not is worth building nowhere.

[A] So bring the questions together. Does the window have good light, a good view, a workable height? Does the space have room, to spare, and clear of obstacles? Will you truly use the seat, for a real purpose, suited to your life? Answer these honestly, and the decision makes itself.
