## COLD OPEN

[A] Two things quietly ruin more window seats than any bad joinery ever could - cold and hardness. A seat pressed against icy winter glass, topped with a thin firm cushion, becomes a spot nobody chooses. Beautiful to look at, miserable to sit in. The very window that makes the seat can also make it unwelcoming.

## PART ONE - WHY IT GETS COLD

[A] First, understand the cold. Glass is a poor barrier against the weather - heat escapes through it easily, and cold radiates in. So the air right beside a window is always the chilliest in the room, and a seat placed there sits in that pool of cold, especially in winter.
VISUAL: A window seat sitting in the pool of cold air beside chilly glass; clean interior, cool light, no people.

[A] Draughts make it worse. Older or ill-fitting windows leak air around their edges, and that thin cold draught falls directly onto anyone sitting in the seat below. A seat can be cold not just from the glass, but from a steady creeping draught you feel on your neck and hands.
VISUAL: A cold draught creeping down from a window onto the seat below; clean interior, cool light, no people.

[A] And the space beneath the seat can trap cold too. A hollow storage bench against an outside wall can hold a pocket of cold air, chilling the seat from below as well as behind. The seat ends up surrounded by cold - from the glass behind, the draught above, and the cold pocket beneath.
VISUAL: A window seat chilled from glass, draught, and a cold pocket beneath; clean interior, cool light, no people.

[A] So the cold comes from three directions - the glass, the draughts, and the space below. Knowing this points straight to the fixes. Warm the seat means blocking the cold from all three: insulating against the glass, sealing the draughts, and keeping the cold from rising beneath. Each is simple once you see it.
VISUAL: A window seat with the three sources of cold identified; clean interior, cool light, no people, no text.

## PART TWO - HOW TO WARM IT

[A] The first warmth fix is heat at the seat. If you can, place a radiator or heat source near the window seat, or leave the space beneath it open so warm air from a radiator below can rise into the nook. A seat with warmth reaching it stays cosy through the coldest months.
VISUAL: A window seat with warmth rising into it from below; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] The second is sealing the draughts. Make sure the window itself is well sealed around its edges, so no thin cold draught falls onto the seat. A well-sealed window, or heavy curtains that can be drawn across the recess at night, keeps the creeping cold out and the warmth in.
VISUAL: A window seat with a well-sealed window and curtains against draughts; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] The third is insulating against the cold. If the seat sits against an outside wall, insulate behind and beneath it so the cold cannot seep through. A well-insulated seat holds the room's warmth rather than the outdoor chill, and stays far more comfortable to sit in on a cold day.
VISUAL: A window seat insulated against the cold outside wall; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] So warm the seat from all three directions - bring heat to it, seal the draughts, and insulate against the cold. Do these and the seat stops being the coldest spot in the room and becomes one of the cosiest, a warm nook to retreat to precisely when the weather outside turns bitter.
VISUAL: A cosy warm window seat defying the cold outside; clean interior, warm light, no people, no text.

## PART THREE - HOW TO SOFTEN IT

[A] Now hardness, the second killer. A window seat is wood, and wood is hard. Sit on it bare, or with a thin pad, and it is uncomfortable within minutes. The fix is softness, and generous softness at that - a seat you sink into, not one you perch on stiffly.
VISUAL: A hard bare bench beside one with a deep soft cushion; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] Start with a thick, generous seat cushion - deep enough that you never feel the wood beneath, made of a good soft filling that holds its comfort over hours. The cushion is not a decoration but the seat's true sitting surface, and a generous one is the single biggest cure for hardness.
VISUAL: A thick generous seat cushion curing the hardness of the bench; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] Then add cushions and pillows to lean against. A hard wall at your back is as uncomfortable as a hard seat beneath you. Soft pillows against the back and sides let you settle in and lean, turning a rigid bench into an enveloping nest that welcomes you to stay a while.
VISUAL: A window seat softened with pillows to lean against; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] And use warm, soft fabrics and a cosy throw. Soft textures invite touch and add warmth both real and felt, while a throw pulled over your knees makes the seat instantly snug. Layered softness and warm fabric together turn the hardest bench into the coziest seat in the house.
VISUAL: A window seat layered with soft fabrics and a cosy throw; clean interior, warm light, no people, no text.

## PART FOUR - THE COSIEST CORNER

[A] So bring warmth and softness together. Fight the cold from all three directions - heat to the seat, sealed draughts, good insulation. Fight the hardness with a deep cushion, pillows to lean on, and warm soft fabrics. Do both, and the seat's weaknesses become its greatest strengths.
VISUAL: A window seat both warm and soft, deeply cosy; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] There is a special magic to a warm, soft window seat in cold weather - snug and cushioned within, while the rain or snow beats against the glass just beyond. That contrast, warmth against the cold world outside, is the very heart of why we love a window seat at all.
