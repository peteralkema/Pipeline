## PART ONE - WARMTH OVER BRIGHTNESS

[A] The first rule of cosy lighting is warmth over brightness. A window seat at night does not want to be flooded with hard bright light like a workspace. It wants a soft, warm, golden glow - the kind of light that relaxes the eye and invites you to settle and stay a while.
VISUAL: A window seat lit with soft warm golden light, relaxing and inviting; clean interior, warm lamplight, no people.

[A] Cool, bright, white light kills cosiness instantly. It feels clinical and awake, the opposite of restful. Warm light, the colour of candle flame or low sunset, does the reverse - it wraps the seat in a gentle glow that makes it feel snug, safe, and calm. Choose warmth every time for a seat.
VISUAL: A warm-lit cosy seat beside a cold bright one, the warm one far more inviting; clean interior, contrasting light, no people.

[A] And keep it soft, not harsh. A bare bright bulb throws hard glare and sharp shadows. Light softened through a shade, or bounced off a wall, falls gently and evenly, with no harsh point of glare. Soft warm light is the whole foundation of a cosy window seat after dark.
VISUAL: Soft warm light through a shade falling gently over a window seat; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] So before anything else, choose warm and soft. A window seat lit with a gentle golden glow is already halfway to cosy. One lit with cold or harsh light will feel unwelcoming no matter how beautifully it is built. Warmth and softness are the heart of it.
VISUAL: A window seat bathed in gentle golden light, already deeply cosy; clean interior, warm lamplight, no people, no text.

## PART TWO - LAYER THE LIGHT

[A] The secret of truly cosy lighting is layers - more than one source of soft light, at different heights, rather than a single bright fixture overhead. A room lit only from the ceiling feels flat and institutional. A seat lit by a few gentle sources at different levels feels warm and alive.
VISUAL: A window seat lit by several gentle light sources at different heights; clean interior, warm layered light, no people.

[A] The overhead light is the least cosy of all. That single bright ceiling fixture flattens everything and casts hard downward shadows. For a window seat, turn it down or off in the evening and let lower, softer sources take over. Cosiness lives close to the ground, not up on the ceiling.
VISUAL: A window seat lit by low soft sources with the harsh overhead off; clean interior, warm low light, no people.

[A] Build the layers from lower sources. A table lamp beside the seat, a wall light just above it, a string of soft lights, even a candle - each adds a gentle pool of warmth at a human level. Together they wrap the seat in overlapping soft glows rather than one flat wash.
VISUAL: A window seat wrapped in overlapping pools of warm light from low sources; clean interior, warm layered light, no people.

[A] So layer the light - several soft warm sources at different low heights, and the harsh overhead turned down. This is the single biggest secret of a cosy nook. Layered low light makes a window seat feel like a warm retreat; a single overhead makes it feel like a waiting room.
VISUAL: A window seat made a warm retreat by layered low lighting; clean interior, warm light, no people, no text.

## PART THREE - LIGHT FOR READING

[A] If the seat is for reading, it needs one more thing - its own dedicated reading light. Cosy ambient glow is lovely for resting, but too dim to read by for long without straining the eyes. A reading seat needs a focused, adjustable light aimed at the page, on top of the cosy glow.
VISUAL: A window seat with a focused reading light aimed where a book would be; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] The best reading light is positioned to fall over the shoulder onto the page, not into your eyes. A small adjustable lamp beside the seat, or a wall light angled down onto where you sit, lights the book cleanly while the softer layers keep the whole nook warm around it.
VISUAL: A reading light angled to fall onto the page from beside the seat; clean interior, warm focused light, no people.

[A] Make it adjustable if you can. The pleasure of a reading nook is choosing your light - bright and focused for reading, then dimmed to a warm glow for resting or dreaming. A seat where you can turn the reading light up and the cosy layers down, or the reverse, serves every mood.
VISUAL: A window seat with adjustable light serving both reading and resting; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] So give a reading seat two kinds of light - the cosy warm layers for atmosphere, and one focused adjustable lamp for the page. Together they make a nook you can read in for hours and rest in just as happily, simply by adjusting the light to the moment.
VISUAL: A reading nook with both cosy layers and a focused page light; clean interior, warm light, no people, no text.

## PART FOUR - GLOWING AFTER DARK

[A] So bring it together. Choose warm, soft light over bright and cold. Layer several gentle sources at low heights and turn the harsh overhead down. And if the seat is for reading, add one focused, adjustable lamp for the page. Do that, and the seat glows invitingly long after the daylight is gone.
VISUAL: A window seat glowing warmly and invitingly after dark; clean interior, cosy evening light, no people.
