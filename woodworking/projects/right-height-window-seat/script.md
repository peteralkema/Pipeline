## PART ONE - THE RULE FOR HEIGHT

[A] Here is the rule, and it's the same one every good chair obeys. A seat should sit at the height where, when you sit, your feet rest flat on the floor and your thighs run roughly level, with your knees bent at a comfortable angle. That is the height the body has always found easiest.
VISUAL: A window seat at chair height where feet rest flat and thighs run level, clean and diagrammatic; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] In plain terms, that's about the height of an ordinary dining chair - the seat set at the distance from floor to the back of your knee when you stand. Sit at that height and you rise and settle with no effort, feet planted, weight balanced.
VISUAL: A window seat matching the seat height of a dining chair shown beside it; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] Why does this height work? Because it lets your feet carry some of your weight and your legs settle at a natural angle. Too high, and your feet leave the floor, so your thighs take all the pressure and your legs dangle.
VISUAL: A comfortable mid-height seat where feet share the weight and legs settle naturally; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So the rule for height is simply this - match a good chair. The height that has made chairs comfortable for centuries makes a window seat comfortable too. The body has not changed, and neither has the height at which it likes to sit. Borrow it, and you cannot go far wrong.
VISUAL: A window seat and a dining chair sharing the same comfortable seat height; clean interior, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART TWO - THE WINDOWSILL TRAP

[A] Now the trap that catches almost everyone - building the seat to the windowsill instead of to the body. It is tempting. The sill is already there, a natural line to build up to, and it feels to have the seat meet it neatly.
VISUAL: A window seat built up to meet a windowsill rather than to a comfortable height; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] Windowsills sit sorts of heights, few of them right for a seat. Build to a high sill and the seat is too high, feet dangling. Build to a low sill and it's too low, knees rising. Letting the sill decide the height is the single most common way a window seat ends up uncomfortable.
VISUAL: A window with a high sill forcing a seat too tall to sit on comfortably; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] The seat height and the sill height are two separate things, and they don't have to meet. It is perfectly fine - often better - for the seat to sit below the sill, at proper chair height, with a little wall between the seat back and the glass.
VISUAL: A window seat set at proper height below the sill, a little wall between seat and glass; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] So free yourself from the sill. Decide the seat height by the body - by the height of a good chair - and build to that, whether or not it meets the window. A seat that sits where the body wants beats one that lines up with the glass but strains you.
VISUAL: A well-judged window seat built to body height, independent of the sill line; clean interior, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART THREE - THE CUSHION CHANGES EVERYTHING

[A] Here is the detail that undoes many a careful measurement - the cushion. You don't sit on the wooden bench. You sit on the cushion on top of it. So the height that matters is the wood plus the squashed thickness of the cushion under your weight.
VISUAL: A window seat with a cushion on top, the true sitting height raised by the cushion; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] This trips people up constantly. They build the wooden bench to perfect chair height, add a thick cushion, and now the seat is too high - the cushion has lifted them above where they meant to sit. The wood was right, but the cushion pushed the real seat too tall.
VISUAL: A bench built to correct height but made too tall once a thick cushion is added; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So build the wood a little lower than your target, to leave room for the cushion to bring it up. Measure how much your chosen cushion compresses when you sit, and drop the wooden bench by that amount. Then wood and cushion together land at exactly the right height, not the wood alone.
VISUAL: A wooden bench built lower so that wood and cushion together reach the ideal height; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] It sounds fussy, but it's the difference between a seat that fits and one that's subtly wrong. A thick soft cushion needs a lower bench; a thin firm one needs the bench closer to full height. Plan the cushion and the wood together, as one, and the finished seat lands where the body wants it.
VISUAL: A window seat where bench and cushion were planned together to sit at the perfect height; clean interior, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART FOUR - GETTING THE HEIGHT RIGHT

[A] So before you build, test again. Sit in a chair you find easy to rise from, and measure how high its seat sits with you on it, cushion and all. That combined height is your target - the height your window seat should reach once its cushion is squashed beneath you.
VISUAL: A comfortable chair measured for its true seat height as a target; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] Then work backward to the wood. Subtract the squash of your cushion, and that is the height to build the wooden bench. Ignore the windowsill entirely while you do this - the body sets the height, and the sill can meet the seat or not, as it happens to fall.
VISUAL: A wooden bench height worked out by subtracting cushion squash from the target; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] Get the height right and the seat welcomes you - you sit and rise without thought, feet flat, back easy, comfortable for hours. Get it wrong and no cushion or finish will save it. Height and depth together are the two measurements that decide whether a window seat is loved or quietly left empty.
VISUAL: A window seat at the perfect height, welcoming and easy to settle into; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.
