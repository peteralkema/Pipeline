## PART ONE - THE CLASSIC TYPES

[A] The first and most beloved is the bay window seat. A bay window pushes outward from the wall in three angled panes, and that bay creates a ready-made alcove begging for a bench. The seat wraps into the recess, wrapped in light from three sides.
VISUAL: A bay window with three angled panes and a cushioned bench nestled into the alcove beneath, bathed in light; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.

[A] The second is the built-in bench seat - a simple straight bench set beneath an ordinary flat window, built into the wall as if it grew there. It has no bay to nestle into, so its magic comes from looking permanent and intentional, a piece of the architecture rather than furniture pushed against a wall.
VISUAL: A clean straight built-in bench beneath a flat window, seamless and architectural, part of the wall; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] The third is the freestanding window seat - a bench that sits beneath a window without being built in. It is the easiest to add to any home, movable and flexible, asking no permanent change to the room.
VISUAL: A freestanding wooden bench with a cushion placed beneath a window, standalone and flexible; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.

[A] These three - the bay, the built-in, and the freestanding - are the foundations. Most window seats are a version of one of them. The bay uses an existing alcove, the built-in creates a permanent one, and the freestanding needs no alcove at all. Already, the right choice depends on the window you have.
VISUAL: Three window seat styles shown together in a calm comparison, a bay, a built-in, and a freestanding bench; clean interior study, soft light, no people.

## PART TWO - THE COSY AND CORNER TYPES

[A] The fourth type is the reading nook seat - smaller, deeper, and wrapped on more sides, designed not to sit on but to curl into. Where a plain bench lets you perch, a reading nook seat cradles you, often tucked into a recess with a wall at your back and your feet drawn up.
VISUAL: A deep cushioned reading nook tucked into a recess with pillows, cosy and enveloping; clean interior, warm soft light, no people.

[A] The fifth is the corner window seat - an L-shaped bench that turns a corner where two windows meet. It makes use of a spot most rooms waste entirely, the awkward corner, and turns it into a generous wrap-around seat flooded with light from two directions. One of the most underused ideas in home design.
VISUAL: An L-shaped bench wrapping a corner where two windows meet, light pouring in from two sides; clean interior, bright daylight, no people.

[A] The sixth is the daybed window seat - longer and deeper than a bench, wide enough to lie down on fully. It blurs the line between a window seat and a bed, a place for an afternoon nap in the sun as much as a place to sit.
VISUAL: A long deep daybed beneath a wide low window, cushioned for lying down, sunlit and luxurious; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] And these three add the dimension of how you use the seat. The reading nook is for curling, the corner seat for gathering light and turning a corner, the daybed for stretching out fully. Not just where the seat goes, but what you will do once you are in it.
VISUAL: A calm comparison of a reading nook, a corner seat, and a daybed together; clean interior study, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART THREE - THE STORAGE TYPE AND CHOOSING

[A] The seventh type cuts across all the others - the storage window seat. Lift the cushion and the bench itself is a chest, a deep hollow box for blankets, toys, or clutter. It can be a bay seat, a built-in, or a freestanding bench, but with a hinged lid it does double duty.
VISUAL: A window seat bench with its lid raised to reveal a deep storage cavity inside, practical and clever; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] This is why storage is so often the deciding feature. In a small home, a bench that is also a chest earns its space twice over. That hidden storage is one of the biggest reasons people build a window seat at all - a beautiful seat that quietly swallows the clutter of a room.
VISUAL: A neat window seat with the lid closed, storage hidden invisibly beneath the cushion, tidy and calm; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] So how do you choose? Start with your window. A bay window points you to a bay seat. A flat wall window suits a built-in or a freestanding bench. Two windows meeting in a corner call for a corner seat. Let the window you have narrow the field before anything else.
VISUAL: Different window shapes shown in a room, each suggesting a different seat type; clean interior study, soft light, no people.

[A] Then ask how you will use it. To perch and pass through, a simple bench. To curl and linger, a deep reading nook. To nap, a daybed. And if the room is short on storage, choose the version with a lifting lid. The window sets the shape; your life sets the depth.
VISUAL: A thoughtfully chosen window seat sitting perfectly in a considered room, the right type for the space; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.
