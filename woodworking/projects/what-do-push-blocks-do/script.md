## PART ONE - WHAT THEY DO

[A] A push block or push stick lets you push and hold wood as it passes a blade, so your hand grips the tool instead of the wood near the danger. It lets you feed the wood safely while keeping your fingers well away from the blade. Keeping hands clear is its aim.
VISUAL: A push block feeding wood past a cutter; clean workshop, warm light, no people.

[A] On the table saw, a push stick pushes the wood past the blade at the end of a cut, when your hand would otherwise come dangerously close. On the jointer, a push block holds the wood down and moves it over the cutter, keeping your hands clear. Each keeps hands from the blade.
VISUAL: A push stick pushing wood past a saw blade; clean workshop, soft light, no people.

[A] They also hold the wood firmly and controllably. A good push block grips the wood so you can press it down and forward steadily, guiding it accurately through the cut. This firm, controlled hold keeps the wood moving smoothly and evenly, as well as keeping your hands safe. Control comes with the safety.
VISUAL: A push block holding wood firmly and steadily; clean workshop, warm light, no people.

[A] So push blocks and sticks let you feed and control wood through a machine while keeping your hands safely away from the blade. They are simple tools with one crucial job - putting a safe barrier between your fingers and the cutter. For safe feeding at the saw and jointer, they are the basic answer.
VISUAL: A push block keeping the cut safe; clean workshop, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART TWO - HOW THEY KEEP YOU SAFE

[A] The whole point of push blocks is keeping your hands away from spinning blades, and this matters because the table saw and jointer are among the tools most linked to serious hand injuries. A moment's slip near the blade can be catastrophic; a push block removes that risk. It is a barrier against disaster.
VISUAL: A push block as a barrier from the blade; clean workshop, soft light, no people.

[A] They protect most at the riskiest moments - the end of a cut, small pieces, and narrow strips, when your hand would otherwise pass close to the blade. These are exactly when accidents happen, and exactly when a push block keeps your fingers safe. They guard the dangerous moments other habits leave exposed.
VISUAL: A push stick guarding the end of a cut; clean workshop, warm light, no people.

[A] They also help you keep good habits. Using a push block every time builds the reflex of never letting your hand near the blade, so safe practice becomes automatic. This ingrained habit is worth as much as the block itself, protecting you across every cut you ever make. Safety becomes second nature.
VISUAL: A push block building safe habits; clean workshop, soft light, no people.

[A] So push blocks keep you safe by putting a barrier between your hands and the blade, especially at the riskiest moments, and by building safe habits. For tools that can cost you fingers, this protection is priceless - and comes from the cheapest accessories in the whole workshop. Their value far exceeds their cost.
VISUAL: A push block giving priceless protection; clean workshop, warm light, no people, no text.

## PART THREE - WHY THEY IMPROVE CONTROL

[A] Beyond safety, push blocks improve control, which many beginners do not expect. By holding the wood firmly and letting you press it down and forward steadily, they keep the wood moving smoothly and evenly through the cut, without the wobble a bare hand might introduce. Firm control means a cleaner cut.
VISUAL: A push block giving smooth even feeding; clean workshop, soft light, no people.

[A] On the jointer especially, a good push block holds the wood flat and steady against the cutter, producing a cleaner, more even surface. The firm downward pressure it allows is hard to match with hands alone, so the result is better as well as safer. Control and quality rise together.
VISUAL: A push block flattening wood evenly on a jointer; clean workshop, warm light, no people.

[A] They also let you handle small and awkward pieces you could never safely feed by hand. With a push block gripping the wood, small offcuts and narrow strips can be cut safely and accurately, opening work that would otherwise be too dangerous to attempt. They extend what you can safely and cleanly cut.
VISUAL: A push block handling a small piece safely; clean workshop, soft light, no people.

[A] So push blocks improve control by holding wood firmly for smoother, cleaner cuts, especially on the jointer and with small pieces. The steady grip that keeps you safe also makes your cuts better and extends what you can do. Like the featherboard, they give both safety and quality from one simple tool.

## PART FOUR - DO YOU NEED THEM

[A] So do you need push blocks? If you use a table saw or jointer, the answer is an emphatic yes - they are essential safety tools for the machines most likely to injure your hands. Working without them is a real and needless risk. They are basic safety equipment, not optional extras.
VISUAL: Push blocks essential for saw and jointer; clean workshop, warm light, no people.

[A] They are so cheap, and easily made from scrap, that cost is never a reason to skip them. A few push blocks and sticks cost almost nothing, yet protect your hands and improve your cuts. Few purchases in woodworking are so cheap and so important. There is simply no good reason to be without them.

[A] The only mistake is not using them, or not using them every time. Their protection only works when you reach for them at every cut, especially the risky ones. Owning push blocks and building the habit of always using them is one of the simplest, wisest safety choices a woodworker can make.
