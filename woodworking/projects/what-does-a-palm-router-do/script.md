## PART ONE - WHAT IT DOES

[A] A palm router is a small, light router you guide with one hand, spinning a bit to shape wood just like its bigger cousin, but on a smaller, handier scale. It does the router's common jobs, especially edge work, with far more ease and control for its light weight. Handiness is its point.
VISUAL: A palm router shaping wood one-handed; clean workshop, warm light, no people.

[A] Its most common job is shaping and rounding edges. Running a palm router along a board's edge to round it over, bevel it, or add a profile is quick, controllable, and comfortable - the light tool is easy to guide precisely. For the everyday edge work that routers do most, the palm router excels.
VISUAL: A palm router rounding over an edge; clean workshop, soft light, no people.

[A] It also trims, chamfers, cuts small grooves, and does light template work. For all the smaller, lighter router jobs - trimming edging flush, easing an edge, cutting a shallow groove - the palm router handles them with a control and ease the big router's bulk cannot match. Light work is its comfortable home.
VISUAL: A palm router trimming an edge flush; clean workshop, warm light, no people.

[A] So the palm router does the router's lighter, common jobs - edge shaping, trimming, chamfering, small grooves - with ease and control thanks to its small size. It is not for heavy cutting, but for the everyday light router work that comes up constantly, it is quick, comfortable, and precise.

## PART TWO - WHY SMALL IS AN ADVANTAGE

[A] The palm router's small size, which seems a limitation, is actually its great advantage for common work. Being light and one-handed, it is far easier to control precisely than a heavy two-handed router, so your edge work is smoother and more accurate. For delicate, controlled jobs, less bulk means more control.

[A] It is also quicker and easier to set up and use. Grabbing a light palm router for a quick edge is far less fuss than hauling out and gripping a big router, so you actually use it more, for more jobs. This convenience is why it so often becomes the most-reached-for router in the shop.

[A] Its comfort matters for longer work too. Guiding a light router for a long edge or many pieces is far less tiring than wrestling a heavy one, so the palm router makes extended edge work pleasant rather than exhausting. Comfort and low fatigue add to why it gets used so much.

[A] So the palm router's small size brings real advantages - better control, easier use, and less fatigue on the light, common jobs that make up much router work. Far from a lesser tool, it is often the more useful one day to day. Its size is exactly why it earns so much use.

## PART THREE - PALM ROUTER VS BIG ROUTER

[A] So how does it compare to a full-size router, and do you need both? The big router has the power and capacity for heavy work - large bits, deep cuts, mounting in a router table. The palm router has the ease and control for light work. Each leads at opposite ends of router jobs.

[A] The big router wins for heavy cutting, large profiles, and table-mounted work its power and size demand. The palm router wins for light edge work, trimming, and everyday shaping, where its control and convenience shine. They are not rivals but partners covering different weights of work - heavy and light.

[A] For many woodworkers, the palm router is the better first router, since so much router work is light edge shaping it handles beautifully. The big router is added later for the heavier jobs. Starting small often makes more sense than starting big, given how much everyday work suits the palm router.
