## PART ONE - WHAT IT DOES

[A] A shop vac is a rugged, powerful vacuum built for the workshop, able to suck up sawdust, chips, debris, and even liquids that would wreck a household vacuum. Its strong suction and tough drum handle the coarse, heavy, messy waste of woodworking that ordinary cleaners cannot. It is built for the hard, dirty jobs.
VISUAL: A shop vac sucking up sawdust and debris; clean workshop, warm light, no people.

[A] Its first job is cleanup, but on a workshop scale - sweeping up piles of chips, fine dust, offcuts, and mess quickly and thoroughly. Where a broom scatters fine dust into the air, the shop vac captures it, keeping the shop cleaner and the air clearer. For workshop tidying, nothing beats it.
VISUAL: A shop vac cleaning up workshop mess; clean workshop, soft light, no people.

[A] But its more important job is dust extraction at your tools. Connected to a sander, a saw, or a router's dust port, the shop vac draws dust away as you work, capturing it at the source before it fills the air. This partnership with your tools is where much of its real value lies.
VISUAL: A shop vac extracting dust at a sander; clean workshop, warm light, no people.

[A] So the shop vac cleans up heavy workshop mess and, crucially, extracts dust directly from your tools as you work. It is both a cleaner and a dust-control partner, tough enough for anything a workshop produces. Its dual role, cleanup and extraction, is why it earns a place so early in a shop.
VISUAL: A shop vac cleaning and extracting dust; clean workshop, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART TWO - MORE THAN A CLEANER

[A] The shop vac's role as a dust-extraction partner is what raises it above a mere cleaner. Many power tools - sanders, routers, saws - have a dust port designed to connect to a vacuum, so the tool's dust is captured the instant it is made. The shop vac turns your tools into cleaner, healthier ones.
VISUAL: A shop vac connected to a tool's dust port; clean workshop, warm light, no people.

[A] This source extraction matters for health and quality. Capturing dust at the tool keeps it out of your lungs and off your work, so you breathe cleaner air and see your cut line clearly. A sander connected to a shop vac makes far less airborne dust than one running free. The difference is immediate.
VISUAL: A shop vac keeping tool dust out of the air; clean workshop, soft light, no people.

[A] It also handles wet messes, a trick no household vacuum shares. Spills, wet debris, even water can be sucked up safely by a wet-dry shop vac, making it useful far beyond dust. This versatility, dry dust, wet spills, tool extraction, and cleanup, is why it becomes one of the most-used tools in a shop.

[A] So the shop vac is far more than a cleaner - it is a dust-extraction partner for your tools, a health and quality aid, and a versatile wet-and-dry workhorse. Seeing it as merely a vacuum badly undersells it. Its partnership with your power tools is what makes it quietly indispensable.

## PART THREE - SHOP VAC VS DUST COLLECTOR

[A] People often confuse the shop vac with the dust collector, but they suit different jobs. A shop vac moves a smaller volume of air at high suction through a narrow hose - ideal for sanders, hand tools, and cleanup. A dust collector moves huge air volumes through wide hoses, suited to big chip-making machines.
VISUAL: A shop vac and dust collector compared; clean workshop, soft light, no people.

[A] The shop vac excels at the small end - sanders, routers, and tidying - where its high suction and portability shine. The dust collector rules the big machines - table saw, planer, jointer - whose chip volume overwhelms a vac. Each is built for a different scale of dust, and neither fully replaces the other.
VISUAL: A shop vac excelling at small-tool dust; clean workshop, warm light, no people.

[A] For a beginner, the shop vac usually comes first. It is cheaper, more versatile, and covers the small tools and cleanup that fill early projects, while the big dust collector waits until you own big machines. The shop vac is the sensible starting point for dust control in most new workshops.
VISUAL: A shop vac as a beginner's first dust tool; clean workshop, soft light, no people.

[A] So the shop vac and dust collector are partners at different scales - the vac for small tools and cleanup, the collector for big machines. The shop vac is the versatile, affordable starting point; the collector arrives with the big machinery. Knowing this tells you the shop vac is where dust control begins.
