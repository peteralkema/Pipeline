## COLD OPEN

[A] The single biggest thing that decides whether a window seat gets loved or ignored is not how well it is built. It is where you put it. The finest seat in the wrong spot sits empty. A plain seat in the right spot becomes the heart of the room.
VISUAL: A window seat placed perfectly in a room, clearly the heart of the space; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.

[A] Yet placement is the decision people give the least thought to. They choose a window because it is there, or because it looks good in a photo, and never ask whether that spot suits a seat at all. The wrong window makes even beautiful work go unused.
VISUAL: A window seat in a poorly-chosen spot, awkward and ignored; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] Because a room speaks, if you learn to read it. The light, the view, the way people move, the rhythm of daily life - all of them point to the one spot where a seat belongs. Find that spot, and the seat almost builds itself into a place people love.
VISUAL: A room whose light and flow point naturally to one ideal seat location; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] So let us learn to read a room - the light, the view, the traffic, and the life of the space - and find the perfect place for a window seat, in any room you have.
VISUAL: A calm study of a well-placed window seat in a considered room; clean neutral interior, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART ONE - FOLLOW THE LIGHT

[A] Start with the light, because light is what a window seat is truly for. Walk the room at different hours and notice where the good daylight falls. A seat wants gentle, generous light - bright enough to read by, soft enough to rest in. Follow the best light, and you are half done.
VISUAL: Soft generous daylight falling across one part of a room, marking the best spot; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.

[A] Beware the two extremes. A window in harsh, glaring sun becomes uncomfortable, too bright to sit in for long. A window in perpetual shade feels cold and dim, and draws nobody. The sweet spot is a window with warm, kind light for a good part of the day.
VISUAL: A window with warm kind light beside one in harsh glare, the gentle one clearly better; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] Think about when the seat will be used. A morning reading spot wants the east light of sunrise; an evening perch wants the west light of sunset. Match the light of the window to the hour you will sit there, and the seat will glow at exactly the right time.
VISUAL: A window seat catching warm light at the hour it will be used; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.

[A] So let the light lead first. The best-built seat in a badly-lit spot will always lose to a simple seat bathed in kind daylight. Find where the room's light is loveliest, and you have found where the seat wants to be.
VISUAL: A window seat placed exactly where the room's light is loveliest; clean interior, soft daylight, no people, no text.

## PART TWO - FIND THE VIEW

[A] Next, the view, because a seat gives the eye somewhere to go. Look out of each window and ask what you would be looking at, sitting there. A window onto a garden, a tree, a stretch of sky, or a lively street gives the mind somewhere pleasant to rest.
VISUAL: A window seat facing a lovely garden view, the eye drawn outward; clean interior, soft daylight, no people.

[A] A good view turns a seat into a destination. People are drawn to sit where there is something to watch - the changing light, the passing world, the seasons turning in a garden. The better the view, the more a seat pulls people toward it, again and again.
VISUAL: A window seat with an inviting view, drawing the eye to it, empty; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] Avoid the dead view. A window facing a blank wall, a fence a step away, or a neighbour's window gives the eye nothing and offers no reason to sit. If the only window is a dull one, the seat will need other charms - light, cosiness, purpose - to earn its keep.
VISUAL: A window facing a blank wall with nothing to look at, an uninviting spot; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So weigh the view alongside the light. The ideal spot has both - kind daylight and something worth looking at. Where a room offers a window with good light and a good view together, that is very often exactly where the seat belongs.
VISUAL: A window seat with both kind light and a good view, the ideal spot; clean interior, warm daylight, no people, no text.

## PART THREE - READ THE TRAFFIC AND THE LIFE

[A] Now read how people move through the room. A window seat wants to sit a little apart from the busiest paths - not blocking a doorway, not in the rush of traffic, but in a calmer eddy to the side, where someone can settle without being jostled or in the way.
VISUAL: A window seat placed in a calm eddy away from the room's main traffic; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] But not too far apart. A seat in a room nobody enters will be used by nobody. The trick is a spot that is off the main path yet still within the life of the room - near enough to feel part of things, calm enough to be a retreat.
VISUAL: A window seat off the main path yet still within the life of the room; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] Match the seat to what the room is for. A kitchen seat becomes a breakfast nook. A living-room seat becomes a reading perch. A bedroom seat becomes a quiet retreat. Let the purpose of the room shape the purpose of the seat, and place it where that purpose naturally happens.
VISUAL: A window seat suited to the purpose of its room, naturally placed; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So place the seat where daily life already flows past but does not trample - a calm spot within a used room, matched to what that room is for. A seat that fits the life of the room gets swept into daily use without anyone having to try.
VISUAL: A window seat fitting naturally into the daily life of its room; clean interior, warm light, no people, no text.

## PART FOUR - CHOOSING THE SPOT

[A] So bring the three together. Find where the light is kindest. Find where the view is best. Find the calm spot within the life of the room. Where those three overlap - good light, good view, calm and used - that is the perfect place for a window seat.
VISUAL: A window seat placed where good light, good view, and calm all meet; clean interior, warm daylight, no people.

[A] When they do not perfectly align, let light and comfort win. A seat with lovely light and a calm spot will be used even with a modest view. A seat with a grand view but harsh light or awkward placement often will not. Comfort and light are what truly keep a seat in use.
VISUAL: A window seat chosen for light and comfort over a merely grand view; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] And test before you build. Put a chair in the spot you are considering and live with it a week. If you find yourself drawn there - to sit, to read, to watch the light - the spot wants a seat. If the chair sits empty, keep looking. The room will tell you.
VISUAL: A test chair placed in a candidate spot to gauge whether it draws use; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So read the light, the view, and the life of the room, and let them point you to the one spot a seat belongs. Next time, we will look at how wide a window seat cushion should be. Until then, may you place your seats where the room wants them.
VISUAL: A calm final study of a perfectly placed window seat, at home in its room; clean neutral interior, no people, no text.
