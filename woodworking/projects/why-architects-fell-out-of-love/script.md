## PART ONE - WHY IT FELL FROM FAVOUR

[A] The window seat fell from favour because the way we built changed profoundly. The thick walls that once created deep recesses gave way to thin modern walls, and with them went the natural hollow that had always cradled the seat. A flat window in a thin wall offered no nook to sit in.
VISUAL: A thin modern wall with a flat window and no recess; clean modern interior, cool light, no people.

[A] Modern architecture also chased new ideals - open plan living, clean uncluttered lines, vast expanses of glass. The cosy enclosed nook ran against all of this. Where the modern home wanted openness and flow, the window seat offered enclosure and shelter, and it came to seem fussy, dated, against the spirit of the age.
VISUAL: An open-plan modern interior valuing flow over enclosure; clean modern interior, soft light, no people.

[A] Efficiency and cost played their part too. In an age of mass building, the window seat was an extra - a built-in feature that took time, skill, and money, offering no measurable function a developer could price. Cut it out, and a house was quicker and cheaper to build. So, quietly, it was cut.
VISUAL: A plain efficient modern room with the window seat cut for cost; clean modern interior, cool light, no people.

[A] So the window seat fell from favour for reasons of building, fashion, and money - thin walls, open plans, and the drive for efficiency all pushed it out. It came to seem a sentimental relic, out of step with a modern age that prized openness, cleanness, and cost above cosiness and character.

## PART TWO - WHAT WAS LOST

[A] It lost the intimacy of scale. A window seat brings a room down to human size, offering one person a spot that is theirs. The vast open plan, for all its grandeur, can lack these small human places - the corners and nooks where individual life happens, apart from the great shared space.

[A] And it lost a certain soul. The window seat gives a home character, warmth, a sense of care and craft. The efficient modern room, stripped of such features, can feel a little cold and anonymous - a space rather than a home. In cutting the window seat, something of the heart was cut too.

The modern home gained openness and efficiency, but lost the sheltered human nook that people, it turned out, deeply missed. [A] The window seat came back because people missed what it gave. After decades of open, glassy, efficient homes, a hunger grew for cosiness, for shelter, for the small warm nook. In an ever busier, more exposed world, the enclosed comfort of a window seat began to feel not dated, but deeply desirable.

[A] The rise of cosiness as a value drove it. Movements celebrating warmth, comfort, and the cherished small pleasures of home brought the nook back into fashion.
