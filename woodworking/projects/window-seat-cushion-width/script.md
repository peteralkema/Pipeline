## PART ONE - WIDTH AND DEPTH

[A] Start with width and depth - how far the cushion runs along the seat and how far front to back. The simple rule is that the cushion should cover the seat almost exactly, running the full length and the full depth of the bench, so no bare wood shows around it.
VISUAL: A cushion covering the full length and depth of a window bench, no bare wood showing; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] But almost exactly, not exactly. A cushion cut to the precise size of the seat will be a tight, fiddly fight to fit, and will look crammed. Leave the smallest allowance - a whisper smaller than the seat - so it drops in cleanly and sits neat, without a hard squeeze or a bulging edge.
VISUAL: A cushion sitting neatly with the smallest allowance around it, clean and unforced; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] Watch the corners and the walls. A window seat often meets walls on one, two, or three sides, and a cushion must fit into those corners cleanly. Measure into the actual corners, not just the open front, because walls are rarely perfectly square and a cushion cut to a guess will gap or jam.
VISUAL: A cushion fitted cleanly into the corners where the seat meets the walls; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] So size the width and depth to fill the seat with the barest allowance - full coverage, no bare wood, no hard squeeze - and measure into the real corners. A cushion that fills its seat cleanly is the foundation of a bench that looks finished and sits right.
VISUAL: A cushion filling its seat cleanly and completely, looking finished; clean interior, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART TWO - THICKNESS AND HEIGHT

[A] Now thickness, which is where comfort and height collide. The cushion must be thick enough that you never feel the hard wood beneath - a thin pad on a wooden bench is barely better than the bare board. Generous thickness is what turns a bench into a seat you want to stay on.
VISUAL: A generously thick cushion on a window bench, comfortable and inviting; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] But thickness raises the seat. Remember that you sit on top of the cushion, so a thick one lifts the sitting height. If the bench was already at full chair height, a thick cushion pushes the seat too tall, leaving your feet reaching for the floor. Thickness and height must be planned together.
VISUAL: A thick cushion raising the sitting height of a bench, the height lifted; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So the trick, as with height itself, is to plan the two as one. Build the wooden bench a little lower to leave room for the cushion, so that bench and cushion together land at comfortable sitting height. A thick soft cushion needs a lower bench; a thin firm one needs the bench higher.
VISUAL: A bench built lower so bench and thick cushion together reach ideal height; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] Match the thickness to the use, too. A perch for pulling on boots wants a firmer, thinner cushion. A reading nook for long lazy afternoons wants a deep, soft, generous one. Let how you will use the seat guide how thick and how soft the cushion should be.
VISUAL: A firm thin cushion beside a deep soft one, matched to different uses; clean interior, soft light, no people, no text.

## PART THREE - THE STORAGE LID PROBLEM

[A] There is one case that changes everything - a seat with a lifting storage lid. Here the cushion cannot be fixed down, because you must lift the lid to reach the storage. So the cushion has to lift too, and that shapes how you size and choose it.
VISUAL: A storage window seat with a cushion that must lift with the lid; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] For a lifting lid, a single loose cushion that comes off cleanly works best - light enough to move, sized to sit neatly back in place afterward. Avoid a cushion so deep and heavy it becomes a chore to lift, or you will stop opening the storage altogether and lose the very point of it.
VISUAL: A single loose cushion lifting cleanly off a storage seat lid; clean interior, warm light, no people.

[A] Mind the gap the lid needs. A storage lid must have room to swing up, so the cushion should not be wedged so tight against the walls that it jams the lid or must be wrestled out every time. A little clearance keeps the storage easy to use for years.
VISUAL: A cushion with clearance letting the storage lid swing up easily; clean interior, soft light, no people.

[A] So for a storage seat, choose a loose, liftable, sensibly-sized cushion with a little clearance - comfort that never fights the lid. The storage stays easy to reach, and the seat stays comfortable, instead of the two working against each other.
VISUAL: A storage window seat where cushion and lid work together easily; clean interior, warm light, no people, no text.

## PART FOUR - GETTING IT RIGHT

[A] So bring the rules together. Size the width and depth to fill the seat with the barest allowance, into the real corners. Make it thick enough for comfort, but plan that thickness with the bench height. And for a storage lid, keep it loose and liftable with clearance.

[A] Measure carefully and, where you can, have the cushion made to your real measurements rather than forcing a standard size to fit. A window seat is rarely a standard shape, and a cushion cut to the actual seat will always fit better than one bought to a guess and squeezed in.
