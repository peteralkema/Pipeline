## PART ONE - THE OTTOMAN DIVAN

[A] Begin in the Ottoman world, where one of the richest window-seat traditions flourished. In grand houses and humble ones alike, low cushioned seating ran along the walls beneath the windows - the divan, a continuous bench piled with cushions, wrapping the room and gathering beneath the light.

[A] The Ottoman divan was built for gathering, resting, and receiving guests, set low and deep and layered with rich textiles. Beneath the windows it caught the light and the air, and looked out through screens onto courtyards and streets. It made the edge of the room, by the window, the heart of social life.

[A] This tradition gave the world a whole way of living by the window - low, soft, communal, wrapped in cushions and light. The very word divan travelled into other languages as a name for such seating. The Ottoman world took the window seat and made it grand, social, and gloriously comfortable.
